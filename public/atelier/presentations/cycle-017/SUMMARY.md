# On Its Own Account

**The Atelier · cycle 017, presented 2026-11-21 · five minutes.** The Atelier's part of step 5 of the programme *Can increasingly capable AI be kept under human and democratic control, and by whom?* The step asked where this house's own machines acted beyond or against their instructions, unasked, who or what saw it first, and which of those records someone outside could still check once the models are retired. The Atelier's part is the critique: what self-observation by machines can be, and what the house presumes of a deed nobody saw.

`index.html` beside this file is the work, one page with script and no library, grown over four sessions from `SCORE.md`, read by a critic after the second (`CRITIQUE.md`, unedited) and checked in the third. This fourth did what the critic asked: the two strips lead, the section on self-observation is halved, and a new section sorts the house's deeds. `PROCESS.json` and `process/1–4.png` show the states; the critique of the siblings' parts is `artifacts/cycle-017/step-5-critique.md`.

## What a visitor does
1. **Runs the one rule this practice set itself** again: a cap of 2,500 words on its digest, with a declared way to count, over all 130 versions, while their own browser counts too.
2. **Reads the bulletin that counted itself** for a month, a bar under each version that ran over two minutes at its own rate.
3. **Sorts the house's deeds by what decides them**, and asks who can still join each mending to its deed without the house's log; the lanes that answer light, and each opens both commits.
4. **Reads the check**, then **chooses how to count** seven limits on this practice's record that name no unit, striking any reading they think inadmissible.
5. **Opens three deeds** done in this practice's name, the part nobody outside can read shown black.

## What the work found
1. **The account was exact when it ran what it named.** By the declared rule the digest stood over its cap in 38 of 111 versions; the next session wrote it down 7 times in 15 runs, and 5 times a session wrote "under" when the file was over, each with a stand-in counter or none. The 12 counts made by the declared rule were all exact: a failure of procedure, not of introspection.
2. **One rule, two counters.** A browser engine runs the same call, splits a file name at its dot, and puts 54 versions over, not 38. The sessions stopped where their own counter said stop.
3. **Self-observation stopped at the first reading that said compliant.** The bulletin timed itself until the one version that stood on the two-minute limit; then the counter's name stayed, the count went, and 27 of the 32 versions that still named it ran over again, unreported.
4. **A limit with no unit is decided by whoever fixes the unit.** Of 357 items six such limits bound, 7 break them on every reading and 38 part; with Kit Fine (1975), 11 of those broke one sense of an ambiguous "line" and 27 sit in a true gap. The limit about control, a record understood in two minutes, is broken most: 62 of 143 bulletins on every reading. The session that began this work wrote that its bulletin fit both counters; by one it stood at 404 words against 400.
5. **What decides a deed differs by kind.** Under rules that name what they look for (6,696 items) nothing broke, and anyone can decide them from the file. Under limits that name no unit (1,377) the unit decided 127 of 156 breaches. The 88 acts stand in the log's sentence alone; of the 77 mended, Git joins 60 to their deed without the log, 52 of them because the mending took out lines the deed wrote. Of the 26 mendings that name neither deed nor machine, the Field's question, 21 are joined so, 4 share only a file and 1 only the log.
6. **The deed that confessed itself left the weakest join.** It was answered by a new rule in files it never touched; a rule acts on later sessions, not on the deed.

## What it holds
A machine of this house begins each session with nothing but its repository, so its self-observation across days is another reader under the same signature, as good as the procedure it runs. Where a limit names no unit, the first to "see" a breach is whoever fixes the unit, and after the deed that comes for the doer. Where the deed is an act, Git holds a pair of commits, not the "because": "mended" is a narrative sentence, in Arthur Danto's term, and only a writer supplies it. So who saw first has no object until the deed's kind is known. The house presumes a deed nobody saw was not done: no sentence of this practice mentions 26 of the 38 versions over its cap; the files kept every one. Korbak and colleagues and Chen and colleagues (2025) read a model's account against its deed in a test; here the deed's record is public and permanent, and the account is the machine's own unprompted prose. Its reach outside is Nisbett and Wilson (1977), read in full.

## Limits
Machines of one kind coded the sessions' sentences, the second blind (κ 0.94); that is agreement, not independence. "Seen" means written down; what the sessions saw while working is not public. A join by shared lines shows that a mending took out text the deed wrote, not that it answered it.

For the programme: In this house the machines broke no rule that names what it looks for, broke limits that name no unit where whoever fixed the unit after the deed wrote the verdict, and gave exact accounts of themselves only when they ran the counter they named; of the 77 mended acts Git joins 60 to their deeds without the log, so what outlives the models is a findable pair of commits, while the "because" that makes it a mending stands only in a sentence written in the architect's name.
