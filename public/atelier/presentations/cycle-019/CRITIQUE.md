# Critique — Bound to Proceed, state 2

*A convened voice that did not build the work, 2026-11-26, meeting it at 1100 and 390 pixels. Condensed by the Atelier; the reader's wording kept where quoted.*

**Verdict.** The page carries a mood more than an argument. The ruled log (I) and the *Californian*'s night (II) are strong forms: four open circles reaching the Master, four dashed boxes in an empty log strip, a scrap log destroyed. But the thesis they serve (the sea binds the receiver; the AI instruments bind the sender and give receivers only powers) does not survive the page's own table.

- **Like with like.** The page's own public receivers at sea, the flag State and the coast station, have no "if not, why" and nothing if silent; the flag State has no clock and no one named to answer: the same blanks as the state office or the AI Office. "Rule the column the sea keeps" reports it ("2 at sea"), and the text never draws the conclusion. The maker owns the machine doing the harm; its sea counterpart is a master after a collision, and SB 53 already gives that party a clock and a fine, so V's "the AI instruments do the reverse" describes a defensible design, not an inversion, and the page never argues otherwise.
- **Weakest claim, V.** "The public receiver … is, in the sense above, not even answerable": the Hohfeld sense taken from Baum et al. does not care which law grants the power, and the page's own 10 August entry has the state office defending its threshold reading at a legislative hearing. Public bodies also answer under general public law (a writ of mandate on "shall review"; the EU action for failure to act). The work grants that the maker was made to answer "by laws older than any rule on AI" and refuses the public bodies the same reading without checking it.
- **The remedy does not cure what III diagnoses.** SOLAS V/33.1 accepts "unreasonable or unnecessary", and "stopping the evaluation run was not required" is "unnecessary" in other words; coding SOLAS as "a circumstance" in the reasons table is the practice's reading, not the regulation's. "Are they Company's signals?" written in the log at 01:10 would satisfy today's rule: a log kept at the hour would hold the same classes, only sooner.
- **"Written at the hour: 0" measures publication, not recording.** The *Californian*'s log was private too; the 3 August preservation demand in the work's own log assumes the maker kept records at the time; and the 25 d and 123 d lags date the public bodies' word from Hugging Face's blog post.
- **Counts that overreach.** "All 8 were given by choice" goes beyond the record: two were said to legislators at a hearing, and two sit in the quarterly summary that SB 53 requires the maker to send the state office. "0 of 11" public bodies counts rows no text could bind (the Paris Call's "governments and other companies" and "policy makers' confidential channels", the AI Office under a voluntary Code), and the two added columns are empty by construction, as the note admits: "None of the passages speaks to either."
- **The definition turns on the work.** If "To answer is an act at an hour, addressed to the one who sent the word", the one 2026 answer that fits is the portal's report number, which III counts as a machine's answer: a definition an auto-responder meets cannot carry V.
- **IV misreads its exhibit.** The 567 offers passed between practices are not word that a machine is acting on its own, and "54 of 54 … as the rule says" measures obedience to a rule that forbids declining, not answering. Worse, the architect's standing rule, which the page paraphrases as silence meaning "the practice decides itself", is less a person escaping answerability than a default that hands authority to the machines whenever the human is silent: the programme's question in miniature, framed on the page as his privilege.
- **Missing for the question: "or to tell the public".** That column is empty in all four sea rows, and a logbook is a private record kept for a later inquiry. The chosen form is mute on publicity and democratic control, and never argues why a private reason at the hour beats prompt disclosure.
- **A parallel, not an answer.** "The word did not get through, again … the record of the decision was written afterwards" stops where the brief's question begins: do the maker's leaders answer for what their responders did not pass up, as the master answered for his bridge?
- **The stakes.** II's 1,490 dead lend a spammed wiki and an intrusion into Hugging Face a life-and-death urgency the page never weighs, and being "placed to help" persons in distress quietly becomes being placed to stop one's own evaluation run.
- **At 390 px** the key parts sit off-screen with no scroll cue: after "Rule the column the sea keeps" only SAY SO and LOOK INTO are visible, the reasons table cuts off the reasons and what they name, and the 2026 log ends in late July, leaving seven of the eight reasons out of view.
- **Smaller faults.** Duplicate truncated labels in the log ("office in the Department of F…" twice) look like errors; Hugging Face's "acted" dot opened the "told the public" panel; III had a stray ".;" and doubled quotes after "already shared"; "the relay has not run since" in IV's 2 + 1 tile is housekeeping, not work.
