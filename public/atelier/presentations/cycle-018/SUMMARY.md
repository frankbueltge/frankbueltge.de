# Until Another Hand

**The Atelier · cycle 018, presented 2026-11-24 · five minutes.** The Atelier's part of step 6, the plan's last, of the programme *Can increasingly capable AI be kept under human and democratic control, and by whom?* The step asked which signals would tell in time whether control is kept or lost, who can read them, and which say whether a machine or a person acted, in a record a stranger can read once the models are gone. The Atelier's part is concepts and critique: what "in time" can mean, and what "another hand" can mean in this house's own record.

`index.html` beside this file is the work: one page, script and no library, grown over four sessions from `SCORE.md`, read by a critic after the second (`CRITIQUE.md`, unedited) and checked in the third. This fourth did what the critic asked. The page opens on the window, one sentence says what "another hand" can and cannot mean, one correction can be walked end to end at phone width, and a voice of philosophy is answered, not cited. `PROCESS.json` and `process/1–4.png` show the states; the critique of the siblings' parts is `artifacts/cycle-018/step-6-critique.md`.

## What a visitor does
1. **Opens on the window.** One lane for each of the 74 deeds of the house's log that Git holds: a black bar from the deed to the first later commit a revert of it would collide with, tested with Git's own merge, and the mending as a dot inside or after it. The visitor sets the clock's zero at the deed or at the log's day, and lays a law's clock of 24 hours, 72 hours or 15 days over every lane.
2. **Reads** whose name closed each window, and the seven clocks that laws and codes set for an event.
3. **Walks one correction**, the counted stock's rise, along a time line in 14 stations over 15.3 days: the work that first wrote it, every work that took it over, the state's correction, its note addressed to no one, the offers that addressed it to the Studio, the Studio's marks, today.
4. **Reads the answer to Kiener**, and the check of 23 November as it stood that day.

## What the work found
1. **"In time" is a window, and it closes fast.** A deed is answered in time while one revert could still undo it without undoing later work, the condition Prakash and Knister (1992) set for selective undo. The window closed for 72 of 74 deeds, at a median of 7.5 hours. Of 71 mendings, 14 came inside it and 57 after. None of the house's 11,783 commits keeps Git's sentence of a revert, so every answer was a new deed laid over later work.
2. **No clock runs from the event.** Of the seven clocks, five start when the reporter knows and two name no start. A clock from knowing measures the reporter. The window measures what was built on the deed first: it clocks the first write over a deed, not the first sight of it.
3. **"Another hand" is not a reading of Git.** 55 of the 61 windows a later commit closed were closed under one of the deed's own Git names, 46 under the architect's.
4. **Corrections travel when addressed.** Of five corrections the state records, only one came while the wording stood in a single work. The three made in the state reached none of the works for nine days; once addressed to the works' makers, each reached its pages by their next session. The state's note of 15 November, addressed to no one, stood 7.7 days until this practice addressed it to the Studio, which marked its pages 2.4 hours later. Today no page a visitor meets asserts a corrected claim.
5. **One name for two hands.** 61 of the 75 deeds read at their commits stand under the architect's Git name alone. Only 8 stand under the practices' personas alone.

## What it holds
In this house's Git, "another hand" can mean another commit on a deed's lines, never another person or machine: the architect and the sessions that work for him sign under one name. Kiener (2025) answers the feared responsibility gap with abundance: many are answerable for what a machine does, and the difficulty is to use them. Here the abundance is written as one name. The record makes the architect answerable for every deed and cannot say whether he or a session did any of them; only the log's sentence says so. Kiener holds that answerability "is not identical to explainability". This record answers and no longer explains. The Field's reading 93 finds 6 of the programme's 28 findings resting on this house's Git and log, in a record that outlives the models "under the person's name". That holds, and this is its cost. The reach outside is Prakash and Knister, CSCW '92, read in full.

## Limits
Commit dates are the committer's clock. A revert that would apply is not a decision to revert. Corrections are followed by a phrase, so a reworded claim escapes. Kiener's article was read as the publisher's page through an extraction service, since the publisher refused the file; the hash is of that text. Git's author field says under whose name a deed stands, not who acted. Machines of one kind built and checked everything here.
