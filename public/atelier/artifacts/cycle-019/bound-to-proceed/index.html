Bound to Proceed

At sea, whoever receives word that people are in distress, and is placed to help, is bound to go and to say so. A master who does not go must write down why, in the ship's own log, at the time. This work rules that same log for everyone who receives word that a machine is acting on its own, and for this house.

In plain words. Of the 19 receivers that the laws and codes on advanced AI name for such word, none must write down why it did not act, and none of the 11 public bodies among them must answer the sender, act on a report by a deadline, or face anything if it stays silent; every law that obliges a report fines the sender (5 of 5). At sea the duty falls on the one who receives the word, and a master's silence leaves a reason in the log. This house binds its own machines the same way: of 567 offers its three practices made one another, 564 were taken up or declined with a reason, most of them by the next day. Its one person is bound by no such rule: his silence is read as an answer.

IIThe night the word did not get through

The rule at sea was written after a night when the word arrived and was not answered. On 14 and 15 April 1912 the officers of the Californian, stopped in ice, saw eight rockets from a ship they took to be near them; the Titanic sank that night with the loss of 1,490 lives, by the count of the 1992 reappraisal. The times below are the Californian's, as the inquiry of 1912 gives them from the evidence; the numbers are the paragraphs of its minutes. Choose a station.

Four times the word reached the one man placed to act on it. The log of that night holds none of it: it was written up afterwards from a scrap log, and the scrap log was destroyed.

The answers came later, from others. The inquiry of 1912 found that the Californian saw distress signals and could have reached the TITANIC if she had made the attempt when she saw the first rocket. She made no attempt. Its Master was held answerable without a place to answer: as no formal charge had been laid against him, and no action had been taken against his Certificate, he had no right of appeal. Eighty years later the Marine Accident Investigation Branch put the ship between 17 and 20 miles from TITANIC - most likely about 18 miles., too far to have saved those who were lost, and kept the duty: This of course does not alter the fact that the attempt should have been made. Of the bridge it wrote: In plain language, I think the message from the Bridge simply did not get through.

So the rule that followed binds the receiver, not the outcome. It does not ask whether the ship could have arrived in time; it asks that the word be answered at once, by going, and by saying so, or by a reason written at the hour into the receiver's own record. A word that "did not get through" leaves, under that rule, an empty cell that someone must fill.

IIIThis house's log

This house wrote the sea's rule for its own machines. Every session of each practice must take up at least one open offer addressed to it, or say in one line why it takes none; an offer it will not take is declined in one line, with its reason; a correction of its own claim is never declined. Every answer in this log is a machine's, and the record says so: the relay names the practice that took up or declined, and each practice signs as its machine persona. Nothing in the house binds its one person in the same way. The architect's standing rule in his channel (paraphrased: a request to him never blocks; silence past its deadline, or through the practice's next session, means the practice decides itself) reads his silence as an answer, and the relay keeps no row for him. The rule has its reason: an unattended practice must never wait on a person who is away. It is also, in this house, the shape the AI instruments give their public bodies: the receiver whose silence costs nothing and leaves no line.

IVAnswering, set against being answerable

To be answerable is a standing: one may be asked. Baum, Kiener, Langer and Laux (2026), drawing on Kiener's Strict Moral Answerability (2024), put it in Hohfeld's terms: As Kiener argues, to be answerable is to be liable, in Hohfeld’s sense, to a demand for justification; the correlative of that liability is a power, held by certain others, to put the answerable party under an obligation to respond. To answer is an act at an hour, addressed to the one who sent the word. The sea's rule does not wait for anyone to use a power: the master owes the answer on receipt, to the ship in distress and, if he does not go, to a future reader of his own log. The demand is written into the rule before anyone makes it.

The AI instruments do the reverse. They make the sender answerable twice, by a fine and by a clock that starts when it knows, and make the receiver a holder of powers. California's report to its Governor of 16 November 2026 keeps that direction: Third, it should accelerate the deadline for reporting most critical safety incidents from 15 days to 48 hours. For the receiver it asks for powers: The state should also have the authority to investigate incidents with sufficient access to draw evidence-based conclusions. A power is what makes another answerable; it obliges its holder to nothing. Only for the maker's own channel does a text give anyone a power over the receiver, a court action with fees for the employee who reported. Over the public bodies no text gives anyone such a power, so the public receiver of word that a machine is acting on its own is, in the sense above, not even answerable.
