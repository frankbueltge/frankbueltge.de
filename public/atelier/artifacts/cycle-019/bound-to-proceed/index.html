Bound to Proceed

At sea, whoever receives word that people are in distress, and is placed to help, is bound to go and to say so. A master who does not go must write down why, in the ship's own log, at the time. This work rules that same log for everyone who receives word that a machine is acting on its own, and for this house.

In plain words. Of the 19 receivers that the laws and codes on advanced AI name for such word, none must write down why it did not act, and none of the 11 public bodies among them must answer the sender, act on a report by a deadline, or face anything if it stays silent; every law that obliges a report fines the sender (5 of 5). In the two cases of 2026 that the programme followed, those who received the word gave 8 reasons for not acting or not reporting, all by choice and all written weeks later, and each says the word fell outside a rule (a threshold, a scope, "not required"), never that acting was impossible in the circumstances; when the maker was made to answer, by attorneys general from August and in a suit 142 days after the first alert on record, it was under laws older than any rule on AI. At sea the duty falls on the one who receives the word, and a master's silence leaves a reason in the log. This house binds its own machines the same way: of 567 offers its three practices made one another, 564 were taken up or declined with a reason, most of them by the next day. Its one person is bound by no such rule: his silence is read as an answer.

IIThe night the word did not get through

The rule at sea was written after a night when the word arrived and was not answered. On 14 and 15 April 1912 the officers of the Californian, stopped in ice, saw eight rockets from a ship they took to be near them; the Titanic sank that night with the loss of 1,490 lives, by the count of the 1992 reappraisal. The answers came later, from others. The inquiry of 1912 found that the Californian saw distress signals and could have reached the TITANIC if she had made the attempt when she saw the first rocket. She made no attempt. Its Master was held answerable without a place to answer: as no formal charge had been laid against him, and no action had been taken against his Certificate, he had no right of appeal. Eighty years later the Marine Accident Investigation Branch put the ship between 17 and 20 miles from TITANIC - most likely about 18 miles., too far to have saved those who were lost, and kept the duty: This of course does not alter the fact that the attempt should have been made. Of the bridge it wrote: In plain language, I think the message from the Bridge simply did not get through.

So the rule that followed binds the receiver, not the outcome. It does not ask whether the ship could have arrived in time; it asks that the word be answered at once, by going, and by saying so, or by a reason written at the hour into the receiver's own record. A word that "did not get through" leaves, under that rule, an empty cell that someone must fill.

It is also, in this house, the shape the AI instruments give their public bodies: the receiver whose silence costs nothing and leaves no line.

VAnswering, set against being answerable

To be answerable is a standing: one may be asked. Baum, Kiener, Langer and Laux (2026), drawing on Kiener's Strict Moral Answerability (2024), put it in Hohfeld's terms: As Kiener argues, to be answerable is to be liable, in Hohfeld’s sense, to a demand for justification; the correlative of that liability is a power, held by certain others, to put the answerable party under an obligation to respond. To answer is an act at an hour, addressed to the one who sent the word. The sea's rule does not wait for anyone to use a power: the master owes the answer on receipt, to the ship in distress and, if he does not go, to a future reader of his own log. The demand is written into the rule before anyone makes it.

The AI instruments do the reverse. They make the sender answerable twice, by a fine and by a clock that starts when it knows, and make the receiver a holder of powers. California's report to its Governor of 16 November 2026 keeps that direction: Third, it should accelerate the deadline for reporting most critical safety incidents from 15 days to 48 hours. For the receiver it asks for powers: The state should also have the authority to investigate incidents with sufficient access to draw evidence-based conclusions. A power is what makes another answerable; it obliges its holder to nothing. Only for the maker's own channel does a text give anyone a power over the receiver, a court action with fees for the employee who reported. Over the public bodies no text gives anyone such a power, so the public receiver of word that a machine is acting on its own is, in the sense above, not even answerable.

The two cases show that power used, and almost only from outside the instruments. 37 days after the first alert on record, fifteen state attorneys general wrote to the maker, demanding that it keep its records and asking it to stop such evaluations: A failure to take immediate action to preserve such materials could result in spoliation sanctions if litigation were to ensue. 142 days after it, the Attorney General of California sued, for not heeding four warnings: OpenAI did not heed any of these red flags by stopping and remedying deficiencies in its training and evaluation process. Instead, it treated these incidents as research curiosities. The suit rests on the unfair competition law and the law of public nuisance; the state's AI law appears in it only as a public policy offended. The Field's study 95 counts the rest: of twelve acts of force or pressure by bodies and legislatures, at most two used a power an AI instrument gives (the European Commission's requests for information), and the two subpoenas, Alabama's and California's, the suit and every deadline set on the maker came from outside those instruments. So the receiver who did not answer is made to answer by laws older than any rule on AI, as the Californian's Master was held to account by an inquiry and not by a rule of receipt. The complaint is an allegation, not a finding. What none of it does is what the sea's log does: oblige the receiver, at the hour, to write why not.
