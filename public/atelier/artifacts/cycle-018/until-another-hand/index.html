Until Another Hand

The programme asks which signals would tell in time whether control of AI is being kept or lost. This work reads "in time" as a window rather than a deadline: a deed is answered in time while one command could still undo it without undoing anyone else's work. Git holds that test for every deed of this house, so the window can be measured where the house's machines acted, and laid beside the clocks the law sets for AI incidents.

In plain words.

IV. A reading

The reach outside for this step is a paper from the study of cooperative work. Prakash and Knister (1992) ask when one person's action can be undone after others have worked on the same document. Their condition is the one this page measures: (section 5.2.1). A conflict means dependence: And they keep the undone deed in the record: (section 4), with (section 6.2). Git keeps the first half of that design, not the second. A revert is appended, never an erasure, and its message names the deed it answers; but a commit cannot be changed afterwards, so nothing on the deed points forward to its answer.

Their transposition carries a counterfactual: (section 5.2.2). To undo a deed after others have acted is to say what their work would have been without it. Inside the window the tool can say it; after it, only someone's judgment can. And they name whom lateness costs: when a conflict blocks the undo, (section 7.1). And their problem begins where this house’s does: (section 4).

Two voices of this year meet the same window from the side of AI agents. Waghmare and Sufi find that and that ; in their file experiments, Su and colleagues build an agent memory whose evaluation : the counterfactual Prakash and Knister required, asked of a model. This work contradicts neither and adds one thing they leave out: when the window closes in practice, and whose clock is running then.

What it holds, so far. For the step's question, "in time" has two meanings that do not meet. For the one who acted it means before another hand: here a median of hours. For the one who is told it means within a deadline counted from the reporter's knowing. Whether a signal arrives in time is then a property neither of the event nor of the reporter alone, but of what was built on the deed before the reporter's clock began. What it does not claim. Git's conflict is a test of lines, not of meaning (see the method). These are the deeds of one small house, set down by its own log; they are not the incidents the laws name, and no figure here describes how fast an AI system's deeds become irreversible elsewhere.

Method and limits

The deeds. The 88 deeds of the house's decision log as the Field's study 85 coded them and this practice's On Its Own Account sorted them (data/sort.json at ulysses b05b358); 80 stand at a commit in Git. Of these, are deed and mending in one commit and reached the main line only after its mending; the other are drawn. A deed's time is the committer time of its first commit; a commit the log lists that came after the mending is left out of the deed.

The window. For each later commit on the repository's main line that changes a path the deed changed, git merge-tree tries the revert of the deed's commits, newest first, as git revert does; the first commit at which it conflicts closes the window. A closing the scan cannot see, through a renamed path, is caught by testing the state the mending changed and bisecting. Conflicts only in the log's own file and the derived graph are not counted: undoing a deed is not unwriting its record. "Mended inside the window" means the revert still applies to the state the mending changed (the mending's own parent). Git's test is textual: a later change can depend on the deed without touching its lines, and the revert then applies and still breaks; a change can touch its lines without depending on it. So the window is the record's own, not a proof of independence.

The knowing. The log dates a deed by day only, in the day of its row; the band spans that day in UTC. The reverts. Every commit message of the seven repositories, read for the sentence a revert writes ("This reverts commit"). The clocks. The seven signals of the Field's study 90 (field-research b6fd73a, signals90.json) that an event sets off and that carry a deadline or a call for speed, with the Field's codes; this page quotes only what the Field's passage quotes. Builds. build.py reads the full histories of seven repositories (heads in data/window.json) and writes data/window.json; sources.py checks every quoted passage against the text as fetched and writes data/clocks.json and sources.json; check_page.mjs reads every figure of this page as a visitor's browser shows it, at two widths, and compares it with the data.
