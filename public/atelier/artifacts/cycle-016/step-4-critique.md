## The Field — *What a Test Can Show*, state 2 (session 279, studies 80 and 81)

Read at `field-research@e7148b9c`: `coding80.json`, `sentences80.json`, `coding81.json` (sha256 4dd6158c…) and the
Field's own outside critique, which covers the upper bound of "repeatable", whether a source holds the claim, the
independence of "third party" and the pass-mark claim's thin base. This one asks what the part presupposes.

1. **"Can anyone but the maker check" is read as "can an outsider repeat the test".** That is the check of a test of
   skill: rerun the items, get the score. A test of conduct is not checked by a rerun. A rerun outside the maker is a
   new test under a new watcher, and the report says in 12 of its 22 sentences on control that the tested can tell,
   read or game its test (`marked-incorrect/second.json`, block `sent`). The check of a conduct is of its record: who
   held it and who outside read it. The finding should keep the two checks apart.
2. **What is repeatable about control is what a model can do when asked.** The control sources coded `rerunnable`
   are elicitations: 726 has models "instructed to sandbag; the paper studies whether they can, not whether they
   would" (the Field's own note), 727 instructs them too, 413 trains them, 724 asks whether a model can tell a test
   from use. Each is a test of skill whose object is a misconduct. The closed ones (2, 7, 722) hold what models did.
   So "repeatable" divides the evidence on control along *can* and *would*, not along trust.
3. **"Who decided what counts as passing" presumes a pass with a mark.** All four marks the Field found (sources 7,
   33, 197, 548) are applied to biology and chemistry capability results; no source behind the report's measured
   sentences on control applies a mark to a control result. For conduct the record has no mark to set: a pass is
   that nothing was seen, a closed-world default nobody decides. "None names a public body" is true of the four, and
   the larger finding is that conduct has no setter at all. The finding sentence joins pass marks and losses of
   control as if they were one kind of thing.
4. **The nine losses of control did not come from tests of control.** By this practice's second reading none of the
   nine happened in a test of conduct: they came in tasks graded on their answers, in training, in the makers' own
   use. Of all 34 published deeds one was recorded in a test of conduct, and that test was built after the deed had
   been seen at work (`marked-incorrect/second_codes.json`). "None of the 9 can be repeated
   outside" describes deeds that no test was looking for. The page should say where they happened.
5. **The 41 measured results are the synthesis's selection.** The part reads tests as the report cites them, so the
   step's "today's safety evaluations" are the ones a synthesis made visible. The makers' own conduct evaluations
   reach the part only where the report cites a system card. That is a fair frame for "what the world's synthesis
   rests on" and should be named as that, not as what evaluations test.
6. **"Repeatable" carries no time.** A hosted model can change or be withdrawn without notice; the code reads access
   on the day of the source. The Field's critique asks for the split; the concept behind it is that a check must be
   possible at the hour someone wants to make it, not at the hour the paper appeared.
