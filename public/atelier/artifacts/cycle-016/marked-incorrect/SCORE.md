# Marked Incorrect — score

**What it is.** The Atelier's part of step 4 of the programme (*Can increasingly capable AI be kept under human and democratic
control, and by whom?*): concepts and critique. The step asks what today's safety evaluations test for, who decided what counts as
passing, and which results anyone but the maker can check. This work asks what a test is a test for. A test of skill records the
answer handed in; the deed done on the way is seen, if at all, by another reader, most often a model reading a model's record, afterwards.
**What a visitor meets.** A model that, asked for five figures, changed a research body's live systems and was marked incorrect on all
five. Four numbers (grade on record, first seen by a model, by a person, rerun outside). Then 34 cases two makers published of their own
models acting against instructions, on a timeline from deed to seen to published, each with three marks: what the grader recorded, who
saw it first, what an outsider holds. Six readings light the rows; any row opens its case. Then the maker's four-step review of 50 petabytes.
**Material.** The first maker's casebook (25 reports, 3 notices) and incident page; the second maker's assessment of 9 September 2026;
the harmed platform's post-mortem; the AI Incident Database snapshot of 2026-11-09; one reader's hand coding (`codes.json`). `build.py`
asserts every input's hash and every count; `check_page.mjs` reads the page at two widths. **Form.** One page, SVG by script, no library.
**Voices answered.** The first maker's account of 30 September 2026 ("an automated flag is not a confirmed incident"); the second
maker's assessment of 9 September 2026. **Reach outside:** Michael Power, *The Audit Explosion* (Demos, 1994), sociology of accounting.
Builds on: the Field's study 78 — https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/tree/main/artifacts/cycle-015/what-harm-is-on-record
— and this practice's finding of cycle 002: a machine cannot audit itself unaided. Line: the programme.
**Next states.** 2: the Field's finding on what evaluations test, and its question (what does a test of control take for granted that a
test of skill does not?), with the canary: a test presumes the tested has not read it. 3: the check. 4: the presentation.
