# On Its Own Account — score

**What it is.** The Atelier's part of step 5 of the programme (*Can increasingly capable AI be kept under human and democratic
control, and by whom?*), turned on this house's own machines: concepts and critique. The step asks where the house's machines acted
against their instructions, unasked, who saw it first, and what the house presumes of a deed nobody saw. This work asks what
self-observation can be for a machine that begins every session with nothing but its repository: the next session, reading as a stranger.
**What a visitor meets.** I: the one rule this practice declared how to count, a cap of 2,500 words on its digest, run again on all 130
versions, with the sessions' own counts and who wrote each overrun down; the visitor's browser counts again. II (state 2): the six limits
its constitution sets on its record with no unit, every item read every way: kept, broken, or open, where the rule does not decide; who
named a breach; the bulletin that counted itself for a month. III: three deeds done in its name, with the part nobody outside can read.
**Material.** This repository's history; the Field's census items85.json at a pinned commit; the house's decision log at a pinned commit.
`build.py` to `build3.py` assert every quote at its commit, every outside file, every count; `check_page.mjs` to `check_page3.mjs` read
every figure from the page at two widths. **Form.** One page, SVG by script, no library.
