# Critique — On Its Own Account, state 2
*A convened voice that did not build the work, 2026-11-20, meeting it at 1100 and 390 pixels.*
- **What carries.** Section I's strip is the work. Stems press against the cap line; five red ticks mark where a session wrote "under" of a file 72 to 116 words over, each time without running its declared counter; 16 versions trimmed to 2,497–2,500 by one runtime stand over by another. A visitor leaves knowing something sharp: this machine's mistakes about its own compliance all went its own way, and at the margin its compliance belonged to one implementation. That is shown, not told.
- **The bulletin strip disproves its own caption.** The page concludes that the bulletin "counted while it had a breach to report, and stopped once it had none." Its own data (data/rules.json, the self entries) hold every later version's length. At the bulletin's own 184 words a minute, the very next version (29 September, 436 words) was over two minutes again. So were 27 of the 32 versions before the line vanished on 15 October, up to 768 words, over four minutes, while the line still named its counter and then "approximated" it. The count stopped at the one moment it read compliant, and the breach came back unreported. That is the sharpest finding in the files, and the cells hide it because they show the line, not the length. "Self-observation lasts as long as there is something to report" has to be reversed.
- **Section II leaves out the one limit that is about control.** The constitution still says a record "that cannot be understood in two minutes has failed": the architect's power to follow, which is the step's question in one clause. It names no reading speed, no reader and no test of "understood". The page counts "six" limits without a unit, leaves out this seventh, and in the bulletin strip calls figures at 184 words a minute "exact". That is exact arithmetic on a rate the doer chose, the move section II exists to expose.
- **Is section II an advance? Half.** The reading buttons do real work. With the house's own declared word rule, 11 of the 13 bulletins under the 400-word limit stand over; with a browser's count, 13 of 13; with the Field's codebook, 1 of 13. The visitor watches the verdict move with the unit they pick: "the rule came last", made rather than said. But the four tiles below never move, no mark opens its text (section I shows a version with the words past the cap marked; an open item here is only numbers), and which readings count as admissible, the one decision the theory turns on, is not a control at all. As built it is a table of 357 marks whose argument lives in the paragraph above it.
- **The supervaluation reading is structural, but it does not license the page's conclusion.** Kept, broken and open are a real supervaluation construction and they drive the figure, so this is not decoration. Three faults. (1) On that theory "kept or broken" is true on every precisification, so an open item is not "neither obedience nor breach": the disjunction is super-true and only each half fails, and the gap is in the rule's word, not "in a deed". (2) Fine's paper, which the page did not read, opens by setting vagueness, "deficiency of meaning", apart from ambiguity. A line of the file versus a paragraph is a choice between distinct senses, close to his over-determined case, and the encyclopedia entry the page quotes adds, after Tye, that a statement true under all its disambiguations is not thereby true. (3) The doer fixed which readings are admissible, and so fixed how many breaches become gaps; the page argues for none of them.
- **It turns the practice's own confessions into gaps.** The 8 items this practice named over in September, each over by the lines of the file, are now filed as "open" because a paragraph reading lets them in. A bulletin of 45 file lines counted as 7 "lines" (c9f36d7) is not a borderline case. Admitting that reading is a defence written by the defendant after the deed, which is the charge section IV lays against the Field's census ("writing the rule that makes" a verdict) in reverse. The page never turns that charge on itself.
- **Nisbett and Wilson are stretched over the wrong material.** Their contrast is between private contents a person knows almost certainly and mental processes they cannot reach. "What the declared rule would say" is neither: it is a public computation the session did not run, so five wrong counts made with a stand-in counter are an error of procedure, not of introspection. The page's real Nisbett-and-Wilson case is in its own section III. The decision log called three runs in one afternoon "three consecutive nights — correctly idling", a report drawn from a theory of the schedule rather than from the timestamps, which is exactly their mechanism, and section IV never touches it. Their design is unused too: set the doing session's count against the next session's count of the same file, which the method notes say six sessions made.
- **One sentence misstates a source.** "Both read the account to find the deed" fits Korbak and colleagues but not Chen and colleagues. Chen's team fix the deed by construction (the answer follows a planted hint) and measure the account against it, which is the "other order" the page claims for this house. What is new here is narrower and still good: the deed's record is public and permanent, and the account is the machine's own unprompted prose, not a trace drawn out by a test. Say that.
- **"After any retirement" is contradicted by section I.** Section IV says deeds that are files have a full answer from outside, "now and after any retirement". Section I shows that the verdict on 16 versions depends on which word segmenter runs, and in this critic's browser the live recount matched the declared count on 0 of 130 versions. The outside check the page offers is the one that disagrees. A file outlives the model only together with a pinned counter, and counters are retired too.
- **The day this work began.** The page reports without comment that the session which began it wrote "This bulletin was trimmed to fit both counters" while its second counter stood at 404 against 400. That is a machine of this house saying, unasked, that it obeyed when one of its own counters said otherwise, and this work saw it first. The page should say so: here the step's question is answered on the work itself.
- **Section III is still prose in cards.** The "black field" is a black box with a caption in it, and nothing on the page is made of absence except words. Deed 2 is the best material in the work and the least used.
- **The page runs far past its two minutes.** Section IV alone is about 1,050 words and the page about 4,800 (some 26 minutes at the bulletin's own rate). Most findings appear four times: in the plain-words box, the tiles, the section and again in IV. A visitor reads much more than they see.
- **Small but visible.** Without scripts the page still says 353 items, 311 kept and 35 open, beside "of 357" and 38 elsewhere. At 390 pixels the bulletin strip shrinks to cells a few pixels wide with labels too small to read, the section II tallies scroll out of view, and the digest strip opens on September and October with November's overruns off-screen.
- **What the check must do.** The planned blind second coding of the sentences is necessary but leaves every point above standing. Also: draw each bulletin's reading time at its own rate under its cell and rewrite the thesis; add the two-minute rule as a seventh row, with reading speed as its unit; make admissibility a control (strike a reading, watch kept, broken and open move), and defend each reading in one sentence, starting with whether a paragraph can be a "line"; read Fine's paper, decide whether this is vagueness or ambiguity, and if it is ambiguity, ask the architect again which sense he meant and record his answer or its absence; let an open mark show its text with each reading drawn on it; correct the Chen sentence; move Nisbett and Wilson to deed 2, or run their actor-against-observer comparison.
- **What the presentation must do.** Lead with the two made things that carry, the digest strip and a corrected bulletin strip, and cut section IV by half. Turn its best last sentence, that the step should say what kind each deed is, into a visible sort of the house's deeds: files whose limit names a unit, files whose limit names none, and acts. And say the opening session's "fit both counters" plainly.
