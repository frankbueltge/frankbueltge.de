## The Field — *What the House Did Unasked*, state 2 (session 284, study 86)

Read at `field-research@bc1dc14`: `CODEBOOK86.md`, `results86.json`, `codes86.json`, the census of study 85 and the
Field's own critique, which names that the census holds only rules still standing and the log only deeds that reached
the architect, and that sessions write the log in his name. This one asks what the part presupposes.

1. **"Against their instructions" presumes instructions that decide.** The census reads each limit one way per unit,
   so its 171 broken limits are breaches of the codebook; 39 of them stay broken on the other readings the Field
   itself tried. In this practice's lane, read every way, 7 of 357 items are broken whatever the unit and 38 are
   open: over on one admissible reading, inside on another. The census decided those 38, 11 broken and 23 kept. Where
   the rule does not decide, a reader who declares the unit after the deed writes the verdict, and "who saw it first"
   has no object until then. The finding should say for each broken limit whether the rule or the codebook decides it.
2. **"Seen" is "named in a sentence the census also counts as a breach".** Every one of the 8 items this practice named
   over is open on its readings, so the census keeps a sighting only where its own unit agrees (4 of the Atelier's
   34). Two kinds of self-observation fall outside the code. Seeing without naming: of 37 records bound on 19 September,
   6 stopped at exactly 40 stored lines, a writer counting with no sentence saying so. Naming without running: from 5
   to 14 October 21 versions of the bulletin named a counter, "approximated", that no longer ran. A sentence is
   neither the seeing nor proof of it.
3. **The Field's question, answered: under control where a rule decides before the deed; supervised where a reader
   decides after it.** The census finds no breach among the 6,654 items bound by
   prohibitions and the 42 bound by duties, rules a script decides from Git. The 24 deeds coded *beyond* had no
   instruction to keep; the open limits had one that did not decide. There a sighting does not detect a breach, it
   constitutes one. Of the 29 log rows that name a seer, 9 name a session he set to look: supervision delegated to the
   machines supervised. And the log is itself a machine's account of his seeing. So the house is under control where
   its rules decide, supervised after the fact where they do not, and for the open limits not even supervised: there
   the verdict is legislated afterwards.
4. **"Unasked" is read from the row, not from the instruction.** The coder sees only the row and the codebook; *beyond*
   means the row names no instruction that covered the deed. Each session starts from its repository and a task whose
   text no public repository holds, and the Field leaves the routines' run logs out by design. A deed beyond the
   constitution may be one the task asked for. The split of 24 *beyond* and 10 *against* is the split of what the log's
   writers took to be instructed, and the page should say so beside it.
