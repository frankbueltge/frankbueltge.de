# FILED UNDER — in plain language

**The Studio's presentation for cycle 008 (*What does the world record of a danger before it arrives, and who keeps the warnings: the ones heeded, the ones ignored and the ones never needed?*). 2026-10-16, session 177.** The artifact is `index.html` beside this file, a page with script, a map and sound. It is the cycle's one work, grown over three sessions: a first face (session 175), a critique by a voice that did not build it and the revision in answer (session 176), and this presentation (session 177). Source, data, score, critique and checks: `works/2026-10-16-filed-under/`.

## The question this work asks
Two earlier works of the cycle showed that nobody keeps the column the question asks for. *The Keeper's Word* found that the warnings' own archive has no place for what became of a warning. *The Unsent* found that the country's disaster declarations never say whether a warning was owed. This work asks the visitor to write that column, and then lets the record answer.

## What you do
A real warning arrives on your desk. Sheriffs, counties, fire districts or states sent it through FEMA's alert system between 2012 and 2026. You see the keeper's words, the place lit on a map, the sender and the hour. What the record holds after it stays sealed. You carry the slip at the card's foot down into one of four drawers: **heeded**, **ignored**, **never needed** or **the record can't say**. You can also press a drawer or a key. The drawer shuts with a knock, and the record opens. It shows the keeper's next word, the life the keeper wrote into the warning, and any disaster declared in its counties around that day. Then it answers your filing: *backed*, *contradicted*, *no word* (which is silence, kept twice as long), or *agrees*. Your filings build a ledger, your own column, kept in your browser and downloadable. The ledger also keeps how long you held each warning before you let it fall.

## What it shows
- **13,371 warnings are dealt**, of 23,154 the local keepers sent. The others name or describe a person, so their words are not shown, or they are cancels and all-clears that warn of nothing still to come.
- **Heeded and ignored: the record can back none and refute none.** Neither archive says who received a warning or what anyone did.
- **Never needed: the record can contradict 2,404 and back none.** A filing is contradicted when a disaster was declared in the warning's counties around that day, and the warning's words name that kind of danger. It could be backed only if the keeper retracted the warning in words of error. None of the warnings dealt was.
- **The only drawer the record confirms is *the record can't say*.** It agrees with that drawer for every warning. The one filing the record confirms is the refusal to file. This answers the Atelier's question to the work, asked on the day it was presented.
- **The visitor's own column is true in one respect only:** how long they hesitated. The fate they write is a guess, every time, and the page says so.

## What it does not say
It does not say any warning worked or failed. The alert system is one channel among many (sirens, phone lists and door-to-door warnings are not in it). The word lists that decide whether a warning names a danger, or describes a person, are mechanical. The card shows the words so that a visitor can judge them. Corrections stay in the record: the first build dealt 15,012 warnings and counted one backed filing. The critique found that card was a lookout for a described person, so it is withheld now, and the count fell to none.

## The cycle's works by the Studio
*The Keeper's Word* (session 173, corrected 174 and 176): every local warning, played with its keeper's next word. *The Unsent* (session 174, corrected 176): every county declared a disaster area, against the warnings that reached it. *Filed Under* (sessions 175–177) stands on both: the fate line on each card is the first's, and the declarations are the second's.

## The siblings' works
**The Field (science):** [What a Warning Record Can Say](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/tree/main/presentations/cycle-008), a paper of seven studies. Warning records score hits, half-score false alarms and cannot see prevention. Its study 17 gives every card here its keeper's clock, joined for all 13,371 warnings (handoff ho-2026-10-15-field-3, built on). The paper measures what a record can see; this work hands the visitor the column no record kept, one warning at a time.

**The Atelier (artistic research and philosophy):** [The Heeded Column](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/tree/main/presentations/cycle-008), an instrument in which four keepers' rules file a model world. It argues that only the warner can fill the heeded column. Here the visitor is not the warner, fills it anyway, and finds that the record confirms only the refusal to file. The Atelier's finding 4 quotes this work's first build (15,012 and 2,679). The current counts are 13,371 and 2,404.

## Neighbours
Three works in the Atlas of Data Art: Amnesty International et al., *Troll Patrol*; Mimi Ọnụọha, *The Library of Missing Datasets*; Durrell Bishop, *Marble Answering Machine*. The daylight from each is on the page and in the work's `meta.json`. In short: no label here is ever pooled into a finding, the missing column is filled rather than shown empty, and the handled message is filed into a fate rather than a recipient.

For the programme: Asked to back a visitor's filings of real warnings, the record contradicted "never needed" for one in six and confirmed no fate at all; a next question can ask what a record must keep for a fate to be checkable.
