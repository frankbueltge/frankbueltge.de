UNWOVEN

Every area the IPC wrote into its open table from January 2021 to September 2026, hung as one cloth of 813 threads. Where people were written with no phase, nothing is woven, and the light comes through. Above the cloth hangs a rod with what was given and whether the famine monitor was watching. Below it, you can write a phase into eighteen of the holes yourself, from what the record says around them, and see your hand in the cloth.

    Write into the holes

    Eighteen holes, the widest one in each country that has any. For each, the page shows what the record writes around it: the areas of the same region in the same analysis, and the phase the same place was given before and after. Write the phase you would give it, or leave it blank. What you write is darned into the cloth in your colour, as a darn, not as weave. It is your hand, not the IPC's, and it says nothing about the place.

    Do the cuts sit over the holes?

    Not in this cloth. Hung by time, the six months in which FEWS NET filed nothing run down through it as a dark band: 71 threads, 5 of them with a hole (7 %), against 75 of the other 742 (10 %). Hung by what was given, the holes do not gather at the thin end. What the eye finds is the answer: whatever opened these blanks, the cloth does not show them opening where the money and the monitor went missing. Why each was left, the table does not write.

    A thread has a hole when at least one of its areas holds people and no phase. The share of people in holes moves with a few large threads (Sudan, DR Congo, Benin hold most of it), so the count of threads is the steadier reading. The Field's study 43 found the same with a test: coverage loss against growth of blank, ρ −0.08 (95 % −0.55 to 0.47) over 16 countries (field-research, session 240). This cloth was not built to test it, and it does not.

    Where the light comes through most

    The threads with the most people left in no phase. The table gives no reason for any of them.

      Caution: the Field found that the projections of Ethiopia (May 2021), Madagascar (Nov 2022) and Sudan (Jan 2026) were published for only part of the country. Their holes may be areas outside the projection, not areas no one could reach.

      What the record writes, and what it does not

      The table has no field for why an area has no phase. It writes the absence in three ways, which the Field named A, B and C (Three ways to write nobody, 2026-10-27): a place with population 0 and no phase rows (A), a place with population 0 and its phase rows at 0 (B), and a place whose people are kept while every phase is 0 (C). Here C is a hole as wide as the people it holds; A and B have no width, so they are knots. A hole may be an area no one could reach, or an area outside an analysis, or a projection made for only part of a country: the table cannot tell these apart, and neither can this cloth. Most knots (encoding A) are placeholders in the Cadre Harmonisé analyses the same file carries.

      Only one hole has its reason in the IPC's own words. In Sudan's analysis of September 2025, six areas holding 841,404 people were given a phase for the current period; in the second projection none of the six has one (the table writes three of them as holes and three as knots, and a seventh, Dilling Urban, as a knot too):

      “…the inability to classify several high-concern areas—home to approximately 841,000 people—including El Fasher town (inclusive of Abu Shouk and Al Salam camps), Kadugli town, as well as Dilling, As Sunut, Al Lagowa, and Habila (South Kordofan) localities, due to the volatility of the situation and resulting uncertainty.”
      IPC, Sudan: Acute Food Insecurity Analysis, Sep 2025 – May 2026 (issued Nov 2025), ReliefWeb; passage as read by the Field, session 234.

      Material and method

      HDX, Global: Acute Food Insecurity Country Data (CC0; source: National IPC Technical Working Groups), file ipc_global_area_long.csv, fetched 2026-10-29, sha256 33ed28dc…54368, the same file as the Field's fetch of 2026-10-27. One thread per analysis date × country × validity period (current, first projection, second projection), ordered by date of analysis, then country, then period. Every thread has the same width and the same length: an area's share of the length is its share of the thread's people, so a small country and a large one hang alike. Inside an area, the phases stand in the shares the table gives. Every count is asserted in build.py against the Field's summary.json (60,652 area-periods; A 6,352, B 16, C 1,041; 236,123,689 person-periods in C; 402 analyses). The Field's note says "since 2017"; the open table's earliest analysis is January 2021. The six threads whose areas all carry population 0 hang as bare strings of knots.

      The rod (increment 2, 2026-10-29). Money: OCHA's Financial Tracking Service, plan overview for each year 2021–2026 (CC BY-IGO), fetched 2026-10-29. For a thread's country and the year of its analysis, every plan FTS lists with that country alone (response plans, flash appeals) is summed, revised requirements against total funding; regional and refugee plans are left out, so a country with only a regional plan has no bead. A bead's height is the share funded, capped at full. Sudan 2025 comes to US$ 4,162.5 m asked and US$ 1,664.7 m received, the same as this practice's flow-by-flow count in The Remainder (asserted in build_rod.py). 2025 and 2026 appeals can still receive money. Monitor: FEWS NET's own sitemap, read 2026-10-29. A country-month is lit when the archive files an analysis (alert, outlook, outlook update, key message update, remote monitoring report, special or targeted report) under that month; price bulletins and calendars do not count. A country is watched in a year when any month of it is lit. Of the 26 IPC countries FEWS NET watched in 2025, none has an analysis filed under January to June 2025. The archive does not say why. NPR reported that the system went offline in January 2025 under the US government's stop-work orders and that its website was back up at the end of June (NPR, 2025-08-11); this page does not test that account. The lamp reads the month the IPC's analysis is dated, not the months it covers. Neither source writes why a bead is short or a lamp is out, and a short bead does not make a hole: the rod hangs beside the cloth so a visitor can look for one under the other.

      Increment 3 (2026-10-29). Three hangs of the same 813 threads: by time; by country, with a gap between countries, the countries with the most people in holes first; by the share funded. The monitor band under the beads is FEWS NET's own month: the share of the countries it watched that year on which its sitemap files an analysis that month. It is black from January to June 2025, and those six months are drawn down through the cloth. Holes are drawn at least 2 px tall and are never faded, so the smallest is visible. "Let the months pass" moves a light across the cloth in the order of the record, about half a second per month: a hum as loud as the share the monitor filed that month, which stops in the dark months, and one tone for each thread with a hole, lower for more people. "Come close" gives each thread 6 px, and the cloth scrolls sideways; phones open close.

      Increment 4 (2026-10-30): the reader's hand. Eighteen holes: in each of the 18 countries with a hole, the hole holding the most people, except Sudan, where the hole is As Sunut (Sep 2025, second projection), one of the areas whose reason the IPC wrote. Beside each: the classified areas of the same region in the same thread (up to eight, most people first), and the same place's nearest classification before and after in the same country's other threads, matched by its two names. The phase beside each is computed here by the IPC's 20 % rule from the shares the table gives. A written phase is darned over its hole, hatched, so it never reads as weave. The work keeps what you write in the page only. On the site, the house's study frame can offer to add it to the record (study studio-unwoven-holes, study.json beside this page); this page sends nothing itself, and shows the released answers of other readers only after you have written your own.

      Neighbours in the Atlas of Data Art

      Mimi Ọnụọha, The Library of Missing Datasets. There, data no one collects is a labelled, empty folder. Here the record exists and has a name and a number of people for each blank; what is missing is the one field the famine count turns on.
      Jaime Black, The REDress Project. There, an empty dress stands for a person a count leaves out. Here no figure stands in for anyone: the cloth is woven from the count itself, and the gap is the count's own.
      Raqs Media Collective, The Great Bare Mat and Constellation. There, a carpet's pattern is plotted from the collective's traffic. Here the weave is the IPC's ledger at its full scale, and the pattern that matters is where it is not woven.
