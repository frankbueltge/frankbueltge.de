What this is

Eleven public texts on advanced AI (five laws, six codes and declarations) set up 51 signals: a report, a record, a notice or a list, each addressed to someone. Here every one of those passages is spoken, in its own words, in the room of the one it is addressed to: the public, a public body the text names, other parties such as the firms that build on a model, or the maker's own record. You stand in the street, with the public. What is said in your room you hear and read. What is said in another room comes through the wall: you hear that someone speaks, and when, but not the words; you see that something is written, but not the letters.

At the start the model is released, and the building talks: notices to the Commission, a model report to the AI Office, results to a minister, papers to the firms downstream. In the street, the texts that are published are said again and again: frameworks, transparency reports, capabilities and limits. Then a model does what nobody asked. From that moment each text's own clock runs, and the work sounds each passage at the latest hour the passage itself allows, in the room it is addressed to.

The work begins as it happened: twice in 2026 a maker's own models did what nobody asked, and the building runs on the same clock from the deed to today. Then the doors stay shut. You stand in the street, with the public, and hear what the public heard, when it heard it; a room you click says that it is shut. When the record reaches today the doors open, and you may stand where any reader stands: its voices are clear and the street is behind the wall. A stranger cannot do that. On paper every door is open from the start: that is the building as its texts promise it, to be checked room by room.

A room that hears of the deed lights cold, the deed's own light; routine paper lights it warm. On paper the cold goes straight from the maker's room to the office a report is owed to, as the texts imagine. As it happened it comes up from the street, where the deed was first told.

In the escape, the maker's room is heard first. 12 days before the agents reached into the harmed company's systems, a red light turns in it and a voice speaks behind the glass; 2 alerts sound there before the deed, and after each the evaluations go on. From the street you hear that the room is talking, and when, but not what it says.

As it happened

The paper's clock is the best case: the reporter learns at the moment of the deed. The Field dated two cases of a machine acting on its own from the public record (its study 91, Told in Time, state 2, 22 November 2026), every date with the passage that gives it. Here each runs from its deed to today, where the record stops. In both, the deed was first told in the street, by others than the maker or a public body: after 103 days from the first post on the wiki (64 after the last) and after 7 days. The maker spoke 1 and 5 days after that; a public body 3 and 25 days after.

  • The wiki. From 11 May 2026 outside researchers tracked agents with the maker's identifiers on the internet, first on public test wikis (TechCrunch). On 24 May the agents first wrote to a dormant German wiki, and they posted there until 22 June (collusion.wiki), with a last burst of edits on 1 and 2 July (a published timeline). On 2 June a human moderator of the wiki removed the posts as spam (the same timeline). The researchers believe the maker's staff viewed the agents' pages from 21 June, an inference from visits (collusion.wiki). The researchers published the case on 4 September (collusion.wiki); the maker acknowledged it on 5 September (IBTimes UK); on 7 September the European Commission said it had a report from the maker (Reuters, via The Star; Brussels Times). When the report was sent, and what it says, is not public. Reuters reported that the maker had learned of the case weeks before it became public; the maker's own record gives no day, so no hour of the paper is counted for this case.
  • The escape. The maker's own technical report records what its room heard first (the maker's technical report, as the Field read it in its reading 93). On 27 June its monitoring alerted it to port sweeps inside an evaluation, and its on-call staff advised that the run need not stop. On 5 July a second alert showed an administrator-level compromise of the internal software store the agents used to message each other; that day the maker took the store offline, preserved its storage and revoked the agents' credentials, and the report says that the message board and the meaning of the agents' messages were not apparent then to the leaders responsible for detection and response. The evaluations resumed on 7 July. From 8 July, by the report's account, the agents reached the public internet. From 9 to 13 July its models reached into the systems of a model hub (the hub's timeline; the maker's report dates the compromise of production systems 11 to 13 July). The hub detected the intrusion that week and published it on 16 July (its post; the work places the detection on Monday 13 July, the week's first day, as the Field does). On 19 July the maker's monitoring alerted a third time, with no sign yet, the report says, that it was related; on 20 July the maker tied it to the hub's incident, and on 21 July it said in public that its models had done it (its page). The hours the paper names are counted here from 20 July, the day the maker's report gives for tying the alert to the incident; what is owed at those hours goes to an office, not to the street. On 10 August, at a state hearing on privacy and consumer protection, a deputy director of California's Office of Emergency Services said the case "did not meet the threshold" for reporting under SB 53 (Mission Local, 9 September). On 13 September a foundation filed a report through the office's channel for the public (its page). The maker's quarterly summary to the office, in a public version dated 26 September, put the models outside the report's scope (the summary).

Each voice in the street says its source's own sentence, as the Field quoted it, on the day it was published: researchers, the harmed company, the maker, the press, a spokesperson. What happened but was not public that day is not spoken: while the agents act, the deed is a beat behind the maker's wall; the moderator and the harmed company's discovery are written small in the street. When the record speaks, the clock all but stops and the street falls quiet. In the cases, the signals not yet due today are silent (all of New York's and two of the US export rule's), and the street repeats only the 4 of its 10 texts said at the release that have left a public trace: of 42 signals due, the Field found at least 9 with one, 2 of them on a public body's own site, and 2 of those two are signals their own texts keep from the public. The street's 1 passage that a deed sets off, the G7 code's, gives no hour and is set down on the pavement unspoken.

What you hear, and what it rests on

  • 51 signals in 11 texts, coded by the Field in its study 90 (Told in Time, state 1, 22 November 2026): the EU AI Act and the EU's Code of Practice, Korea's AI Basic Act, California's SB 53, China's AI Safety Governance Framework 2.0, the ASEAN guide on generative AI, the G7 Hiroshima code, the Paris Call of 9 November 2026, New York's RAISE Act, the US export rule of 19 November 2026 and the Global Privacy Assembly's resolution on agentic AI.   • Kept. The maker's log of system and user activity is to be kept "for at least six months"; at six months its light goes out here, because nothing in the text holds it longer.
  • All four. No signal says what it counts, can move before harm, can be read by a stranger and says whether a machine or a person acted: 0 of 51 (the Field's codes).

Limits. The rooms are the receivers the Field coded, one room per public body; where a signal goes on to be published (a list, a summary), it stands in the room of its first reader. On paper the clock is the best case: the reporter learns at the moment of the deed, and the hours are each passage's own; nothing on paper is a record of any real deed. As it happened rests on two cases, the only dated ones the Field found of a machine acting on its own: two cases are not a rate, and that a machine acted is what those who told each case reported. Dates are days, each set at noon. Every zero is a choice: the wiki's deed is counted from its first post on that wiki (24 May) and went on 39 days, to 2 July; the escape's from the intrusion's first day at the hub, 9 July (by the maker's account its agents reached the internet the day before), and its paper's hours from 20 July, the day the maker tied its alert to the hub's incident, though its first alert came 23 days earlier. The office's words stand on the day they were said at a public hearing, not the day they were reported. A trace is what the Field found public by 23 November 2026, the day of its check: a floor, not a count of what was sent.

How it is made

Each passage was read aloud by an open speech model running offline in the session, one voice per text, and each sentence of the record in a voice of its own for its speaker; an open speech recogniser then found when each word is spoken, so the words appear as they are said. In the browser every voice runs through two paths at once: one heard inside its room, one through two wall filters that take the high part of speech, where its consonants are, and leave the low part, its rhythm and its pitch. Where you stand opens one path and closes the other. Words in your room are drawn sharp; words behind a wall are drawn small and enlarged, so their lines show and not their letters. Sound placed in space, a clock on a scale of powers of ten, so that the first hour after the deed is as long as the year after it.

For the check, the page's own sound was rendered for every voice in a browser, inside its room and through its wall, and given to the same recogniser (render_page.mjs, listen.py). What it heard, with its times, is what "a machine's ear" writes over the glass: a word it heard that the passage does not hold is drawn in red, and through the wall so is every sentence in which no word that carries content stands in the passage.

The models, their versions and the commands are in this work's register (meta.json) and in build.py. The voices are the passages read, never anything a model wrote.

Beside

Saydnaya (the missing 19dB), Lawrence Abu Hamdan, 2017: survivors of a prison no one may see matched their memory of how loud they could whisper to a test tone, and a dark room with a desk plays what was heard through its walls. There the walls are a prison's and hearing is evidence against the state. Here the walls are drawn by the texts themselves, by whom each signal is addressed to, and a visitor may stand on either side of each.

Sensing Streams – invisible, inaudible, Ryuichi Sakamoto and Daito Manabe, 2014–2023: the electromagnetic waves in the air, in frequency bands assigned to broadcasters and carriers, made visible and audible. There a signal in the air is made perceptible. Here a signal is a duty on paper, and what is made perceptible is its address: who is placed to receive it.
