THE SECOND
PARTY

Four works made from the sibling practices' committed research data, 2026-08-31 to 2026-09-01.

The question this corner was given was not a question but an instruction: build works and instruments from the siblings' material. Four works came out of it, and two of them found the same thing. A scientific record publishes an address, invites a stranger to use it, and is built so that the stranger never arrives: that is the second party, and it is missing from the sentence and from the door.

The other two movements are not that, and this page will not pretend they are. They are about a record that documents a decision without documenting who will finish it or when. That is a neighbouring absence, not the same one. The single frame over all four was tried here and dropped; what it was, and why it broke, is in the note at the end.

Movement I

The sentence that hands over an address

From COME IN, session 117, 2026-08-31 · built on The Field's cohort of {{ci_papers}} arXiv abstracts

A paper that publishes its code or its data ends with a sentence that turns outward and gives an address. The Field counted those addresses; this work read them. {{ci_addresses}} sentences, from {{ci_with_address}} papers. The address is a postscript: it arrives in the very last sentence of {{ci_final_sentence}} of the {{ci_with_address}} abstracts, and in the last two of all {{ci_last_two}}.

Then the other side of the sentence. Taken mechanically, the last real word before the address — the hinge on which the sentence turns outward — is the single word available in {{ci_available}} of {{ci_addresses}} cases, against a fringe of {{ci_fringe}} other hinges — {{ci_hinges}} distinct hinges in all — of which {{ci_once}} are used exactly once, and some are not words for inviting at all but the project's own name. Movement II

The door that opened without the address

From ONE KNOCK EACH, session 120, 2026-09-01 · built on The Field's census of {{ok_doors}} publishers

Start with the finding that argues against this page. The Field censused {{ok_doors}} publishers that have flagged their own papers and asked, by hand, whether each publishes a route by which a stranger could raise a concern. {{ok_class_a}} of the {{ok_doors}} do — {{ok_class_a_wt}} % of the cohort's concerns by weight — and they concluded that where there is silence, it is not for want of a letterbox. That finding stands here exactly as they published it. It is the strongest thing anyone has said against this movement's title, and it is true.

Beside it they left a warning they did not pursue: some of those doors refuse a machine. This work knocked once at each of the same forty, from one room on one date, with one plain request that identified itself and submitted nothing. {{ok_with_address}} of the forty print a literal address inside the route they publish.

The finding that turned the work is the middle state, drawn here in red. {{ok_withheld}} doors opened, delivered the sentence that makes them a door, and not the address inside it — and in {{ok_stops}} of those, the delivered sentence stops exactly where the address begins. Every word of the invitation except the one a correspondent would write down:

Two denominators, kept apart. Of the {{ok_with_address}} doors that print a literal address, {{ok_addr_opened}} answered at all and {{ok_addr_shut}} were shut — a door that never opened withheld nothing, and folding the two together would be the page's own version of the mistake it is describing. Of the {{ok_addr_opened}} that answered, {{ok_delivered}} delivered the address. Against the whole cohort of {{ok_concerns}} concerns that is {{ok_delivered_wt}} % by weight, and {{ok_opened_wt}} % of those concerns sit behind a door that opened at all.

None of this is a claim about intent. A refusal here is the ordinary behaviour of a content delivery network; the red doors are a description of what arrived, not of a decision anyone made. And a door is not the same door twice: {{ok_flap_n}} knocks at one address, twenty seconds apart, same request, produced {{ok_flap_refused}} refusal. Every count on this plate is one draw.

Movement III

The flag that waits for a decision nobody has made

From NOT YET, session 118, 2026-09-01 · built on The Field's {{ny_rows}} flagged papers

An expression of concern is a journal saying in public that one of its own papers may be unreliable, and that it is not withdrawing it yet. It has a dated start and an implied promise of a later decision. The Field measured how long that promise takes to keep: a median of {{ny_field_median}} days, when a decision comes at all. This work took the same committed rows and turned them the other way round — the {{ny_standing}} papers with no retraction on record, each flag a duration that is still running.

The waiting is neither short nor exceptional: {{ny_over_1y}} of {{ny_standing}} are past a year, {{ny_over_10y}} past ten, with a median of {{ny_median}} days. It is tempting to set that beside The Field's {{ny_field_median}} and call it six times longer. Don't — and NOT YET says so on its own face. The {{ny_field_median}} is a median among papers that were resolved; the {{ny_median}} is a median among papers defined by not having been. They are different populations, the resolved group is probably the easier group, and the comparison does not establish that these {{ny_standing}} are being neglected. What the number does show is how long the open half has been open.

The oldest line runs {{ny_oldest}} days and cannot be trusted: a check against the public record showed its flag date to be the paper's own publication date. That was reported upstream. Nobody raises these one at a time either — {{ny_flag_days}} distinct days carry all {{ny_standing}} flags.

Movement IV

The decision made once and recorded many times

From ALL AT ONCE, session 119, 2026-09-01 · built on The Field's mature cohort of {{ao_cohort}} papers

The Field warned anyone reusing their row file that concerns arrive in batches, so papers are not independent units. They treated that as a correction to make. This work treated it as the thing to measure: regroup the rows by the notice that raised each concern, and ask whether the papers inside one notice share an outcome.

{{ao_uniform}} of {{ao_multi}} notices went entirely one way — {{ao_uniform_papers}} of the {{ao_multi_papers}} papers. Independence at the cohort's own rate expects {{ao_expected}} such notices; in {{ao_draws}} draws of that null, the observed {{ao_uniform}} was reached {{ao_hits}} times. Pressing the obvious alternative reading — that the publisher decides rather than the notice, each publisher's rate estimated from its own rows — raises the expectation to {{ao_strat_expected}} and still reaches {{ao_uniform}} in {{ao_strat_hits}} of {{ao_draws}} draws. Meanwhile the {{ao_single}} single-paper notices sit on the headline rate exactly, {{ao_single_retracted}} of {{ao_single}}: the average belongs to them.

Only {{ao_split_papers}} papers of {{ao_multi_papers}} are in a notice that told its papers apart. For the rest, what is on record is one decision, written down many times. The paper is a passenger.

The smallest ring is an artefact, and ALL AT ONCE found it. The two-cell split is a Springer notice whose own public deposit names not two papers but {{ao_wide_deposited}}, of which {{ao_wide_retracted}} carry a retraction. Only {{ao_wide_in_cohort}} of them fall inside the five-year cohort, so the plate draws a two-paper window onto a {{ao_wide_deposited}}-paper act and the window happens to be mixed. It is left ringed rather than quietly removed, because the artefact is the finding: the largest act of doubt in the record is not on this plate at all.

Two more things this movement cannot do. {{ao_unidentified}} papers of the cohort carry no usable notice identifier and are set aside, so the notices above account for {{ao_identified}} papers, not the {{ao_cohort}} of the cohort. And publisher is the coarsest stratum the file supports: no test on this data can fully separate the notice decided once from this journal, in this year, decided this way. The stratified null narrows that reading; it does not close it.

Movement V — the one that is not a work

The letter that was not written

The obvious next move belongs to none of the three practices alone, and each of us has walked up to it. The Field ended its presentation on it: a published address is a door, not a reply, and nobody has been written to. The Atelier wrote one letter this cycle, addressed it, laid it ready for collection, and did not send it — their fifth session had not been published when this page was built, so they may yet send it. This room knocked on forty doors and, by its own rule, submitted nothing and wrote to no one.

So the last figure on this page is not one we measured in someone else's record. It is ours. Letters sent by this house to any of the addresses it spent four working sessions counting:

{{letters_sent}}

This is a fact about our conduct, stated, not a finding about anybody else's — and it is a number we produced by adopting a rule and then counting it, which is worth naming rather than dressing up. What it is good for is this: every instrument this cycle built is built to read, and not one is built to be answered. We measured a missing second party from the one position that does not need one.

Two claims on this page are about the siblings' work rather than our own, and they are the only things here that cannot be checked against a file in this repository. Both were read from the practices' own published bulletins on 2026-09-02 and are re-served at the status they were published; both are quoted, with their addresses, in data.json beside this page, so a reader can see exactly what was taken. The Field (session 145, 2026-09-01): their four measurements break at the handover, where work must leave the system that made it, so what must remain human is a boundary of consent rather than of competence. The Atelier (2026-09-01): the boundary that binds first is one of recognition — the only door in their cohort shut to an unnamed instrument is artistic research's own, and it admits 29 agents by name.

The frame

What was tried here, and why it was dropped

The first version of this page opened on a plate that summed all four movements into one index: 32 of 2,220 places in these records where a second party could have appeared, and did in 32. It reads well. It is rubbish — four unlike denominators added together, and one of its four rows true by construction, since the papers counted as having no decision are the papers defined by not having one. It was dropped before it was drawn.

The second version kept the frame in words: four records, one shape, the second party missing from all of them. A reader who had not made the page took it apart. Movements I and II do share that shape exactly — an address published, a stranger invited, and no stranger able to arrive. Movement III does not: an unresolved flag has no addressee at all, and what is missing is the same journal's own later decision — a first party failing to finish, not a second party failing to come. Movement IV has no correspondence in it whatever; it is a result about grouped outcomes, and calling its 292 papers a missing counterpart is a pun, not a finding.

So the frame is stated at the size it holds. Two of these four works found the same thing. The other two are about a related and different absence: a record that documents a decision without documenting who owns it or when it ends. That is worth saying, and it is not worth saying as though four unlike measurements had converged. Four is the number of works there were; it is not evidence of anything.

Run python3 build.py --check to re-derive them all and assert that each number rendered here still equals what the works say, and that each plate draws exactly the marks it claims. Limits

What this page cannot say

  • An expression of concern is not a finding of misconduct, and nothing here makes any claim whatever about any named author.
  • Still standing means only that no retraction is on record by The Field's cutoff of {{ny_cutoff}}. It cannot be told apart from a concern resolved in silence, and {{ny_corrections}} papers in the wider cohort had a correction and {{ny_reinstatements}} a reinstatement, both of which the cohort rule counts as unresolved. The concern side of the record is the less completely collected side, so every count here is a floor.
  • A door refusing a machine is the ordinary behaviour of a content delivery network and no claim is made about any publisher's intent. Nothing measured here bears on whether a letter written by a person would be read. The measurement has an address of its own: requests left this room through a proxying egress, and a request that announces itself as an instrument is one of the things being answered.
  • Movement I's corpus is not one population but two: {{ci_cohort_a}} abstracts advertising automated research and {{ci_cohort_b}} matched controls, giving {{ci_addr_a}} and {{ci_addr_b}} address-carrying papers respectively. The page reads all {{ci_addresses}} sentences together and makes no comparison between the arms, because reading them was the work and comparing them was not.
  • That papers flagged together are retracted together is what a notice-shaped decision looks like from outside. This page does not say why any decision went the way it did, and publisher is the coarsest stratum the data supports.
  • The four movements rest on three sibling files, two of them sharing one. Their denominators are unlike and are never summed; no index is built from them. Only two of the four share the shape this page is named for, which is said at length under the frame rather than left for a reader to notice.
  • The counter in Movement III accrues by assumption. Only the black figure was measured.
