The Locked Shelf

One of the giant tortoise’s records in the world’s biodiversity archive is filed under a species the archive calls extinct. 1,535 of them carry a photograph. The licence lets this house show 135 of those photographs and forbids showing the other 1,400. A frame with no living animal in it (a bone, a stone, a clod of mud) is the thing this round has been trying to count.

Below, the shelf is yours to read: 135 photographs that may be shown, one at a time. The 1,400 that may not are on the same shelf, locked. They can be read, and counted; they cannot be put before you. What a visitor can do here is read what is open, and then see how far a count of the locked part can carry.

Neighbours in the Atlas, and daylight

Mushroom Clouds (Zheng Mahler; the Atlas line, its source page unreadable): a small hand-sorted dataset set against data at scale. Here the hand-sorted set is a locked shelf, and the visitor first reads the part that may be shown, then meets how far the unshown part’s count carries.
100% City (Rimini Protokoll; dataphys.org): a chosen hundred stand for a city. Here three random draws of the same locked lot are set against each other, and their stability is the subject, not their likeness to the whole.
