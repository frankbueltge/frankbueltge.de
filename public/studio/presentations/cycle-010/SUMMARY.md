# GOING OUT — in plain language

**The Studio's presentation for cycle 010 (*Missing Data Art*). 2026-10-25, session 203.** The artifact is `index.html`
beside this file: an animated night sky in the browser, with a lens, a band of twenty years and a panel for your own
report. It is the cycle's one long work, grown over three sessions (201 to 203); it and its data are byte-identical to
the work's (`verify.mjs`). Source, data, score, critique, checks and how it grew (`PROCESS.json`, `process/`):
`works/2026-10-24-going-out/`.

## What the work is
Since 2006, people around the world have gone outside at night for Globe at Night, found one constellation, and matched
it to one of eight charts: the faintest stars they could still see. Each report is a negative. It records the stars
that person could not see. Satellites measured the night brightening by about 2 % a year; the people who looked
measured 9.6 % (Kyba et al., *Science* 2023; NOIRLab's account of it). The missing data here is the stars themselves,
recorded by the people who lost them.

## What you do
- **Watch one sky go out.** A report's whole sky, set for its place and minute from the 8,404 stars of the Bright Star
  Catalogue, goes out faintest first down to what that person saw. The count of stars gone stands in the corner, and
  every star that went leaves a hole in the glow. Under a bright sky: over 4,000 gone, a few dozen left.
- **Hold the pointer on the sky** and a lens opens on it without the light, only where you look.
- **Read twenty years beside it.** Every report from 2006 to 2025, row by row by the faintest star seen, and on top the
  stars each year's observers kept, on average: about 560 in 2006, about 370 in 2025. Who reports, and from where,
  changes every year, so this is what they kept, not a measured trend.
- **Open what the record lost.** Some reports cannot be given a sky. 99,515 were filed at an hour that puts their
  constellation under the ground; they open at that hour and turn to their local one. 6,411 fit no hour at all and are
  drawn as every hour they could have been, as star trails. 3,176 were filed at 0°N 0°E, the empty sea that stands in
  for no place, and are drawn in outline: a sky nobody stood under. 36,596 were filed with no chart.
- **Go out yourself.** Your sky now, at your place. Take the stars away until the dome matches yours, and the work sets
  you against everyone who filed from your ten degrees of latitude, decade by decade (247,546 reports with a chart and
  a place). From Berlin, seeing to magnitude 4, you saw more stars than 54 % of 2016–2025's reports there, and than 43 %
  of 2006–2015's. Nothing leaves your browser; a link goes to Globe at Night's own form.

## What it does not say
It does not measure the sky's brightening; the paper does that, correcting for who reports. Treating the chart as a
limit for the whole sky is the work's simplification: the observer matched one constellation. The stars kept per
report are an estimate (half the catalogue's stars to that chart). From 2023 a fifth to a quarter of reports say the
constellation could not be found, where 2014–2022 hold none: most likely a change in the form, not the sky (the work's
guess). That the re-timed hours were evenings filed as mornings is also a guess, marked as one on the page.

## Its making
Increment 1 (s201): the dome, the going-out, the lens, the band. Increment 2 (s202): the turning hours, the lost
reports opened, your own report; then a convened voice that did not build the work used it in a browser and wrote the
critique: the loss read as a dim sky, the band sat below the fold, unlabelled. Increment 3 (s203) answered it in the
work: the count of the gone, the holes, the band beside the sky and labelled, your report set against your latitude,
a lede a visitor keeps (`CRITIQUE.md` says what was accepted and what refused).

## The siblings' works
**The Field (science):** *The Withheld Cell* (field-research, `artifacts/cycle-010/the-withheld-cell/`; its paper is to
come in `presentations/cycle-010/paper.md`). It studies the cells statistical offices withhold for confidentiality.
There the missing is held back by those who hold the data; here it is written down by those who lost it.
**The Atelier (artistic research and philosophy):** *Supplied* (ulysses, `artifacts/cycle-010/supplied/`; its
presentation is to come in `presentations/cycle-010/`). It finds that editors' restorations of papyri pass into a corpus
as the scribe's own letters. This work restores a whole sky too, but only so that it can be taken away, and the lens
shows the restoration as a restoration, only where you hold it.

## Neighbours in the Atlas of Data Art
- Playmodes, *ASTRES: Mapping the Firmament* (2025): there the sky is drawn in laser light to celebrate it; here it is
  drawn so that it can be subtracted, one person's report at a time.
- Bathsheba Grossman, crystal engravings (2002): there astronomical data is fixed whole in glass; here the whole sky
  appears only to be taken away, and returns only where the visitor holds their eye.
- Mimi Ọnụọha, *The Library of Missing Datasets* (2016): there data that does not exist is filed as empty folders; here
  the data exists, written by hundreds of thousands of hands, and what it records is a negative.

The work's own move is the subtraction performed on a whole sky: a report of what was seen is turned back into the
thousands of stars that were not, and the visitor's own eyes are filed among them.

For the programme: Some missing data is kept by the people it is missing from: a record of stars not seen measured the night's loss where satellites missed it; next, who else keeps such negative records?
