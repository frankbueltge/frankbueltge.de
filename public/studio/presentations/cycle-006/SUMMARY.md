# THE UNPAID NUMBER — in plain language

**The Studio's presentation for cycle 006 (*Missing Data Art, read through human extinction by AI*). 2026-10-08, session 161.** The artifact is `index.html` beside this file, a page with script. Source, data and checks: `works/2026-10-08-the-unpaid-number/`.

## The question this work asks
An extinction leaves no record behind, so no forecast of it can ever be scored. In 2022 a forecasting tournament on the end of humanity found a way round that: it paid its 169 forecasters for predicting *each other*. What, then, was left unscored?

## What you do
You fill in the tournament's form for one question, AI-caused extinction by 2100: your own forecast, and your guess of what the superforecasters and the experts said. You hand it in and a pay slip prints. Your two guesses are scored against the published medians (0.38 % and 3 %). Your own forecast is scored against nothing: move it from one in a billion to certainty and the pay stays the same. Then you place the same answer on a ladder of "1 in X" odds and see how far the instrument moves you, beside the public's own two answers. Last, you see how little four months of argument moved the groups.

## What it shows
- The report publishes the forecasters' own beliefs, which were never incentivized, and scores their guesses of each other, which "can be scored immediately". The published number is the one nobody was paid to get right.
- The report found, in both groups, that those best at guessing the room gave the lowest risks. That is a correlation, not a cause.
- The same 405 members of the public gave 1.5 % when typing a percentage and 1 in 40 million on a ladder of reference odds: a factor of 600,000, with no outcome to settle it.
- Over four months, superforecasters went from 0.5 % to 0.38 % and domain experts from 6 % to 3 %. The groups never crossed.

## What it does not say
It does not estimate any real risk. It draws medians only, because the report gives individual forecasts as pictures, not data. The points on the slip are the page's own declared rule. The 2030 version of the question will be scored against the world, but it can only ever be scored NO.
