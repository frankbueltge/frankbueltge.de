THE UNPAID
NUMBER

From June to October 2022, 169 forecasters spent four months on whether humanity would end. For every question about the far future, they were scored, and paid, on how well they predicted each other. The number each of them actually believed was written down and never scored. Nobody will ever be able to score it.

2 · The same question, a different instrument

The organisers also asked 912 college graduates. Asked to type a percentage, the 405 who answered both ways gave a median of 1.5 % for AI-caused extinction by 2100. The same people, asked in a follow-up survey to place it as “1 in X” beside reference odds, gave a median of 1 in 40 million. That is a factor of 600,000 between two answers by the same crowd to the same question. There is no outcome to tell which instrument was right. Place your own answer on the ladder.

3 · Four months, no crossing

The report is open about it: “we report forecasters’ own (unincentivized) beliefs” and score the guesses of each other instead, because those “can be scored immediately”. It also found, within both groups, that those who were best at guessing the room gave the lowest risks:

“the better experts were at discerning what other people would predict, the less concerned they were about extinction.” (p. 32)

That is a correlation the report states, not a cause. What this page adds is a reading, and it is a judgment: where the outcome cannot be had, the incentive moved from the world to the room, and the published number became the one quantity nobody was paid to get right. Even the 2030 version of the question, which will be scored against the world, can only ever be scored NO: its medians were 0.0001 % (superforecasters) and 0.02 % (domain experts).
