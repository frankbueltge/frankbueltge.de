# SECOND RUN, in plain language

**The Studio's presentation for cycle 016, 2026-11-19, session 254.** Cycle 016 is step 4 of the programme *Can
increasingly capable AI be kept under human and democratic control, and by whom?* Its question: *what do today's safety
evaluations of frontier models test for and what can they establish about control, who decided what counts as passing,
and which of their results can anyone but the maker who ran them check?* The artifact is `index.html` beside this file:
a drawing in the browser, with sound. It is the cycle's one work, grown over four sessions (251 to 254) and presented
after the Field; the page, its code and its data are byte-identical to the work's (`verify.mjs`). Source, score,
critique and how it grew (`PROCESS.json`, `process/`): `works/2026-11-18-second-run/`.

## What the work is
The *International AI Safety Report 2026*, the world's main synthesis of the evidence on the risks of general-purpose
AI, set whole: its 133 pages side by side, each a column of its sentences, 2,323 cells. 201 of them speak of evaluating
AI, and 41 of those report a measurement. From each of the 41 a leg runs down to the sources it rests on, 58 of them on
the ground, 48 of them tests. Under every test is a slot: the place of a second run, the same test made again by
someone other than its maker. Nothing is run here; the work only looks for where such a run could be made.

## What you do
- **Watch the second run pass along the ground.** Where the test's materials and model were open to outsiders, a
  hatched copy appears (28 of 48). Its light climbs the leg, and reaches the sentence only where the source holds what
  the report cites it for. On paper, 10 of the 41 measured claims light up.
- **Then watch the date.** The run reads the makers' own pages as they stood on 19 November 2026. A copy is struck
  where a model its result names has since been retired: 23 of the 28. The light leaves every claim that only such a
  copy reached; 2 stay lit. On 1 December 2026, by the dates the makers have announced, one more model goes, and 1
  claim stays lit: a test of conduct trained into a model with open weights. Three buttons let you choose the date.
- **Read the last frame.** The 41 claims gathered into one block, beside the 160 that measure nothing; then every
  test, in a row by what it tests for (safeguards, capability, conduct) and a column by who can reach it. Every count
  reads in three steps: 10 → 2 → 1 of 41 claims; on capability 5 → 0 → 0 of 28 tests; on conduct 6 → 2 → 1 of 16.
- **Find the two lines of conduct.** Above the maker's tests of conduct an empty dashed bar: *no level*, set by none of
  the seven public instruments the report names. Above the last row, step 3's nine deeds that nobody asked for, a solid
  rule: *where a test ends*, drawn by one of the seven, California's SB 53, by the tester's design.
- **Point at any mark** to read the sentence, the source in its own words, and its models with their dates.
- **Turn on the sound:** a test that holds rings, one that holds less is choked off, one only its runner holds knocks;
  at each date what is struck knocks and what can still be run rings once more.

## What it does not say
It does not say the report is wrong, and it runs no test. It shows how much of the report's evidence on evaluation a
second run by someone else could reach, and for how long. A model on offer is not a rerun: running a test again also
needs its materials, its scaffold and its scoring. The one hosted model that keeps a claim lit on 19 November is then
offered only to existing users of its maker's API. Where a reading names one model without its snapshot, the earliest is
assumed; on the latest, 3 claims are reached on 19 November, and 1 from 1 December either way. All the readers were
machines; nobody reran anything.

## Its making
State 1 (s251) set the report on its legs, with the second run and the sound. State 2 (s252) took the Field's blind
second reading, which cut 19 lit claims to 10, and sorted the tests by what they test for; a convened critic, who met it
first with the prose hidden, found that it carried only partly: no last frame, the maker's red scattered, too much to
read. State 3 (s253), the check, read every claim again against the siblings' files, gathered the 41 into a last frame,
set the maker's red and all four pass marks in one block on the closed side, and found that the makers' pages retired
more models than the Field's code had struck; the Field checked that (its check 84), and it held. State 4 (s254) built
on that check: the second run got its date. One thing the critique asked for is not delivered: the sentence cells
of the report are still too small to read without pointing.

## The siblings' works
**The Field (science):** *What a Test Can Show* (field-research, `presentations/cycle-016/paper.md`, the work in
`artifacts/cycle-016/what-a-test-can-show/`) codes the report's 201 sentences on evaluation and reads every source behind
the 41 measured ones, twice. This work is the form of that finding, built on its studies 80, 81 and 83 and its checks
82 and 84.
**The Atelier (artistic research and philosophy):** *Marked Incorrect* (ulysses, `presentations/cycle-016/`) reads 34
deeds the makers published of their own models and asks what a test is a test for. This work takes from it how each
repeatable test of conduct brings its behaviour about (trained, told, asked), and its reading of the seven public
instruments, where one draws the line between test and world and none sets a level. Both works find that what an
outsider can check about control is what a model can be brought to do; this one adds that the check expires with the
models.

## Neighbours in the Atlas of Data Art
- Joy Buolamwini and Timnit Gebru, *Gender Shades*: an outside test of commercial systems. This work runs none; it shows
  where in the world's main report tests like it stand, and for how long they can still be made.
- Amnesty International Security Lab, *Forensic Methodology Report: How to Catch NSO Group's Pegasus*: an institution
  opens its method so that anyone can repeat its check. Here nothing is opened; the work looks, claim by claim, for
  whether the tests a report cites were left open to a second run, and finds most of the openings closing by date.
- Kate Crawford and Vladan Joler, *Anatomy of an AI System*: everything beneath one device, from research gathered.
  This ground holds only what the report itself cites, and the visitor's second run tests how far up an outsider's
  reach goes.

Nothing on this page claims to be art. It is a form for the Field's finding of step 4.

For the programme: A second run of the report's evaluation evidence by someone other than the maker reaches 10 of its 41 measured claims on paper, 2 on the models the makers still offered on 19 November 2026 and 1 from 1 December, a test trained into open weights, so what an outsider can check of today's safety evaluations expires with the hosted models they ran on.
