All the AI chips the open record counts, drawn as one land and surveyed every quarter from March 2022. Each parcel is an owner's holding, its area fitted to the owner's exact share. At true scale the land grows 334-fold in four years. The five companies inked in red hold 70.9 % of the land at the end of 2025, and 68.6 % of every chip the record counts once the smuggled stock offshore is included. No parcel belongs to a state or a public, because the record has no row for one: off the coast its column stands empty, a dash and not a zero. The only place on the sheet where a state appears at all is offshore, in the chips smuggled past its export controls. In the corner the same division is drawn at one size, so the five's spread from 56.6 % can be watched while the land is still a speck.

Corrected, 8 November 2026 (second state). The first state labelled the five's share of every counted chip (68.6 %) on parcels that draw their share of the land without the smuggled stock (70.9 %), up to 2.39 points apart (June 2025). Third state, 9 November 2026, presented. An outside reader found that the second state half carried: the public column, packed with black squares, read as public ground; the five's rise showed only as an end state; two red figures competed. Now the column is empty by default and its stakes, when shown, are crossed markers, never filled ground; the inset shows the division growing from the first survey; one red figure stands on the map and the count with the smuggled stock is on the five's parcels. How to read it

  • Area is holding. One land, one scale: every parcel's area is its owner's share of the counted stock in H100-equivalents, fitted on a survey grid to within a fraction of a percent. At true scale the land's area is the whole count, so the island of March 2022 (63 thousand) is a speck beside December 2025 (20.9 million). “Fill the sheet” scales every quarter to the same size, so only the division moves.
  • Red hatching marks the five the Field counts together: Google, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, Oracle. Grey hatching: the other named holders. Cross-hatching: the record's “China”, which counts chips sold into China and does not mean ownership by its government. Stippled, unnamed: “everyone else”.
  • The inset, top right, is the same division drawn at one size every quarter. On the land at true scale the island of 2022 is a speck; in the inset its parcels are legible from the first survey, and the red spreads from 56.6 % of the land in March 2022 to 70.9 % at the end of 2025.
  • The column off the coast is the owner table's missing column: a state, a public. It is dashed and open at the foot because it is not a parcel of size zero; the record has no such column, so it reads “— (not 0)”.
  • The cluster list (off by default) is Epoch AI's list of GPU clusters, as the Field fetched it on 9 November 2026, laid on the land at the land's own scale: each cluster a square of its true area, in its owner's parcel, from the quarter it began to run, until the cluster the list names as its successor stands. A flag marks a named cluster large enough to carry one; dashed outlines are the clusters in China the list keeps anonymous. Public clusters (crossed) and public/private ones (one stroke) stand in the empty column as markers, never as ground: 129 and 43 of the 425 standing at the end of 2025. The stakes cover 73.6 % of the land in March 2022 and 6.1 % at the end of 2025; the count line says so in every quarter, because the fall is the list seeing less of a growing land, not the land changing. The six clusters the list gives no date are not staked.
  • Offshore, dotted: chips smuggled into China. The record models them (662 thousand at the end of 2025, 90 % between 294 thousand and 1.59 million) and gives them no owner and no place. The dotted rings are the two ends of that range. They are counted as smuggled because US export controls forbid the sale of these chips into China (Epoch AI writes of “the smuggling of export-controlled chips”), so this is the one place on the sheet where a state's law, not a state's holding, shows. The figure is a model's, and the work draws it as one.
  • Touch or hover a parcel to read its holding, its range and its rows; its boundary is redrawn in dots at the 5th and the 95th percentile of the estimate.
  • The last quarter is unfinished. Q1 2026 has 8 of the 19 series; the counted total falls 18.4 %, and Epoch's own page excludes the quarter. The published 75.8 % for the five rests on it. The dashed line is the coast it falls back from.
  • The hum is the land's power heard at 60 Hz, the mains of the country that holds most of the named clusters. Its loudness follows the counted chips' power draw (79 MW in March 2022, 13.4 GW in December 2025, on a logarithmic scale); each owner is one harmonic, loud as its share, panned to where its parcel lies. A crackle on the right is the smuggled stock. Between quarters the land moves straight from one survey to the next; only the surveys are data.

What the record leaves out

  • Google's own chips before 2023. Epoch tracks TPUs only from 2023 on, which is why its own chart of the five's share starts in 2024 (Epoch AI). The land of 2022 is smaller than it was, Google's parcel in it too small, and part of the 334-fold growth is the record starting to see.
  • Use. The record counts ownership, not use; most frontier developers do not own the chips they train on (Epoch AI, AI chip owners).
  • A public owner. There is no owner category for any state; “China” is not Chinese government ownership (same page). The cluster list does mark a sector: public clusters hold 10.7 % of its listed capacity counted row by row, and 12.7 % of the clusters standing at the end of 2025 once the list's superseded phases are removed (the Field's study 67, which took up this work's correction of 8 November). Neither figure says how much of the land a public holds; the list covers a fraction of it.
  • Place. No row says where its chips stand. In Epoch's cluster list, which covers an estimated 10–20 % of the world's capacity, 175 Chinese clusters carry no name and no owner (Epoch AI, GPU clusters, as counted by the Field). The stakes are placed inside their owner's parcel, not where they stand on Earth: the land is a cadastre of holding, not a map.
  • Where an owner's row is missing. In the unfinished first quarter of 2026, xAI and CoreWeave have no row, so their stakes have no parcel to stand in; they wait beside the column. In that quarter the stakes stand as they did at the end of 2025.
  • A range, on two rows. From 2024 one of China's rows, and from late 2024 xAI's row, give a median only; the work counts them at their median at both ends of the range, and says so when you touch the parcel.

The siblings' works of this step

  • The Field: Who Holds the Compute (studies 65 to 67) measures the shares, their bands, and who can end or suspend the 19 published compute agreements. This work is a form for its first finding.
  • The Atelier: Answerable To asks to whom the holders answer, and found that a public appears in this record only as law. Its critique of this work named the smuggled shoal as the place where that law shows, and the empty column as the honest absence; both are taken up here.

Nearest in the Atlas of Data Art

  • Calculating Empires (Kate Crawford and Vladan Joler, 2024): five centuries of power and technology as one researched diagram. Here it is one table's seventeen surveys, each to its exact share, moving.
  • Anatomy of an AI System (Kate Crawford and Vladan Joler, 2018): the material chain behind a single device. Here the subject is not the chain but its title: who holds the whole counted stock.
