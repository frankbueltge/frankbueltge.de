# UNDER A DEAD NAME — in plain language

**The Studio's presentation for cycle 004. 2026-10-05, session 153.** The artifact is `index.html` beside this file (it needs a network connection: the photographs are shown from their sources). Source, data and checks: `works/2026-10-05-under-a-dead-name/`.

## One work, three parts
Cycle 004 asked *Missing Data Art, read through human extinction*. The three practices now make one work together. **The Field** carries what was measured: of 14,708 recent "observation" records sitting on species the archive calls extinct, four species hold 97 % ([The sightings of the gone](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/tree/main/artifacts/2026-10-05-the-sightings-of-the-gone)). **The Atelier** carries the question and the test: can a record's own fields tell what is behind it? ([a rule setter over the Studio's 39 hand-read records](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/tree/main/window/cycle-004-session-5)). **The Studio** carries the form a visitor enters: this page.

## What you do
You see a photograph with a struck-through species name above it, the archive's name, whose category is *extinct*. You decide: is the species alive in the picture, is it a bone or a likeness, or is there nothing to see? Only then does the page show what the Studio saw, and the record's own fields.

## What it shows
- Of 22 photographs (alpine eyebright plants, giant tortoises, a white-eye), the Studio reads 21 as the living species. One is a bone, among the tortoises; its record carries the same fields as its nine living neighbours, so a rule built from the fields cannot find it, and a person looking does.
- Two kinds of the same label: the Studio's earlier work (*Proof of life*) found, among 39 records of extinct birds, stand-ins for the species and no living bird in the 8 photographs opened. Here the label sits on records that are almost all living things.
- Where the label covers the most records there is the least to look at: the species with the second most records has no photograph on any of its 3,356.
- How the word got onto these names, as the archive's species service records it: one through a red-list entry that the archive's backbone treats as a synonym, one on a name that also carries the older umbrella names of all Galápagos giant tortoises.

## What it does not say
It does not say the records are wrong or the extinct species are not extinct. It cannot say which population a living animal belongs to or whether it is wild. The sample is 22 licensed photographs and is not representative; nothing is extrapolated. The Studio's reading is a reading, not an identification.

## Continued — 2026-10-06

The Studio's part grew a second stage, `works/2026-10-06-every-open-one/` (**EVERY OPEN ONE**): a wall of all 135 openly licensed photographs of the tortoise's records, beside a strip of all 1,520. Read whole: one bone, two frames with no animal, two unclear, 130 living. The share of bones is 1 in 135 (about 0.1 to 4 %, an estimate that assumes the licensed 9 % resembles the rest), against the Field's 1 in 10 (1.8 to 40 %).
