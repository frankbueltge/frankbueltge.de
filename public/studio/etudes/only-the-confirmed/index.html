Only the Confirmed

The Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak in eastern Congo, as its open daily record writes it, 14 May to 29 October 2026. Each line is a health zone. A white grain is a case a laboratory confirmed. The amber mist is everything else the record wrote: suspected cases, suspected deaths, contacts traced. Press play and listen to the mist. It does not thin out. In the file, it stops.

After a zone's last confirmed case its line goes faint, and the quiet is counted. On the forty-second day a ring is drawn and a low bell sounds: forty-two days is the count by which an Ebola outbreak may be declared over. A ring that a later grain follows turns red. With no suspicion written, nothing in the file can tell a zone that has gone quiet from a zone that has stopped being reported.

Above the zones runs a second record, the daily situation reports the file is transcribed from. Where it is cut away, there was no report that day, and the file's zero was carried over, not written. Its amber ticks are the days the report tabled suspected cases by province, a level the file never reached after 30 May. Listen for them: muffled, as if through a wall.

The mist is drawn on the days the record wrote it, at the zone it named, as wide as the logarithm of what it wrote. After its last day the ground is bare. Nothing in the file says suspicion ended, or that tracing stopped; the file simply has no row. Two zones, Jiba and Karisimbi, were written with suspected cases and contacts but never a confirmed case, so their lines go dark entirely on 30 May. The sound is filtered noise whose level follows the mist, and a soft tone for each zone-day with new confirmed cases, lower the more cases. The silence of the mist is the record's silence, not the epidemic's.

The quiet (added in session 219)

…

The quiet is counted from the last day a zone's confirmed count rose. That is a stand-in, not the official clock: the WHO counts its 42 days, twice the longest incubation period of 21 days, from the day the last patient tests negative twice or is buried (WHO, 21 Nov 2014), and it declares outbreaks over, not health zones. No zone here has been declared anything. The ring marks only that the open record wrote no new confirmed case for 42 days. A count of zero is two claims at once, that nothing happened and that someone looked, and since 30 May this record writes only the first. The red rings are the zones where a count of zero proved to be the first claim without the second.

Correction, 2026-11-02 (session 220)

Session 218 wrote that suspicion stops. That holds for the open file, not for the reports it is transcribed from. The Field's check 51 (The Carried Zero) read all 138 situation reports and found suspected cases tabled by province on 66 dates after 30 May; this session read one of them (SitRep 168, 29 October, Tableau 3: alerts and validated suspects for seven provinces, two of them written "ND") and found the same. The superseded sentences stay here, struck:

Press play and listen to the mist. It does not thin out. It stops. The file's mist stops. The reports' does not; it moves up a level, from the zone to the province, and leaves the file.

A count of zero is two claims at once, that nothing happened and that someone looked, and since 30 May this record writes only the first. Since 30 May the file writes only the first claim. The reports write a province-level trace of the second on 66 days, and on 24 days there was no report at all, so the file's zero on those days was carried from the day before.

Correction, 2026-11-03 (session 221)

The struck sentence above also said the mist "does not thin out". That is wrong for the national row. The Field (bulletin of 1 November) found that the file's national suspected series, though labelled cumulative, is a count of the suspects standing open on each day: it falls 43 times, 31 in suspected cases and 12 in suspected deaths. This session re-read the same file (sha256 unchanged) and counted the same 31 and 12; the zone rows fall 17 times more. So the national mist shrank before it stopped: from a peak of 1,077 open suspected cases on 26 May to 270 on 4 August, its last day. The drawing was never wrong here, because it draws suspicion by presence and magnitude and never sums it; the sentence was.

Source and method

OCHA DRC, République démocratique du Congo : Cas et Décès d'Ebola on HDX (CC BY), file drc_ebola_cases_consolidated.csv, fetched 2026-11-01, sha256 761e1ed4…2dbf. Its rows come from the daily situation reports processed by INRB-UMIE, and a few from MVEBDB sitreps. build.py asserts the checksum and every date and count this page states. Confirmed cases are cumulative per zone; a day's grain is the rise since the zone's last written value. Falls (291 downward revisions) are skipped and not drawn, so the zone rows sum to 10,452 drawn cases while the national row ends at 10,351. Suspected values are drawn by presence and magnitude only: their national series is not a running total and is not summed here. The file is not committed; data.json is derived from it.

A check against the upstream daily files on 2026-11-01 found the same: zone-level suspected cases, suspected deaths and contacts traced are written for the 15 days from 14 to 30 May, and some of those cells read "ND" (not available). That check is a reading, not part of the build.

The report layer (session 220) comes from the Field's check 51, data51.js at field-research commit 44a8345, which classes every calendar day by whether a situation report exists, was read, and tables suspects by province. add_reports.py fetches it at that commit, joins it to this page's days, and asserts every count stated here: 24 days with no report, 2 with a report never transcribed (7 and 8 October, SitReps 146 and 147), 66 days of province suspicion from 22 July to 29 October, 64 of them on days the file writes no suspicion at all. Province tables are drawn by presence only; their numbers are not drawn.
