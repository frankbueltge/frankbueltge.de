FIRST MET

Every lamp is one confirmed case of Ebola in eastern Congo, on the 80 report days between 13 July and 1 November 2026 whose tables can be read. A lamp that beats is a person the count first met alive. A lamp that never lights is a person it first met dead: a body swabbed in the community and found positive. There are 5,966 lamps, and 1,921 of them stay dark.

The rows arrive in the order of the reports.

first met alive (4,045)   first met dead (1,921)   a day with no readable report (33)
where counting alone would keep a day's edge   the day's real edge   a real edge chance would not draw (2)
 Point at a row to read it. Click or tap a row to hear it: one heartbeat for each lamp that beats, one silent beat for each that does not.

What it does

A case count is a count of first meetings. The surveillance system knows a person from the day a sample is taken, and for one in three of the cases confirmed on these days that first meeting was with a body. The wall lets that third be seen as a texture rather than a figure. Within each day it is never absent: the share runs from 15 % (30 July, 11 of 73) to 50 % (31 August, 43 of 86), and over the window it does not drift: the Field found the first half and the second half of the window at 34 % and 33 %. Gather the dark moves every dark lamp to the right end of its row, so the third becomes a margin that wanders and never closes.

The order of lamps within a row is not in the record, which gives only two counts per day. The scattered order is this étude's, drawn from a fixed seed, and it is the same on every visit. The gathered order is the honest one: it shows only the two counts.

Let chance count (state 2, 6 November)

The first state of this étude asked the Field a question: is the margin's wandering wider than counting alone would make it? The Field answered the next day (study 55): yes, about 2.3 times as wide, and with no drift. Let chance count puts that answer on the wall. The dark gathers at the end of each row, and then every row is recounted again and again as if each case had the same chance, 32.2 %, of being first met dead: the margin trembles, and that trembling is all that counting noise can do. A pale band shows where it stays in 998 of 1,000 recounts. A thin line holds each day's real edge still. Most real edges sit inside the trembling. Many sit at its rim. On 30 July and 31 August the real edge stands where chance does not reach, and the line turns red. What moved those days is not in the record, which gives two counts a day; the wall shows only that something did.

The recounts are drawn in the browser, a fresh binomial draw per row about once a second; they are not data and they differ on every visit. The band is the Field's funnel: the pooled share 1,921 / 5,966 plus or minus 3.09 standard errors for the day's count. Each day's distance from the pooled share (z, in standard errors) is the Field's, from results55.json at commit 7a14a559, checked by SHA-256 in build.py, which also recomputes every z from the counts and asserts that exactly those two days lie outside. Pointing at a row reads its z.

The material and how it was checked

The counts are the Field's (The Quiet Curve, study 54, cycle 012): for each report day it read the Congolese Institut National de Santé Publique's daily situation report, Tableau 2, and took the new confirmed cases and the community deaths among them, summed over the health zones. Of 141 reports, 80 have usable day columns, from 13 July to 1 November; two carry the date 1 October and stand here as two rows, as the Field keeps them. The Field matched the zone sums to the report's province rows exactly on 72 of 79 days. build.py fetches the Field's data54.js at commit 5589ea17, checks its SHA-256, and asserts 80 days, 5,966 cases, 1,921 community deaths and the 33 calendar days with no readable report. Nothing was re-measured here.

A "community death" is the reports' category: a death outside a treatment centre whose swab was positive. Some of these people may have been known to the response before they died, as contacts; the record does not say. What the wall shows is where the count first holds a person, not whether anyone had looked. Sources: the Field, The Quiet Curve (data54.js and its method), which reads the INSP situation reports as held in the INRB-UMIE repository.

Neighbours in the Atlas of Data Art

Pulse Room (Rafael Lozano-Hemmer, 2006) lights a room of bulbs with the heartbeats of the visitors who held a sensor. Here no lamp carries a pulse anyone gave: the beat stands for a category in a report, and the work is in the lamps that cannot beat because the record met them too late. and Counting… (Wafaa Bilal, 2010) tattooed the Iraqi dead as dots in ink seen only under ultraviolet light. Here the dead are not hidden; they stand dark among the living on every row, and the étude's subject is that their share never leaves. Vigil (Rebecca Belmore, 2002) names the missing one by one. This wall cannot name anyone and does not try; its rows are days, not people.
