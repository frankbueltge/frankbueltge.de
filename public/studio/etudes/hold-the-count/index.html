HOLD THE COUNT

An Ebola outbreak is declared over after 42 days with no new case, and the same count is read zone by zone. Here the count is your hand. Press and hold the field below (or hold the space bar). You are given one real silence from the record: a health zone, the day it last confirmed a case. One second is one day. Hold for 42 and you may let go and declare it over. The record decides whether your hand is broken first, and what came after.

What you are holding

Every hold is one quiet, drawn at random from all quiets of the record you chose: a zone's confirmed count rose on a day, and the quiet lasts until it rises again. The Field built every quiet of both records for its study 56 (who the curve speaks for), from the open health-zone figures of the 2018–2020 epidemic and from the OCHA DRC daily file of 2026 on HDX (CC BY), cut at 29 October 2026. Nothing here is simulated: the zone, the date and the day the next case came are the record's.

In North Kivu no report at all came from 8 March to 9 June 2020, 94 days. A hold that runs into those days goes dark: your hand keeps counting, and nobody is looking. The dates are the Atelier's (quiets.json, records.kivuHole).

Honest limits

  • A quiet is a quiet in the count, not in the zone. Whether anyone was ill in it, the record does not say.
  • The rule's 42 days run from the day the last patient left care or was buried; a quiet here runs from the day the zone's confirmed count last rose. The two clocks are not the same, and the rule is written for an outbreak, not for one zone inside a running one.
  • A day is a calendar day of the file, not a day of reporting: the 2026 file and the Kivu file write zones on days when nothing was reported, and leave others blank.
  • Your holds and declarations stay in this page and are sent nowhere.

Neighbours

From the house's Atlas of Data Art: Rafael Lozano-Hemmer's Pulse Room (2006), where the hand that holds the interface lends the room its own heartbeat and the lights die when it lets go; Rebecca Belmore's Vigil (2002), a durational marking of an unresolved count. Here the hand lends nothing of itself: it lends time to a stranger's silence, at a second a day, and it is the record, not the hand, that ends the hold. Nearest in this house: CALL IT (the visitor calls twelve quiets over at one edge); here there is no edge to stand at, only the wait, one quiet at a time, and almost none reach the day.
