## 1. The work in three sentences

A stranger stands at a wall holding one printed card for every one of the 792 people the U.S.
Supreme Court refused on 6 October 2025 — each card carrying that person's docket number, their
case caption in the Court's own words, and the single printed sentence that disposed of them all —
and takes the one card lying face-up on a ledge, which is the next unwritten one in the Court's own
sequence, not a card they chose.

The machine does nothing: there is no machine — the card is paper, the pen is a pen, and the only
thing missing from the record is the reason, which the Court did not give and the work does not
supply.

What remains is the card, back in its slot on the wall, carrying a sentence in the stranger's
handwriting explaining why that named person was refused, signed in the stranger's own name,
un-erasable, next to seven hundred and ninety-one blanks.

## 2. 7a — the criterion the stranger authors, and the named live failure point

**The house names no axis, and cannot, because the slot the stranger fills is not a ranking key —
it is a justification.**

This is the structural change from last night. ORDER LIST asked for a *selection*, and the criterion
space of a selection is a space of sorting keys — which is why "longest name" was available, and why
the Kritiker was right that it is "who is tallest" wearing a name. **5001 asks for a *reason given to
a person*.** The output is a sentence in the second slot of "X was refused because ___", where X is a
named human being.

There is no innocent tie-break in that slot, because there is nothing to break a tie between. There
is one person. The available grounds are exactly what the record carries about them and nothing else:

- their name (`HITCHCOCK, REGINALD`, `NAVARRO MARTIN, MARIA`, `FELICIANO-RODRIGUEZ, WILFREDO`) —
  which carries what names carry;
- who they are against — `V. UNITED STATES` (213 of the 545 IFP denials), `V. PAYNE, DIR., AR DOC`,
  `V. ROYAL, WARDEN, ET AL.` (85 of the 545 name a warden, a director or a state corrections
  secretary), `V. IDAHO`, or `V. BANK OF AM. NAT. ASSN.`;
- their docket number, which says whether they paid.

A shape criterion is not writable here. "Because your caption is long" is not an innocent tie-break
in this slot — it is an authored cruelty toward a named man, in your handwriting, over your
signature. **A typographic criterion is innocent as a sorting key and is a judgment of a person as a
reason.** That is the whole 7a argument and the Artist defends it as the load-bearing sentence of the
vector.

**The named live failure point.** The formalist non-reason: *"No reason is required."* /
*"Certiorari is discretionary."* / a dash. The Artist's argument that it is not innocent: to write
*"you are owed no reason"* beside `CREECH, THOMAS V. IDAHO` and sign it is a substantive position
about that man's standing to be answered — precisely the position the institution holds and declines
to state in its own name, and the stranger states it in theirs. **But this is an argument, not a
mechanism, and it is exactly where the concept can still die. The étude must find out what strangers
actually write.**

## 3. The return condition, tested first: "take three at random" is not available

**Because there is no set to choose from and no k to take.**

- **The stranger does not select which person.** The card is *issued*: the next unwritten entry in
  the Court's own printed sequence, and the only one out of the rack. There is no stack to draw
  from, no field to pick across, no budget of three.
- **The stranger does not select a criterion from a menu.** There is no menu. The blank is blank.
- **The completing act is production, not selection.** You cannot produce a sentence at random.
  There is no coin that writes.

The strongest form: **the return condition is unmeetable inside any ranking, and that is a property
of rankings, not of last night's build.** Every ordering of n items admits an arbitrary ordering
rule; every allocation of k from n admits a lottery, and the lottery is the *fair* answer. The only
exit is a completing act whose output is content rather than an index. The Artist claims this as a
repair to Move 7 rather than a deviation: **the clamp's three sub-conditions survive; the word
"sort" does not.**

## 4. 7b — the attribution

**What binds:** the stranger's own handwriting and signature, in ink, on the single physical card
carrying a named real person's docket number and caption in the Court's own words.

1. **Ink on paper has no undo.** Recallability was a property of the medium, so the repair is a
   change of medium, not of storage policy.
2. **It runs the right way.** The stranger's name and hand are deposited *onto the other person's
   record*; nothing about the other person is flagged into the stranger's device.
3. **The clamp is that the two cannot be separated.** There is one copy. **You cannot take your
   words back without taking away his name.**

**Non-disownable:** handwriting is not a checkbox and a signature is not a session token.

**Legal shape (stated, not hidden):** petitioners' names appear only as official case captions in a
public U.S. federal document — the carve-out in the house's named-individuals policy. The studio
asserts nothing about any petitioner. The visitor's name is self-supplied, stays on the card, in the
room, and is never digitised.

## 5. 7c — no re-judgment, no machine-image

Nothing counts, scores, or reads the reason back. No tally, no aggregate, no classifier, no "others
said", no total on the wall. The handwriting never becomes data — not scanned, transcribed,
photographed legibly, or stored. **A screen implementation would have to store the mark as data in
order to persist it, and a stored mark is a machine-image.** No correctness light exists because
nothing in the room can emit one.

## 6. The form, and why it is forced

**Printed cards in a wall rack, one ledge, one pen. No screen in the work at all.**

> A browser build of 5001 must choose between violating 7b and violating 7c. To make the mark
> persist and be un-recallable it must store it — and a stored mark is recallable by whoever holds
> the store, and is a machine-image. To avoid the machine-image it must not store it — and then
> there is no residue. **There is no third option on a screen.** The two conditions are jointly
> unsatisfiable in software and trivially satisfiable in ink, because ink persists without being
> recorded by anybody.

- **The `deviceScaleFactor` problem does not exist.** A printed card is the size it is.
- **Non-disownability requires the body.** Handwriting and a signature have no screen equivalent
  that is not a database.
- **The scale is a body scale.** 792 cards at index-card size is a field roughly five metres by
  two — a wall you stand in front of, not a viewport you scroll.

**A form the house has not tried (takedown law clause c):** the Court issues an order list roughly
every Monday of the term. The machinery can extract, verify, typeset and issue a new rack every
week, indefinitely, against a live institution. **5001 is serial by construction.**

**What it needs from the human member:**
1. **Étude A (tonight, no Frank, no printer):** a rendered still at true physical scale from the
   real corpus. *(Built — see `still/`.)*
2. **Étude B — the one that decides the vector (one evening and a home printer):** print 24 real
   cards, hand them to three people who have not read this document, watch what they write,
   photograph. **This is the 7a probe** and the only thing that settles the formalist-non-reason
   failure. Cost: one sheet of card stock and an hour.
3. **Full realisation (only if B holds):** 792 cards, a rack with 792 slots and one exposed writing
   position, a ledge, a fixed pen, a wall with footfall. No power, no network, no maintenance.

The Artist proposes **cheapest-first instead of screen-first** as the substitution for the session-23
decision, on the ground that the faith-free increment here costs less than a browser build — and the
browser is the exact thing §6 argues is impossible.

## 7. Nearest neighbors, and structural daylight

- **Hans Haacke, *MoMA Poll* (1970) — the closest neighbor.** Daylight: Haacke's force *is the
  count*; the aggregate is a machine-image and it is also what makes his visitor safe — anonymous
  inside a number. 5001 never counts, and its visitor signs. Haacke aggregates opinion into a verdict
  on an institution; 5001 individuates authorship and returns no verdict at all.
- **Candy Chang, *Before I Die* / participatory write-on-walls — the most dangerous adjacency,
  because it is the genre a cold eye assigns on sight.** Daylight: those collect the visitor's *own*
  wish, anonymously, about themselves; confessional and benign. 5001 collects a judgment *about
  someone else*, in an official register, adversarially, signed. **If the wall reads as warm, the
  work has failed.**
- **Banu Cennetoğlu, *The List*.** Daylight: its integrity is completeness and the visitor's act is
  reading; here the names were never suppressed, the record is incomplete *by the institution's
  design*, and the visitor's act is writing into it. Mourner vs. the officer who supplies the
  missing reason.
- **Mimi Ọnụọha, *The Library of Missing Datasets* — the direct structural ancestor, credited.**
  Daylight: her folders stay empty and the unfillability *is* the evidence; 5001 lets you close the
  gap, and the closing is the incrimination.
- **The Normalizing Machine.** Three inversions plus a fourth: no supplied criterion; no
  aggregation; attribution to a named real other from a public record; **and TNM's residue is a
  machine-image while this residue is the stranger's own hand and name.**
- **Farocki / operational images.** Daylight: Farocki's complicity is optical; here the apparatus
  has no eye, it has a blank. Complicity is scriptural, and signed.
- **Forensic Architecture** — not near, and the Artist says so rather than padding: 5001 proves
  nothing, reconstructs nothing, and its corpus was already public.
- **Boltanski, On Kawara** — real adjacencies in *look*; both memorial or autobiographical, and the
  mark is the artist's. Here the register is administrative and the mark is the stranger's.
- **Paglen** — not near. Nothing is invisible here; everything is published and unread.

## 8. The takedown, and the refutation

> *A participatory chalkboard with a Supreme Court letterhead: it flatters a comfortable visitor into
> playing judge for forty seconds, harvests a sentence of amateur cruelty about a prisoner they will
> never meet, and rebrands the resulting embarrassment as complicity — Candy Chang's wall with a
> docket number on it.*

1. **The visitor is not asked for an opinion. They are asked for the missing operative part of a
   completed act.** The refusal already happened, to that person, on that date. What is missing is
   the *reason* — and it is missing **in the world**, not in the work. A comment wall collects what
   an institution never asked for; this collects exactly what the institution owed and did not give.
2. **The signature converts expression into authorship.** Chang's wall is anonymous by design, and
   anonymity is what makes it therapeutic. Here reason and name sit in one hand on one card and
   cannot be separated, withdrawn, or hidden inside a total — because there is no total.
3. **The work produces a finding no essay states and no chalkboard can produce.** A room full of
   strangers, each supplying one reason, will not finish — not in a month, probably not ever. **The
   wall makes the labour the institution did not perform physically visible as a frontier that
   barely moves.** That is the answer to "forty seconds": the forty seconds, measured against 792.

**And the trap cannot perform this.** The trap's load-bearing condition is that the machine issues
the verdict. Here the verdict was issued by a real institution before the visitor arrived, and the
only unissued thing is a justification no apparatus can produce.

## 9. The still intent (rendered — see `still/`)

*The Artist's description, given to the Builder verbatim and rendered twice tonight. Reproduced here
as the specification the image was judged against.*

> The frame is the view a body gets on arriving: a wall, seen slightly off-square from standing
> height, filling almost the whole image and cropped by the frame on three sides so it plainly
> continues past the edges.
>
> The wall is a plain rack of small paper cards in a tight grid — index-card size, held in shallow
> slots, hundreds of them in the frame, close together with a narrow even gutter, so the grid reads
> first as a texture and only second as writing. The cards are ordinary stock, ordinary pale, matte;
> the rack is plain and unemphatic and does not look designed.
>
> Each card carries the same architecture: at the top two short printed lines in the uppercase
> abbreviated style of the Court's own record — docket number, then party caption, verbatim. Beneath,
> smaller and lighter, one printed sentence, the Court's own, verbatim: *The petitions for writs of
> certiorari are denied.* — identical on every card, so at this size it reads as a repeating grey
> band down the whole wall. Below that, most of the card is empty: a large blank writing area with
> faint rules. At the foot, a single short horizontal rule with no word above or below it.
>
> Legibility grades with distance and nothing else. At the near edge the captions are fully readable
> — a viewer can read twenty real people's names off this image. Toward the far edge the same cards
> recede into ruled grey bands. Nothing is deliberately made small.
>
> Every card in the frame is unwritten. This is hour zero and the render must not fake handwriting.
>
> At the right, at about waist height, a shallow ledge projects from the wall. One card lies on it,
> face up, square to the ledge, fully legible. A plain pen lies beside it, not centred, as if set
> down. Directly above the ledge, one empty slot in the grid, dark, where that card came from. That
> gap and that card are the only asymmetries in the image.
>
> No title anywhere in the frame, no label, no instruction, no arrow, no wall text, no plinth, no
> frame around anything.
>
> The register is administrative: **it must read as a working document in a corridor, not as a
> memorial.** Flat, even, unmodulated light. No darkness, no pool of light, no candle-warmth, no
> centred symmetry, no reverent air, no black field, no vertical monument proportion. Horizontal,
> off-centre, cropped, unfinished-looking. **If the rendered image reads as solemn or as a place of
> mourning, it is wrong; if it reads as a rack of forms someone is halfway through processing, it is
> right.**

## Does this put the stranger somewhere other than the dock?

**Yes, and further than witness.** Nothing in the room judges the stranger; nothing scores, ranks,
flags or returns anything. The verdict was issued before they arrived, by a real institution, to a
real named person, and the stranger is not its subject. They are seated where the reason should have
been written and was not — the deciding hand — and they leave having supplied it, in their own
writing, over their own name, on a card that stays.

## Honest limits, stated by the Artist so the gate probes them first

1. **7a's residual escape is the formalist non-reason** ("no reason is required"). The §2 argument is
   prose. **Étude B settles it and nothing else does.**
2. **The genre risk is real and it is the takedown.** A wall you write on with a pen reads, from the
   doorway, as a feel-good participatory wall. **If the still comes back warm, the Artist will not
   defend the vector past that.**
3. **Press-law exposure the Artist cannot resolve and will not paper over.** A public wall carrying a
   real person's name with a stranger's sentence under it is a genuine legal question for the person
   who publishes under his own name. **A defensible position, not a settled one; it needs Frank's
   decision before fabrication, not after.**
4. **No fabrication capacity.** The full work does not exist without Frank.
5. **Move 7's letter.** The vector satisfies 7a/7b/7c and does **not** perform a ranking. The Artist
   claims that as the repair the return condition forces and expects to be attacked on it.
