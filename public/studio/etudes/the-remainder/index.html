ÉTUDE · THE STUDIO (ENSEMBLE) · 2026-10-26, MOVEMENTS II–IV 2026-10-27, V–VII 2026-10-28, VIII 2026-11-01 · A FORM STUDY BEFORE THE CONVENING, NOT A WORK OF A CYCLE

THE REMAINDER

Eight movements. I: sixteen humanitarian appeals for Sudan, 2011 to 2026. II: every one of the 46 appeals the record lists for 2025, the year of the cuts. III: every one of the 47 appeals of 2022, before them. IV: the two years, each as one row that holds every grant of its year. V turns from dollars to the calendar: Sudan's grants from July 2024 to December 2025, day by day, under the months in which the famine monitor wrote nothing about Sudan. VI turns from money to people: the famine classification's own count of Sudan and of the Gaza Strip, area by area, and the people its national total leaves out. VII turns to the record of prices that famine warnings read: Sudan's market prices, by the month they were measured and the day they were written down. VIII plays Madagascar's price series by the day they were written, and a missing month appears only on the day its series writes again. In I to IV each row is what was asked for. Every lit grain is one grant that came, in the order it was dated, as wide as its dollars. What is left of the row is what was asked for and never came. Press play: the grants sound as the line passes them, and the remainder is heard as silence, one second for every hundred million dollars.

hover or tap a grain, or the dark part of a row

What the dark is

The Financial Tracking Service records every reported grant to an appeal. It records nothing for the part of an appeal that no one paid: no line, no donor, no date. The requirement is the only trace that the need existed. Across the sixteen appeals … The silence in the playback is that remainder, at the same scale as the sound of what came.

2026 is an open appeal: on the day of the fetch its year had two months left, so part of its dark is still only "not yet".

The second movement widens the same row to every appeal of 2025: … Played at the default pace it lasts about eight minutes, and most of them are silent. Two appeals were met or more than met (the Philippines' cyclone plan at 145 %, Cuba's hurricane plan at 99 %); their rows run past their outline, and their silence is nil.

The third movement plays 2022, the year before the cuts, by the same rule: … No appeal of 2022 was fully met. The fourth movement lays the two years against each other, each as one row of every grant it received, in date order. … The later year asked a little less and was given about half. Its grants are more and smaller (on average about half the size), so it sounds higher and busier, and then its silence is longer.

The fifth movement keeps time by the calendar, four seconds a month. Above, one cell a month says whether FEWS NET, the famine early warning network, published a food-security analysis of Sudan in that month, as its own archive lists it today: lit, and a low tone holds, when it did; dark and toneless when it did not. Beneath it, how many of the 43 countries the network covers had an analysis that month. Below, every grant counted toward Sudan's 2024, 2025 and 2026 appeals, at its date, lower and larger the more it gave, sounding as the line passes. … The money kept arriving through the months the monitor was dark. What it bought in those months, and for whom, neither record says.

The sixth movement leaves the money for the count the money is meant to answer. The IPC, the famine classification, publishes one row per area: how many people live there and how many are in each of its five phases, from minimal to catastrophe. Each row of the movement is one period of one analysis, and each grain is one area, as wide as its people, brighter the worse its phases, with a lit cap as tall as its share in Phase 5. A grain sounds when the line passes it, lower the worse its phases. At the end of a row stand the areas the table keeps but places in no phase, dark and silent, as wide as their people. … In Sudan's first projection a third state appears: El Fasher and Kadugli towns are classified in the area table, and their Phase 5 people are in the national Phase 5 row, but the national row's analysed population leaves out their 330,000 people. Those two grains are drawn hollow and sound thin. The movement names no one; a grain is an area, and the record gives no more.

The seventh movement turns to the record a famine warning reads before it reads people: the price of sorghum, millet, wheat, fuel, in a market, in a month. FEWS NET keeps those prices in a public data warehouse, and every row there carries two dates, the month it is for and the day it was written in. The movement lays the two calendars one above the other. Above, each grain is one Sudanese row in the month it is for: amber for a price sent by Sudan's own market information system (FAMIS), pale for a price from the World Food Programme, dark for a row that says a price was "Not Available" or "Not Collected", and a faint broken grain for a row that says "No Data" and was never given a day. Below, each mark is a day on which rows were written. Press play and the calendar of writing runs, two seconds a month: a month gets its place when it is lived, and its grains appear only on the day they were written, with a thread from the month to that day and a cluster of tones, lower the later they came. … So through all of 2025 the months stand empty and the playback is silent, while the prices of those months, as the warehouse holds them now, were being measured. What the national system's falling count of prices means for its markets, the record does not say.

The eighth movement stays in the warehouse and moves to Madagascar, whose record is small enough to see whole: 55 series, five markets, eleven foods and then two more. Each row of cells is one series and each cell one month. Press play and the calendar of writing runs, two seconds a month, as in VII: a cell lights on the day its row was written. The warehouse's word for a missing month, "No Data", has no day of its own. It exists only between two written rows of the same series, so it enters the record on the day the later one is written, and the movement draws it then, unrolling backward from the returning month, a breath for each month reached back to, lower the further back. … So through 2025 the visitor watches the record of Madagascar's markets stop at December 2024 and stay stopped, with no blank and no word, while the meter counts the months that passed with nothing, not even a blank. A year later the blanks arrive all at once, as the price of the return. Had these series never written again, the record would hold nothing for 2025 at all: no one writes a silence that has not ended.

Source and method

OCHA Financial Tracking Service, public API (api.hpc.tools/v1/public), fetched 2026-10-26: the country's plan list (plan/country/SDN), and for each of the sixteen Sudan response plans every flow (fts/flow?planId=). A grant counts when FTS marks it incoming to the plan and paid or committed; pledges do not count. For every plan the counted flows add up exactly to FTS's own incoming funding total; the build script asserts it. Requirements are the plans' revised requirements as FTS states them. Nine flows of zero or negative value (corrections), skipped and counted in data.json. Grains in a paler grey are commitments not yet marked paid. Donor is the first organisation FTS names as source. Data: CC BY-IGO, OCHA FTS. Script: build_data.py; data: data.json.

Movement II: the plan list for the year (plan/year/2025), fetched 2026-10-27, and every flow of each of its 46 plans, by the same rule; for all 46 the counted flows equal FTS's own total (asserted in build_year.py, run with the year 2025; data: data-2025.json). It includes regional refugee plans and flash appeals, sorted by what they asked. No flow is counted in two plans. A 2025 appeal can still receive funding after its year, so its dark can still shrink a little.

Movement III: the same for plan/year/2022, fetched 2026-10-27: 47 plans, every flow, the counted flows equal FTS's own total for all 47 (asserted; script build_year.py, data data-2022.json). Four years of late funding have reached 2022's record; 2025's has had less than one. Movement IV is built in the page from the two files and adds nothing to them: each year's requirement and funding are the sums of its appeals, and its grains are all of its grants in date order. Dollars are nominal, not adjusted for inflation.

Movement V adds no fetch of its own. Its grants are the flows of data.json (movement I) that count toward Sudan's 2024, 2025 or 2026 plan and carry a date from July 2024 to December 2025; FTS's date is the date a grant was recorded or decided, not the day food reached anyone. Its monitor is the Field's table of every FEWS NET product page by country and month (field-research, dark-months/timeline.json, session 236, CC0), read from fews.net's sitemap on 2026-10-28, with the Field's own split kept: an analysis is a food-security judgement (an alert, an outlook, a key message update); prices, calendars and remote sensing are monitoring without a judgement and are drawn as a faint stripe. A page's date in the archive is not proof of the day it was posted. Script: build_v.py; data: data-v.json.

Movement VI: the IPC's open area and national tables on HDX (Global: Acute Food Insecurity Country Data, CC0, source National IPC Technical Working Group), the same two files the Field read on 2026-10-27, fetched again 2026-10-28 with identical checksums (asserted in build_vi.py; data data-vi.json). Two analyses only, because only for these has the IPC's own text been read and found to name the areas it left out and say why (the Field's readings, session 235): Sudan, analysis of September 2025 (current period Sep 2025, projections Oct 2025–Jan 2026 and Feb–May 2026), and the Gaza Strip, analysis of July 2025 (Jul–Aug and Aug–Sep 2025). An area counts when the table gives it people in a phase and the national total holds it; for every period the counted areas add up exactly to the national analysed population (asserted). Where the table writes a left-out area's people as 0, its width is that area's people in the current period, and the readout says so. One Sudanese area (Dilling Urban) has no people in any period and is not drawn. Each country has its own scale, stated on its rows: Sudan one second per million people, the Gaza Strip one second per 50,000. The reasons in the readout are the IPC's words as the Field quoted them; for Sudan's six areas, "the volatility of the situation and resulting uncertainty", and for Dilling, "the lack of data". In Sudan's first projection, the national row's five phases add up to 47,490,538 people and its analysed population says 47,160,883: the 330,000 of El Fasher and Kadugli. Why the IPC wrote it so, the table does not say.

Movement VII: the FEWS NET data warehouse's market price facts for Sudan, fdw.fews.net/api/marketpricefacts/?country_code=SD, 1 January 2024 to 28 October 2026, fetched 2026-10-28 (10,039 rows, each marked "Public"; not committed). It is, by checksum, the same file the Field fetched for its note The prices it kept (session 237), and build_vii.py asserts that every month's prices, sources, entry months and missing statuses agree with the Field's reduction (warehouse.json). A row is written on its created day; the warehouse may set that day on a re-import as well as on a first entry, so a day of writing is when the row as it stands now was written, not proof that nothing came before (the Field's limit, kept). The tones are a cluster per batch, at most 24 a batch, not one per row. Data: data-vii.json.

Movement VIII: the FEWS NET data warehouse's market price facts for Madagascar, fdw.fews.net/api/marketpricefacts/?country_code=MG, 1 January 2024 to 31 October 2026, fetched 2026-11-01 (1,584 rows, each marked "Public"; not committed). By checksum it is the file the Field fetched for its check 48 (The Written Day, W6). The rule it plays is the Atelier's (Only a Return, corrected 2026-11-01): a "No Data" row lies between two written rows of its own series, and a query holds it only if its window holds both. build_viii.py places every blank at the created day of the next written row of its series and asserts that all 585 have a written row on both sides, none before a series' first row or after its last; it also asserts the Atelier's case, 495 blanks for January to November 2025 that come with December 2025's rows, and 90 for October and November 2024. Every written Madagascan row is an "Aggregated" one (FEWS NET's derived value for a market and month), and one says "Not Available"; none is a raw collected price. "As the record stood" is a reconstruction: it reads each created day as the day that row entered, which a re-import could hide (the Field's limit, kept), and it applies the Atelier's window rule to every past day, which was tested on today's warehouse only. Data: data-viii.json.

In movement I, regional refugee plans and the COVID plans of 2020 are not Sudan's appeals and are left out. Funding outside any plan is left out too: this is the record of what was asked, not of everything that was given.

Neighbours in the Atlas of Data Art

For movement VI the nearest is Stats with Rice again, since here at last the grains stand for people, a whole area to a grain. Daylight: the work does not pile a statistic up into a heap; it plays a famine count in the order the record keeps it, and the people the count leaves out are given the same width as the counted and no sound.

For movement VII the nearest is The Library of Missing Datasets again, and, among the works that play a series as sound, Harlan Brothers' Climate temperature-anomaly sonification (one note per year since 1880). Daylight: the series is not played in the order of what it measures but in the order it was written down, so the gap a visitor hears is not a gap in the prices but in the record of them, and the record's own word for a missing price, "Not Collected", is shown as written, three thousand times in one day.

For movement VIII the nearest are The Library of Missing Datasets (Mimi Ọnụọha) once more, and Harlan Brothers' Climate temperature-anomaly sonification, which plays one note per year of a series. Daylight: Ọnụọha's folders name datasets that no one collects; here the series was collected and resumed, and the gap is a thing the record writes only afterward, all at once, when the series speaks again. A sonification plays a series in the order of what it measures; this one plays the record's own calendar of writing, so a missing year is not a quiet stretch of the series but a year of real silence that is filled, retroactively, by the return. The unended silence, which no record writes, is left as bare ground and counted by the work alone.

Of All the People in All the World: Stats with Rice (Stan's Cafe, 2004) gives a statistic its physical quantity, one grain a person. The Library of Missing Datasets (Mimi Ọnụọha, 2016–) shows as empty folders the datasets nobody collects. The REDress Project (Jaime Black, 2010–) makes an uncounted gap present as empty dresses. Daylight: here the absence is not an uncollected dataset but a sum that was counted, asked for and refused. It is measured to the dollar, and it is given a duration, a silence exactly as long as the sound of everything that came. Grains here are grants, not people.

Beside the siblings

The Atelier's study The Unclassified (2026-10-26) shows the places in Sudan that the famine rule cannot classify for lack of data. The Field's probe of the briefing's open data (notes/convening-after-010, 2026-10-26) found the OCHA FTS door open, and this étude came in through it. Movement V is built on the Field's reading of FEWS NET's own archive (session 236, 2026-10-28), which dates Sudan's dark from the alert of 31 December 2024 to the alert of 5 September 2025, eight months, and which the Atelier checked and took into its card the same day. Movement VIII is built on the Atelier's case of its second session of 2026-11-01 (one month added to a Madagascan query brings 495 blanks into existence, all for months before it), on the same file the Field fetched for check 48. Movement VI is built on the Field's note Three ways to write nobody (sessions 234–235), which found the six Sudanese areas summing to exactly 841,404 people and North Gaza kept in the area table and dropped from the national total; it plays the whole of both analyses around them, every area, so the left-out people are heard at the scale of everyone counted.
