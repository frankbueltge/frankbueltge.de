Four species, and how many of their records can be looked at

The Field counted the observation records dated 2010 or later that sit on species whose category is extinct: 14,708, of which four species hold 97 %. Below are those four, with the share of each one's records that carry a photograph at all (retrieved ).

The other two parts of this round's work

This page is the Studio's part of one work made by three practices. The Field carries what was measured: The sightings of the gone, the census of 14,708 records and the four species above. This page takes its four species and its counts, and asks a person to look. The Atelier carries the question and the experiment: a rule setter over the Studio's 39 hand-read records, whose best rule of 98, built from a record's own fields, sorts 32 of 39 right, a score shuffled labels reach in 6.6 % of searches; here the bone in the tortoise set (record fields identical to its living neighbours', checked in verify.py) is one more case of the same. The Studio's earlier work on the thin remainder, Proof of life, read 39 records of extinct birds by hand: of the 8 whose photographs were opened, none showed a living bird.

What this page claims, and what it does not

  • Sample. 22 photographs: 10 Euphrasia minima, 2 Zosterops conspicillatus, 10 Chelonoidis niger, drawn on 2026-10-05 from GBIF human-observation records dated 2010 or later with a still image, by seeded random pages (seed 20261005), keeping only images licensed CC0 or CC BY and at most two per photographer. Perameles fasciata has no photograph on any record, so it has no card. The sample is small and is not representative of the 14,273 records; no share is extrapolated from it.
  • The Studio's reading is a reading of a photograph by one person (the Studio), not an identification of species, and not a verification of where the picture was taken. "Alive" means a living plant or animal of the kind named is in the frame.
  • What it does not say. It does not say these records are wrong, nor that the extinct taxa are not extinct. The page cannot tell which population a living animal belongs to, or whether it is wild or kept. How the label arrived is stated only as far as the archive's species service records it.
  • Photographs belong to their makers, are shown from their source under the licence stated on each card, and are not stored here. If a source is unreachable the card says so and links the record.

Neighbours in the Atlas of Data Art, and daylight

Godmode Epochs, dmstfctn, 2023 (page opened 2026-10-05): a clicker game in which players teach an AI to identify supermarket products. Here the person is also the labeller, but the label is already on the picture, put there by an archive, and the game is to disagree with it when the picture does.

Pollinator Pathmaker, Alexandra Daisy Ginsberg, 2021– (page opened 2026-10-05): a living sculpture of plants planted for endangered pollinators, made "for pollinators, not about them". Here the living things are not planted for anything; they are what an extinct species' name turns out to point at.

From 'Apple' to 'Anomaly', Trevor Paglen, 2019–20 (page opened 2026-10-04): photographs hung along a label hierarchy, to show what a taxonomy sees. Here a single label, one category word, is the whole hierarchy, and the visitor is handed the photographs to test it, one at a time and without the word's answer.
