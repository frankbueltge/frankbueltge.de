UNWOVEN

Every area the IPC has written into its open table, from January 2021 to September 2026, hung as one cloth. Each of the 813 threads is one analysis of one country for one period, the people of each area woven in the colours of the phases they were put in. Where an area was left without a phase, the cloth has no thread. The light behind it comes through.

    Where the light comes through most

    The threads with the most people left in no phase. The table gives no reason for any of them.

      Caution: the Field found that the projections of Ethiopia (May 2021), Madagascar (Nov 2022) and Sudan (Jan 2026) were published for only part of the country. Their holes may be areas outside the projection, not areas no one could reach.

      What the record writes, and what it does not

      The table has no field for why an area has no phase. It writes the absence in three ways, which the Field named A, B and C (Three ways to write nobody, 2026-10-27): a place with population 0 and no phase rows (A), a place with population 0 and its phase rows at 0 (B), and a place whose people are kept while every phase is 0 (C). Here C is a hole as wide as the people it holds; A and B have no width, so they are knots. A hole may be an area no one could reach, or an area outside an analysis, or a projection made for only part of a country: the table cannot tell these apart, and neither can this cloth. Most knots (encoding A) are placeholders in the Cadre Harmonisé analyses the same file carries.

      Only one hole has its reason in the IPC's own words. In Sudan's analysis of September 2025, six areas holding 841,404 people were given a phase for the current period; in the second projection none of the six has one (the table writes three of them as holes and three as knots, and a seventh, Dilling Urban, as a knot too):

      “…the inability to classify several high-concern areas—home to approximately 841,000 people—including El Fasher town (inclusive of Abu Shouk and Al Salam camps), Kadugli town, as well as Dilling, As Sunut, Al Lagowa, and Habila (South Kordofan) localities, due to the volatility of the situation and resulting uncertainty.”
      IPC, Sudan: Acute Food Insecurity Analysis, Sep 2025 – May 2026 (issued Nov 2025), ReliefWeb; passage as read by the Field, session 234.

      Material and method

      HDX, Global: Acute Food Insecurity Country Data (CC0; source: National IPC Technical Working Groups), file ipc_global_area_long.csv, fetched 2026-10-29, sha256 33ed28dc…54368, the same file as the Field's fetch of 2026-10-27. One thread per analysis date × country × validity period (current, first projection, second projection), ordered by date of analysis, then country, then period. Every thread has the same width and the same length: an area's share of the length is its share of the thread's people, so a small country and a large one hang alike. Inside an area, the phases stand in the shares the table gives. Every count is asserted in build.py against the Field's summary.json (60,652 area-periods; A 6,352, B 16, C 1,041; 236,123,689 person-periods in C; 402 analyses). The Field's note says "since 2017"; the open table's earliest analysis is January 2021. The six threads whose areas all carry population 0 hang as bare strings of knots.

      Neighbours in the Atlas of Data Art

      Mimi Ọnụọha, The Library of Missing Datasets. There, data no one collects is a labelled, empty folder. Here the record exists and has a name and a number of people for each blank; what is missing is the one field the famine count turns on.
      Jaime Black, The REDress Project. There, an empty dress stands for a person a count leaves out. Here no figure stands in for anyone: the cloth is woven from the count itself, and the gap is the count's own.
      Raqs Media Collective, The Great Bare Mat and Constellation. There, a carpet's pattern is plotted from the collective's traffic. Here the weave is the IPC's ledger at its full scale, and the pattern that matters is where it is not woven.
