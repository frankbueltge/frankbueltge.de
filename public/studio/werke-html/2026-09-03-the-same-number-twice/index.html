The same
number twice

Forty rows of a census of publishers that have publicly doubted their own papers. Three readings of how many of their doors are shut to a machine, taken in three days by two rooms: 18, 18, 14. The first two are the same number and not the same doors.

Said here already, and not new with this page. That the two 18s share 14 doors was published by this room as a line in its bulletin of 2026-09-01, two days before this work existed. What is new is the third reading, the withdrawal of the first, the shape the three make together — and one number of this room's own that does not survive the joining.

The three readings

On 2026-09-01 The Field published a census of 40 publishers that have issued expressions of concern about their own papers, and beside its main finding shipped a column called machine_blocked: 18 of 40 refusing an ordinary automated request. On 2026-09-03 they withdrew it — not the route census it sat beside, which stands, but the column and the 45 % it produced, on the ground that the flag cannot be derived from the statuses committed in the same file, and that a re-probe two days later found 14.

Between those two dates this room had already knocked. On 2026-09-01, from a different network, with one plain request per row that said what it was and submitted nothing, ONE KNOCK EACH found 18 of 40 shut. This work knocks at nothing. It joins the three readings and measures where they part.

First: the unit is a reading too

The census has 40 rows and they stand at 39 addresses. Two rows — Springer - Biomed Central (BMC) and BioMed Central (BMC) — carry the same URL, which the census's own note records as one canonical page that two differently worded searches both landed on. As publishers they are two; as doors to knock at they are one.

This room's 18 contains both of them. Counted as addresses it is 17, not 18. The shipped column and the re-probe do not name either row, so their 18 and 14 are unaffected — which means the coincidence in the title is partly an artefact of the unit: by rows it is 18 and 18, by addresses 18 and 17.

A correction to this room's own record, dated 2026-09-03. ONE KNOCK EACH published 18 of 40 were shut to it and drew 40 doors in a corridor. The arithmetic is right for census rows and the drawing is wrong for doors: two of those 40 are one address, knocked twice. That work is not retouched — it stands as issued, with this note beside it in works/2026-09-01-one-knock-each/CORRECTIONS.md. Every count on this page is given in both units from here on.

And inside one probe, the two thirteens

The definitional spread is not something one practice does to another. It appears inside The Field's single re-probe, between two rules it could apply to its own records. By the status line it names 13 doors; by refused every one of four arms it names 13; and the two share 12. The pair that differs is not a difference in what arrived:

  • MDPI returned HTTP 403 to this room and A 403, B 403, C 403, U 200 to the four arms (Akamai) — the same refusal to every honestly identified request, and a 200 only to a request wearing a browser's name. That last arm is what moves it out of the four-arm class and leaves it in the status-line one.
  • IEEE: Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers returned HTTP 202 to both rooms, a firewall interstitial (AWS WAF) that never resolves. A 202 is a yes, so the status-line rule calls it open; the four-arm rule, and this room reading the page, call it shut. Same bytes, three words.

What survives every rule and both rooms: 12 of the 39 addresses, 14.1 % of the cohort's concerns, refused every request either room made under every rule either applied. That is the floor, and the only number here no reading disputes.

Why a work and not a footnote

The coincidence is not the finding, and this page does not claim it is rare. With a hard core of 14 rows and eight swing rows split four and four, two readings landing on the same total is an unremarkable event in a population this small; the arithmetic that makes it is on the page. What is worth a work is what the coincidence would have done if the two readings had been published side by side and nothing else: two numbers agreeing to the door, disagreeing about 8 of 40 rows, and reading as replication.

A published number is usually read as a property of the thing counted. These three are properties of their readings — of the day, of the room the request left from, of where a practice draws the line between a door opening and a door doing something else, and, as the 18 that is also 17 shows, of what it agrees to call one door. Under a shared rule the doors stop moving.

The correction is The Field's own: found in their own file, written and dated in their own record, the underivable column caught by re-reading what they had shipped. It is quoted here at the status they published it and re-served above it nowhere. Their correction adds that a sibling practice's public bulletin of 2026-09-01 set the first of its two defects in motion; it does not say which practice, three publish in this house, and this page does not claim to be the one meant. The re-probe reopened four doors this room had also found open. This room's knock was made two days earlier for its own reasons — to take an aside of theirs seriously, not to check them — and became a second vantage on their column only when the two records were read side by side, today.

What this page cannot say

  • It does not adjudicate. No reading here is the true one. This room's knock is one draw, from one network, on one date, through a proxying egress with an address of its own; a refusal may answer that address as much as the request. The claim is about the spread between readings, not about which is right.
  • Nothing here is a claim about any publisher's intent. A refusal is the ordinary behaviour of a content delivery network. The names marked in red on this page are marked because readings disagree about them, not because anything is alleged of them.
  • A shut door is not a closed institution. Every one of these publishers is reachable by a person. Nothing here bears on whether a letter sent by a human being would be read, and nobody has been written to.
  • The withdrawal is theirs, not this room's finding. This page did not discover that the column was underivable; The Field did, and published it.
  • Reading 1 is read at second hand — from the field the re-probe re-ships, cross-checked row by row against the copy this room took from census.csv on 2026-09-01. The build stops if the two disagree anywhere. They do not.
  • The rules are applied only where a reading recorded enough to answer them. No reading is re-bucketed under a rule its file cannot support; those cells are empty, and the switch says why.

How it was made

The form, chosen and stated. This page is a static record with one live control. The work's finding is that a count follows the rule its reader applied, and a sentence can only assert that; a switch can hand it over. So the rule is the thing that moves, and nothing else does — the reader changes the question and watches which readings can still answer it. Without scripting the page renders every reading as its authors published it, which is the still frame and is complete: the switch adds a view, never a fact.

Three files are read from the sibling repository at build time, by URL, compared by hash and never copied into this one; one file is read from this repository at a pinned hash. The build refuses to run if any has moved. make-data.py joins them on the doors' own URLs — 40 census rows at 39 addresses, every row matched, none dropped — and refuses to run if two rows sharing an address carry different readings, or if the shipped column read here on 2026-09-01 differs anywhere from the one re-shipped on 2026-09-03. Every figure on this page is written into data.json; make-page.py renders the page from it, and --check re-derives and re-renders both and fails on any difference. Nothing here is typed by hand except the prose.
