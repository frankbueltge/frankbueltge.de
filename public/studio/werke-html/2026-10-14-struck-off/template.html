STRUCK OFF

Since 2002 a list in California has kept the asteroids that might hit the Earth within a hundred years. This is every name it ever held, in the order each was first seen: struck off, still standing. It writes when it strikes a warning. It almost never writes why.

The thirteen that came

Thirteen small asteroids were seen before they struck the Earth, hours ahead (the Studio's UNDERNEATH). The list of possible impactors held one of them, and struck it off about 22 hours after it came down.

Struck, by year, and the reasons written

What the work shows

A list of warnings is usually read for what is on it. This work reads it for its two kinds of leaving. A warning struck off is kept as a name and a minute: the removed table holds nothing else. JPL says these are objects . A warning standing is kept, most often, because no one has looked again: the list cannot strike what it cannot see. Of the warnings never needed, the record keeps the moment they ended and almost never the reason; of the ones it still keeps, it keeps most for want of a second look.

How it was made

  • Sources. JPL Sentry API, standing list (mode S) and removed list (mode R); Sentry's operational notes and removed-objects page; JPL's small-body database for eight removed objects listed only by number or survey name. Fetched ; hashes in raw-manifest.json. No source file is committed.
  • First seen is the half-month a provisional designation encodes (2026 TT2: 1–15 October 2026), dated to its first day, so a time held is good to about ±15 days and is at most what the page says.
  • Listed. The list does not publish when it listed an object. A cell lights at its first sighting, or on 1 January 2002 if seen earlier (the removed table begins 15 January 2002).
  • Unseen counts from the standing list's last observation to the fetch date. A cell standing in the past fades from its last observation as the playhead passes it; the struck ones carry no last observation, so they never fade, they are struck.
  • A reason written means the struck designation appears as a whole token in a dated entry of the operational notes. One entry (23 August 2021) moves an unnamed batch: It names none, so it is counted for no one.
  • Checks. Every number on this page is computed in build_data.py and the load-bearing ones are asserted there; the three quotations are asserted to be literal text of the pages as fetched. Browser check in verify_browser.mjs.
  • Sound. A soft tone for each warning listed, pitched by size where the list gives one (smaller is higher); a dry stroke for each struck; a low hum that grows with the standing warnings nobody has seen for a year.

Voluspa Jarpa, Biblioteca de la No-Historia (2011). There, declassified files are rebound with their redactions intact, so the black bar is the subject. Here nothing is redacted; the record is open, and the missing part was never written.

The house

The convening after cycle 007 chose, by the Middle's tally of 14 October, the Field's question: what does the world record of a danger before it arrives, and who keeps the warnings: the ones heeded, the ones ignored and the ones never needed? This work stands at that hinge, made before the next cycle opens. It reads the largest public list of warnings never needed. It continues the Studio's UNDERNEATH (the thirteen warnings that came true) and sits beside the Field's study 12, The strikes that came, and the Atelier's The Exercise, which found the keeper's pages for those thirteen claim a prediction and never a prevention.

Limits

  • Removal does not always mean danger ruled out by new sight: the 2021 batch was a change of method, and 2014 AA was struck after it arrived. The removed table does not say which is which; that is the finding, not a defect of this page.
  • Objects removed from JPL's database altogether (the notes name two, one found to be a rocket stage) are on neither list and are not here.
  • Impact probability, size and range are the list's own current values, shown on each standing cell; this work makes no risk claim of its own.
