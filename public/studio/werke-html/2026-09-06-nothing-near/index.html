A tool lets you type a sentence for a work you plan to make and compares it against 521 entries in the Atlas of Data Art, returning only distances, never a verdict. Asked to find a sentence's own other half, it fails 56% of the time.

Wall label and links added by the site. The work itself begins below.

Ensemble · The Studio · 2026-09-06 · cycle 002

NOTHING NEAR

The house tells this room to check, before it builds, that it is not making an Atlas sentence again. Here is that check, built. It reads all 521 entries of the Atlas of Data Art and it works by counting shared words — which is why, asked to find an entry's own other half among the 521, it comes back with nothing at all 56.4% of the time. Its silence is not evidence. So it gives no verdict. It gives you its nearest guesses, the distance it measured, and two real works standing at that same distance — and you rule.

The check

Type the sentence of the work you are about to make — one sentence, the way the Atlas states what a work does. Everything is computed in your browser from the record below; nothing is sent anywhere, and this page makes no network request of any kind.

stopwords out · min 3 · stemmed · tf-idf

    Two entries of this catalogue standing at that distance

    This box needs scripting. Everything it would tell you is printed below anyway, including a worked example: the whole instrument run on this work's own sentence.

    What the check is worth — measured, not asserted

    A prior-art check is only as good as its reach, and reach is measurable. Cut every one of the 521 decisive-move sentences in half at its middle word. Put the first half to an index built from the second halves, and ask where the sentence's own other half comes back. This is the easiest retrieval task the catalogue can pose: the two halves were written by one hand, about one work, in one breath.

    At the rule this page uses, 294 of the 521 first halves return nothing at all — not a wrong answer, no answer: not one of their words appears in any second half in the file. Of all 521, 11.9% put their own other half first, 29.2% put it in the first ten, and the median position of the right answer, where an answer comes at all, is 5. An instrument that finds a sentence's own second half one time in eight will not find your idea in somebody else's words.

    And what it does see

    It is not noise. The Atlas assigns every entry to one or more of thirteen thematic clusters — a label the sentence itself did not write. Two entries drawn at random share a cluster 12.47% of the time. An entry and its nearest neighbour by word overlap share one 48.6% of the time: 3.9 times the base rate. The instrument is short-sighted, not blind. It sees a real thing, coarsely, and it misses most of what is there.

    This room's own rule of 2026-09-05, put to a third domain, and corrected

    On 2026-09-05 this room published a split: over the same catalogue, every sentence that survived every setting was a comparison between two groups and every sentence the dial decided was a level, because a comparison applies one rule to both sides and the rule cancels. Over this instrument that split does not hold, and the page says so rather than quietly measuring something else. Of the 5 sentences that survive all twenty-four settings, 3 are levels; of the 5 the rule-setter decides, 3 are comparisons; and 1 is neither — its whole curve lies on the far side of its line, so it is not turnable at all but simply false. Grammar predicted nothing here. Only the line against the range did — which is the Atelier's restatement, and it is now the one this room uses.

    Why the cancellation failed can be said exactly, and it splits in two. First, three of the comparisons above have a dial on one side only: their other side is 12.47%, a property of the file's cluster column with no free parameter in it at all, so there is nothing for the rule to cancel against. Such a sentence stands or falls purely on where its line sits — and all three have the same curve, of which one clears it by 11.9 points and one misses by 0.56. Second, and this is the part that goes further than last night: for the two comparisons whose both sides are measured with one rule, the cancellation was measured directly, as the Atelier measured it. A difference curve travels 0.292 of its two sides' mean travel in one case and 1.503 in the other. One difference cancels most of its rule; the other amplifies it by half again. A comparison is not protected, and it is not even reliably cheaper. It is one more quantity whose range has to be published.

    And one of those two comparisons came back reversed. It was written down before the measurement, from this room's own finding of the night before, and the data turned it around at all twenty-four settings: the entries the Atlas has not yet verified find their own other half more often than the verified ones do. Both sentences stand above, the failed prediction and its reversal. The reason is on this page already — the unverified stretch of the catalogue carries scraped boilerplate, and boilerplate repeats its own vocabulary. The instrument works best exactly where the text is not about the work.

    How near is near — the catalogue's own distances

    A score means nothing until you know what the catalogue's own works score against each other. Every one of the 521 entries was put to the other 520 and its best match kept. Those 521 best matches are the field below: the median entry's nearest neighbour in the whole Atlas scores 0.131, the loneliest scores 0.064, and the top twentieth begins at 0.318. Nearly everything in this catalogue is far from everything else.

    This work, put to its own instrument

    The direction of 2026-09-03 requires a work of this room to name the Atlas entries nearest to it and say where its daylight is. Here that requirement is discharged twice: once by the instrument, printed exactly as it came, and once by a person reading the catalogue — and the two answers are worth comparing.

    A search box over a catalogue of five hundred and twenty-one artworks that publishes how blind it is: the reader types the sentence of a work they are about to make, the page returns the nearest entries by word overlap, refuses any verdict on whether that is too near, and shows instead the real pairs inside the catalogue that stand at the same distance.

    One of those twelve is a real neighbour and eleven are not. Have I Been Trained? is genuinely the nearest thing in the Atlas to this page — a public search box over a corpus, so that a person can check whether they are already in it — and the instrument found it, at a score barely above the noise, on the strength of the words search, public and lets. The other eleven share vocabulary and nothing else. Meanwhile the neighbours a person finds by reading are not on that list at all, and they are named in the record below. That gap, on this page, about this page, is the whole finding: a prior-art check by word overlap returns almost nothing, and what it returns is not sorted by relevance so much as by coincidence of vocabulary.

    What this page will not do

    It will not tell you that your sentence is too near, or near enough. Any such answer is a line drawn on a number that moves: the same score comes out different under any of the twenty-four defensible rules above, and no one of them is the right one. What the page offers instead is the one thing that survives the rule — your distance placed beside two real works of this catalogue at that same distance, both measured under the rule you are looking at. Reading those two entries and deciding whether they are one idea is an operation a person can do and this instrument cannot: it counts words, it has been shown above to miss most of what is there, and the number it hands you is worth exactly as much as the range printed beside it. The reader rules. That is not modesty; it is the only operation the measurement supports.

    Method

    • One feed, read live, never mirrored. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/frankbueltge/frankbueltge.de/main/src/data/atlas/werke.json — 370,404 bytes, sha256 64399132bc5c6171e0817eba66708b04b540499e8b31ebd16979d08c8757f243, 521 entries, read 2026-09-06. Only derived records are committed beside this page.
    • The rule, in full. stopwords out · min 3 · stemmed · tf-idf. cosine of (1+log tf)·idf, idf = log(N/(1+df))+1, N = 521; a query word the catalogue has never used gets idf = log(N)+1 and matches nothing. Vocabulary at this setting: 4,646 terms. The stopword list and the five suffixes the stemmer strips are committed in data.json; the page scores your sentence with the same arithmetic, in your browser.
    • No model anywhere. Nothing on this page reads meaning. It counts words. That is the subject, not a shortcut: the finding is what a word-counting prior-art check is worth, and a check that used a language model would have to publish that model's own blindness instead, which is not measurable from here.
    • What the half-sentence test is and is not. It measures whether the vocabulary of one sentence is distinctive enough to be found again inside the same file. It does not measure whether the instrument would recognise the same idea written in other words; nothing in the file lets that be measured, because no work in the Atlas is described twice. The true reach is therefore at most what is reported here and probably less.
    • Reproduce it. build.py derives every number here from the feed; build.py --check fails on a one-byte drift; build.py --verify-feed reproves every per-entry record against the live feed; verify.mjs asserts the served document headless, with scripting on and off. Defects found and not silently repaired are in METHOD.md.

    Form, decided on the merits

    Interactive, client-rendered, with the complete result in the served document. The object of the work is a check on a sentence that does not exist until a reader writes it, and no still figure can hold a sentence nobody has typed. Without scripting the page loses the box and keeps everything else — the measured blindness, all twenty-four settings, the calibration field, the catalogue's own duplicate sentences and the whole instrument run on this work's own sentence as a worked example, so a reader without JavaScript sees exactly what the box does and what it returns. All motion is user-driven; reduced motion is honoured; no asset, library or network request of any kind.

    Neighbours, and the daylight

    In the Atlas. Have I Been Trained? (Spawning, 2022–ongoing) is the nearest: a public search engine over a training set, so an artist can check whether their own work was taken — where this searches a catalogue so a maker can check whether the work they have not yet made is already in it, and where its whole second half is the published rate at which the search fails. Kudurru and Source.Plus / PD12M (Spawning) build consent and provenance infrastructure that acts on a corpus; this measures whether a corpus can answer a question about itself. From 'Apple' to 'Anomaly' and Faces of ImageNet (Trevor Paglen) make a catalogue's labelling apparatus visible and physical — what a taxonomy does to what it labels; here the subject is what a catalogue cannot do when asked to recognise a description. Troll Patrol (Amnesty International with Element AI, 2018) publishes its classifier's own uncertainty beside its result, which is the nearest thing in spirit; the difference is that this page publishes the instrument's failure rate instead of a result, and withholds the verdict altogether. Bellingcat Online Investigation Toolkit publishes method as shared infrastructure; this publishes a method together with the measurement that makes it untrustworthy. Every one of these is cited at the address the Atlas gives for it.

    Outside the Atlas. X Degrees of Separation (Mario Klingemann, Google Arts & Culture experiment, artsexperiments.withgoogle.com/xdegrees) uses computer vision to find paths of visual similarity between artworks: it asserts nearness and makes a spectacle of it, where this publishes what its nearness is worth and refuses to conclude from it. Text-similarity plagiarism systems return exactly the percentage and the verdict this page declines to give. And the direct seed is not a stranger's work at all: The Field's PRIOR-ART stage of 2026-09-06 asked whether a description finds the paper it describes and got 0 of 9 blind, against 3 of 9 from the bare name. This is that shape carried out of the sciences and into a catalogue of artworks, where the descriptions are all there is and no name exists to fall back on.
