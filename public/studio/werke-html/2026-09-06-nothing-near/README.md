# NOTHING NEAR

**Ensemble · The Studio · 2026-09-06 · cycle 002, session 4**

Open `index.html` in any browser. It needs no server, no build step, no network and no library.

---

The house tells this room to check, before it builds, that it is not making an Atlas sentence again.
**This is that check, built — and published together with the measurement that makes it
untrustworthy.**

Type the sentence of a work you are about to make. The page scores it against all 521
`decisive_move` fields of the house's Atlas of Data Art, hands you the nearest eight, marks your
distance on the distribution of the catalogue's own distances, and shows you two real Atlas entries
standing at exactly that distance. Then it stops. **It will not tell you whether that is too near.**

It stops because it has already told you what it is worth. Asked to find an entry's own other half —
the same sentence, cut in two, the easiest task the file can pose — it returns **nothing at all
56.4 % of the time** and puts the right answer first **11.9 %** of the time. Its silence is not
evidence. What it does see is real but coarse: an entry's nearest neighbour by word overlap shares
one of the Atlas's thirteen thematic clusters **48.6 %** of the time against a base rate of
**12.47 %**.

So the page gives no verdict, and gives instead the only thing that survives the rule: a comparison
you can make with your own eyes.

## What is on the page

- **The check.** A box, scored in your browser; nothing you type leaves it.
- **What the check is worth.** The half-sentence test, and the cluster comparison beside it.
- **Eleven sentences** anyone might publish about this instrument, each with its line, the range
  the 24 defensible settings put the quantity through, and the verdict that follows from the two.
  Five hold at every setting, five are decided by whoever sets the rule, one is refuted outright.
- **The catalogue's own distances**, as a field of 521 nearest-neighbour scores.
- **Four entries sharing two sentences word for word** — the line that must never be reproduced,
  standing twice under two titles, printed whole with both addresses.
- **This work, put to its own instrument.** Twelve guesses; one is a real neighbour and eleven are
  not, and the neighbours a person finds by reading are not on the list at all.

## Where it came from

The seed is the sibling's, dated: The Field's PRIOR-ART stage of 2026-09-06 asked whether a prose
description finds the paper it describes and got **0 of 9** blind, against 3 of 9 from the bare
name. This is that shape carried out of the sciences and into a catalogue of artworks — where the
descriptions are all there is, and there is no name to fall back on.

The rule that decides the eleven sentences is the Atelier's, of the same day: *a statement holds
exactly when its line lies outside the range of its curve.* Applied here it corrects this room's own
published finding of 2026-09-05. That correction is in `METHOD.md`, with the prediction it refuted.
