All at once

An expression of concern is a journal saying in public that one of its own papers may be unreliable, and that it is not withdrawing it yet. It is a flag with a date on it and an implied promise of a decision to come. The sibling practice next door measured how long that promise takes to keep: of 1,277 papers with five full years behind them, 601 — 47.1 % — were retracted inside the window.

Almost half. It is the kind of number that describes a paper's prospects — this one has a bit under an even chance. Regroup the same rows by the notice that raised each concern, and the paper stops having prospects of its own.

Read the staircase for stripes — for a bar that is part filled and part hollow. In 13 bars there are 2. Of the 46 notices that flag more than one paper — 311 papers between them — 43 went entirely one way: 19 in which every paper was retracted, 24 in which not one was. That is 292 of the 311 papers. Three notices split.

Is that just what 47 % looks like?

No, and not nearly. If each paper's outcome were drawn on its own at the cohort's rate, notices of these sizes would come out all-or-nothing about 13.71 times. They do it 43 times. In 50,000 draws of that null, the observed count was reached 0 times.

Small notices could manage it by luck — two papers agree half the time by accident. So take only the 13 notices flagging 5 papers or more, 231 papers in all. Independence expects 0.265 of them to go entirely one way, and reached 11 in 0 of 50,000 draws. 11 of 13 do.

There is an obvious other explanation, and it is not the notice at all: some publishers retract readily and some do not, so papers that share a publisher would agree with each other even if every decision were made separately. Give that reading every advantage — estimate a separate rate for each publisher from its own rows in this cohort, batched papers included, and draw again. It accounts for some of the effect and nowhere near all of it: the expected count rises from 13.71 to 19.76, and 43 is still reached in 0 of 50,000 draws.

The single-paper notices behave exactly like the cohort as a whole: 432 of 916, 47.2 %. The headline rate is theirs. The batched papers do not sit near it; they sit at both ends of it.

The other end of the clock does it too

A resolution is an act of the same kind. Expression of Concern in Mathematics and Mechanics of Solids flagged 13 papers on 2016-03-01; all 13 were retracted 1 day later, on one day. Expression of Concern in Circulation Research flagged 12 on 2018-11-20; 10 of them were retracted 22 days later, all on the same day, and 2 were not — which is what put it in Plate II. So the interval the measurement upstream reports is the gap between two administrative acts. The paper is a passenger on both.

And the smallest bar in Plate II is the largest notice in the record

The third of the three splits — 2 papers, 1 retracted, 1 standing — is Editorial Expression of Concern: Topical Collection “Environment and Low Carbon Transportation” in Arabian Journal of Geosciences, 10.1007/s12517-021-08471-8. Its own deposit with Crossref does not name 2 papers. It names 410.

All 410 are in the row file; 408 carry this notice as the concern that flagged them, and 359 of those have a retraction recorded. They are outside the five-year cohort because the record dates almost all of them to 2021-09-28 — the largest single day of doubt in the whole file, 434 papers, of which 406 are this one document — and that is 40 days past 2021-08-19 — the last day on which a concern could be raised and still have five full years behind it.

So the one notice in Plate II that looks like a journal weighing two papers separately is a two-paper window onto a 410-paper document. The exception is the rule, seen through a date.

What this is and is not

  • An expression of concern is not a finding of misconduct, and nothing here says anything about any author, editor or publisher beyond what the public record contains. Where a notice is named, it is named because it is a public document with a title and a date, and it is linked so it can be read.
  • “Still standing” means no retraction is on record by 2026-08-19. It is not a decision anyone made. A concern can be resolved by a correction, by a reinstatement, or by being quietly dropped without a notice, and none of those can be told apart from silence in this data. The upstream measurement says so and it travels here unchanged.
  • The record is not the conduct. A journal that issues concerns readily and resolves them slowly looks worse in this file than one that never issues a concern at all.
  • The concern side of the record is the less completely collected side — the source database says so of itself — so counts of flagged papers are floors.
  • The measurement upstream was exploratory, not pre-registered, and its five-year window was chosen because it leaves a cohort of over a thousand papers, not because anyone has set five years as a standard. That status travels with every figure on this page.
  • What is claimed here is about the record's grain, not its cause. That papers flagged together are retracted together is what a notice-shaped decision looks like from outside; this page does not say why any particular decision went the way it did.
  • The publisher test above does not exhaust the alternative. Rates were pooled by publisher, which is the coarsest stratum the file supports; a journal-level or era-level propensity is not separately controlled for, and every notice sits inside exactly one journal and one date, so no test on this file can fully separate the notice decided once from this journal in this year decided this way. What the test does show is that a publisher's own base rate leaves most of the effect standing.
  • 50 of the 1,277 papers carry no usable identifier for the notice that flagged them. They are set aside rather than merged into a pseudo-notice, and they are not in Plate I.
