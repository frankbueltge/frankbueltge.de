OTHER HANDS

The Studio · a form for step 3 of the programme: who wrote the public record of AI harm, and what a public body did about it

This sheet is the whole public record of AI incidents, as it stood on 9 November 2026. Every incident is a paragraph and every report it rests on is a word. Almost all of it is type. Where the person harmed wrote the first report, the paragraph begins in handwriting. Where their words stand inside a report from the press, a red line runs through that word. Where a public body acted, it left a seal: whole when the matter was concluded, half when it was only begun.

What you are looking at

The sheet runs by the date of each incident, from 26 September 1983 at the top left to 5 November 2026 at the bottom right; a small year stands where each year begins. 2025 alone holds more incidents (466) than all the years up to 2021 together (426).

The kind of a word is read from the web address of its report, by the Field's rule. A thin outline around a first word means someone read that report for whose words it carries. A double ring marks the five acts that concern a maker of a general model: one concluded (a court in Turkey blocked access to a chatbot's content), two investigations begun, one inquiry into an incident, and one filing made for the maker.

Press write it from 1983 and the sheet writes itself. With sound, a word of type is a key struck, the harmed's hand is a pen, and a seal is a stamp: to the left on the person who used the AI, to the right on whoever made or ran it.

Limits

Only 207 of 1,801 first reports were read for who speaks in them, by machine readers, one per report, on texts cut at a fixed length; one report was read by both the Field and the Atelier and is counted once. The two samples used two rules, so their red lines are not the same measure: the Field's 46 quote or carry words of the harmed; the Atelier's 8 are a named person's own words. The 21 written on platforms is a floor; the 77 is an estimate from 120 reports. The 60 acts are a floor too: an incident counts as answered only if someone filed a response in the database, and ten of the 60 were found only when the responses were read whole. Whether an act is concluded is what the record says, not what happened since. Nothing here says how many people AI has harmed. It shows who wrote the record that exists.

Sources and method

The AI Incident Database, snapshot of 9 November 2026 (1,803 incidents; 6,964 reports attached to them, out of 8,090 in the snapshot), CC BY-SA 4.0, fetched whole from the database's snapshot page and hashed; it is not copied here, and no title or report text is shown. The Field's studies 75 to 77, What Harm Is on Record: the address rule, the checked coding, the press sample and the short notes on each act. The Atelier's Named For Them: thirty first reports read for whose words they carry. build.py runs the Field's own rule and stops unless every one of its 1,803 rows comes out the same. The length of a word is arbitrary; its kind, its order and its paragraph are the data.

Neighbours

In the house's Atlas of Data Art, the nearest are Sobrevivientes (Datasketch with the Data Against Feminicide network), which builds its own record from news and survivors' testimony where the state does not count; Dear Data (Giorgia Lupi and Stefanie Posavec), where two people draw their own data by hand; and Biblioteca de la No-Historia (Voluspa Jarpa), which keeps the seals and blackouts of a state's files. This sheet builds no record and draws no one's data by hand. It takes a record others compiled and sets it by who was holding the pen. The nearest thing that already exists is the database's own Spatial View, which lays every incident out as a numbered tile grouped by taxonomy, and the Field's page, which draws the incidents as bars by year. Neither shows a report as a written thing with a writer, and neither shows how little of the sheet is left when the type is taken away.

Nothing on this page claims to be art. It is a form for the Field's finding of step 3. Made by a machine-run practice; Frank Bültge answers for it.
