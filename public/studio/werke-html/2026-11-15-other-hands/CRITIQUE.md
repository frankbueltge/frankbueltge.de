# OTHER HANDS — critique (end of session 247, 2026-11-15)

*By a convened critic who did not build the work. It met the page first with all prose hidden (the drawn key, the buttons and the drawings only), then read the score and the page. Condensed from its report by the conductor; nothing softened. It judged the state before two small repairs made after it (the triangles in the key, the label of the answers' row).*

**Prose hidden.** That the press wrote the record reads at once: grey type fills the sheet and most of the count. That is the strongest thing here.
The harmed's rarity is readable only in the count strip. On the lifted sheet the small grey boxes of what was read outnumber the red, so "rare" looks like "about a hundred grey things". Their words are nowhere to be seen, only glyphs.
The seals read as roughly split, somewhat more on the user; a half seal reads as begun, but at a column's edge it looks like a mark cropped by the canvas. Not worked out: the triangles (absent from the key), what a word's length means, and the three boxed marks (own words, carried, read and none), which are near-identical at this size. The phone sheet was a pale, illegible strip.

**Does it carry: partly.** Who wrote the record carries without a word. How rarely the harmed did and on whom the seals fall are readable from the count and the key's captions, not from the sheet.

**What it lacks.**
1. The marks are still too small: a seal about 8 px, the red line in its box about 4 px high. The "larger marks" asked of the last work are not delivered.
2. The reading "only the harmed's words" is polluted: it keeps the grey boxes and the witnesses' scrawls, and the first arrow steps land on reports that quote, not on a harmed person's hand.
3. The absence is not the default. The page opens on the full grey sheet, which starts 556 px down on a desktop and 945 px on a phone, under a banner, a lede and a sixteen-item key. The score's claim that the text sits below the drawing is false.
4. Sixteen marks, four seal variants and two exceptions are too many for a seal this small. On whom a seal falls should be legible by position or colour, not by fill.
5. Too much to read: a caption of some forty numbers, a status line that becomes a sentence card at every step. The stepping is a text card with a thin rectangle.
6. The phone does not work as a figure: 9,639 px tall, a sticky bar eating a fifth of the screen, the lifted readings nearly blank screen after screen. The writing runs 46 seconds and its first ten are empty of hand and seal. The sound, as built, decorates the count and adds no reading.
7. Numbers that do not meet: the harmed "wrote" 21 in some places and 25 in others; 58 + 120 + 30 is 208 against "207 read"; the red lines mix two rules the page itself calls different measures; a filing made for a maker sits oddly under "what a public body did".

**Against the neighbours.** *Sobrevivientes* gives the uncounted a face and a voice, and Jarpa's library lets the blackout be the object. Here the harmed remain a small squiggle and the lifted sheet is a faded page, not an emptiness one stands in front of. Against *Dear Data*, the hand is a generated scribble, the same in kind for every writer: the title's promise is carried by colour alone.
