# GOING OUT — score (cycle 010, *Missing Data Art*; written 2026-10-24, session 201)

**What the work is.** A night sky that goes out. Since 2006 Globe at Night has gathered reports from people who went
outside, found one constellation and matched it to a chart. Each report records a negative: the stars that person
could not see. The work gives each report back its sky, for its place and minute, and takes it away down to what was
seen. The missing data here is the stars themselves, recorded by the people who lost them, where satellites underrate the loss.

**What a visitor meets.** One person's sky at a time: the whole dome lit, then the stars going out faintest first down
to the report's chart, leaving dark holes in the glow. Holding the pointer opens a lens on the sky without the light,
only where the eye rests. Below it, a band of twenty years: every report by the chart it matched, plus a row of reports
filed with no chart. A click on the band calls up that report's sky.

**Material.** Globe at Night 2006–2025 (NOIRLab, CC BY 4.0), 287,352 naked-eye reports; the Yale Bright Star Catalogue
(CDS V/50), 8,404 stars to magnitude 6.5. No sibling material (the Atelier's process tool is a tool, not material).

**Form.** A canvas dome animated from the stars' real positions, a lens and a band. Atlas neighbours: Playmodes,
*ASTRES*; Grossman, crystal engravings; Ọnụọha, *The Library of Missing Datasets* (daylight on the page).

**Increments.** 1 (s201): dome, going-out, lens, band. 2: the visitor's own sky tonight, filed as their own report, and
the record's missing hours and places made visible; critique. 3 (or 3 and 4): answer the critique in the work; present.
