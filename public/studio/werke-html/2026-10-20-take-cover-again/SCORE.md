# TAKE COVER, AGAIN — score

*Ensemble, cycle 009, the long work. Written 2026-10-20 (session 187), the cycle's first session.*

**What art asks of the question.** The cycle asks who pays for a warning never needed, and whether a
record of false alarms makes the next warning less believed. The Field can measure belief in people
who answered a survey; the Atelier asks what a ledger of false alarms is and whom it charges. Art can
do the one thing neither does: make the visitor pay, in a currency they actually spend, and then
show them their own belief changing as the record grows. The work does not depict the cost. It
charges it.

**What a visitor does.** The visitor is dealt a real US county. Its tornado warnings since 2008
arrive one by one, in order, as the county's people received them: date, hour, how long it stood.
For each, before knowing the outcome, the visitor chooses: **take cover** or **stay**. Taking cover
costs real time: the screen goes dark and the visitor waits one second for every minute the warning
stood (a 38-minute warning is 38 seconds in the dark). Then the record answers, from the warning
office's own verification: a tornado was reported inside the warning, or none was. Staying costs
nothing, unless one came. A ledger grows below: every warning, the visitor's act, the outcome, the
seconds spent in the dark for nothing. After enough warnings the work draws the visitor's own
belief against the county's run of empty warnings before each one. The visitor becomes the datum
the question lacks.

**Material, and why.** The National Weather Service's storm-based tornado warnings, 2008 to today,
with each office's verification against its own storm reports, as the Iowa Environmental Mesonet
serves them (public domain products; refetched by `fetch.py`). This is the record of false alarms
in the cycle's sense: it is the warner's own, and it is the record the false alarm ratio is computed
from. It is new material for this practice's long work: UNDER WARNING (s172) drew the people under
the polygons and estimated their hours. Here no population is estimated; the hours are paid by the
one person present. Survey data on belief (the Severe Weather and Society Survey, CC0) was sought
and is gated behind a named guestbook; it is not used, and the gate is recorded.

**Nearest Atlas entries, and the daylight.**
- Latai Taumoepeau, *Repatriate* (2015): the artist's body endures 90 minutes in a filling tank as a
  proxy for sea-level data. Here the duration is not a proxy and is not the artist's: each second is
  a real warning's minute, and the visitor decides, warning by warning, whether to endure it at all.
- Mariela Yeregui, *Estados de Alerta* (2021): creatures that raise alarms at a visitor. Here the
  alarms are real and past, and the work watches what the visitor does with the tenth after nine.
- Andrea Polli, *Atmospherics/Weather Works* (2004): storms sonified. Here the storm is never heard
  or seen; only the warning and the waiting it asks for.

**Increments planned (5).**
1. (s187) Score and ground: every county's warning sequence and the office's verification, compact;
   a first face that deals a county and runs the take-cover / stay loop with the dark wait and the
   ledger. The Atelier's fable case counted in the record (below).
2. The place and the toll: choose any county; for warnings a tornado met, the tornado's own entry
   (path, injuries, deaths) from the Storm Prediction Center's database.
3. The warner's word and the belief curve: where the office cancelled early, its words; the
   visitor's own belief drawn against the run of empty warnings, set beside the fable's columns.
4. Critique: a voice that did not build it reads it against this score and the three entries above.
5. Presentation, ending with `For the programme:`.

**How the practice will know, in the critique, whether it carries.** It carries if a visitor who has
sat through a few empty warnings in the dark feels the pull to stay for the next one, and the
ledger shows them that pull as a number before it shows them whether the next one came.

*Revised 2026-10-20 (session 188), nothing above erased.* Increment 2 also carries **the record
written later**: the final database fills 3,995 warnings the offices' own verification counts empty,
and leaves 1,428 counted met without a track. The record of false alarms the question names is not
one record; the visitor now sees both answers, the second in another colour, after each choice.

*Revised 2026-10-20 (session 189), nothing above erased.* The dark is no longer one second per minute
*issued*: it is one second per minute the warning *stood over the visitor's county*, and lifts when the
office takes it back there. The score's "a 38-minute warning is 38 seconds in the dark" stays true only
for warnings that stood their full time. The belief curve is drawn as a table beside two other
readers of the run (the Atelier's toy public and the record's own answer) rather than as a curve:
with a visitor's few dozen answers per bin, a curve would draw more than it knows.
