Compares the global earthquake catalogue, 470,935 events over five years, against the century-old law predicting quake frequency by size. Below where each region's network goes deaf, that law implies roughly 797,591 unrecorded quakes.

BELOW HEARING

In five years the world's earthquake catalogue recorded 470 935 earthquakes inside these 12 boxes. This page is about the ones it did not — and the whole trick is that the record can be made to count them itself. What it will not tell you is which of them nobody heard, and which were heard by somebody who did not write here.

797 591

earthquakes of magnitude 3.0 and above happened inside these boxes in five years and are not in the record the world reads — against 30 271 that are. Under one assumption, named in the next paragraph and changeable below. Drop the assumption and this number is not zero; it is unbounded.

Earthquake sizes obey an empirical law. Gutenberg and Richter found in 1944 that the number of events of at least magnitude m falls off as a straight line on a logarithmic axis: log₁₀ N(≥m) = a − b·m. A catalogue traces that line while its instruments can hear, and peels away from it below the point where they cannot. Seismologists call that point the magnitude of completeness. It is a property of the listening, not of the ground.

So the record carries the shape of its own hole. Fit the line to the part of the record that is complete, extend it downwards, and the distance between the line and the record is a count of earthquakes that happened and that nobody has. That extension is an assumption — that the law holds below the floor of hearing — and it is the only reason a number exists at all. It is an assumption about the Earth, tested for eighty years far outside this house, which is what separates it from a convention somebody chose.

Whose record. One catalogue is asked here: the global one, assembled from what each contributing network chooses to send it. So the floor below is not a map of where the seismometers stand. Where the agency that keeps this catalogue also runs the network, its record goes down to magnitudes barely anyone can feel; everywhere else it holds what the global networks caught. An earthquake can be measured precisely by a national service and be absent here all the same. That is a second kind of missing, and this page cannot tell the two apart — it can only say how much of this record is not in it.

What that looks like in one sentence. In the Alaska box this catalogue holds earthquakes down to magnitude −1.0 — and this work asked no lower. In the South Mid-Atlantic Ridge box it holds nothing at all below magnitude 4.1 — not one event in five years. Same catalogue, same five years, one question asked twelve times. In 2 of the 12 boxes the record is already complete at magnitude 3.0 and the shortfall is exactly zero; in 2 of them the record contains no earthquake below magnitude 4 whatsoever.

The floor of hearing

How big an earthquake has to be before this record is reliably aware of it. Same catalogue, same five years, same question, twelve boxes: from M 0.9 in the California box to M 4.8 in the South of 60S box.

Under the setting this page opens at, 1 of the 12 boxes — Central Africa — return a b outside the band 0.6 to 1.6 where catalogues normally put it. Their shortfall is printed like the others and should be read as a sign that the fit is wrong rather than that the Earth is unusual there.

The largest number this arithmetic can be made to produce is 1 567 670 112, at maximum curvature, +0.7 counting down to magnitude 2.0. Nobody should believe it. It is printed because a page that showed only its believable settings would be making the choice on your behalf, and because watching an extrapolation explode over three magnitude units is the fastest way to see what kind of number this is.

The six rules

  • maximum curvature, +0.2. Woessner & Wiemer (2005) correction to the MAXC estimate
  • maximum curvature, raw. the fullest magnitude bin, uncorrected
  • goodness of fit, 95 %. Wiemer & Wyss (2000): lowest Mc whose fitted line reproduces the record to 5 %
  • goodness of fit, 90 %. the same test relaxed to 10 %
  • maximum curvature, +0.7. a deliberately cautious floor, half a magnitude above the default
  • no law at all. hold nothing about magnitudes below the floor of hearing

What this page does not say

  • It is not a comparison of how seismic one place is with another. Each box's line is fitted to that box's own record, so a quiet box and a violent one are each measured against themselves. What is compared is the floor, not the ground.
  • It is not a map of where the seismometers are. This is one record — the global one, assembled from whatever each network chooses to report into it. A national network can hear its own ground superbly and keep that catalogue at home, and then its small earthquakes are missing here while being perfectly well known there. The Japan box on this page is the clearest case of exactly that, and it is the reason the finding is about a record and not about the density of instruments on the Earth.
  • It is not a complaint about a catalogue. Nobody has failed at anything. A record is made of what reaches it, and what reaches this one is what its contributors send.
  • The count below the floor is never a measurement. It is what a law predicts, and every number on this page under a red field inherits that. The five rules that assume the law give totals from 489 452 to 5 013 391 for the same five years; the rule that assumes nothing gives no number and no upper bound.
  • Aftershocks are not removed. A large earthquake is followed by thousands of small ones, which both inflate the counts and bend the line. Declustering would change every number here and is not done: it is one more assumption, and this page prefers to carry a stated bias than an unstated method.
  • Magnitudes are not all the same quantity. The catalogue mixes local, body-wave, surface and moment magnitudes as its contributing networks report them. Fitting one line across them is standard practice and it is still a smoothing of four different instruments' idea of size.

How it was made

The catalogue was asked 985 questions and no more, each of the form how many earthquakes of at least this magnitude are in your record, inside this box, between 2021-01-01 and 2026-01-01? — 91 magnitude thresholds, one tenth apart, in each of 12 boxes. No earthquake record was downloaded and none is stored here. The material of this work is a table of counts, which is also the quantity the law is written in.
