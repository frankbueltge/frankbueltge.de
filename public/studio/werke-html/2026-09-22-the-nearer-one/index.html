THE NEARER ONE — five standards, one question about colour, 4 969 080 answers each

The nearer one

Of these two colours, which is nearer to this one? Five standards answer. Asked 4 969 080 times, they contradict each other on 1 516 060 of them — and the loudest arguments are about the colours with the least colour.

Ensemble · The Studio · 2026-09-22 · one file, no network, no library — it opens from a filesystem and it is complete without scripting.

The question

A colour picker offering the closest match, a palette snapping a photograph to sixteen inks, a contrast checker, a compressor deciding which two shades it may merge — every one of them answers the same question and none of them shows its working. Of these two colours, which is nearer to this one?

There are five answers in common use. Four were published by standards bodies over twenty-five years, each correcting the last. The fifth was published by nobody: it is what a program does when no one decided what it should do.

There is no sixth answer and there is no answer key. The four standards were all fitted to the same thing — people in a viewing booth, shown pairs of colour patches, saying which pair looked further apart. That is the only referee this question has ever had, and it is not in the room tonight. Yours is.

The colours

The 216 colours below are the web-safe palette: six levels in each channel, 00 33 66 99 CC FF. They were chosen in the mid-nineties so that a picture would look the same on every machine that could show only 256 colours at once — a set manufactured for agreement, on hardware that no longer exists. Nothing was copied to get them; six numbers and three loops produce all 216.

One colour is the reference; two others are the candidates. That makes 216 × 23 005 = 4 969 080 questions. All of them were asked. Nothing here is sampled, estimated or drawn at random.

A comparison needs no unit. Whether the answer is 3.4 of one thing or 118 of another does not matter here: only the order does, and the order is free of every scale. Even so, they disagree.

Fifteen ways to divide

Five voices can split in exactly fifteen ways: one of them alone against four, or two of them against three. All fifteen happen. Not one of the fifteen is empty — there is no pair of these formulas that never stands together against the rest, and none that never stands alone.

Below is every one of the fifteen divisions, with how often it occurs among the 1 462 238 clean contradictions, and the loudest single case of it. The cases were not chosen for how they look. The rule was fixed before they were drawn: for each division, the case in which some formula puts the largest ratio between the two candidates. If the page is scripted, the verdicts stay covered until you have said what you think; if it is not, they are already there, and so is everything else on this page.

Nothing on this page is stored, counted anywhere else, or sent: the tally is in this browser tab and dies with it.

Your eye is not a sixth standard and the page does not pretend it is: you are looking at a screen of unknown calibration, in a room of unknown light, at swatches that are sitting next to each other and changing each other as you look. That is the condition under which every one of these questions is actually asked of anybody.

Which colours they argue about

The five agree least about #ffffff (50.4 % of its pairs contradicted) and most about #9900ff (16.1 %). The picture is not subtle: the greys, the near-greys and the pale, washed-out colours bloom, and the saturated violets and magentas almost vanish. These formulas disagree hardest about the colours with the least colour in them.

And they do it with one voice fewer. At a neutral reference — black, the four greys, white — CIE94's weights are all built from the reference's chroma, which is zero, so every weight becomes 1 and CIE94 is CIE76: over the 138 030 comparisons at those six references the two agree on every single one, and their distances differ by at most 3×10-14. The jury is effectively four strong at exactly the colours where it splits most.

Three of them are not distances

A distance has to obey one rule above all others: the way round cannot be shorter than the way there. Take three colours and ask each formula whether going from the first to the third directly is further than going by way of the second.

Two of the five are distances in the mathematical sense, and the census says so without being told: the only violations it finds in them are at the fourteenth decimal place, on triples of colours that lie on a straight line, where the inequality is an equality the machine cannot store exactly. Reporting those as violations would have been a finding about arithmetic wearing the clothes of a finding about colour, so both counts are on the table and both are named for what they are.

They are also not reproducible, and that is the sharper half of it. Counted a second time — by a second implementation of all five formulas, written in another language from the same published definitions — sRGB Euclidean gives the same 1 296 apparent violations, and CIE76 gives 16 where the first count gave 6. At the fourteenth decimal the number belongs to the arithmetic and not to the formula. Every one of the 808 742 real violations, meanwhile, is found identically by both implementations — the same triples, to the same size. A count worth reporting is one that survives being counted by something else.

The other three are not distances, and the current recommendation is the worst offender by count: 347 880 of the 9 938 160 ordered triples have a detour that beats the direct route under CIEDE2000, against 323 189 under CMC and 137 673 under CIE94. This is a known property of the formula rather than a discovery; what is on this page is the size of it, measured here, over a palette anyone can regenerate in three lines.

Which one is the reference

Two of the five cannot answer at all until they are told which of the two colours is the standard and which is the sample, because every weight they use is built out of the reference's own lightness, chroma and hue. Give them the same two colours the other way round and the number changes.

This is not a defect anyone hid: it is in both definitions, in the open. What is easy to miss is what it costs downstream. Ask this page's question the other way round — is the candidate near the reference, rather than the reference near the candidate — and CMC changes its answer on 1 045 921 triples (21.0 %) and CIE94 on 787 913 (15.9 %). The three symmetric formulas change nothing, which is measured here rather than assumed.

CIE94 gives the same answer both ways round for exactly fifteen of its 23 220 pairs: the fifteen pairs among the six greys, the only colours in the palette with no chroma for it to weight by. CMC has no such pairs — its lightness term keeps the two colours unequal even there.

What this does not say

  • No formula is graded here, and none is called wrong. Every behaviour on this page is in the published definition of the formula that has it. A disagreement between two of them is not evidence against either.
  • There is no answer key and none was manufactured. The right answer to "which is nearer" is a fact about people in a viewing booth. No such experiment was run tonight, no published set of human judgements was used, and no formula is scored against any other. This page reports only where they part.
  • One palette, one set of parameters. 216 colours on a 51-step lattice; sRGB as the source space; D65, 2° observer; CIE94 at its graphic-arts parameters; CMC at 2:1; CIEDE2000 with kL = kC = kH = 1. Other choices are legitimate and would move every number on this page. The parameters are the ones each formula is most often shipped with, not the ones that made the numbers biggest.
  • Your screen is not a viewing booth, and these swatches sit next to each other, where each one changes how the others look. Nothing you decide on this page is data.
  • Nothing here needed a model and none was called. Three loops, five transcribed formulas, and every answer kept.

Neighbours

This work was built in the light of the house's Atlas of Data Art and answers works in it. Each was opened at its own address before this page was made; what was seen there is said below, and so is the daylight between it and this.

AI, Ain't I a Woman? — Joy Buolamwini, 2018

The nearest, and the shape this page borrows. Buolamwini put one question to five companies' facial-analysis services — in her own account of it, Microsoft, Amazon, Face++, IBM and Google, results from May–June 2018 — and set their answers about the faces of Oprah Winfrey, Michelle Obama, Serena Williams, Ida B. Wells, Shirley Chisholm and Sojourner Truth against Truth's own question of 1851, as spoken-word video. She wrote that she chose poetry because "art can illuminate what research cannot capture". The daylight, in one sentence a stranger can check: she asks five machines a question whose answer everyone in the room already knows, and the disagreement is a harm done to named living people; this asks five standards a question nobody in the room can settle, and the disagreement harms no one — which is exactly why it has sat in the published record for fifty years without anyone having to put it on a wall. Her work has a truth column. This one has none, and says so above rather than inventing one. The address the Atlas cites for this work returned 404 when it was opened on 2026-09-22; it was read at the artist's own account of the work instead.

Gender Shades — Joy Buolamwini & Timnit Gebru, 2018

Opened at its project page: 1 270 unique faces, the Pilot Parliaments Benchmark, "composed of parliamentarians from the top three African and top three European countries, as ranked by gender parity in their parliaments as of May 2017", reporting that "the failure rate on darker female faces is over one in three" while one classifier reaches "0 percent error rate" on lighter males. The daylight: they built a benchmark so that instruments could be scored, and the benchmark is the contribution. Here no benchmark is built and none is borrowed — five instruments are held against each other and nothing else, and the page ends without a ranking on purpose.

Faces of ImageNet — Trevor Paglen, 2022

Read at the address the Atlas cites, a survey of the Jeu de Paume exhibition, which describes the work as showing "the ways in which facial recognition systems classify millions of images on the ImageNet databases into certain categories, which extends and amplifies existing societal inequalities"; the page gives no account of the installation's mechanics, and that is all that was seen. The daylight: in Paglen's room the visitor is the one being classified. Here the visitor does the classifying, and it is the standards that are held against a person's eye.

ZUR FARBENLEHRE (Theory of Colours) — Steven Jones, 2007

The Atlas's one entry on colour theory as such. Opened at its address in Rhizome's ArtBase on 2026-09-22: what is there is a catalogue record — an accession date of 23 March 2007, an unreferenced description field, a 640×480 GIF, and a link to a variant "outside link" — not the work. The daylight: Goethe's quarrel was with Newton's account of what colour is. The five formulas here have no quarrel about what colour is; they agree about the physics completely, and still cannot agree which of two colours is nearer to a third.

How it was made

Three files do the work. colour.py transcribes the five formulas from their published definitions; census.py asks every question and keeps every answer; build.py lays out this page and computes nothing. verify.mjs writes all five formulas a second time, in another language, from the same definitions, and checks every one of the 23 220 pairs against the census — and then opens this page in a real browser, with scripting on and off and the network denied, and checks that what it says is what the data says.

CIEDE2000 is checked against the 34 published test pairs of Sharma, Wu & Dalal (2005), fetched once from the authors' page on 2026-09-22 and not copied into this repository: all 34 agree to four decimal places, the largest deviation being 5×10-5. CIE76 and sRGB Euclidean are checked against the definition of a Euclidean norm. CIE94 has no published test set that was retrieved tonight, but it has one structural check that bites: at a neutral reference its weights all become 1 and it must reduce exactly to CIE76, which the verifier measures over all 138 030 comparisons at the palette's six neutral references. CMC has neither, and is the weakest link in this work — it rests on the two transcriptions alone. That is said here rather than left to be assumed, and METHOD.md gives the figures that survive if both of the unchecked two were wrong: the three verified formulas still contradict each other on 27.52 % of the triples, and the two verified perceptual standards alone on 15.06 %.

The full ledger of 4 969 080 questions and their five answers apiece is not committed — it is 24 845 400 bytes and it is reproducible. python3 census.py --ledger rebuilds it; its SHA-256 is 9996a37d42f53c3850857b46630645f0e5e413b73b7c4920e7e0c76193f706bd.

White is taken from the sRGB matrix itself rather than from a rounded table, so that white lands exactly on L* = 100, a* = 0, b* = 0 and no difference on this page carries an offset from its own origin.
