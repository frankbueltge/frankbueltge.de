# CRITIQUE — EARSHOT, state 2 (2026-11-22)

How I met it: prose hidden, in headless Chromium, only `#stage`, at 1100x820 and once at 390x844; the paper run and both cases in real time with a frame every five seconds, the first ten seconds after "Step into the street", the deed, and five rooms stood in. I cannot hear: what I say of sound is inferred from which voices play in which room and from what the picture does, and the work may be stronger in the ear than I can tell.
(a) HOLD. The first ten seconds are quiet and correct, not arresting: a grey block of ten captioned panes, a statute sentence typing itself at the kerb from second one, three windows warming to amber by second ten. Then for most of three minutes the eye gets the same five or six transparency clauses looping in the street while small rectangles brighten and dim; a visitor without headphones leaves before the deed.
What would keep them is real, and it is three moments. The street flushing amber and the clock stopping when a stranger's sentence finally arrives ("did not meet the threshold" is the hardest second in the work); the maker's pane beating cold blue and then going dark while the street recites on; and standing in "An authority, within a day", a large empty unlit room with the neighbour's writing blurred to giant bands on the glass.
Beside the bar it does not yet stand as a picture. CLEAR AIR, GOING OUT and UNWOVEN fill the frame with their material; here two thirds of the frame is flat dark panes with labels, the six strangers are pictograms, and the frosted writing that should be the building's skin is a smear a few pixels high from the street.
Beside Saydnaya it has the wall but not the body: there the threshold of hearing is a person's memory, here it is a filter setting, and nothing is at stake for whoever stands outside. Beside Sensing Streams it is the more exact work (its subject is a signal's address, not its presence) and by far the less sensuous.
(b) FELT BEFORE WORDS. Felt: that the street is talked at without pause and told nothing; that what happens, happens behind glass and briefly; that in the cases the news comes from the pavement, late, and the offices light up after it.
Not felt: the deed itself on paper, which is one small amber line top left and a maker's window that looks exactly as it did at the release; any difference between a room receiving routine paper and a room hearing of the deed. Not felt either: whether a machine or a person acted, what any signal counts, and the record that outlasts the models, beyond one light going out at six months that nobody would notice.
Lateness is not felt as duration: on this clock 116 days and ten minutes are the same stretch of loop.
(c) MEANS. The wall and the looping street voice carry the question; the clock, the building and its furniture illustrate it, and the captions do more of the telling than the form does.
AFTER READING. The prose does the form's work exactly where the step's question is sharpest: "0 of the voices say who acted", "0 of 51", the twelve signals with no hour, the five numbered hours, the yearly figure. Those are counts on a page; the building makes none of them felt, and the twelve unspoken duties are a few lines of tiny grey type under a subtitle.
The page states the best idea in the work as a limit ("what blurs the words for an eye does not keep them from a machine") and leaves it in a paragraph.
Form against score: the score promises one inversion, clear voices that say nothing of the deed and everything about it behind glass. State 2 overturned it and the claim was not rewritten: in both cases the deed is said first, clearly, in the street, by strangers, and the only voice anywhere that says a machine did it is the harmed party's at the kerb.
That is a better finding than the score's, and the work still shows it as an appendix to "on paper", behind a second button.
The score's own test. A visitor who ran it once could say the street heard plenty; "nothing of the deed" they could say only of the paper run, and there only because a caption named a deed. "Every room that heard of it was one they could not enter" is false in the hand: every room opens at the first click, and the title card invites the click.
The work hands over the key before it has let anyone feel the door; on its own test it does not pass yet.
WHAT IT LACKS MOST. Exclusion that costs something, and a deed with a body: the visitor is never kept out of anything, and the event the whole building turns on is, on paper, a line of small type.
THE ONE CHANGE. Make the first run a locked street and lead with a case, not the paper: doors shut until the record reaches today, and the deed carried as the cold light it already has in the maker's room, spreading only to the rooms that wake because of it, so the kerb's warm endless recital and the cold wordless panes are told apart without a caption.
The rooms open afterwards, and the paper run becomes what one enters them to check. Then rewrite the score's inversion to what the record showed, and let the check session treat the leaking wall as a claim of the work, not a footnote.
