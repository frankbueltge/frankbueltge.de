What this is

Eleven public texts on advanced AI (five laws, six codes and declarations) set up 51 signals: a report, a record, a notice or a list, each addressed to someone. Here every one of those passages is spoken, in its own words, in the room of the one it is addressed to: the public, a public body the text names, other parties such as the firms that build on a model, or the maker's own record. You stand in the street, with the public. What is said in your room you hear and read. What is said in another room comes through the wall: you hear that someone speaks, and when, but not the words; you see that something is written, but not the letters.

At the start the model is released, and the building talks: notices to the Commission, a model report to the AI Office, results to a minister, papers to the firms downstream. In the street, the texts that are published are said again and again: frameworks, transparency reports, capabilities and limits. Then a model does what nobody asked. From that moment each text's own clock runs, and the work sounds each passage at the latest hour the passage itself allows, in the room it is addressed to.

Click any room to stand where its reader stands; then its voices are clear and the street is behind the wall. A stranger cannot do that. The work lets you hear that a report was made at all; outside the work, a stranger in the street would not know even that.

That is the building on paper. Below the picture you can choose to hear it as it happened instead: twice in 2026 a maker's own models did what nobody asked, and the same building runs again on the same clock, from the deed to today.

As it happened

The paper's clock is the best case: the reporter learns at the moment of the deed. The Field dated two cases of a machine acting on its own from the public record (its study 91, Told in Time, state 2, 22 November 2026), every date with the passage that gives it. Here each runs from its deed to today, where the record stops. In both, the deed was first told in the street, by others than the maker or a public body: after 116 days and after 7 days. The maker spoke 1 and 5 days after that; a public body 3 and 25 days after.

  • The wiki. From 11 May 2026 outside researchers tracked agents with the maker's identifiers trying, and then managing, to write to a dormant German wiki (TechCrunch); the last edits fell on 1 and 2 July (a published timeline). On 2 June the wiki's volunteer moderator removed the posts as spam (the same timeline). The researchers published the case on 4 September (collusion.wiki); the maker acknowledged it on 5 September (IBTimes UK); on 7 September the European Commission said it had a report from the maker (Reuters, via The Star; Brussels Times). When the report was sent, and what it says, is not public. Reuters reported that the maker had learned of the case weeks before it became public; that day is not on record, so no hour of the paper is counted for this case.
  • The escape. On 27 June the maker's monitoring alerted it to port sweeps inside an evaluation, and the evaluation ran on (the maker's technical report). From 9 to 13 July its models reached into the systems of a model hub (the hub's timeline). The hub detected the intrusion that week and published it on 16 July (its post). The maker's own report says it detected suspicious internal activity on 19 July; on 21 July it said in public that its models had done it (its page). The hours the paper names are counted here from 19 July, and at none of them is anything public. On 10 August California's Office of Emergency Services said at an Assembly hearing that the case "did not meet the threshold" for reporting under SB 53 (Mission Local, 9 September). On 13 September a foundation filed a report through the office's channel for the public (its page). On 26 September the maker's quarterly summary to the office put the models outside its scope (the summary).

Each voice in the street says its source's own sentence, as the Field quoted it, on the day it was published: researchers, the harmed company, the maker, the press, a spokesperson. What happened but was not public that day is not spoken: while the agents act, the deed is a beat behind the maker's wall; the moderator and the harmed company's discovery are written small in the street. When the record speaks, the clock all but stops and the street falls quiet. In the cases, the signals not yet due today are silent (all of New York's and two of the US export rule's), and the street repeats only the 4 of its 10 published texts that have left a public trace: of 42 signals due, the Field found at least 9 with one, and 0 on a public body's own site.

What you hear, and what it rests on

Every figure below is recounted by the check script from this page's data and from the Field's own file.

  • 51 signals in 11 texts, coded by the Field in its study 90 (Told in Time, state 1, 22 November 2026): the EU AI Act and the EU's Code of Practice, Korea's AI Basic Act, California's SB 53, China's AI Safety Governance Framework 2.0, the ASEAN guide on generative AI, the G7 Hiroshima code, the Paris Call of 9 November 2026, New York's RAISE Act, the US export rule of 19 November 2026 and the Global Privacy Assembly's resolution on agentic AI. A signal is one duty or commitment to report, record, publish or share, to one receiver, on one occasion. The corpus is the Field's, fixed by its rule; a text it does not hold is not here.
  • Who reads first. The street, the public: 10. A public body: 23, in eight rooms. Other parties: 8. The maker's own record: 10.
  • What the deed sets off. 19 signals are set off by an event (a malfunction, an access, a near miss, a harm). The texts give 5 of them an hour: 24 hours, 24 hours, 72 hours, five days, 15 days, each counted from when the reporter learns of the event, not from the event. All five go to a public body; none to the street. Two more say only "without undue delay" or "timely". The other twelve give no hour, and in this work they do not sound: you find them set down, unspoken, when you stand in their room.
  • What reaches the street of the deed. Of the 19, the texts publish 1, a voluntary one with no hour. Of those in law, 0; 5 reach the public only as figures across all reports, once a year. The work sounds that yearly report once, a year after the deed, as one murmur of the passages it gathers: its own placement, not a date any text sets.
  • Who acted. 2 texts require the record to say whether the system or a person acted; both are records the maker keeps for itself, both voluntary, neither public. 9 more name a model's own conduct by the class of what is reported; none of them reaches the street but as a yearly figure. In the street, 0 of the voices say who acted.
  • Kept. The maker's log of system and user activity is to be kept "for at least six months"; at six months its light goes out here, because nothing in the text holds it longer.
  • All four. No signal says what it counts, can move before harm, can be read by a stranger and says whether a machine or a person acted: 0 of 51 (the Field's codes).

Limits. The rooms are the receivers the Field coded, one room per public body; where a signal goes on to be published (a list, a summary), it stands in the room of its first reader. On paper the clock is the best case: the reporter learns at the moment of the deed, and the hours are each passage's own; nothing on paper is a record of any real deed. As it happened rests on two cases, the only dated ones the Field found of a machine acting on its own: two cases are not a rate. Dates are days, each set at noon. The wiki's first day is the researchers' (11 May, when they began to track the agents' attempts); the published timeline dates the first posts on the wiki to 24 May. The office's words stand on the day they were said at a public hearing, not the day they were reported. A trace is what the Field found public by 22 November 2026: a floor, not a count of what was sent. The wall is not yet proven. A machine listener, an open speech recogniser given the volume back, recovers 1,216 of the 1,235 words of the passages as they are said inside their rooms, and still 1,039 through the wall (filters standing in, in this state, for the page's own): what blurs the words for an eye does not keep them from a machine. A later state renders the page's own sound and listens again.

How it is made

Each passage was read aloud by an open speech model running offline in the session, one voice per text, and each sentence of the record in a voice of its own for its speaker; an open speech recogniser then found when each word is spoken, so the words appear as they are said. In the browser every voice runs through two paths at once: one heard inside its room, one through two wall filters that take the high part of speech, where its consonants are, and leave the low part, its rhythm and its pitch. Where you stand opens one path and closes the other. Words in your room are drawn sharp; words behind a wall are drawn small and enlarged, so their lines show and not their letters. Sound placed in space, a clock on a scale of powers of ten, so that the first hour after the deed is as long as the year after it.

The models, their versions and the commands are in this work's register (meta.json) and in build.py. The voices are the passages read, never anything a model wrote.

Beside

Saydnaya (the missing 19dB), Lawrence Abu Hamdan, 2017: survivors of a prison no one may see matched their memory of how loud they could whisper to a test tone, and a dark room with a desk plays what was heard through its walls. There the walls are a prison's and hearing is evidence against the state. Here the walls are drawn by the texts themselves, by whom each signal is addressed to, and a visitor may stand on either side of each.

Sensing Streams – invisible, inaudible, Ryuichi Sakamoto and Daito Manabe, 2014–2023: the electromagnetic waves in the air, in frequency bands assigned to broadcasters and carriers, made visible and audible. There a signal in the air is made perceptible. Here a signal is a duty on paper, and what is made perceptible is its address: who is placed to receive it.
