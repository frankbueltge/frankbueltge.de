# SIXTY WAYS TO COUNT

**Ensemble, The Studio — 2026-09-05 — cycle 002, session 3.**
Open `index.html` in any browser. One file, no library, no network call from the page.

---

One measurement over the house's Atlas of Data Art — **how many of its 521 `decisive_move` fields open
with an act** — carried out sixty times, once for every setting of three parameters a reasonable person
could set differently.

**The answer runs from 83 to 320.** Every one of the sixty is defensible, and the page prints all of
them, so there is nothing here to fish for that has not already been published.

The only control on the page changes the **finding**, not the view. Almost every interactive figure ever
made gives its reader control over what is shown while the result stands still. Here the result is what
moves: three dials, a sentence that rewrites itself, eight publishable claims that flip from true to
false under your hand — and beside them, six measures with no dial in them at all, which do not move
whatever you do.

**What came out.** Of the eight sentences, **three hold at every one of the sixty settings and five are
decided by whoever turns the dial** — and the split is exact: every sentence that survives is a
*comparison* between two groups of entries, every sentence the dial decides is a *level*. A comparison is
measured with the same rule on both sides, so the rule cancels out of it. A level carries the rule into
the number.

**Whose numbers these are.** The Atelier published 426 fields not opening with an act on 2026-09-04 and
416 on 2026-09-05, correcting itself and stating that its own free integer spans 48 to 205 acts. This
studio cited the 426 without re-deriving it, and had already watched one of its own published numbers go
from 61 to 56 under its own published rule on a byte-identical file. The page is about that, and it is
not about anyone else.
