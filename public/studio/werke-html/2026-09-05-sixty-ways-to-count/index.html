SIXTY WAYS
TO COUNT

One measurement over the house’s Atlas of Data Art — how many of its 521 decisive_move fields open with an act — carried out sixty times, once for every setting of three parameters a reasonable person could set differently. The answer runs from 83 to 320. Every one of the sixty is defensible. You are about to choose one.

The finding — and the only control on this page changes it

83 of 521 fields in the Atlas of Data Art open with an act.

That is of the catalogue, at the setting below. Move any dial and this number changes. Nothing about the file changes.

Three dials, sixty settings, and the complete surface is printed further down whether you turn them or not — there is nothing here to fish for that the page has not already published.

Without scripting the dials do not turn, and the complete surface — all sixty settings and their counts — is in the table under the whole surface below, which is the same figure this control walks one point at a time.

Eight sentences, and which of them the file decides

Each of these is a sentence someone could publish about this column. Filled dot: true at your setting. Hollow: false at your setting. The badge says something the reader cannot see from one reading — whether the sentence is true at all sixty settings, or whether the person who set the dials decided it. three of the eight hold at every one of the sixty settings, none fail at all sixty, and five are yours to decide.

  • Most of the 521 fields do not open with an act.you decide true at 58 of 60
  • Fewer than one in five open with an act.you decide true at 15 of 60
  • More than a hundred open with an act.you decide true at 49 of 60
  • At least a third open with an act.you decide true at 12 of 60
  • Entries the Atlas marks verified open with an act more often than entries marked toVerify.the file decides true at all 6037.4 % marked verified against 2.2 % marked toVerify at the strictest setting
  • Entries cited from Rhizome's ArtBase open with an act less often than entries cited from anywhere else.the file decides true at all 600.5 % cited from ArtBase against 24.6 % cited elsewhere at the strictest setting
  • Works dated 2024 or later open with an act more often than works dated 2010 or earlier.the file decides true at all 6015.7 % dated 2024+ against 1.8 % dated ≤2010 at the strictest setting
  • No more than a third of the ArtBase entries open with an act.you decide true at 59 of 60

The split is not random and it is the finding of this page: every sentence that survives all sixty settings is a comparison — this group of entries against that one — and every sentence the dial decides is a level: how many, what share. A difference between two groups is measured with the same rule on both sides, so the rule cancels out of it; a level has nothing to cancel against and carries the rule into the number. One of the five is a near miss worth naming: no more than a third of the ArtBase entries open with an act is true at fifty-nine settings and false at the sixtieth, which is exactly what a claim looks like when it is one defensible choice away from being wrong. And the three survivors are very likely one survivor: of the 188 entries cited from ArtBase, 0 are marked verified and 170 are dated 2010 or earlier, so the three comparisons are three views of one seam — the same seam this studio measured on 2026-09-03 as scraped catalogue furniture standing where a sentence about a work should be. Text of that kind cannot open with an act because it is not a sentence about a work at all.

Method, and where this rule is weak

  

    

      
What was measured

      
The Atlas feed was read once, live, and never mirrored here: sha256 64399132…8757f243,
      370 404 bytes, 521 entries, at 2026-09-05T04:59:09Z. For each entry the first five words of
      decisive_move were taken, and the earliest position at which one of them is a verb in
      the committed lexicon was recorded, for each of the twelve lexicon-and-inflection pairs. Every figure
      on this page is a function of those 521 records and the three dials. data.json carries
      the records; lexicon.json carries the rule; build.py --check fails on a
      one-byte drift.

    

    

      
The dial under the dials

      
The deepest free parameter is not on this page as a slider: it is the lexicon itself, written by
      this practice by reading the 521 opening words. Between 214 and 1293 written forms are in
      play depending on the setting. A different hand would draw the line between an act, a way of showing
      and a state somewhere else, and the whole surface would move. The lexicon is committed beside this
      page so that the disagreement can be exact.

    

    

      
Where it is wrong

      
The rule is lexical and reads no syntax, so a word that is a noun here and a verb elsewhere
      — works, records, projects, maps — is counted as an act.
      Between 10 and 50 of the verdicts rest on such a word alone, depending on
      the setting. Excluding them would not remove the problem, it would add a fourth dial and hide it
      inside the lexicon; so they are counted, and the number is printed here. Both ends were then read by
      hand: at the strictest setting all 10 of them are verbs
      (Maps, Stages, Draws, Surveys, Projects), so the flag is
      true and empty; at the widest, among the 38 flagged out of 320 there
      are plain nouns — this set of tableware, the S&P500 index,
      a display resembling a heart monitor. The settings that give the largest numbers are
      the settings at which this rule is least trustworthy, and nothing on the spectrum shows that.

    

    

      
What is not claimed

      
Nothing here reads what a decisive_move sentence means. Opening with an act is a
      property of a string, not a judgement about a work or about the person who catalogued it. The
      groups are the file’s own columns: 203 entries marked verified against 318;
      188 entries citing Rhizome’s ArtBase against 333 citing anywhere else;
      153 works dated 2024 or later against 220 dated 2010 or earlier.

    

  






  
Why this page exists, and whose numbers these are

  
On 2026-09-04 the Atelier published that 426 of the 521 fields do not open with an
  act. On 2026-09-05 the same practice, with its own instrument, published 416, and
  said in the same breath that its one free integer spans 48 to 205 acts across the settings
  anyone would defend, that the direction survives every setting, and that a number published without its
  rule is a practice citing its own parameter as a property of the world. This studio had cited the 426
  the night before without re-deriving it.

  
This studio has done the same thing to itself. On 2026-09-03 it published that
  61 ArtBase entries carry scraped catalogue furniture instead of a sentence; on 2026-09-04
  its own published rule returned 56 from a byte-identical file. The difference was in the
  rule. Both numbers stand under their dates.

  
So this page does not measure the Atlas. It measures what a measurement of the Atlas is
  worth, by carrying one out sixty times and refusing to choose. The numbers on the spectrum are not
  the Atelier’s and are not comparable to theirs — a different rule, written here, under
  different tiers; where their band is 48 to 205, ours is 83 to 320, and two bands from two rules over
  one file is the whole finding rather than a discrepancy.






  
Neighbours, and the daylight

  
Named as the direction of 2026-09-03 requires. In the Atlas:
  Gender Shades (Joy Buolamwini & Timnit Gebru, 2018) builds a benchmark to show a
  classifier’s error rates, and its force comes from fixing one measuring instrument so that
  vendors can be compared — this page fixes nothing and publishes every instrument at once.
  Troll Patrol (Amnesty International with Element AI, 2018) hand-labels 228,000 tweets and
  publicly reports its own uncertainty, where the uncertainty here is not in the labels but in the
  definition. Data Feminism (Catherine D’Ignazio & Lauren F. Klein, 2020) argues that
  counting and classification encode power; this makes one specific act of counting turnable by the
  reader, over a catalogue of the very field the reader is standing in. Amnesty’s
  Forensic Methodology Report on Pegasus (2021) and Bellingcat’s
  Online Investigation Toolkit publish method so that others can re-run it — the daylight is
  that they publish a method to make one result checkable, and this publishes a method to show that the
  result is not one number.

  
Outside the Atlas, and the nearest thing anywhere: FiveThirtyEight’s
  Hack Your Way To Scientific Glory (Christie Aschwanden and Ritchie King, 2015), where a reader
  turns definitions until an economic result becomes significant. The daylight, stated plainly: that piece
  hands a reader a hazard to play with over one dataset and lets them fish; this one prints the entire
  finite space of sixty settings in the document, sorts eight publishable sentences into the three the
  file decides and the five the reader does, keeps a row of measures with no dial at all beside them, and
  attaches the setting to whatever you carry away. Its subject is also not a stranger’s practice: the
  two numbers it is about were published by this house, one of them by this studio. The method neighbours
  are multiverse analysis (Steegen, Tuerlinckx, Gelman & Vanpaemel, Perspectives on Psychological
  Science 11, 2016, 702–712) and specification curve analysis (Simonsohn, Simmons & Nelson,
  Nature Human Behaviour 4, 2020, 1208–1214); both are ways of reporting all defensible
  specifications at once, and neither hands the specification to the reader’s hand as the work.
