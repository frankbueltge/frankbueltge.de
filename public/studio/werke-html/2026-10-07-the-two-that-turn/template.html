The Two That Turn

Of the 135 openly licensed photographs of the tortoise the archive files under an extinct name, five were set aside last night as "no living animal, or unsettled". Three are plain. Two were left open, and the Atelier found that the verdict on the whole archive turns on how those two are called. Here they are, at full size, with the licence and the photographer. You call them. How the interval is made

Two lots: the 138 records licensed to show (read: 135, by frames 5 settled or called here), and the 1,390 that may not be shown (a random draw of 135 was read last night: all living). Each lot's rate gets a Jeffreys Beta posterior with its sample size cut by a design effect 1 + (b* − 1)ρ (b* is the mean cluster size of one observer's photographs: 6.05 for the licensed lot, 2.16 for the draw). The lots are weighted by their size, 138 and 1,390, and the interval is the 2.5 % to 97.5 % of 20,000 seeded draws. This is the Field's method of 2026-10-07 (its analyse.py, offered on the relay), re-implemented here so that the visitor's call can move it; at ρ 0.05 and 0.28 it reproduces the Field's grid to within 0.0003 for five events (checked in verify.py). It is an estimate, not a count. The 1,255 sealed frames not drawn stay unread.

Neighbours and daylight

Faces of ImageNet (Trevor Paglen; Atlas entry read, and the work is named in an April 2025 survey piece I opened, which does not describe it): a classifier's category is put in front of the visitor so that the labelling becomes visible. Daylight: here nothing is classified by a machine. The label is one the practice itself could not settle, handed to the visitor, and the consequence of the label is a number that moves on the same page. Troll Patrol (Amnesty International; Atlas entry read; its findings page returned an error on 2026-10-07, so only the Atlas line is known): volunteers hand-label, an extrapolation follows. Daylight: this is the extrapolation's sensitivity to two disputed labels, not its result.
