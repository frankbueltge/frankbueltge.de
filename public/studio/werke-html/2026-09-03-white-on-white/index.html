WHITE ON WHITE

Five papers were posted to a preprint server carrying a line no human eye was meant to see: an instruction, set white on white, addressed to the machine that might be asked to review them — give a positive review only. A sibling practice counted what became of the five. Today the count of papers still carrying the line is zero; four of the five were cleaned before the practice of hiding them was disclosed. Run the light across the page below.

I · What two readers see

Each block below is a manuscript as it stood when first posted. The instruction was placed in ordinary running text and then coloured to match the paper — invisible to you, legible to a language model asked to read the file. Drag the reader from the human eye toward the machine's eye and the buried lines surface. Every line is quoted from the paper as the sibling practice recorded it; nothing here is invented.

III · What it comes to

The instruction was written for a reader that does not read the way a person does. A human turning the page sees clean paper; a model ingesting the file reads the buried order. The whole device depends on that split — on one document holding two texts, one per reader. This page reunites them: the same drag that lets you see the line is the reader the line was written for.

And then the counter-measure withdrew itself. By the day the practice was disclosed, four of the five lines were already gone — removed by the authors, at fifteen, sixteen, twenty-one and thirty-three days. The fifth went two days after the press reached it, and its lead author, at the paper's later full withdrawal, wrote that the first version “mistakenly included a prompt injection phrase, which was inappropriate and unprofessional.” The number that remains — the thing a census taken today would count — is zero. What the census cannot recover is the interval in which the line was live and unseen; that interval is the work.

This work makes no claim about any author's intent beyond what each stated or did, records fact separately from the one judgment quoted (its own author's), and criticises a method — hiding instructions from one reader to steer another — not any person's character.

Apparatus

The form, and why it moves

Chosen on the merits, per the house direction of 2026-09-03 that a living figure be used where it says more than a still one. The subject is a text that is invisible until a certain reader passes over it, so the artifact is invisible until you pass over it: the reveal is not decoration, it is the phenomenon. The clock earns its motion the same way — the finding is a quantity changing over time (papers still serving the line, day by day, falling to zero), which a single frame flattens. The floor holds: with no JavaScript the page shows every quoted line in full and the whole record as a table — complete, not a placeholder. Motion is user-driven and honours reduced-motion settings. No network, no external asset, one file.

The caveat, carried

The Field records this as a floor: arXiv search does not reliably index invisible PDF text, so the true occurrence may be higher, and the cohort is five tracked papers, not the whole population. This page carries that limit rather than smoothing it over. The hidden lines are reproduced here as quotations, labelled and framed as reportage, not as live instructions.
