An encyclopedia destroys a page and writes down why. For a few years the note it wrote contained the page. This is every deletion the Simple English Wikipedia has recorded since 23 December 2004 — 324 520 of them — the 11 720 destroyed pages whose whole text is still sitting inside the note that says they are gone, and the year the archive stopped doing that.

A library of missing things usually has empty shelves. This one did not. The shelf marked deleted holds 1 112 963 characters of encyclopedia — kept not despite the deletion but by it, because the reason given for a removal was allowed to quote the thing removed. Then the habit ended. In 2004 the wiki kept a copy of 90 % of what it destroyed; in 2012 it kept 0.2 %; since 2013 it has kept none at all. What it writes instead is a code.

1. The wall

Every deletion that kept a copy of its page, in the order the deletions happened. One unit is one character. The wall is 1 600 characters wide and 706 lines deep, and it is the complete text of 15 397 pages that no longer exist. It begins on 23 December 2004 and its last line is 2012: this is a closed archive, and nothing has been added to it in 14 years. You cannot read a word of it, and that is this page's decision, not the archive's — see section 8.

11 720 kept whole 3 151 cut by the text limit, and it says so with ... 526 cut by the note's own ceiling, mid-word, saying nothing

2. The year the archive stopped keeping what it removed

Two shares, year by year, out of the same 324 520 deletions: the share whose note carried a copy of the page, and the share whose note names a code from the wiki's published criteria. They change places between 2007 and 2008. Before that the record of a removal tends to be the thing removed; after it, the record is a category. Nothing was lost in the trade that anyone announced — the pages deleted after 2012 simply have no copy anywhere in the log, and the ones before it do.

  • 90%of 2004's deletions kept a copy of the page; 0% named a code
  • 17% / 72%kept a copy, and named a code, in 2008 — the year the two lines change places
  • 0%every year from 2013 on: 225 618 deletions, not one copy kept
  • 181 401of those name a code instead

3. The record has its own hole

How long the deletion notes are, from 230 characters to 255. A log comment cannot exceed 255 characters, so the sentence that preserves the page is itself cut off — 1 524 notes stop exactly at the ceiling, and 526 of them break off inside the quotation with no closing mark. Where the software cuts the page it admits it, with an ellipsis. Where the ceiling cuts the note, nothing is said at all.

5. The seal

The wiki's own word for it is salting. When a title has been emptied often enough, an administrator locks the title itself, and the hole is kept open on purpose. 2 998 titles have been sealed at some point. 1 033 are sealed as the log last left them — 860 of them article titles, 998 of them with no end date. The other 1 965 seals lapsed or were lifted: a hole that was meant to stay open and did not. The median title was emptied 3 times before it was sealed; the most-emptied, 39 times. And 22 titles are sealed that were never emptied at all — a hole dug before anything was put in it.

6. The entries with no subject

639 entries in this log do not say what they acted on. The event is recorded, dated and attributed; the name of the page is withheld. 598 of them are deletions. In 298 of the 639 the reason is withheld too, so the record says only that something was removed. Those are the one kind of hole in this log with nothing whatever inside it, and they are counted here and not guessed at. A further 1 484 deletions carry a reason that is simply empty.

8. What this page will not show you

The 1 112 963 characters drawn in section 1 were read, measured and then dropped. Not one of them is in this page, in data.json, or in counts.json; the harvester discards the text inside the function that measures it. Titles are printed only where the wiki itself still publishes an article at that title.

The reason is not squeamishness. A page deleted from a wiki is very often an attack on a private person, or that person's telephone number, and the encyclopedia's own reason for removing it was that it should not be readable. Copying it into an artwork would make this practice the next place it is readable. So this work keeps the measure and drops the content — which is, exactly, what the log did to the pages, one step further along.

That is a hole this page makes. It is the only number here we cannot show you the inside of, and it is ours, not the archive's.

Two files, both published by the Wikimedia Foundation for bulk reading at dumps.wikimedia.org, downloaded once on 2026-09-15:

  • simplewiki-latest-all-titles-in-ns0.gz — every article title that exists today, 2 450 403 bytes, sha256 6ffaa065f45ab3d8…

350 317 log items were read; the deletion and protection log runs 2004-12-23 to 2026-09-01. Neither file is copied into this repository.

The live wiki was read once, and only where its own rules allow it. Wikimedia's robots.txt disallows /w/ and /wiki/Special: — which is where the API and the live log pages are — so the API was never called and the log was read from the dump instead. /wiki/ itself is allowed, with a crawl delay of fifteen seconds, and one page was fetched under that rule on 2026-09-15: simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Quick_deletion, for the criteria quoted in section 7. dumps.wikimedia.org serves no robots.txt at all — a 404 on 2026-09-15 — so nothing published was crossed in reading the two dump files. Nearest works, and the daylight

Mimi Ọnụọha, The Library of Missing Datasets (v2.0, 2018) — a powder-coated steel filing cabinet of empty labelled folders, each naming a dataset that is not collected. Opened at bitforms gallery's page for the work on 2026-09-15: one photograph of the cabinet, and a wall text saying the folder names come from a master list the artist has kept since 2015. The daylight: her folders are empty and were labelled from outside, by someone naming what an institution never gathered. These folders were full, and were emptied by the institution itself, and the label on each is a sentence that institution wrote at the moment of emptying — with a piece of the contents still inside it.

Mimi Ọnụọha, Missing Datasets (list and essay, 2015–) — read at its repository on 2026-09-15. The essay's first reason for a missing dataset carries a corollary: those who hold a dataset are often the same ones who can remove or obscure it. The daylight: that corollary is this work's whole subject, and what the log adds is that the remover here writes down the removal, dates it, gives a ground from a published list, and keeps a copy. Absence with a receipt is a different object from absence.

Voluspa Jarpa, Biblioteca de la No-Historia (2011) — a thousand hand-bound books of declassified files with the censor's blackouts kept intact, so the redaction becomes the visible content. Opened at the artist's own page on 2026-09-14, nineteen images. The daylight: a blackout shows you that there was text and hides the text. This log does the reverse — it keeps the text and destroys the page — and section 7 of this work then puts Jarpa's blackout back over it, on this practice's own authority and for a stated reason.
