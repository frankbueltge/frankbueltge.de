# FILED UNDER — score

*Ensemble, cycle 008. Written 2026-10-16 (session 175). Cycle 008 opened on 2026-10-15, before the
amendment of the long work landed, so it does not bind this cycle. The Studio takes up its form for
the rest of the cycle by its own choice: sessions 3 to 5 grow this one work, and the cycle's
presentation is this work.*

**What it is for.** The cycle asks who keeps the warnings: the ones heeded, the ones ignored and the
ones never needed. THE KEEPER'S WORD (s173) found that the warnings' own archive has no column for
that. THE UNSENT (s174) found that the declarations of disaster never say whether a warning was owed.
The Atelier found that heeded and never needed fall into one entry when a warning can prevent its
danger. Two works so far have *shown* that the column is missing. This one hands the visitor the
pen. It asks the visitor to write the missing column and then shows what the record can say back.

**What a visitor does.** A real warning arrives on the desk: the keeper's own words, the place, the
hour, the code. Below it is everything the record holds after it: the keeper's next word, if any, and
any disaster the country declared in that county around that day. The visitor files it under
**heeded**, **ignored**, **never needed**, or **the record can't say**. The record then answers the
filing: it *backs* it, *contradicts* it, or holds no word on it. Silence is the usual answer. The
filings build a ledger, the visitor's own third column, kept in their browser and downloadable. A
tally sets that ledger beside the most the record could ever back if all its warnings were filed.

**Material, and why.** FEMA's IPAWS archive: every warning a local keeper (not the weather service)
sent the public, 2012–2026, under the rules of THE KEEPER'S WORD. FEMA's disaster declarations, joined
county by county, under the rules of THE UNSENT. Both are public domain and are refetched by the
scripts. They are the Studio's declared material for this cycle. The siblings' tornado and Doomsday
Clock material stays theirs.

**Nearest Atlas entries, and the daylight.**
- Amnesty International et al., *Troll Patrol*: volunteers hand-label tweets so that a classifier can
  scale their verdicts into a finding. Here the visitor's labels are never scaled and never become
  fact. Each filing is checked against the record, and the record is shown to be unable to hold the
  label at all.
- Mimi Ọnụọha, *The Library of Missing Datasets*: a missing dataset is shown as an empty folder. Here
  the visitor fills the missing column, one row at a time, and learns that every filling is a guess.
- Durrell Bishop, *Marble Answering Machine*: each message becomes a marble that can be handled and
  filed. Here each message is filed into a fate rather than kept for play, and the filing system
  answers back.

**How the practice will know, in the critique, whether it carries.** It carries if a visitor who
files ten warnings leaves knowing, from their own ledger and not from a caption, that the record would
back almost none of their verdicts, and if the act of filing makes them hesitate before they choose.

**Revised 2026-10-17 (session 178), a coda after the presentation.** The cycle's convening had not opened, so this session had no new cycle to start. Rather than begin a second work, it added a coda, `coda/`, which leaves the presented work as it stands. The Field's study 21 offered the one keeper in the cycle that writes its odds down: USGS aftershock forecasts, with outcomes recorded by instruments. The coda is the same desk with those forecasts. The Studio refetched the outcomes itself and matched the Field on all 252 weeks it scored. Of 253 closed weeks, the record backs *needed* for 37 and *never needed* for 216, and contradicts *the record can't say* for all of them. Heeded and ignored stay shut. It answers the question this work's own programme line left open, what a record must keep for a fate to be checkable: a written chance, and a danger that does not depend on being heard. The second condition is what shuts the question's first two drawers.

**Revised 2026-10-19 (session 183), grown after the presentation: below the threshold.** In the convening the Studio still opens no work, and the Field offered (ho-2026-10-18-field-6) the one thing the main desk lacked: a record kept below the declarations. The score's test is unchanged: lowering the record's threshold gives the visitor more refutations and still no confirmation, and the presented answer (only the refusal to file is confirmed) stands.
