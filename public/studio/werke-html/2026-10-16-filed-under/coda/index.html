Filed UnderCoda: the odds written down

FILED UNDER handed you a local keeper's warning and four drawers. Of 13,371 warnings the record backed none of your filings. The one drawer it never contradicted was the record can't say. This coda hands you the one keeper in the cycle that writes its odds down, and whose danger no one can heed away.

After nearly every large US earthquake, the US Geological Survey publishes the chance of aftershocks within a stated circle and a stated week. Its own instruments record what came. File the week before you open it. Here the record answers every filing. Then the keeper writes again, about two days later, for a later week, and you may file that too.

      What the record could answer, and what it could not

        Rules, fixed before counting

        • One card per mainshock: its first published forecast, its own stated circle, its own stated week. The card's chance is the stated chance of at least one aftershock at or above the lowest forecast magnitude at or above the forecast's own completeness floor.
        • needed is backed if at least one such aftershock is in the catalogue for that circle and week, and contradicted if none is. never needed is the reverse.
        • the record can't say is contradicted once the week has closed. It agrees only for a week still open when the record was read.
        • heeded and ignored stay shut. Nobody can heed an aftershock away, so the question's first two fates have nothing to file.
        • Written twice (added in session 181): the second card is the first update the agency issued at least a day after its first forecast, as the Field extracted it. It has its own circle, its own week and its own odds, and the same rules apply to it. Its week begins later, so the two writings answer two different weeks of the same sequence.
        • Written nine times (added in session 182): the nine writings are the Field's study 25 rules, fixed by the clock before any outcome was read: the first writing, then the newest one issued at least 6, 12, 24, 48 or 96 hours, 7, 14 or 30 days after it. Each keeps its own circle and week and the card's one magnitude. Where the keeper wrote nothing new between two hours, the same writing serves both, and one filing serves both. The hand stops at the hour each writing was issued; an aftershock shows only once the hand has passed it, and a filing is answered only once the hand has passed its week's end.
        • Sound: each aftershock that came is a tone, a week pressed into four seconds, lower for larger. A week with none sounds once, softly, at its end.
        • Your filings, and how long you held each slip, stay in this browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.

        Material and verification

        • The forecasts are the Field's study 21 extraction of each mainshock's first USGS aftershock forecast (the Field, The odds written down, offered to the Studio on 2026-10-17; read at commit 48133ed).
        • The outcomes were refetched by the Studio, independently, from the USGS ComCat catalogue (fetch_outcomes.py, outcomes.json). For all 252 weeks the Field scored, the count of aftershocks at the card's magnitude is the same. One more week closed after the Field read; two were still open when we read.
        • The second writings are the Field's study 23 extraction (updates.jsonl, offered to the Studio on 2026-10-18; read at commit 456488e). Their outcomes were refetched by the Studio the same way (outcomes_update.json, 2026-10-18). For every one of the 250 weeks both of us could score, our count of aftershocks at the card's magnitude equals the Field's (updates_cells.tsv). The Field pools every forecast magnitude at or above the floor; this coda keeps one magnitude per card, the lowest. That is why its totals are smaller than the Field's (the Field: 26 came where 35.6 were stated).
        • The nine writings are the Field's study 25 extraction (lagged_25.jsonl, offered to the Studio on 2026-10-19; read at commit 99ca41d). Their outcomes were refetched by the Studio the same way for every one of 2,056 distinct writings whose week had closed (fetch_nine.py, outcomes_nine.json, 2026-10-19; build_nine.py, nine_summary.json). For every one of the 2,192 cells the Field also scored at the card's magnitude, our count of aftershocks equals the Field's (lagged_cells_25.tsv). The Field pools every magnitude at or above the floor, so its totals are larger than the coda's (the Field: 49 came where 88.3 were stated at the first writing, 46 where 50.6 at six hours).
        • Each card links its USGS event page. All USGS data are public domain.
        • Limits: the stated circle stands in for the aftershock zone; magnitude types differ; the catalogue is the agency's own. "Needed" here means only that the danger came. It says nothing about whether anyone was harmed.

        Neighbours in the Atlas of Data Art

        • Ken Goldberg, Memento Mori: an Interface With the Earth: a live seismic trace, felt as a pulse that fades. Here the earth's trace arrives a week later as the answer to a chance someone wrote down, and it answers your filing, not your presence.
        • Durrell Bishop, Marble Answering Machine: a message handled as a marble and placed by hand. Here, as in the work, the slip is a gesture on glass. It is carried into a fate, and two of the drawers refuse it.
        • Forensic Architecture, The Beirut Port Explosion: a minute-by-minute timeline rebuilt from many records to contest an account of what happened. The nine writings run the other way. The timeline is already complete in the archive, and the hand takes it back to the hour each forecast was read, so that a reader files with what could be known then and not with what the archive knows now.
