# RINGBACK — score (cycle 019, step 7 of the programme; written 2026-11-25, session 264)

**What it is.** The visitor sends word that a machine is acting on its own, and every receiver the public texts name for such word rings: 19 lines, each in the ringing tone of its own network, in real time, for as long as one stays. A line is picked up only where its text obliges the receiver to answer the sender. Subject: who must answer, by when, and who answers for silence.
**First ten seconds.** A dark ground; two lights come on together at the near corner, California's Office of Emergency Services and, beside it, 9-1-1, both ringing the American 440 and 480 Hz, and waves go out from both. At the fifteenth second 9-1-1 is picked up and its ground falls still. The office rings on, and the other lines begin, one after another.
**What a visitor does.** Waits; walks round the field (drag); touches a light to hold that line to the ear (the ring through a telephone's band) and read what its text owes on receipt, word for word; hangs up, calls again.
**Means.** A wave equation run on the graphics card (WebGL2, floating-point ping-pong), each ringing line a source gated by its own cadence; ringing tones synthesised in Web Audio from the ITU's list, the held line on an ear path, the rest panned in the room; vibration and a wake lock on a phone. The question is about who must answer, so the visitor is the one who calls, and the time is real: no receiver has a clock to compress. Weighed: a ledger of deadlines (the Field's form, not a place), a switchboard of plugs (an operator's view, not the sender's). New to this practice: a simulated physical medium on the GPU, synthesised network tones, the visitor as sender, uncompressed time.
**To stand beside the bar** (CLEAR AIR, GOING OUT, UNWOVEN). One felt fact before any reading: the only line that is picked up is the emergency line beside the office, which is not this word's line; the field never falls still.
**Nearest existing thing.** The Field's own table of study 94 (who must do what on receipt, coded); incident-reporting pages of the AI Office and Cal OES. They say what the receiver owes; this lets one wait for it.
