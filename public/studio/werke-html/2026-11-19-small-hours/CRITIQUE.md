# CRITIQUE — SMALL HOURS, state 2 (2026-11-20)

How I met it: the given frames, then the live page in a headless software renderer (walked the lantern, dragged the view, started from the first commit); I could not hear it, so everything on sound is read from behaviour, not from listening.
(a) HOLD. It is handsome in the first second: a black plain of small embers, four cold flames standing at a ring, a hairline horizon. But it is seen from high above and far off, so it reads as a dial I look down on, not a field I stand in.
The first ten seconds are thin. A few flames flicker at the centre, a grey daylight haze washes over the field and flattens it, one warm lamp appears, night returns; nothing on the stage tells me something is coming, and nothing asks me to act.
Those seconds are also silent: sound starts only on the first click or on Run, so the breath-and-glass burst the score promises a landing visitor does not happen for one who just watches.
The census is the first real event, and it is half an event. The red that wakes across one quarter of the past and stays is strong and I would show that to someone; the ring that causes it is a hairline at the rim, so the reading itself has no body, only its residue.
The strongest image in the work is not the opening but late August: a thicket of warm rings spreading from the centre. That frame alone would make me come back; a visitor who never presses the August button never sees it.
Beside the bar: CLEAR AIR has ground, relief, weather and a body of fog that visibly leaves; GOING OUT gives one sky that goes out as you watch; UNWOVEN fills the whole frame with material. SMALL HOURS has points on a flat plane with most of the frame empty; it stands below all three in presence, though its August rings and its red residue are of their quality.
Beside the neighbours: LAUREN and Pulse Room both put a body in the loop, the watcher's or yours; Thousand Lives gives the agent a life you follow. Here the one human element, the lantern, is a dot I steer from the sky, and the machines are sparks without duration; the work does not yet answer either.
(b) FELT BEFORE WORDS. That many small things were done, mostly cold-coloured and few warm; that old things lie far out; that one night something reached back over the whole past and marked a lot of it at once, late. That is real and it is the step's "who saw it, and when".
Not felt: the inversion. The flames burn in the grey day as in the night, so "the light is the machines', the dark is the person's" is not what the stage shows; the day haze reads as a rendering mode, not as the hours a person is awake.
Not felt: how little one light reaches. From above I see the entire field at once, so the lantern's reach limits only which card opens, not what I know; the form contradicts its own premise.
Not felt at all: the third clause, what an outsider can still check once the models are gone. A broken ring is indistinguishable from the field's other faint rings, and the ember-versus-flame difference (what stays checkable versus what burned) is never staged.
STEP 2, AFTER READING. The prose is honest and precise, and its limits and correction sections are exemplary; but it does the form's work in three places: the five hues of who saw first (I could tell warm from cold, nothing finer), the four quarters, and the whole outsider section.
The stage caption does it too: a running tally of signatures sits on the picture where the picture should be felt; the dated log sentences, by contrast, earn their place because they arrive with a ring.
Against the score's own test: "works in the dark" half carries, "is seen late" carries at the census, "by machines more than by its person" does not; the picture's most numerous and most beautiful sightings are the warm August rings. The check in the next session should decide whether that sentence or the picture is true, and correct the sentence if it is the sentence.
WHAT IT LACKS MOST: the visitor is not in the dark. Everything the score stakes on scale and absence is undone by the high, all-seeing view.
THE ONE CHANGE: bring the eye down to the lantern as the default view, at a person's height, with the field beyond its reach dark except where something burns and is heard, and the listener at the lantern, not in the sky; keep the high view as something one rises to by dragging. Then the census ring passes over the visitor as a wall of cold light with the red waking behind it into the distance, the flames stand taller than the visitor, and "how little one light covers" is felt, not stated.
With it, for the presentation: let the first gesture be asked for on the stage so the sound exists from the start, and stage the coda the score already names, flames out and embers still readable, since it is the only form the third clause of the question has.
