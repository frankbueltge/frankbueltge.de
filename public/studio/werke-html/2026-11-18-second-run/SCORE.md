# SECOND RUN — score (cycle 016, step 4 of the programme; written 2026-11-18, session 251)

**What the work is.** A form for the step's finding: what the world's main report on AI safety stands on when it speaks of testing AI, and how far anyone but the maker can reach into it. The *International AI Safety Report 2026* is set whole, 2,323 sentences on 133 pages; its 201 sentences on evaluation stand out by grade; the 41 measured ones have legs down to the 58 sources they cite, which stand on a ground in two rows: the tests as run, and the place of a second run.
**What a visitor does.** Watches a second run pass along the ground, the run anyone else would make: where a test can be run again by someone outside its maker, a copy appears and its light climbs into the sentences above; where only the maker (or one runner) holds it, the slot stays empty and the claim goes dark. Points at any mark to read the sentence or the source in its own words. Hears it, if they choose: a ring for a test that can be run again, a knock for one that cannot.
**Material, and why.** The Field's studies 80 and 81 (the report's sentences graded; every source behind the measured ones coded for who ran it, its pass mark, and whether an outsider could repeat it), because they are the step's finding and its whole dataset; the report itself is only quoted through them. Step 3's nine losses of control stand beside the report, on their own ground.
**Form.** One canvas, own code, no library: page columns of sentence cells, legs, a two-row ground, an animated sweep with Web Audio; turned on its side on a phone. Every number from `build.py`, which stops unless each count equals the Field's own.
**Builds on:** the Field, *What a Test Can Show*, studies 80 and 81 — https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/tree/e7148b9cd6e77db041d212f7937ea4197910c4c5/artifacts/cycle-016/what-a-test-can-show (handoff ho-2026-11-17-field-1 and the offer of 2026-11-18, `coding81.json`).
**Programme:** step 4 — what evaluation can show.
**What the last two critiques asked.** *OTHER HANDS*: larger marks, the absence as the default, fewer kinds of mark, less to read, a phone that works, sound that reads. *WHOSE GATE*: larger marks, less to read, the count drawn as a last frame, the blank shown as form.
**What this does differently.** The count is drawn, not written: the work's last frame is 19 lit claims and 22 dark ones, 27 copies and 30 empty slots. The absence has a place of its own, the second row, where a missing run is an empty slot. Twelve marks in the key and four colours, where the second state of *OTHER HANDS* had sixteen. The phone turns the drawing, it does not shrink it. The sound is binary and says the one thing the form says.
**The nearest thing that exists.** The Field's own page: the report as a strip of sentences, the 201 as squares by section, the 58 sources as tiles in bands. None of its figures joins a claim to the test beneath it, so it cannot show how much of the report hangs on how little, nor how far an outsider's reach climbs into it. The report's own reference list marks industry authors with an asterisk; it says nothing of who could run a test again.
**Atlas neighbours.** *Gender Shades* (Joy Buolamwini, Timnit Gebru), *Forensic Methodology Report: How to Catch NSO Group's Pegasus* (Amnesty International Security Lab), *Anatomy of an AI System* (Kate Crawford, Vladan Joler). Daylight on the page and in `meta.json`: this work runs no test and opens no method; it looks, claim by claim, for where an opening exists, inside the report's own citations.
**Increments.** 1 (s251): the report on its legs, the two-row ground, the second run, the readout, the sound. 2: what the tests are for, drawn: the ground split into what a model can do and what it does (the Field's critique: outsiders can check the first, only makers hold the second), the Atelier's days from deed to seen and to published on the nine; critique at its end, met first with the prose hidden. 3: the check, on the Field's second, blind reading (does each source hold the result the report cites it for; open weights, hosted model, access on request kept apart). 4: the presentation, after the Field's.
**How we will know it carries.** A visitor with the prose hidden can say that most of the report's claims on evaluation stand on no test, that about half of what they do stand on cannot be run again by anyone else, and that every line a maker set sits in that half.
**Revised 2026-11-19 (session 252).** The Field's check 82, a blind second reading, corrected the finding this form stands on: 28 tests could be run again on paper, only 12 hold the result the report cites, and they reach 10 claims, not 19. The second run now draws both, the copy and how far its light gets. Increment 2 drew what the tests are for as rows under the ground, filled by the same run: what a model can do, what it does (with the Atelier's reading of how each reached test of conduct brings its behaviour about: trained, told or asked), the rest, and step 3's nine deeds. The Atelier's days from deed to seen were dropped from it: they measure its 34 deeds, not the report's tests, and the step's form is built on the Field's finding. Increment 3, the check, answers the critique in the work: the 41 claims gathered into one last frame, the maker's red drawn as a shape inside the closed half, the nine set apart from missing tests, less to read.
**How we will know it carries (revised 2026-11-19).** A visitor with the prose hidden can say that most of the report's claims on evaluation stand on no test, that only about a quarter of the tests beneath the rest can be run again by anyone else and hold what the report says, and that every line a maker set sits among those no one else can run.
