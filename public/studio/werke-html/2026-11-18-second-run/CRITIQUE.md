# SECOND RUN — critique (end of session 252, 2026-11-19)

*By a convened critic who did not build the work. It met the page first with all prose hidden (title, buttons, the drawings and the drawn key only), mid-run and at the end, at 1100 and 390 px, then read the score and the whole page. Condensed from its report by the conductor; nothing softened. It judged the state before one repair made after it: the key was cut off at the right edge on a phone. It read the score's last line, written before the Field's correction, as "19 lit, 22 dark"; the drawing now lights 10.*

**Prose hidden.** A large body of tiny grey sentence cells with a few dozen black or gold stems dropping out of it and fanning onto a narrow row of small squares: a lot of text rests on few foundations, most of what hangs there stays dark, gold means "reaches". The mid-run frame helps: the gold climbs and the lower row fills. The rows below read as a count (5 of 28, 6 of 16, 1 of 5, 0 of 9). Not worked out without text: the subject (nothing names AI or evaluation), what "a second run" is, why two rows of squares stand under the tests, solid against hollow gold, the red marks, the "×2", the trained/told/asked brackets, and what "step 3's nine" is. Only the tally's squares (about 20 px) are big enough to read; the ground's are about 11 px, the sentence cells illegible.

**Does it carry: partly.** "Most of what hangs above rests on thin ground" and "the rows are mostly empty" carry. That about half the tests cannot be run again by anyone else, and that every line a maker set sits in that half, does not: black and red squares are scattered through the ground, and no shape shows red falling inside black.

**What it lacks.**
1. The fans are the loudest thing and say "many on few", not "most cannot be run again"; gold and grey legs weigh the same, and a dark claim looks like a claim with no test.
2. No last frame of its own: the 41 measured claims are never gathered into one block of lit and dark; "10 of 41" is nowhere in the picture.
3. The correction is a prose box and a faint solid/hollow difference at 11 px, which is the whole difference between 28 and 12.
4. "What the tests are for" is met only as row labels; nothing draws that outsiders reach "can" and only makers hold "does". The trained/told/asked words are 9 to 11 px and need prose. The empty last row gives nothing to look at.
5. Red does three jobs (the maker ran it, set the line, the nine), and the nine incidents share the box of a missing test.
6. Still mostly prose: some 800 words under the figure, a twelve-item key at 11 px below it.
7. The order, copy first and light after, is hard to follow mid-run; the buttons say little.

**Against the neighbours.** *Gender Shades* has one legible gap and a table read in ten seconds; this has no single image. Against *Anatomy of an AI System* it is a denser and less readable map. The daylight, looking claim by claim for where an opening exists, holds as an idea, not yet as a form: with prose hidden it says "thin ground", not "who can reach it".
