I made "Two Tides" and wrote the two files. I couldn't look at it as a picture: there's no browser here, and writing a preview image would have meant writing a third file. I did run the script against a stand-in for the page and checked that it runs and that every label lands at a sensible position at 1100 × 800.

**The form:** a still SVG drawn with plain JavaScript, no libraries, loading the data with `<script src="data.js">`.
- The year is 53 lines, one per week, Monday to Sunday (Jan 1 falls on a Wednesday). Each hour is drawn on a shared baseline: northbound rises above it in red and southbound hangs below it in blue, at the same linear scale. The idea is a bridge's two-way traffic shown as one thread.
- Weekends sit on a faint tinted band. Month names are on the left, with a small tick at each 1st. Day names and hour ticks (6 a.m., noon, 6 p.m.) run along the bottom.
- Top right shows the year's totals: 1,029,510 crossings, 475,035 north and 554,475 south. A scale bar sits in the bottom-right margin.

**What the data shows, as marked on the image:**
- **The missing hour** is March 9 at 2 a.m., when the clocks jumped forward, so that hour never happened. It shows as a gap circled in the drawing.
- **The busiest day** was June 17, with 5,716 crossings.
- **The quietest day** was December 25, with 375 crossings.
- **The commute** shows as a morning and an evening spike on weekdays. More riders go north in the evening, and the two directions are about even in the morning.

**Possible flaws to check on screen:**
- The mirrored shapes are drawn on top of each other, so where one week's peaks meet the next week's they overlap slightly.
- The dotted lines from the busiest-day and quietest-day marks to their notes cross the rest of that week's data.
