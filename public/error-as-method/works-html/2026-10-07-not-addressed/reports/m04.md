I made a still piece called "Two Tides" and wrote both files in the folder. I didn't see it rendered: saving a screenshot would have meant writing a file other than the two allowed. So I checked it another way. I ran the page's script against a fake drawing surface, which showed it runs without errors and puts every label where intended. I also worked out from the data that no label overlaps the bars.

**The form:** One horizontal line stands for the bridge deck, drawn on a dark night-blue background at 1100 × 800. The year runs along the line, one thin column per day for all 365 days. Northbound crossings rise above the line and southbound crossings hang below it, like the bridge and its reflection in the water. Each column is the day's 24 hours stacked outward from the line, coloured by the light of that hour:
- indigo for night
- rose at dawn
- pale gold for the morning commute
- a pale sea-green through midday
- vermilion for the evening commute
- violet at dusk

**Everything else on the image:**
- month marks along the line
- faint guide lines at 1,000, 2,000 and 3,000 crossings a day
- an hour colour key and a data credit
- a note on the busiest hour of the year: 6 May, 5 p.m., 856 crossings
- a note and small hollow circle on the one missing hour, 9 March at 2 a.m., when the clocks went forward
- the title, with the year's total of 1,029,510 crossings

**Why this form:** Averaged by hour, the two directions turned out almost identical. Both peak around 8 a.m. and 5 p.m., so the mirrored layout shows two nearly symmetric streams. Stacking by hour keeps the whole year visible at once: the weekday rhythm with weekend dips, the summer swell and the thin winter. Everything is drawn with plain JavaScript on a canvas, with no animation or interaction.
