I made "Two Tides": a still canvas image, 1100 × 800, that lays out every hour of 2025 as a grid. Each of the 365 columns is a day and each of the 24 rows is an hour of the clock. A cell's colour shows which way people were travelling (amber for northbound, blue for southbound, pale when the two are about even). Its brightness shows how many bicycles and scooters crossed. I rendered it with headless Playwright to check it.

**What the grid shows:**
- **Commute bands:** a bright band in the morning and another in the evening, broken up by darker weekend columns.
- **Seasons:** traffic swells in summer and shrinks in winter.
- **Daylight:** faint dotted lines mark sunrise and sunset in Seattle, in local clock time. They are calculated from the city's latitude, not taken from the data.
- **Missing hour:** the empty hour is 9 March at 2 am, the hour skipped by the clock change. It is left empty and outlined.
- **Direction labels flip on 1 July:** the evening crowd, which had been counted as mostly northbound, is counted as mostly southbound from that day on. For example, the 4–7 pm counts go from 1192 north / 468 south on 30 June to 243 north / 876 south on 1 July. Riders don't turn around overnight, so the counter most likely swapped how it labels its two lanes, but that's my reading — the data doesn't say why. The image marks the date at the top and explains it in a footer line, and `NOTE.md` covers it too.

The total for the year is 1,029,510 crossings.
