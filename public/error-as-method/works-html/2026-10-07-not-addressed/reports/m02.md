I made "Fremont, Woven": a single still canvas image at 1100 × 800 that draws the year of Fremont Bridge counts as a woven cloth. The drawing script ran without errors against a stand-in canvas, but there was no browser available, so I never saw it rendered. The spacing of labels and annotations is worked out by hand and still needs a look in a real browser.

**How it reads**
- Each of the 365 days is a vertical thread, read downward from midnight to 11 p.m.
- Each hour is a knot on that thread. Its length shows the total crossings (square-root scale, against the year's peak of 856 in one hour).
- Its colour shows which way most riders went: amber for mostly northbound, blue for mostly southbound, pale in between.
- Dark background, serif type, month labels along the bottom, a few hour labels on the left (8 a.m. and 5 p.m. stand out), and a small colour key. The total for the year is 1,029,510 crossings.

**What the data turned up**
- **The missing hour** is 9 March at 2 a.m., the hour the clocks went forward. It is left as an empty gap with a circled note.
- **The directions swap at midnight on 1 July.** Before then the counter records about 65% of riders as northbound; from then on it is about 30%, with morning and evening also reversing. So the cloth changes from amber to blue at that point, and I marked it with a dashed seam.
- **My first caption was wrong.** I had written that the commute was blue going in and amber coming home, but over the whole year the two directions are almost even at commute hours. I replaced it before finishing.
- **The new caption** says only that the numbers alone can't tell whether the riders or the sensor turned around. I don't know which it was.
