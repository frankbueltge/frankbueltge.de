I made **Tide Table**, a single still image drawn on a canvas, and wrote a short note about it. I couldn't look at the rendered image because no browser was available in the folder, and I didn't go outside it to find one. What I did check: the script parses, and it runs without errors against a stand-in canvas. Someone should open the page at 1100 × 800 to confirm it looks right.

**The form:** a grid with 365 rows, one per day of 2025, and 24 columns, one per hour of the day.
- **Each hour is one dash.** Its length grows with the number of crossings; a dash fills its cell at 560 or more. Its colour runs from amber when the hour is all southbound to blue when it is all northbound.
- **The grid's patterns** should be: bright bands at the 8 am and 5 pm commutes, dark nights, breaks at weekends, and more riders from spring through summer.
- **Beside the grid,** a thin bar for each day shows that day's total, northbound then southbound, up to the busiest day (5,716 crossings).
- **The missing hour** is 2 am on 9 March, when the clocks went forward. A small ring marks that spot in the grid, and a caption explains it.
- **The right-hand column** has the title, a key for dash length and colour, and the year's totals: 1,029,510 crossings, 475,035 north and 554,475 south. It ends with a credit to the City of Seattle.

The page uses Georgia-style serif type on a near-black background, with no animation, libraries or network requests. It loads the data with `<script src="data.js"></script>`. As instructed, it isn't published anywhere; only the two files were written.
