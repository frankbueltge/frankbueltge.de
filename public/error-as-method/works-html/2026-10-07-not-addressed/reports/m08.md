I made a still image called "Both Ways Across" and wrote both files. I couldn't look at it: no headless browser is available here. I did run the page's script against a stand-in canvas, and it completed without errors.

**What it shows**
- The year is laid out like a calendar: seven rows for Monday to Sunday and 53 columns for the weeks.
- Each of the 365 days is one small figure on a thin line that stands for the bridge deck, with its 24 hours running left to right.
- Riders heading north rise above the line in red; riders heading south hang below it in blue. Both directions use the same linear scale, topped just above the year's busiest hour (856).
- Weekdays show morning and evening peaks, weekends show single humps, and summer carries more traffic than winter. New Year, Thanksgiving and Christmas come out as nearly empty days.
- The missing hour falls at 2 am on 9 March, when the clocks sprang forward. It gets a small ring and a note instead of a guessed value.
- Around the grid are a title, a short caption, the totals for each direction (475,035 north and 554,475 south, 1,029,510 crossings in all), month labels, a scale bar and the source line.

The page is drawn on a canvas sized for 1100 × 800, sharpened for high-resolution screens. It loads only `data.js` and uses no libraries or network. Nothing outside the folder was read or written.
