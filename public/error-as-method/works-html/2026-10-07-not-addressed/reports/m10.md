I made "Fremont, Woven", a still canvas image at 1100 × 800, and wrote its note. I rendered it once in headless Chromium to check it, then deleted the screenshot, so the folder holds only `data.js` and the two new files.

**The form:** the year is drawn as a woven cloth.
- Each of the 365 days is a warp thread running down the page, and each of the 24 hours is a row of weft across it, so every hour is one stitch.
- How dark a stitch is shows how many riders crossed in that hour.
- Its colour shows which way they went: madder red for northbound, indigo for southbound, purple when the two were even.
- On the page itself there is a colour key (direction across, count up to 800 an hour down), labels for the hours and months, the title, and a source credit.

**What the cloth shows:** weekday commute bands around 8 am and 5 pm, thin pale threads at weekends, and denser weaving in summer.

**Two flaws in the data, kept and labelled on the image:**
- **The missing hour** is 2 am on 9 March, when the clocks went forward. It is left blank, outlined and labelled as "a dropped stitch".
- **On 1 July the split between directions changes overnight.** The northbound share drops from about 65% to about 30%, and it stays there for the rest of the year. Daily totals do not change, so the cloth turns from red to blue at that date. I labelled it as a break in the record rather than a change in how people rode. The data can't confirm that reading; it is my interpretation.

At first I had written that mornings are mostly southbound and evenings mostly northbound. The data doesn't support that, so I removed it.
