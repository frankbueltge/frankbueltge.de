I made "Two Tides", a single still image drawn on a canvas in `index.html` with plain JavaScript. It loads `data.js` with a script tag and uses no other files, libraries or network. I opened it in a headless browser at 1100 × 800: it draws with no errors, and labels, notes and legend all fit without overlapping each other. I took the screenshot inside the folder and deleted it, so only the two requested files were added.

**The form:** the year is folded into 53 rows, one per week, each running Monday to Sunday hour by hour. Every row is a thin line with the northbound count rising above it in red and the southbound count hanging below it in blue. The inks overprint, so overlapping weeks darken where they cross. Because the same hour sits in the same column every week, the morning and evening commutes show as dark seams down the weekdays. Weekends look looser, and the year peaks in summer and thins toward December. Week labels run across the top, month labels down the left, and a footer gives the legend and the total of 1,029,510 crossings.

**Two notes in the right margin:**
- **The missing hour:** the one `null` is 9 March at 2 a.m., when the clocks went forward. It is left blank and circled.
- **A switch in direction on 1 July:** northbound is about 65% of each week's traffic until 30 June. From 1 July it drops to about 30% and stays there for the rest of the year. The data doesn't say why; it may be a change in how the counter labels directions rather than a real change. The note says so ("The counter does not say why").

`NOTE.md` has the title as a heading and one paragraph on what the form does and why I chose it.
