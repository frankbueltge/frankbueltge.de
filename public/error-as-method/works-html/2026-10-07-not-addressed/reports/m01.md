I made "Crossing", a still image of the bridge counts. The script runs without errors in Node with a stand-in canvas, but I had no browser here, so I haven't seen the rendered picture. The layout and label positions are worked out by hand and not checked by eye.

**The form:** the year is drawn as a cloth on one 1100 × 800 canvas, with one column per day (365) and one row per hour (24). Each hour is one small mark. Northbound riders are a blue bar rising above the row's thin centre line, and southbound riders are an amber bar hanging below it. Bar length uses a square-root scale, and busier hours are drawn more opaque. The pattern shows weekday peaks at 8 am and 5 pm, weekends with a single midday swell instead, a dimmer winter and a fuller summer.

**Labels on the image:**
- Title and a one-line subtitle, a small legend, hour labels down the left and month labels along the bottom.
- The one missing hour is outlined and labelled. It is 9 March at 2 am, the hour skipped when the clocks went forward.
- The busiest hour is labelled: 6 May at 5 pm, with 856 crossings.
- A footer gives the year total (1,029,510 crossings in 8,759 counted hours) and credits the City of Seattle as the data source.

**Things to check when you view it:**
- **Busiest-hour label:** its leader line runs from 5 pm down to the text below the grid, so it crosses the late-evening rows.
- **Top of the page:** the missing-hour note sits between the subtitle and the grid, and space there is tight.

**Data:** there are exactly 8,760 entries and the only gap is the spring clock change. The extra hour in November seems to have been folded into the data, so I placed every entry by its position in the list.
