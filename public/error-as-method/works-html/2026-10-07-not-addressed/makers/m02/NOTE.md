# Fremont, Woven

The year is laid out as a piece of cloth. Each of the 365 days is a vertical thread, read downward from midnight, and each hour is a knot on it. A knot's length shows how many people crossed, and its colour shows which way most of them went: amber for north, river-blue for south. I chose weaving because the data already behaves like a textile. The daily commute lays two bright weft lines across every weekday, weekends open small regular gaps, and summer thickens the fabric while winter wears it thin. The form also keeps two faults visible instead of smoothing them over. One is a single dropped stitch on 9 March, the hour the clocks skipped. The other is a seam at midnight on 1 July, where the cloth turns from amber to blue because the counter's northbound and southbound totals suddenly swap. The data alone cannot say whether the riders or the sensor turned around.
