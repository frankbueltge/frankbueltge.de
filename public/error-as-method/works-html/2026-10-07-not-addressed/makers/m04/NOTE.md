# Two Tides

A single horizontal line stands for the bridge deck, and the year runs along it with one thin column per day: northbound crossings rise above the deck and southbound crossings hang below, like a span and its reflection in the water. Each column is built from that day's 24 hours, stacked outward from the deck and coloured by the light of the hour (indigo night, pale-gold morning commute, vermilion evening commute, violet dusk). Every hour gets its own band, so the whole year stays visible at once: the steady weekday pulse with weekend notches, the summer swell, the winter thinning, and the two commute colours that appear in both directions. I chose the mirrored form because the counter sees two streams that are nearly mirror images of each other, and because the bridge itself suggests a line with something above and below it. Two small notes mark the data's own oddities: the busiest single hour (6 May, 5 p.m.) and the one missing hour, 2 a.m. on 9 March, which never happened because the clocks went forward.
