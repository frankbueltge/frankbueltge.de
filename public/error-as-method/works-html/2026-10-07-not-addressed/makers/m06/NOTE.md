# Orchard

The year is planted as twelve rows of trees, one row per month and one tree per day. Each tree's crown is that day's 24 hours laid around a clock. Midnight sits at the root, 6 am points left, noon points straight up and 6 pm points right. The crown reaches farther in each direction as more riders crossed in that hour, scaled to the square root of the count. Southbound riders, heading toward downtown, make a green canopy. Northbound riders, heading toward Fremont, make a rose-colored one. The two canopies overlap, and wherever one direction carried more riders, its color shows at the edge. I chose an orchard because the counter's rhythm already looks like pruning. A workday splits into an open vase, with one limb for the morning commute and one for the evening return. A weekend grows a single round head around midday. The seasons show up as growth and thinning down the rows, from small winter trees to thick summer canopies, so the whole year can be read as a planted field.
