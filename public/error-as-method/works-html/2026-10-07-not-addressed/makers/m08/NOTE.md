# Both Ways Across

The year is laid out like a calendar, with weekdays as rows and weeks as columns, and each of the 365 days is drawn as a small mirrored figure on a thin line that stands for the bridge deck. Its 24 hours run left to right. Riders heading north rise above the line in red and riders heading south hang below it in blue, so every day looks like a bridge and its reflection. I chose this form because the counter records one thing over and over: people crossing in two directions on a weekly rhythm. Small repeated figures let that rhythm show without any reading. Weekdays come out as twin morning and evening spikes, weekends as single soft humps, summer swells, winter thins, and holidays such as New Year, Thanksgiving and Christmas look nearly empty. The one missing hour, lost when the clocks sprang forward in March, is marked rather than filled in.
