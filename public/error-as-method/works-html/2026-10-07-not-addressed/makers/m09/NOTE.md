# Two Tides

The year is folded into 53 lines, one per week, each running Monday to Sunday hour by hour, so the same hour of every week stacks into a column. On each line, northbound riders rise above it in red ink and southbound riders hang below it in blue, printed in overprint so that where weeks overlap the colours darken like wet ink. I chose this form because the bridge moves the way a tide does: people cross one way in the morning and back in the evening, five days a week. Folding by week turns that daily rhythm into dark vertical seams down the weekdays, loose and scattered weekends, a crest in summer and thinning toward December. The one missing hour, when the clocks went forward on 9 March, is left blank and circled. The form also shows something the numbers do not explain: on 1 July the heavier tide switches overnight from north to south and stays there for the rest of the year.
