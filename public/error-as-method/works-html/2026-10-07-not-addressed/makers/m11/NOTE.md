# Tide Table

The year is set down like a tide table: 365 rows of days, one under the next, and 24 columns of hours. Every hour of crossings is a single short dash. Its length grows with the number of riders, and its colour moves from amber when the flow is all southbound to blue when it is all northbound. Read together, the dashes show the bridge's rhythm the way a printed table shows the sea: two bright currents at 8 am and 5 pm, darker night between them, the weekly gaps of Saturdays and Sundays, and a wide swell from spring into summer that falls back by December. A thin bar for each day runs beside the grid, northbound then southbound, so the season can be read in one glance. A small ring marks the one empty place, 2 am on 9 March, the hour the clocks skipped. I chose this form because the counter records something regular, people going and coming back, and a dense, even grid lets that regularity and its breaks show without any further argument.
