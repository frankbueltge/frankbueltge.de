# Two Tides

The year is laid out as a woven field: one column per day, one row per hour of the clock, so 8,760 cells show the whole of 2025 at once. Each cell's colour comes from the direction of travel (amber for northbound, blue for southbound, pale where the two are even) and its brightness from how many bicycles and scooters crossed. I chose this form because the bridge's story is a daily rhythm repeated 365 times. Reading across shows the morning and evening commute bands, the dark gaps of weekends, and the season widening and narrowing, with faint dotted threads for sunrise and sunset. Reading by colour shows something the counts alone hide: on 1 July the evening tide stops reading as northbound and starts reading as southbound. The riders did not turn around. The counter changed how it labels its lanes. The one missing hour, the 2 am that daylight saving skipped, is left empty and outlined rather than filled in.
