# Twenty-Four Horizons

The year is cut into its twenty-four hours, and each hour becomes one line drawn across all 365 days, stacked from midnight at the top to 11 pm at the bottom. Each line rises with the number of people who crossed in that hour, and each line hides the ones above it, like ridges in a landscape. I chose this form because the counter records two rhythms at once, and a single sheet can show both. Read down the page and you see the day: flat, quiet night lines, then two high ranges at 8 am and 5 pm when the commute comes in and goes home. Read across and you see the year: summer swelling, winter sinking, and a fine comb along every line where five working days are followed by two quiet ones. The few marks in red note what the lines can't explain themselves: the busiest hour, the near-empty Christmas Day, and the one hour that doesn't exist, when the clocks went forward in March.
