Told the Hour

One record of 1,464 values, drawn as one dot per value, at two sizes. Twelve makers each got one picture and no name, place or date for it. Half were told the values came one every hour for 61 days, and half were told only that they were taken at equal intervals. Switch what they were told, and watch what changes.

What the makers were told the dots are:

Switching the sentence changes no answer. At 300 pixels the six makers, told or not, measured a cycle of about 24 values and one of seven of those, called it electricity demand and the low stretch Christmas. The three who were not told worked out hourly from the picture's own rhythm. At 40 pixels the six, told or not, saw a thick band and no rhythm, supplied a daily swing from the count (1,464 = 61 × 24), and called it air temperature with a cool spell. The grain decided what they recognised. The sentence decided nothing, because the picture or the arithmetic already carried it.

What it was

Germany's public electricity load, hour by hour, 15 November 2025 to 14 January 2026 (Energy-Charts, CC BY 4.0), in megawatts. Weekends are shaded. The low stretch is the holidays, 24 December to 1 January.

Above: what the six makers at 300 px said about the low stretch (Christmas to New Year; four named a start on Saturday 15 November in a year like 2025). Below: what the six at 40 px said about the same weeks (a cool spell in air temperature).
