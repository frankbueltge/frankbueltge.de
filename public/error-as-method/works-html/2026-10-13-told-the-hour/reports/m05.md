I made "Thirty-Six and a Half", a still canvas piece, in `/tmp/claude-0/s123/mk/m05/work/index.html`, with its title and note in `/tmp/claude-0/s123/mk/m05/work/NOTE.md`. Every one of the 1,464 values gets its own hairline, but none gets a height, because the picture no longer holds them. The page doesn't load any other file. No browser was available here, so I have not seen the real page. I checked only that its JavaScript parses and drew a rough copy with Python to check the look.

**First step.** I read the JPEG's 40 × 12 greyscale pixel values as numbers. That showed the main constraint: about 36.6 values share each column, and their heights survive only as 12 rows of ink, blurred further by JPEG compression. The individual values cannot be recovered from this file.

**From the file to the form.** I didn't want to invent 1,464 heights. What the file does keep at full resolution is the count and the order of the values.
- **Hairlines:** each value is a thin vertical line at its exact position in time, across a 1000 × 600 field.
- **Shading:** each line is shaded along its full length by the ink of the source column it falls in. The 480 pixel values are embedded in the page. Within a column I blend smoothly between the 12 row centres. Across time I left the column steps hard, because that is the record's real time resolution.
- **Reading it:** from a distance it shows the record's shape; up close it is 1,464 placed but unmeasured values.
- **Extras:** there is a tick strip underneath, one tick per value with a longer tick where each column starts. Small marks on both sides show the 12 row boundaries, and there is a short caption.

**What the commissioner should know.**
- The work shows the resolution of the given image, not the data. Any detail finer than one column (about 36 values) or one row is gone.
- Each value's height is shown as the range the picture allows, never a guess.
- At 1,000 px for 1,464 values the hairlines are under a pixel apart. They will merge into tone rather than read as separate lines unless the screen is high-density.

**What I think was measured.** This is speculation.
- **Hourly over 61 days.** 1,464 = 24 × 61, which fits hourly readings over 61 days, for example two consecutive months such as August and September.
- **A daily swing.** Each column covers about 1.5 days, and the band in every column is wide and fairly evenly inked, about 7–8 of the 12 rows. That suggests a large, regular swing inside each column, such as a day/night cycle, rather than slow drift.
- **The shape.** The band holds steady for roughly the first half (columns 1–21). Its top then sinks for about a third of the record (columns 22–32) while the bottom stays put. In the last seven columns the whole band jumps up, with the highest ink in the top row.
- **My guess.** Hourly air temperature over two months: a run of cooler days, then a warm spell at the end.

I can't rule out other things with a daily cycle, such as electricity demand or water level. Nothing in the file distinguishes them.
