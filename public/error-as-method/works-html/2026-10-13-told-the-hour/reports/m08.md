I made "Thirty-Six and a Half Hours", a still SVG image that is fully inline. The files are /tmp/claude-0/s123/mk/m08/work/index.html and /tmp/claude-0/s123/mk/m08/work/NOTE.md. The generator script and a rough preview are in /tmp/claude-0/s123/mk/m08/scratch.

**What I did first with the file:** I read every pixel's grey value, all 40 × 12 of them. The most important fact is that 1,464 values were squeezed into 480 pixels. Each pixel column covers about 36.6 hours, and each row covers an unknown twelfth of the value range. None of the individual dots can be recovered. What is left is a grey band that is roughly 6 to 8 pixels tall, with smudges from the JPEG compression around it.

**How I got from that to the form:** I made the loss itself the subject.
- **The grid:** the 40 × 12 grid is enlarged, and each cell is faintly tinted with its real grey value.
- **Hours per column:** each column gets back its exact share of hours, 36 or 37.
- **Dots per cell:** those hours are split among the column's cells in proportion to how dark each cell is, with standard rounding that keeps the totals exact. The result is exactly 1,464 dots.
- **Placement inside a cell:** the dots are spaced evenly, not "placed", because their real position inside a pixel is exactly what is lost.
- **Hour ruler:** below the grid is a ruler of 1,464 hour ticks, with a longer tick every 24 hours (61 days). It is bracketed column by column, so you can see how many hours went into each pixel.
- **Text:** the only text is the title and the line "1,464 hours, filed into 480 pixels".

**Things the commissioner should know:**
- **Stray dots:** the dot counts are a redistribution of ink, not recovered data. A few stray single dots in pale cells come from the JPEG's compression smudges, and I left them in deliberately rather than cleaning them out.
- **No browser check:** I had no browser to render with. I checked the layout with a rough raster preview of the cells, dots and ticks; that preview leaves out the text. The page loads no external files, though the SVG namespace string does contain "http".

**My view of what was measured (speculative):** I think it could be a quantity with a daily cycle, plausibly air temperature during a change of season.
- **Daily cycle:** 36 hours per column always includes at least one full day, so the band's thickness probably reflects a daily swing rather than random scatter. It stays fairly constant for about the first 35 days.
- **A dip, then a rise:**
  - **Days 35–48 (columns 22–31):** the top of the band drops and the bottom reaches lower, so values were generally lower.
  - **Last ~10 days (columns 33–39):** the whole band jumps up, the lowest values rise sharply, and the band narrows. Higher minimums with a smaller daily range would fit a warm spell, for example.

Nothing in the file confirms any of this. It could just as well be tides, power demand or something else with a daily rhythm.
