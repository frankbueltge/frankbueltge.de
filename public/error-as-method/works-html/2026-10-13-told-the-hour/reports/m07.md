I made the work, "Thirty-Seven to a Column". It is in `/tmp/claude-0/s123/mk/m07/work/index.html`, with its note in `/tmp/claude-0/s123/mk/m07/work/NOTE.md`. Working files (`build.py`, `data.json`, `preview.png`) are in `/tmp/claude-0/s123/mk/m07/scratch`. Nothing was written anywhere else, and nothing outside the folder was looked up.

**What I did first with the file:** I printed all 480 grey values (40 × 12). That showed what the picture can and cannot hold. With 40 columns, each pixel column holds 36 or 37 of the 1,464 values (1464/40 = 36.6). The dots merge into a solid dark band about seven rows thick, so no single value can be read back. What survives is, for each column, how dark each of its 12 squares is: roughly how the values in that stretch of time were spread up and down.

**How I got to the form:** I treated each column's darkness as a rough distribution of where its values lie. I ignored faint JPEG noise near white (a threshold of 30 grey levels), then placed exactly 36 or 37 dots per column at evenly spaced quantiles of that darkness. The total is exactly 1,464, and more dots fall where the column is darker. The dots sit in a single vertical line per column because the order within a column is gone. Behind them, the original 40 × 12 picture is drawn very large and faded, so you see the record and the coarse grid it was squeezed into at the same time. It is a single still canvas at 1100 × 800 with the data written into the page, a short caption at the bottom, and no animation or outside resources.

**Things you should know:**
- The vertical position of each dot is a reconstruction from the greys, not a recovered measurement. The horizontal position is only "which 1/40th of the record". The form is meant to show that openly.
- JPEG compression slightly smears the edges of the band, so the extremes of each line are approximate to within about one pixel row.
- The first column's band starts unusually low. That comes straight from the pixels, not from a bug.
- I couldn't render the HTML in a browser here because none was installed. I checked the composition with a Python re-drawing of the same coordinates (`scratch/preview.png`), and the canvas code uses the same coordinates.

**My view of what was measured (speculative):** 1,464 = 61 × 24, which fits hourly readings over 61 days, two consecutive months such as June–July or November–December. The band stays thick and fairly even throughout, which suggests a quantity that swings widely and regularly over short periods, like a daily cycle in air temperature. Under it there is a slower drift: level for the first half, sinking to a low around 55–80% of the way through, then a jump to the highest values in the last eighth. That shape fits hourly air temperature over about two months with a cool spell and then a warm one. It rests only on the count of values and the band's shape, so the evidence is weak. It could just as well be any noisy hourly series, or values taken every six hours over a leap year (366 × 4 = 1,464).
