I made "Set in Weeks". It is a single still canvas at 1100×800 in `/tmp/claude-0/s123/mk/m11/work/index.html`, and it loads nothing from outside. The note is in `/tmp/claude-0/s123/mk/m11/work/NOTE.md`. Scripts, the template and a test screenshot are in `/tmp/claude-0/s123/mk/m11/scratch` (`gen.py` builds the page, `shot.png` shows how it renders).

**What I did first with the file:** I looked at the picture, then read it pixel by pixel and printed it as text. The dots run from column 2 to column 297 and from row 7 to row 79, so each pixel column covers about 4.9 hours. The order of the hours inside a column can't be recovered from the picture.

**How I got to the form:**
1. Measuring how the top and bottom edges of the dots repeat across the columns gave a strong 4.8–5 px rhythm, which is one day. The daily swing takes up about half the full height.
2. I then cut the strip into 61 days and summarised each day by its high and low points. In every seven-day stretch, days 0–1 are low and flatter and days 2–6 are high with a large daily swing.
3. The one exception is days 37–50: about two weeks in which the busy days are also quiet or only partly busy. After that the values come back and end higher than before, with the highest peaks in the record.

The form follows from this. The strip is cut into lengths of seven days and set as nine lines, like a ledger page, with the last line short (5 days). Every dark pixel from the original becomes one mark, at its true height and in its place within its week. The quiet pair of days at the start of each line has a faint shaded background, and the two quiet weeks show up in lines 6–7.

**Things the person who commissioned this should know:**
- The marks are the picture's pixels, not values I worked out. Each mark is stretched sideways into a short stroke because one pixel column stands for about five hours. I deliberately didn't make up hour-by-hour detail.
- The day lines assume the record starts at midnight. That assumption fits well, because the low days fall cleanly on days 0–1 of each week.
- The only text is the week numbers, the title and one line: "61 days · 1,464 hourly values · one line per week · higher is more". There are no weekday names, because which day the record starts on is my inference.

**My view of what was measured:** something driven by human activity, most likely electricity demand, for a building or a whole region. It could also be a similar activity measure such as traffic or occupancy. This rests on four things in the file:
- A strong daily cycle.
- A clean seven-day pattern of five busy days and two quiet days.
- A two-week stretch in which even the working days go quiet, which looks like a holiday period. Christmas to New Year is the obvious guess, but nothing in the file proves it.
- A rise in level towards the end, which would fit heating load in midwinter.

The night-time lows also drop on the quiet days, which fits demand better than a simple on/off count like traffic. The weekly rhythm is the observation I'm most sure of. The holiday reading and the season are guesses.
