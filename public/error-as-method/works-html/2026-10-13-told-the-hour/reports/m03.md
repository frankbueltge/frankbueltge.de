I made "Seven Nights to a Line". It cuts the record into one-week lengths and stacks them like lines of text, which makes a five-day/two-day rhythm visible and shows a flat stretch of about 12 days near the end.

**What I did first.** I looked at the picture enlarged and then dumped it pixel by pixel as text. The ink covers x≈3–297 and y≈8–80, with dots about 2 px across. That is roughly 5 values per pixel column. Dots that share a column overlap, so the 1,464 values cannot be pulled back out individually or in order.

**From the file to the form.**
- I scored candidate periods by folding the column-wise ink height at each one.
- **Daily period: 4.82 px**, which is about 24 values. The nightly low falls at x ≈ 2.65 (mod 4.82).
- **Weekly period: 33.74 px**, exactly 7 × 4.82. Within it, five raised cycles are followed by two lowered ones.
- I cut the strip at the nightly low that ends each pair of low days and stacked the pieces into 10 rows:
  - a first row holding only the opening two low days;
  - 8 full weeks;
  - a last row of 3 days.
- Each mark on the page is one pixel column of the original, stretched 28× sideways into short dashes and kept at its JPEG darkness. Nothing is interpolated, so the page shows only the detail the file holds.
- Faint vertical seams mark the record's own nightly lows, not clock midnights.
- The page is a self-contained canvas. The pixel data is embedded as a 0–9 darkness string, there are no external resources, and it uses system serif type.
- I checked it with a headless screenshot at 1100 × 800.

**Things the commissioner should know.**
- The weekday names in the reading below (Monday, Wednesday and so on) are inferred from the five-high/two-low pattern. The day boundaries are where the data's nightly lows fall, not known midnights.
- The resolution is coarse: each dash stands for about five readings, about five hours.
- The paper colour stays light in dark mode on purpose.
- Working files (the profiling, period-search, build and screenshot scripts, and test renders) are in `/tmp/claude-0/s123/mk/m03/scratch`.

**My view of what was measured, with how sure I am.** I think it is **hourly electricity demand (or a similar use of energy) for a large area, over about 61 days from mid-November to mid-January, with the Christmas–New Year holiday in it.**
- **Hourly over 61 days:** there is a strong 24-value cycle, and 1,464 = 61 × 24.
- **A working week:** there are five raised days and two lower ones, and the lower days never drop to zero. That points to the sum of many users rather than one building.
- **The holiday:** in the 6th full week, the raised pattern stops after two weekdays. It stays flat through the whole 7th week and comes back on the following Monday. That is about 12 days, which matches roughly 24 December to 4 January if the record starts on a Saturday in mid-November. The calendar lines up exactly for a start on Saturday 15 November and an end on Wednesday 14 January (2025/26 is one such year; 2014/15 and 2008/09 also fit). This is the weakest part of my reading.
- **Winter:** after the lull the overall level is higher than before, which fits heating or lighting load in midwinter.

None of this can be confirmed from the file alone.
