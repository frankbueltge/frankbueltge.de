# Set in Weeks

The work cuts the long strip of dots into seven-day lengths and sets them one under another, like lines of type or a ledger page, so 61 days become nine lines (the last one short). Every mark comes from a dark pixel of the original picture, put back in its own place in its week and at its own height. Each original pixel column covers about five hours, so marks stand in stacks and are stretched sideways into short strokes; the work does not claim the hour-by-hour detail that the picture has already lost. I chose this form because laying the record out in lines of seven days shows what the single strip hides: two quiet days at the start of every line, five busy days after them, and, in lines six and seven, about two weeks when the busy days drop away as well. The rhythm of the record looks like a working week, and setting it out by the week lets that show without any words.
