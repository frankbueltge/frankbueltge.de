# Seven Nights to a Line

The record is a single strip in which one rhythm, a dip roughly every 24 values, sits inside a second, five raised days and then two lowered ones. In a long strip at this size the second rhythm is hard to see. So I cut the strip each time seven nightly lows have passed and set the pieces one under another, like lines of text. That puts each day under the same day of the week before, and lining them up is enough to show the week: the two low days stack into a column, and the faint seams mark the record's own nightly lows, not a clock. I did not plot anything new. Every mark on the page is a pixel column of the original picture, stretched sideways and kept at its own darkness, so the page shows only the detail the file actually holds, about five values to each column. Near the bottom, a run of about twelve days breaks the pattern: the raised days are missing and the lines go flat. Then the full rhythm returns, a little higher than before.
