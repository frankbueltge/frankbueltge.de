# The Week Refolded

The source strip is cut back into its 61 days and the days are laid out as a calendar: each column is one week, read left to right as the strip was, and each row is one day of the week, so the seven-day beat that the strip hides becomes rows that agree with each other. Every mark is a pixel of the original picture. Because one pixel column of the source covers about five hours, a day falls into only five columns of marks: the hours themselves cannot be recovered, and the form keeps that coarseness instead of smoothing it away. Behind each day, faintly, lie all the other days that share its row, so a day that drops away from its usual shape shows up against them. I chose the fold because the record's main facts are its rhythms: a daily arch, five high days and two low ones each week, and two weeks near the end where the arch sinks out of step. A long single line shows those things poorly, but a calendar shows them at once.
