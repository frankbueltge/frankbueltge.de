# Thirty-Six and a Half Hours

The record arrived as forty columns of twelve pixels, so each column had to hold about 36.6 of the 1,464 hours: a day and a half, never a whole day. The work makes that limit the subject. It enlarges the picture and puts back exactly 1,464 dots, one per hour. Each column gets its 36 or 37 hours, and they are shared out among its pixels by how dark each pixel was. Inside a pixel the dots sit on a plain grid, because the picture does not say where in the pixel the values were. Below, a ruler sets the forty pixel boundaries against the sixty-one day boundaries, and the two never line up. I chose this form because the honest way to give this record a shape is to show what the picture kept: how many hours fell where. Drawing a curve would have claimed a precision the picture does not have.
