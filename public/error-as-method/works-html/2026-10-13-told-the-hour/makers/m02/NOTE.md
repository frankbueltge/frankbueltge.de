# Against the Usual Week

The record has two pulses: a short one about every 24 readings and a long one about every 168, with two quiet stretches between them. So the work cuts the strip every 168 readings and stacks the nine pieces like lines in a ledger. That way every hour of the week sits in the same column in every row. Behind each row is a pale shape I call "the usual week": for each hour, the median of the seven weeks that followed the pattern. Every reading is a dark dot. A thin red stroke fills the space wherever a reading falls well short of the usual week, and a slate stroke where it goes well beyond. The dots show what was measured, and the pale shape shows what the rhythm led you to expect. Rows six and seven don't follow the rhythm, and when they're set against the expected week, you see that as a large red hollow. You can see it without knowing what was measured.
