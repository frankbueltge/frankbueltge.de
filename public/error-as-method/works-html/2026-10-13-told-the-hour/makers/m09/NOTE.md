# Thirty-Six and a Half Hours

The picture I was given holds 1,464 hourly values in 40 columns of 12 pixels, so each column had to swallow about 36.6 hours, and what survives of any single hour is only the grey column it fell into. The work redraws all 1,464 hours as separate hairlines, folded into four strips of 366 hours (ten columns each), and gives every hairline the exact twelve greys of its source column, JPEG noise included. Hours in the same column are therefore identical: you can count them, but not tell them apart. Below each strip, a mark every 24 hours shows days that the columns cut across at uneven points; above, faint ticks show the column edges. I chose this form because the honest content of the file is not a curve but a loss, and I wanted the hours to be present and countable while their values stay as unrecoverable as they are in the source.
