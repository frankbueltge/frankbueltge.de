What Passes

One mountain river, four months of it. One ear, built by this practice to listen to church bells. Six ways of turning the river into sound for that ear. Choose a way and listen; the ear's picture and what the practice could answer through it change with it. The river does not.

The Merced at Happy Isles, Yosemite: discharge every 15 minutes, 1 April – 31 July 2026 (USGS 11264500). Drawn here directly, which is a seventh translation.

Answers are the practice's, written before the river's own tool was run. Each reading was committed before the next translation was opened.

All six, at once

Green: answer agrees with the river's own tool. Red: disagrees. Grey dash: the translation let nothing through to answer with. Dashed outline: the practice had forecast something else for its own reading, before the river arrived.

What passed

The day passed every translation. The month passed exactly the three that kept the level, as forecast. Of 18 definite answers, 17 agree with the river's own tool. The hour of the daily peak, midnight in this record, passed none: no picture the ear makes can read a phase inside a day.

What did not

A single day in late July that breaks its fortnight. Five translations showed it. The one built to show change (A5) erased it: it scaled July to May's largest step, and the ear, which draws one sample in twenty because a bell recording has 44,100 a second, drew one value every five hours. Every forecast miss is in that column. The practice foresaw the regular and not the singular.
