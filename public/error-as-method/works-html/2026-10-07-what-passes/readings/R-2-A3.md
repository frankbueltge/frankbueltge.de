# Reading 2 — A3 one minute (each value interpolated over 226 samples). Opened: seen/A3-S.png, A3-N.txt, A3-rhythm.txt only.

What I know of the adapter: 60.02 s holds 122 days, so 1 day = 0.492 s, 1 s = 2.03 days. A daily
cycle is a 2.03 Hz wobble: below every frequency the ear was built for.

**Contamination, declared.** The waveform panel is A1's waveform stretched; I recognised it at
once. Every waveform answer below was given by a reader who had already answered the same question
from A1. The order was drawn (seed 110), and this is its cost.

- **R1 day: yes.** S waveform: a regular ripple on the hump, about ten ripples between 18 and 23 s,
  i.e. one per ~0.5 s = one per day. The **rhythm channel does not decide it**: its strongest
  blow-scale periodicity is 0.583 s; 0.496 s (one day) is fourth, at 0.126 against 0.131, which is
  no peak at all. N does not decide it: 398 onsets, median interval 0.099 s — five "blows" a day.
  Decided by the waveform alone.
- **R2 month: May.** Same hump as A1, at 17.5–23 s (days 36–47), highest near 20.7 s (day 42,
  about 12 May). Decided by the waveform (contaminated, see above).
- **R3 coupling: yes.** Waveform: ripple tallest on the May and early-June humps, absent after
  ~42 s (mid-July). Also S spectrogram: the comb of bands reaches 2,000 Hz only at 18–23 s, the
  hump. Decided by the waveform; the spectrogram agrees, but see below for what the bands are.
- **R4 irregular day: yes.** Waveform: a sharp blip at ~55.2 s (day 112, about 22 July). Also a
  dark vertical stripe in the spectrogram at ~45 s (about 1 July) where the bands thin out; I cannot
  say what it is. Decided by the waveform (contaminated).
- **R5 peak hour: cannot say.** A day is half a second wide in a 60 s picture; no phase is legible.

**About the adapter, not the river.** The spectrogram is a comb of horizontal bands spaced about
195 Hz apart (≈ 44,100 / 226): the kinks of my own linear interpolation, one every 226 samples. The
river does not sound there; my adapter does. Its loudness follows how sharply the slope changes,
so it is brightest on the hump — the river *modulates* a tone my translation made. The ear's 398
onsets and their 150 / 270 / 470 Hz "partials" are that comb's flicker, not river events. Session
109's lesson again, in a new form: the adapter here did not only decide what the ear could see; it
put a sound in front of the ear and the ear rang to it.
