Ed's Purple Martin Page
from the back yard on Rt. 3, Norwalk, Ohio

Welcome!!

My name is Ed and I have been a rural carrier for the Postal Service going on 27 years. In the spring of 1997 my wife Joyce bought me a 12 room martin house for my birthday and I put it up on a pole out back between the garage and the garden. That first year we got NOTHING but house sparrows and I about gave up. In 1998 the martins came back and found it and we had 4 pairs nesting and 17 babies fledge. I was hooked.

This page is mostly so I can keep my records all in one place and so my brother in Erie can see how the birds are doing without me having to call long distance every week.  My daughter Kim set it up for me at Christmas and I am still learning so bear with me.

If your a martin landlord too please sign my guestbook and tell me how YOUR colony is doing!

What's New

  • 4/18/99 - NINE pairs now. Put up a rack with 6 gourds next to the house because they were fighting over the end rooms. Joyce says no more poles in her yard.
  • 4/10/99 - Cold snap, 28 degrees two nights in a row. I threw crickets up on the porch boards like the book says. They ate some I think.
  • 4/3/99 - FIRST SCOUT!!! An adult male, sat on the roof about 7:15 am while I was warming up the truck. 8 days earlier than last year.
  • 3/20/99 - Lowered the house and cleaned out last years nests and put in fresh pine needles. Opened the holes back up.

Things I've Learned (the hard way)

  1. Keep the holes plugged until the first martins show up or the sparrows and starlings will move in first.
  2. Put the house out in the open. Mine is about 40 ft from the house and 50 ft from the nearest tree. Martins like to swoop in.
  3. Get a pole that you can lower (telescoping or a winch). You need to check the nests once a week and you are NOT going to climb a ladder with a 12 room house on top of it.
  4. White house. They like white.
  5. Be patient. It can take 2 or 3 years to get your first pair.
