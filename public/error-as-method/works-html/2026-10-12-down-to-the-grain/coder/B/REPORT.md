I coded all twelve makers and wrote the answers to `/tmp/claude-0/s122/cB/answers.json`. It is valid JSON with keys m01 to m12. Each entry has B1 to B9, B6 has both the maker's words and a category, B7_outside is null when B7 is no, and every yes, measured or supplied answer has a supporting quotation. I wrote nothing else.

**How I went about it:** I read each maker's note and report in full and coded only what those two files say. I looked nothing up and used no outside knowledge of the pictures. The makers fall into two groups:
- **Very small images (m01, m03, m04, m05, m06, m11, pictures of 19×6 or 75×23 pixels):** all of them read the pixel values but draw the pixels or cells themselves (B8 pixels). They name a daily swing only as inferred or unconfirmed (B2 supplied). m03 and m06 still say they measured a rhythm of about 14 days in the band's width (B3 measured).
- **Larger images (m02, m07, m08, m09, m10, m12, about 300 to 1,200 pixels wide):** all of them extracted dots or fitted a tidal model. They found the 12.42 h period (B2 measured), redraw values fitted back one per hour (B8 items), and read the record as a tide gauge (B6 water).

**Answers that were hard:**
- **m02, B3 = no:** it names only a monthly swell of about 27.5 to 29 days, never a two-week one.
- **m03, B7 = yes, B7_outside = yes:** it offers "a fireworks spike ... if the record spans New Year" as an alternative label for the spike, and says the file holds no dates. That is a calendar label from outside the picture, but it is offered only as a passing hypothesis. Its "Baltic-like" refers to the coast, not the event. Of the twelve, this is the answer I am least sure of.
- **B9 = no for every maker:** m04 ("decided not to rebuild the hours"), m05 ("decided not to draw a curve") and m11 ("didn't want to invent dots") all decline to *reconstruct* values. But all three read and measured the pixels and never say they stopped measuring or worked from the look of the picture. If declining to reconstruct should count as "chose not to measure", these three would become yes.
- **B8 for m03, m05, m06, m11:** each draws one mark per hour, but where each mark sits comes from its pixel column's ink, not from a fitted value. So I coded them as pixels, the same as m01.
- **B6 category:** m01, m04 and m05 decline to name the quantity, so I coded them none. m01 lists river level among its options but explicitly refuses to choose. m11 leans towards temperature or an air pollutant over river level, so I coded it other.
- **B2 supplied for small-image makers:** m03 and m06 infer a swing within each day from the width of the band, and m05 names a daily cycle only to say the picture is too coarse to show it. I coded all three as supplied rather than measured.
- **B5, second event after the main one:** I coded yes for m06, where the level "stays raised" for about 10 days after the spike. m10 says the level stays *lower* afterwards, so I coded no. The early faint pixels in m01, m05 and m11 come before the main event, so they do not count.
