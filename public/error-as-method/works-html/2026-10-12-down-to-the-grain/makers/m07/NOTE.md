# Moon Hours

The record crushes 1,464 hours into 300 pixels, so every column holds about five hours and every tide collapses into a smear. Inside that smear, though, the dots repeat every 12.42 hours: the lunar semidiurnal tide, not the 12-hour or 24-hour clock of the sun. The work takes the 798 dots that can still be told apart in the JPEG, finds each one's place in time from its position, and cuts the record into sixty lines, each one lunar day of 24 h 50 min long, laid one under the next. Colour carries the height (dark is high water, pale is low). Read down the page, the two high waters line up as dark columns that drift slightly, sharpen at spring tides and fade at neaps. One line near the bottom goes dark across its whole width and turns red, where the water rose above any tide in the record. The original strip sits underneath, so the reader can see what was undone. I chose this form because the record's own rhythm belongs to the moon, so I used the moon's clock to set the dots back out.
