# Held to the Moon

The record's strongest rhythm is not the 24-hour day: it is 12.42 hours, the half-day of the moon. So the work cuts the 1,464 hourly values into lengths of one lunar day (24 h 50 min) and stacks them down the page as 60 ridgelines. Each ridge is drawn through the actual hourly points, one small grain per hour like the original dots, and the 25 hours that were hidden in the source picture are drawn as open grains. Each line hides the ones behind it, like a range of hills. Cut to the moon's clock, the record's two daily rises line up into two straight ridges running from top to bottom. As you read down, they swell and thin as the monthly cycle passes through them. Near the bottom, one rise climbs out of its row and over the lines above it, then leaves a gap below. That rise is the one moment the moon does not account for. I chose this cut because it lets the regular part of the record draw itself as an orderly field, so the break in it can be seen without any label.
