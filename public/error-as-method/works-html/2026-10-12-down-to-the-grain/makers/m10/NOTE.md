# Fifty-nine Lunar Days

The source is a 300-pixel-wide picture of 1,464 hourly dots, so each pixel column holds about five hours. Read closely, its grain held a pattern: column averages flip every 5 pixels, and the spread of dots swells and thins about every 72 pixels. Fitting periods to the pixels showed that pattern was a tide, with a 12.42-hour beat and a 14.8-day spring–neap cycle. The work recovers an estimate of the hourly record from that fit and from the dots themselves. It then cuts the record every lunar day (24 h 50 min) and stacks the pieces as overlapping lines, so high waters fall in the same place on every line. Swelling, thinning and drift then read straight down the page: springs and neaps, the slow rise and fall of the mean level, and one high water, in red, that rises far above the rest. I chose this fold because the record's own rhythm is the moon's, not the clock's, and only that fold makes the pattern visible. I put the source at actual size beside it so the viewer can see how little was there to read from. The values are in the picture's own pixel units, because the file gives no others.
