# Nineteen and a Half Hours

The work enlarges the record's 75 × 23 pixels exactly as they arrived, JPEG smudges included, into a field of grey cells. Under it run three rulers: 1,464 hairlines, one per hour, with longer ticks at each of the 61 days; then the 75 pixel columns; then how many hours each column had to hold (19 or 20). Shrinking the plot to this size merged every hour into its neighbours. Each column now keeps only how far its values spread, and the counts never line up with the days. I chose not to invent the hours back. The form shows the loss itself: every hour is still counted along the bottom, and none of them can be seen in the image above. What survives is a steady band that fills most of the height, and one rise, around the forty-fourth day, that climbs to the top edge.
