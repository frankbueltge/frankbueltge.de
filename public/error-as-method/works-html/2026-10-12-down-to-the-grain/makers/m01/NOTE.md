# Seventy-Seven to a Column

The file is 114 grey pixels standing in for 1,464 hourly values, so almost everything was already lost before I saw it; the work does not pretend otherwise. It opens each pixel into a large cell and puts the hours back: every column is owed 77 hours (one is owed 78), and those are shared among its six cells in proportion to how much ink each cell carries, then drawn as dots placed at seeded-random spots inside the cell. The counts are the only thing taken from the record; the positions are admittedly invented, and the faint original greys stay underneath as evidence. A tick line of 61 days under 19 seams shows that no pixel holds whole days, and the compressor's 8-pixel block edges are marked, because the smooth fade across the first eight columns is JPEG's doing, not the measurement's. I chose this because the honest subject of this material is its own grain: how much a record shrinks when it is turned into a picture, and how little can be restored from it.
