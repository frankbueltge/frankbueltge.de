I made the work. It's called **Fifty Minutes Late**, and the files are `/tmp/claude-0/s122/mk/m09/work/index.html` and `/tmp/claude-0/s122/mk/m09/work/NOTE.md`. It is one still canvas at 1100 × 800 with the data written into the page. I checked it in headless Chromium: it draws correctly, and the page requests no other file.

**What I did first with the file:** I looked at the picture. A dense band of dots runs along the bottom third, with a slow swelling and narrowing, one tall spike about three quarters of the way across, and an abrupt step down near the right edge. Then I read the pixels. I found the dot centres as local dark peaks with sub-pixel centroids: 1,356 of them. I mapped each one to an hour using the spacing of 0.804 pixels per hour taken from the first and last dots. No two dots landed on the same hour. The 108 hours with no dot (7%) are places where consecutive dots overlapped near the crests and troughs, and I filled those by straight-line interpolation. So the values are recovered pixel heights in no real unit, and they will be off by a pixel or so here and there.

**How I got to the form:** I searched the series for repeating periods. One period dominates, at **12.42 hours**. It is about four times stronger than anything near 12 or 24 hours. Each day the two peaks come about 50 minutes later than the day before. The daily swing grows and shrinks roughly every two weeks, between about 140 and 67 pixels. Because the rhythm does not match the clock, I folded the record onto clock days, with each row showing that day followed by the next (61 rows × 48 hours). Colour gives the level, running from paper (low) to deep blue-black (high). The high water turns into dark diagonal bands that lean right, and they fade and sharpen with the two-week cycle. Hours above the highest level reached on any other day are drawn in rust. A plain line plot can't show the slant. There are a few tick marks and a short caption, but no numbers.

**What the commissioner should know:**
- The data comes from reading a compressed JPEG of dots, not from the original numbers. The shape and timing are reliable; individual values are approximate, and 108 of the 1,464 are interpolated.
- The scale is pixels. Nothing in the file gives units, dates or a place, and the work makes no claim about them.
- The rust threshold is set from the data itself, at just above the highest level outside days 43–45.

**My view of what was measured:** water level at a coast, from a tide gauge, with weather on top of the tide. What it rests on:
- The 12.42-hour period is the lunar semidiurnal tide. It is what makes high water come about 50 minutes later each day.
- The swing grows and shrinks over about 14 days, which matches the spring–neap cycle. Successive spring tides are also unequal in size.
- Around days 43–45 the daily mean jumps by about 75 pixels for roughly two days, and it peaks on a high tide at about twice the normal maximum. That looks like a storm surge.
- Around days 53–54 there is a smaller raised level, followed by a sudden drop. That could be a second weather event, or possibly a change in the gauge's reference level; the file can't tell which.
