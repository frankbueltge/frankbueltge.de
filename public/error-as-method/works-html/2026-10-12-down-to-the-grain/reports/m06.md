I made "Nineteen and a Half Hours". It's one static SVG page in `index.html`. It loads nothing from outside, uses no animation, and is laid out for 1100 × 800. I wrote `NOTE.md` next to it and left the build script and a preview in `/tmp/claude-0/s122/mk/m06/scratch`.

**What I did first with the file:** I printed the grey value of every one of its 75 × 23 pixels and read through the numbers. That made the main fact plain: the 1,464 hourly values are no longer in the file. Each pixel column covers about 19.5 hours (1464 / 75), so what's left is a smear of darkness per column. There is also faint JPEG ringing (grey values around 225–250) above and below the real marks.

**How I got from there to the form:**
- I treated the pixels themselves as the material.
- For each column, I worked out exactly how many hours fall in it.
- I shared those hours among the column's 23 pixels in proportion to how dark each pixel is. I ignored anything lighter than a ringing floor of about 225 and rounded so the total stays at exactly 1,464.
- The page shows each pixel as an enlarged, faint square, with that many ink dots in it.

So the count of hours and their rough height are kept, and nothing is invented beyond that. The order of the dots inside a square is arbitrary, and the page says so in a short caption.

**What the commissioner should know:**
- The page never shows true hourly values. They can't be recovered from this file, and the work is about that loss.
- Some of the faint pixels in the tall spike got rounded to zero dots, so the spike shows small gaps. Those gaps come from the rounding, not from the data.
- No browser was available here, so I checked the layout with an approximate render made by a script, not a real browser screenshot.

**My view of what was measured (not certain):** I think it's a water level, most likely tidal (coast or estuary), with one storm-surge or flood event. Three things in the file point that way:
1. **A wide band throughout.** The values spread across about 9 of the 23 rows all the way through, which fits a large repeating swing within each day.
2. **The band widens and narrows over time.** It is wide at about columns 0–8 and 32–43 and narrower around 12–15 and 24–31. That's a cycle of very roughly 14 days, close to the spring–neap tide cycle.
3. **One sharp excursion upward.** At columns 52–55 (around day 43) the values shoot far above the band for about 1–2 days. Meanwhile the lowest rows go empty for roughly the next 10 days, so the whole level stays raised and slowly settles. That is what a storm surge or flood looks like.

The cycle is only a rough match at this resolution. A river flood or some other quantity that swings each day and has one extreme episode would fit the file too.
