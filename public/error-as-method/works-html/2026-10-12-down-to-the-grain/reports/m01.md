My only working files are in /tmp/claude-0/s122/mk/m01/scratch (pixels.txt, test.js, t2.js). I couldn't find a headless browser here, so I have not seen the page rendered. I did run its script against a stubbed DOM: it draws exactly 1,464 dots and all of them land inside the frame.

**What I did first.** I read the 114 grey values of the JPEG and printed them as a 19 × 6 grid. That grid is the whole of the material. Each pixel column covers about 77 hours (3.21 days), so the hourly detail was gone before the file reached me. The grid shows:
- The bottom four rows are dark in every column, so most of the time the values sit in one band filling the lower two-thirds of the picture.
- In columns 14–15 (roughly days 42–48) the top two rows are near-black. That is a burst of much higher values, and it holds most of the ink in those two columns.
- Column 11 has a faint grey in the top row (232), probably a single brief high value around day 33.
- Across columns 1–8 the greys change in perfectly smooth ramps. That is JPEG compression smoothing its first 8 × 8 block, not the data. The blocks break after columns 8 and 16.

**How I got to the form.** I decided the honest subject is the grain itself: how much a record shrinks when it becomes a picture, and how little of it can be restored.
- Each pixel is enlarged into a 52 px cell, with its original grey kept faintly underneath.
- Each column is given back its 77 hours (the last one gets 78). Those hours are shared among the column's six cells in proportion to each cell's ink. Every hour is drawn as a dot at a fixed-seed random spot inside its cell.
- Only the number of dots per cell comes from the file. Where each dot sits is invented, and a caption says so.
- Under the grid, a line of 61 day ticks runs against the 19 column seams, showing that no pixel holds whole days.
- Solid seams mark the JPEG block edges. The record is also shown at its true 19 × 6 size, with three short captions.
- Everything is inline SVG built by plain JavaScript, with the pixel values embedded. The page loads nothing else.

**Things you should know:**
- Because the dots follow ink, the near-white 252 pixel at the top of column 5 earns one dot. That dot is compression noise, and I left it in deliberately rather than smooth it away.
- The smooth fade across the first eight columns is compression too, not the measurement.
- Any finer reading of this file would be invention.

**My view of what was measured.** I don't have a confident view, and nothing in the file can settle it. What the file supports:
- A quantity that stays in a broad, steady band for about six weeks.
- A short excursion far above that band around days 42–48, plus maybe one brief spike near day 33.
- A wide band at this scale is consistent with a daily cycle squeezed into each column, but I can't confirm one.

That pattern of a steady band with rare upward bursts fits things like river level after a storm, air pollution during an episode, or wind speed during a gale. I would not choose between these, or rule out others, from 114 pixels.
