I made the work and it's in `/tmp/claude-0/s122/mk/m11/work/`: `index.html`, a single still page at 1100 × 800 that loads no other file, and `NOTE.md`. It's titled "Somewhere in the column". I couldn't open the page in a browser here, so I checked the layout with a rough Python stand-in for the drawing code instead (`/tmp/claude-0/s122/mk/m11/scratch/preview.py`, output `preview.png` in the same folder).

**What I did first.** I read every pixel's grey value. The picture is 19 × 6 pixels, so 114 pixels in all. Each pixel column covers about 77 hours (roughly 3.2 days), and each row covers a sixth of the vertical range. Not one hourly value survives. What remains is a coarse envelope:
- **The lower four rows** are dark in all 19 columns. So in every three-day stretch the values filled the lower two-thirds of the range, right down to the bottom.
- **The top two rows** are white almost everywhere.
- **The exception is columns 13–14**, about hours 1,000–1,155 (days 42–48). There the top row is nearly black and the second row half-dark, so the values reached the top of the range there.
- **Column 10** (about days 32–35) has a faint grey in the top row, perhaps a few high values.
- **Column 8**, and the smooth light-to-dark shift across columns 0–7, line up with the JPEG's 8-pixel compression blocks. I treated them as compression artefacts, not data.

**How I got to the form.** Since no value survives, I didn't want to invent dots. The work draws all 1,464 hours as thin vertical hairlines. Each hour's line runs across every band its pixel column shows as inked, at that pixel's darkness. So the count and the order of the hours are kept, but each value is shown only as the range it could have held. Two other details:
- Grey levels within about 9 steps of white are left out as compression noise.
- Small points under the field mark the 61 days, and faint ticks mark the 19 pixel edges.

A short caption on the page explains the hairlines.

**What the commissioner should know:**
- The image holds far too little to recover the record. The work is mostly about that loss.
- Its strongest feature is one fact: a single high excursion around days 42–48 against a broad, steady band.
- Shading inside a band says nothing about how values were spread within it. It reflects pixel darkness only, which is partly a JPEG artefact.

**My view of what was measured.** I'm not confident about it. Two things in the file point somewhere:
- The record fills the lower two-thirds in every three-day window, with no white gaps. That suggests a quantity that swings widely and continually, such as with a daily cycle. Temperature or an air-pollutant concentration would fit.
- One multi-day event pushes it to the top. That would fit a storm, flood or pollution episode, or, if it is temperature, a heat spell.

A river level or flow with one flood is also possible, but a constantly full lower band fits a quantity that cycles every day better than one that drifts slowly. Nothing in the file can settle this. There are no units, no location and no date, and the vertical scale is lost.
