I made "Twice a Moon-Day" in `/tmp/claude-0/s122/mk/m12/work/index.html`, with its note in `/tmp/claude-0/s122/mk/m12/work/NOTE.md`. It is one still SVG, 1100×800, and loads no other file. The only external reference is the standard SVG namespace string. I checked it in headless Chromium at 1100×800.

**What I did first with the file:** I looked at the picture, then turned it back into numbers. The dots are about 1–2 px and sit 0.80 px apart in x, so dots for neighbouring hours often overlap. I found each dot as a local peak of darkness, worked out its centre, and merged duplicates. The dots span x = 11.4 to 1187.5, which is exactly 1,463 steps of 0.8039 px, so the 1,464-hour spacing fits. That gave me 1,389 separate dots, which I assigned to hour slots.
- **Gaps:** about 110 hours (7.5%) could not be told apart from a neighbour. I filled them by straight-line interpolation between the hours either side.
- **Clean-up:** I swapped 6 neighbouring pairs that had obviously come out in the wrong order, and set aside 10 lone outliers.
- **Units:** the values are pixel heights only. Units and a true zero are unknown.

**How I got to the form:** The autocorrelation is very strong at 12–13 h, and higher at 25 h than at 24 h. I scanned periods from 11 to 14 h and 12.4 h was clearly the best fit; that single sine explains about 66% of the variance. Adding further terms at 12.00, 12.66, 23.93, 25.82 and 24.07 h raises that to 74%.

The form cuts the record into lengths of 24 h 50 min, twice that period, and stacks them as 60 rows of colour from pale sand (low) to deep ink (high). High waters line up as two dark columns. What is left over shows as anything that breaks those columns:
- a roughly 14-day beat of strong and weak contrast;
- a darker or paler shade on some rows;
- one near-black smear late in the record.

**What I tried and dropped:** I first made a stacked-ridgeline version that kept every dot. It read as a tangled mesh, and the big event did not stand out, so I abandoned it.

**What the commissioner should know:**
- The page's caption says "24 h 50 min" and "Moon-Day". That interpretation comes from my analysis; nothing in the file states it.
- The extreme event around hours 1,040–1,100 was where the dots were densest. My extraction is least sure there; the overall shape is solid, but individual hours may be off by one.
- The first and last rows are partial, because the record does not start or end on a cut.

**My view of what was measured:** Sea level from a tide gauge on a coast where the tide comes twice a day. This rests on four things in the file:
1. **The main period is about 12.42 h.** That is the main lunar twice-daily tide, not a 12 h or 24 h clock rhythm.
2. **Smaller rhythms that tides have:** a weaker 12.00 h term at about 0.22 of the main one, and one at 12.66 h. Together they make the range grow and shrink on a cycle of roughly 14–15 days: wide around days 2–7, 29–34 and 57–60, narrow around days 10–12, 19–25 and 49–52.
3. **A storm surge late in the record.** Around days 43–45 the leftover after the tidal fit climbs over about two days to roughly four times the normal tidal swing. It peaks at a high tide (hour about 1,081, the highest value in the record) and falls away within about six hours.
4. **Several shorter weather effects.** The level stays raised for a day or two at days 24–25 and 53–54, then drops sharply.
