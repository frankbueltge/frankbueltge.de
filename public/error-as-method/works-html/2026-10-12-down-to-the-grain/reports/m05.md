I made "Seventy-Seven Hours a Pixel", a still image that rebuilds the 19 × 6 picture out of its 1,464 hours, plus NOTE.md. The file holds too little to say what was measured. I checked the page in headless Chromium and it renders. It loads no outside files, scripts or fonts.

**What I did first.** I read all 114 pixels as grey values. Each pixel column covers about 77 hours (3.2 days), so the 1,464 hourly values are gone; what's left is a coarse map of greys. I also saw JPEG compression at work: in columns 0–7, the first compression block, the greys run as smooth ramps, so even this little detail is partly an artifact of compression. The grey levels I used are written into the page.

**How I got to the form.** I decided not to draw a curve, because the file can't support one. Each hour gets one hairline:
- **Where in time:** exact. This is the one thing the file still knows.
- **Which of the six bands:** drawn by lot, weighted by how dark that pixel is.
- **Where inside the band:** unknown, so the line runs the full height of the band rather than marking a point.

From a distance the hairlines add up to the original picture. Under it is a 61-day ruler. Red marks show where the pixel columns fall, and apart from the two ends none of them lands on a midnight, so the picture's grain never matches the days the record was taken in. The original is shown at its true size in the corner.

**What you should know:**
- Which band each hour falls in is decided by a fixed, repeatable lot. It is not data, and the page says so.
- On a short browser window, headless Chromium cut the lowest part of the page off. Where the full 1100 × 800 shows, I checked that everything appears, and I moved the bottom line up to about y = 706 for margin.

**What I think was measured.** I can't say, and any guess would be weak.
- For most of the 61 days the values sit in a broad band across the lower two-thirds of the range.
- There is one short excursion to the very top, around days 41–48 (columns 13–14). A faint grey at the top of columns 10 and 12 may be single outlying hours, or compression noise.

A steady base with a rare short peak fits things like wind speed, river level, a pollutant or electricity load better than something smooth. A daily cycle, which would separate temperature from these, is far too fine to survive 77-hour pixels. I made the work about that loss instead of guessing.
