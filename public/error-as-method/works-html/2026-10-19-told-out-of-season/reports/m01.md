I made one still piece, "Sixty-One Fires": a spiral with one turn per day, drawn on canvas, with the data embedded in the page. The JavaScript passes a syntax check, but I never saw the page itself in a browser because none was available.

**Files**
- `/tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m01/work/index.html` – the piece. It loads nothing from outside the file.
- `/tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m01/work/NOTE.md` – the title and one paragraph.
- My working files are in `/tmp/claude-0/s137/mk/m01/scratch`: the extraction script (`rec.py`), the series it produced (`series.json`, `data.txt`), a replot of that series to compare with the original (`recon.png`), and the preview script and image (`prev.py`, `prev.png`).

**What I did first with the file**
I looked at it, then enlarged it 4× and read the dark pixels column by column. The plot runs from x≈2.5 to x≈297, so each day is about 4.9 pixels wide. That repeats in the image as a regular sawtooth: one sharp rise, then a long slow fall, every day. I checked where the jumps fall and the rise comes at about hour 22 of the record's own hourly count, every day.

**How I got from it to the form**
1. **Rebuilding the hourly values.** I cut the record into 24-hour windows that each start at the rise. Within each window I sorted the dark pixels from top to bottom and took 24 evenly spaced cut-points. That gives one value per hour, scaled 0–100.
2. **Checking it.** I replotted those values at the original size and they look very close to the source.
3. **The form.** The spiral starts at the rim on 15 January and winds inward to 16 March. Each hour is one segment, coloured on an ember scale from ash to pale gold, with a soft glow on the higher values. The daily rise sits at twelve o'clock, so every turn starts bright and fades as it goes round. A small caption and four date ticks are the only text.

**What you should know**
- **The values are approximate.** There are about 80 height levels, and inside each day the curve is forced to fall steadily. That fits the strong teeth well, but not the small wiggles after late February.
- **The time of day may be wrong.** I counted the first value as hour 0 on 15 January. The image only gives hour positions, so "hour 22" means 22 hours into the record's own day, not a confirmed clock time.
- **Units and true scale are unknown.** Brightness only shows where a value sits between the lowest and highest in this record.
- **Not checked in a browser.** I previewed the layout with an equivalent Python drawing instead, so the glow effect and the antialiasing have not been seen.

**My view of what was measured (a guess)**
I think it is something charged once a day and then left to drain: most likely the core temperature of a night-storage heater or a stove fired once a day, or possibly the level in a store such as a hot-water tank.
- Every day the value jumps up quickly at the same hour, around 22:00 if hour 0 is midnight, which would match an off-peak night tariff. It then falls slowly for about 20 hours.
- The period runs from January to March, which is heating season.
- The daily peaks fall step by step through February, as if the charge were turned down as the weather warmed.
- The teeth are low for about two days around 23–25 January, and there is one unusually strong charge on the night of 10 February.
- Around 26–27 February the daily charging more or less stops. What is left is low, with only a small daily ripple that drifts down and then up slightly by mid-March.

None of this pins down the place or the units.
