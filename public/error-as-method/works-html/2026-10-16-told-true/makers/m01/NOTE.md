# Sixty-One Risings

The record is cut into its 61 days, each day starting at the hour when the quantity is usually lowest. The days are stacked top to bottom as ridgelines, and each later day covers the lower part of the one before. Read across a row and you see one day rise and fall. Read down the rows and you see the days pile into a terrain: a slow sag in the first third, then a recovery. Now and then a day stands up through several rows above it; those hours are marked in red. I chose this form because the most important thing in the picture was a rhythm, not a trend: the quantity swells and subsides once a day, and there is no weekly pattern. A single left-to-right line hides that rhythm in a smear of dots, while stacking the days lets them line up and makes the exceptions stand out. The dots are recovered hourly values; the curves between them only guide the eye. I read the values off a 300-pixel picture, so the order of hours within each ~5-hour pixel column is inferred and no scale is given.
