# Held to the Sun

The work recasts the 61-day record as a double-plotted actogram, the chart chronobiologists use to ask which clock a rhythm keeps. Each row is one day, shown again as the second half of the row above. Each reading's departure from its running 24-hour mean is drawn as height, with the daily highs filled in ink, and readings that jump far above their neighbours are marked as rust stems. I chose this form because the file's most telling property is not its range but its timing. When the dots are read back out of the picture, the dominant rhythm repeats every 24.0 hours with steady strength and steady timing, and the weaker twice-daily part repeats every 12.0 hours. A harbour tide follows the moon: its peaks would arrive about 50 minutes later each day and would lean across the rows like the dashed guide. Here they stand upright, so the figure shows directly whether this record behaves like a tide.
