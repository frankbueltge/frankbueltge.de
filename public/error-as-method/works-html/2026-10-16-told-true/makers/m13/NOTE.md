# Sixty-One Turns

The record is wound into a spiral of sixty-one turns, one for each day, from the first hour at the centre to the last at the rim. The angle around the disc is the time of day, and a mark's distance outside its turn is how much carbon dioxide was measured, with colour moving from slate (less) to rust (more). In the flat strip, the clearest thing in the data, a rise and fall that comes back every 24 hours, is buried under 61 repetitions. Winding the strip at exactly that period stacks every day's high on the same side, so the daily rhythm becomes the shape of the whole image: rust on the upper half, dense slate on the lower. Every mark is one dark pixel of the plot I was given, placed by its own position, with nothing smoothed or invented, so the work stays as coarse and as honest as its source.
