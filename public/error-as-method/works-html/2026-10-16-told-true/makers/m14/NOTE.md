# Wound on a Day

The 1,464 hourly readings are wound into a spiral with one turn per 24 hours: day 1 at the centre, day 61 at the rim, and the same hour of every day lying on the same spoke. Each hour is coloured by how far it sits above or below the 24 hours around it. A dashed line marks where a moon-driven high water would fall, arriving about fifty minutes later each day and so going twice round the clock over the record. I chose this form because folding the record onto the clock is what tests it. The record came labelled as a harbour tide, but when I read the dots back out of the picture, the dominant rhythm repeats every 24 hours and has almost no lunar component. On the spiral, the pale ridge stays at the same hour turn after turn and does not follow the dashed line. A small number of isolated readings, far above the rest in the original, are marked as points of light.
