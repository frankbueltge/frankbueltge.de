# Sixty-One Breaths

The record's strongest rhythm is a 24-hour one, so the work winds the 1,464 hours into a coil with one turn per day. The first day is at the centre and time runs clockwise. A faint spiral marks each day at the lowest reading anywhere in the record. Each hour is a dot, and it sits further out from that spiral the more carbon dioxide was measured. Laid in a straight line, the daily cycle is a fine sawtooth and hard to see. Wound into a coil, every day's build-up lands on the same side. The coil swells and turns ragged at the top, where night air collects CO2. It stays tight and even at the bottom, where the mixed daytime air draws it down to a steady floor. Days that run high all the way round show as rings pushed outward. I chose the coil because the record's main pattern is a repeating daily rhythm, and laying one day on top of the next makes that rhythm visible.
