Sixty-One
Breaths

The air at one place, measured every hour for sixty-one days, wound into a coil: one turn for each day, the first day at the centre.

Every dot is one hour. The further a dot stands out from its turn, the more carbon dioxide was in the air.

Each day the coil swells on one side and lies flat on the other: the air fills in the still hours and is drawn down again in the open part of the day.
