# Sixty-One Piles

The record becomes what a harbour keeps on its own: a row of sixty-one wooden piles, one per day, each stained from below by that day's water. At every height on a pile, the darkness shows how many of the day's 24 hours the water stood at or above that height, measured from the dots in the original plot. The foot of each pile, always under water, is near black. The zone the tide works through fades to olive-grey, and a thin pale salt line marks the day's highest reach. I chose this form because the plot is too dense to read as a curve: about five hours share each pixel column. It is still exact about how long the water spent at each height on each day, and a pile records that same thing. Setting the days side by side shows the stained band shifting from day to day and the rare single readings far above it, without inventing an hourly line the picture can't support.
