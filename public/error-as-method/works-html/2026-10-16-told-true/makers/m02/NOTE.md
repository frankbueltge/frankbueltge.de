# Sixty-One Lines

The work cuts the record at every twenty-four hours and lays the sixty-one days down one under another, like the paper of a drum recorder, so that the same hour of every day sits in the same column. In the original strip a day is less than five pixels wide and its rhythm is lost in the texture; stacked, the daily swell becomes a shared shape that runs down the whole page, while the slow sag and recovery over weeks shows as the lines drifting lower and rising again. Because the source picture can only resolve four or five positions per day, those positions are drawn as dots and the curve between them is admitted to be drawn rather than measured; a pale wash carries the spread of the hours hidden inside each position, and the rare hours that leapt far above their neighbours are pulled out in red, so the record's two temperaments, the steady daily breath and the sudden brief surge, can both be seen.
