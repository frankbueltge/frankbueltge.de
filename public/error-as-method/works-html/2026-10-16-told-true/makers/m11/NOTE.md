# Fifty Minutes Late

The work cuts the sixty-one days of the record into single days and stacks them, each row followed by the next day, the way biologists draw an actogram. Darkness shows how far the level stands above its own surrounding 24 hours. In this layout a rhythm that keeps solar time runs straight down the page, while a tide that follows the moon would arrive about fifty minutes later each day and slide diagonally. The red line traces that lunar path, starting from the record's own first crest. I chose this form because it puts the record's most surprising feature in plain view: the dark band of each day's high stays close to the same hour for two months, drifting only a few hours, and never follows the moon's line. The page carries the original picture inside itself and redraws everything from those pixels, so it adds no data of its own.
