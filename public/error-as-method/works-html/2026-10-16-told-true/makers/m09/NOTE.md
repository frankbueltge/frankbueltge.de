# Sixty-One Turns

The flat strip shows the record as one long smudge. This work cuts it every twenty-four hours and stacks the days as 61 rows. Each row is double-plotted, the way chronobiologists draw an actogram: a day sits on the left with the next day beside it. Every hour is a small ink bar that grows with the value. Hours below the record's usual level shrink to a hairline. The few readings that jump far above their hour are kept as thin red pins. I chose this form because a period of 24.0 hours is the strongest structure in the file, and the long left-to-right line hides it. Folded, the daily rise and fall line up into a dark vertical band. The rows also show what changes from day to day: a quiet stretch in the first third, one day that never drops at night, and days whose peaks run late or early. The image is 300 pixels wide, so each column holds about five hours. The bars are therefore a local estimate smoothed over that width, not the 1,464 exact readings.
