# Sixty-One Breaths

The record is cut into its 61 days and stacked like the sheets of a seismograph. Each line is one day's 24 hours, from left to right, with a dot for each hourly value, and each later day is drawn over the one before it. The single strip of 1,464 dots hides the record's strongest feature: a slow swell and a fast fall that come back every 24 hours. Stacked, those swells line up into one ridge that runs down the middle of the page. You can see at once which days breathed deeply, which were flat, and the one hour that went far higher than the rest. Each line rises from its own day's lowest hour, so the slower drift of the air, from weather and weeks, would not bury the daily rhythm. That slower drift is kept separately as the thin trace at the left: one dot per day, further right meaning higher. One form shows two time scales, and neither one hides the other.
