# Sixty-One Turns

The record is wound into a spiral with exactly one day to each turn, so its sixty-one days lie nested from the centre outward like growth rings. Each ring's width comes from the record itself: the radius builds up as the values add up, so a high day lays down a wide ring and a low day a narrow one. The swing within each day pushes the ring out or pulls it in, in proportion to its size. Because the record rises and falls once every 24 hours, those swings land at the same angle on every turn. The rings crowd together on one side and spread apart on the other, the way a tree grows off-centre, and the daily rhythm shows as the shape of the whole figure rather than as 61 small waves. I chose the spiral because the material's strongest fact is that rhythm, which a left-to-right strip buries. Every dark pixel of the source picture is set back in at its own hour and height, so the occasional single high readings show up as dots flung out across the rings.
