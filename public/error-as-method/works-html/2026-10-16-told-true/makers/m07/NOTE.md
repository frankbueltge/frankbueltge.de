# Sixty-One Breaths

The 1,464 hourly values are wound into one continuous line that turns once every 24 hours, so the 61 days lie one inside the next like growth rings, the first day at the centre and the last on the outside. At each hour the line moves outward and gets thicker and darker in proportion to how much carbon dioxide was in the air, and moves inward and thins where there was less. I chose this form because the strongest thing in the record is not its slow drift but a daily rhythm: when I measured it, the plot repeated itself every 24 hours. In a spiral the same hour of every day falls at the same angle, so sixty-one separate nightly rises pile up on one side into a heavy, off-centre bulge, and the daytime drawdown leaves the opposite side pale and thin. The whorl breathes out on one side and in on the other. The slower changes over days and weeks, and the occasional sharp spikes, show up as rings that wander and cross instead of nesting neatly.
