# Solar Time, Moon Lines

The 1,464 hourly values, recovered from the dots in the picture, are cut into 24-hour lengths and stacked, one day per row and each row showing that day and the next (a double-plotted actogram, the form biologists use to see whether a rhythm keeps to the clock). Each hour is a tile shaded from pale to dark by its value, and the few hours that go past the darkest shade put up a thin red stroke whose length is how far beyond they go. Over the tiles run blue lines where a lunar tide's high waters would land, 50 minutes later each day. I chose this form because folding the record at 24 hours tests the one thing a tide must do. A record that followed the moon would lie along the blue diagonals. This one forms dark bands that stand straight up and keep the sun's hour all through the two months, and the picture lets you see that disagreement without having to take my word for it.
