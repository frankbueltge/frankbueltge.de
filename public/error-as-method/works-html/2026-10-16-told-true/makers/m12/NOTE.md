# Breath over a Floor

The record does two things at once: every day the carbon dioxide rises and falls back, and underneath, the level it falls back to drifts slowly over the two months. In the original plot the two are tangled into one ragged band. This work pulls them apart. I cut the 1,464 dots into days at each day's lowest point, so every daily rise stays whole. In the upper register, each of those 62 pieces (a short first piece, then whole days) is set down on one common line, measured from its own lowest hours. The days then stand side by side like breaths, and you can compare their size: most are alike, and a few reach far higher. The lower register keeps the whole record at its true height, faint, and draws one rust line through each day's lowest hours, the floor all those breaths stand on. That floor sinks through the first half of the record and climbs back by the end. I chose the split because it separates what the place adds each day from the air it starts from. I kept the dots as dots, taken pixel by pixel from the picture, because the picture cannot say in what order the hours fall within a day, and a line would claim that it could.
