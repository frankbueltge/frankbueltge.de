# Sixty-One Turns

The record is pressed as a record: a dark disc with a single groove that spirals from the rim towards the label, one full turn for every day, so 1,464 hours make sixty-one turns. Where the measured value is high the groove turns pale and wider, and it is pushed slightly outward. The hours that jumped far above the rest of their day are drawn as short bright scratches across the grooves. I chose the spiral because the one thing the picture says most clearly is that the quantity rises and falls once every 24 hours. If a day is one turn, that rhythm stops being a wiggle along a line and becomes a direction: the daily highs all land on the same side of the disc and make a pale wedge, like light catching vinyl, and the scratches gather in that wedge too. The slower weather of the record (a dim stretch of about three weeks, then a sudden brighter band) appears as rings, the way it would in a tree.
