Sixty-One Breaths

Carbon dioxide in the air at one station, hour by hour for sixty-one days, wound into a spiral. The first hour is at the centre, straight up. Each turn clockwise is one day, so the same hour of every day lies along the same spoke.

Once a day the level climbs and falls back. Wound so that every turn is one day, that rhythm lines up: the disc darkens on one side and pales on the other, from the first day to the last. The rings across it are slower weather, whole days when the air ran richer or thinner. Black points mark the hours when the level jumped far above its neighbours.

The record carries no clock, so the spiral starts at its own first hour, not at midnight.
