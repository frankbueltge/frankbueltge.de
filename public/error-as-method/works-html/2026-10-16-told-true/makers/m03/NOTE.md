# Sixty-One Turns

The record is wound into a coil: it starts at the centre and makes one full turn for every 24 hours, so the 61 days lie one inside the next like the growth rings of a tree. Each hour is drawn as a short arc held for exactly its hour, and the arc's weight is the size of the value. Reading the source picture showed that its main event is a 24-hour rhythm, repeated about 61 times with no weekly beat, under a slow drift of whole days rising and sinking, with a few lone hours that leap well above the rest. A straight line spends all of its length on the drift and buries the rhythm in a fine comb. Winding the record at the length of a day stacks every hour of the day on the same bearing. The daily swell then becomes one heavy side of the disc, the drift becomes lighter and darker bands of rings, and the lone spikes become short dark strokes. The values are estimates read back out of the ink of a 300-pixel picture, where each pixel column covers about five hours. That is why the coil is drawn as held steps rather than as a precise curve.
