I made **"Sixty-One Turns"**: the 61-day record wound into one coil, one turn for every 24 hours, with each hour drawn as a short arc whose line weight is its value. It is written as `/tmp/claude-0/s130/mk/m03/work/index.html` (8 KB, fully self-contained) with `/tmp/claude-0/s130/mk/m03/work/NOTE.md`. In a headless-browser check at 1100×800 it loaded nothing external and threw no errors. Scripts, the reconstructed data and preview images are in `/tmp/claude-0/s130/mk/m03/scratch`.

**What I did first.** I looked at the picture, then printed it as a grid of pixel darkness values. That showed what the file can and can't give: the dots are 1–2 px blobs, and each pixel column covers about five hours. So individual hourly values can't be read back exactly; what the picture really holds is the spread of values within each five-hour window.

**From the file to the form.**
- **Measuring.** I measured the darkness profile of every column and looked for repeating periods. One period dominates everything else.
  - It first came out at 23.55 h. That turned out to be because the dots only run from x≈3 to x≈296, the usual plot margin, not across the full 300 px.
  - With that corrected (about 4.98 hours per pixel) the period is **24.06 h**, an ordinary day.
  - There is a weaker 12 h component in how densely the dots cluster, and no 7-day cycle.
- **Rebuilding the hours.** I rebuilt 1,464 hourly estimates from the ink. Within each column, the hours get values spread across that column's ink, ordered so the series runs on smoothly. The 28 isolated stray dots are put back as single-hour spikes. Redrawn as a dot plot, this matches the original's envelope, spikes and drift well.
- **Choosing the form.** I tried a straight line and three spiral treatments (dots displaced outward, overlapping arcs, dot size). The dots kept lining up into 24 spokes and the arcs blurred the rhythm. A coil whose line weight is the value was the one where everything showed:
  - The daily swell becomes one heavy side of the disc.
  - The slow drift becomes lighter and darker bands of rings, like tree growth rings.
  - The spikes become short dark strokes.
- **A short caption** sits in the lower-left corner. It is the only text on the page.

**Things the commissioner should know.**
- The values in the work are estimates recovered from a 300×90 JPEG, not the original numbers. Where values fall inside each five-hour window is accurate, but their exact order within the window is inferred. That is why the coil is drawn as held steps rather than a smooth curve.
- Value is shown relative to the record's own minimum and maximum, because the picture gives no units or scale.
- The coil starts at the top. That is the first hour of the record, not a known clock time.

**My view of what was measured (moderate confidence at best).** It is something driven by the sun rather than by people. That rests on:
- A clean 24 h period with no weekly pattern, which argues against traffic, electricity use or other human activity.
- An uneven daily shape. The rise is fairly sharp, the high stretch lasts about 7 h, and the fall is slower. The low stretch lasts about 10 h, and its values sit tightly together.
- Daytime values that scatter much more than night values.

The most likely candidates are air temperature, ground-level ozone or near-surface wind speed. The lone single-hour spikes, the largest about four times the band's daily swing above it, argue against air temperature, which doesn't jump like that for one hour. They point more towards ozone or wind gusts, or simply sensor glitches. There is also a slow drift across the two months: the level is higher in the first week, lower in days 9–27, and jumps abruptly around day 28. That looks like weather or seasonal change, but I can't pin it down further.
