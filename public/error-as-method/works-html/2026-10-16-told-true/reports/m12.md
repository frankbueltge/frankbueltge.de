I made a work called "Breath over a Floor", a single static inline SVG with no scripts and nothing loaded from outside. It's in `/tmp/claude-0/s130/mk/m12/work/index.html` (about 520 KB, laid out for 1100 × 800), with `/tmp/claude-0/s130/mk/m12/work/NOTE.md` beside it. No browser or SVG renderer was available, so I checked the layout only with a rough preview I drew myself in Python. That preview didn't draw the text, so the text has not been seen in a real browser.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at it, then read every pixel into Python as text dumps and 5× enlargements. The dots fill x = 2 to 297 and y = 5 to 84. That works out to about 4.85 pixels per day and about 5 hours in each pixel column.

**How I got to the form.**
- **Trying to rebuild the hourly series failed.** I first tried to trace one path through the dots, one reading per hour. On the enlargement I could see the path hugging the bottom and missing both the daily rises and the spikes. Within one day the dots sit stacked in the same few columns, so the picture does not record which hour comes before which inside a day. I dropped the series and worked from the 2,994 dark pixels themselves, each with its position and darkness.
- **The daily rhythm is certain.** A frequency check on the top and bottom edges of the dot cloud shows one strong period of 4.8 pixels, which is 24 hours at this scale. The top and bottom edges move in step: the whole level rises and falls each day.
- **The form splits two things the plot tangles together.** I cut the record at each day's low point, giving 62 pieces: a short ~4-hour piece at the start, then whole days.
  - Upper register: each piece is set on one common rust line, measured from its own lowest hours. It shows what each day adds.
  - Lower register: the whole record at true height, drawn faint, with a rust line joining each day's lowest hours. It shows the floor the days stand on.

**What you should know.**
- **No units.** There are no numbers, so nothing in the work is in ppm. All heights are relative to the picture.
- **Pixel steps are visible.** The dots fall in visible rows because each one is a pixel of a 90-pixel-tall JPEG, enlarged. JPEG compression smudges a little, and I treated faint pixels as faint ink.
- **The floor is my choice.** It is the low 8% of each day's ink, a cutoff I set. The upper register's baseline depends on that choice.
- **The day cuts are my choice too.** I placed them at the low point of the fitted daily cycle, not at the record's own day boundaries. I don't know the start time, so I can't give clock times.

**My view of what was measured.** I think this is carbon dioxide at a site over land with vegetation or other local sources nearby, not clean background air. Each day it rises well above a floor and falls back, and the rise is about two to three times the drift of the floor itself. A few days shoot much higher: the largest rise is about 70 pixels against a typical 20 to 45. The usual cause of this pattern is plants and soil breathing out at night, with the gas trapped near the ground, and daytime uptake and mixing pulling it back down. The picture can't confirm that the peaks are at night, because it has no clock. The floor sinks from about 22 to about 10 pixels over roughly the first 25 days and climbs back to about 25 by the end. That could fit a growing season fading, but I hold that loosely; nothing in the file dates it.
