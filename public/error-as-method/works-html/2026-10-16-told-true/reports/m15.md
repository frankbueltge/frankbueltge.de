I made **Sixty-One Turns**, a single still image drawn on canvas. The data is built into the page, which loads nothing else. A headless render at 1100×800 made no network requests and showed no console errors.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at it, then converted it to greyscale and printed it as an ASCII map of the pixels. Ink runs from column 2 to column 297. A frequency analysis of where the ink sits in each column showed one strong peak at about 4.82 px, which is 24 hours at 1,464 values across that width. Other rhythms are weak. There is little at 12 hours and no clear 7-day (weekly) rhythm in the daily averages.

**How I got from it to the form.**
- **Reading the dots:** I found 597 separate dark dots. That is fewer than 1,464 because dots overlap. So I estimated one value per hour as the ink-weighted height around that hour's x position (a Gaussian window of about 0.75 px). Hours that sat well above their local level I flagged as outliers: 73 dot positions in 35 clusters.
- **The form:** the file is called `record`, so I pressed it as a record. One groove spirals from the rim inward, one turn per day, 61 turns. The groove gets paler and wider where the value is high and moves slightly outward. Outlier hours are short bright scratches across the grooves. A small notch at twelve o'clock marks where the record begins.
- **What the disc shows:** because one day is one turn, the daily rhythm becomes a direction. All the daily highs fall on one side and make a pale wedge, and the scratches gather in that wedge. The slower changes show up as rings. Days 8–27 form a dim stretch, and a sudden brighter band starts at day 28. Day 21 is a low day with almost no daily swing.

**What the person who commissioned this should know.**
- The image cannot give back the 1,464 values exactly. Each pixel column covers about 5 hours and the dots are about 2 px wide.
- The hourly series is therefore a smoothed estimate and dampens the daily swing somewhat. Some outliers may be JPEG artefacts or merged dots.
- The groove's absolute level is relative to the picture's pixels, not to any real unit.
- Where the disc starts (twelve o'clock) is arbitrary, because nothing in the file says what time of day the record begins.
- The label carries three short lines of text: the title, "one turn, one day" and "1,464 hours, outside in".

**My view of what was measured (tentative).** I think it is an outdoor quantity driven by the sun and the weather, and my best single guess is wind speed. It rests on four things in the file:
1. **A daily rhythm:** a smooth cycle of exactly 24 hours.
2. **Weather-like changes:** shifts over several days, including a three-week low and an abrupt jump. That looks like weather, not like human activity, because there is no weekly rhythm.
3. **No zero floor:** night values vary from day to day, so it is not sunlight itself.
4. **The spikes come at the peak:** almost every high outlier falls within about 2–3 hours of the daily peak. Measured from the record's start, the peak is around hours 11–12 and the low around hours 20–23. The quantity seems to jump in short bursts mainly when it is highest.

That last point argues against plain air temperature, which rarely jumps to twice its daily range for a single hour. Gusty midday wind fits it, and so could something like ozone or another concentration. The fact that the daily peak comes about 11–12 hours after the start would fit early afternoon if the record begins at local midnight, but nothing in the file confirms that.
