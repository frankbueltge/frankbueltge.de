I made "Sixty-One Turns": the CO2 record wound into a spiral with one turn per day. The page and its note are written, and the page renders cleanly at 1100 × 800 with no network or file requests.

**What I did first.** I looked at the image, then decoded its pixels and printed them as a darkness grid. The data covers columns 2 to 297. The dots are about 2 px across and overlap heavily. Taking local darkness peaks gives only about 595 separate dots out of the 1,464 values, because runs of nearby values merge into vertical streaks. So the exact hourly values can't be recovered from this file. What survives is roughly one column of value-spread for every 4 to 5 hours.

**How I got to the form.**
- I measured the periodicity of the upper and middle edges of the dot band, column by column, after removing the slow drift.
- One period dominates by far: about 4.83 px, roughly 3.5 times stronger than the next one. With 296 px of data over 61 days, one day is about 4.85 px, so this is a 24-hour cycle.
- Its phase holds steady between the first and second halves of the record, which is how I know it is real and not an artefact.
- Folded at that period, the cycle is lopsided. Values sit at a low floor for part of the day, then rise into upward streaks and isolated high spikes. The excursions are almost all upward.

That led to the spiral: angle is time of day, each turn is one day, and a mark's distance outward is the value, with colour going from slate (less) to rust (more). Winding at exactly the daily period stacks the 61 daily highs on one side, so the rhythm becomes the shape of the disc: rust on the upper half, dense slate on the lower.

I tried two drawings before settling:
- **Arcs:** drawing each pixel as an arc across its ~5 hours smeared the rhythm into plain rings.
- **Dots:** they showed the cycle, but the turns dissolved into a scatter.

The final version draws every dark pixel (3,684 of them) as a short dash along its turn. Each pixel's time is refined below one pixel using the ink in its neighbouring columns, and its darkness sets the dash's opacity and width. The pixel data is embedded in the page as plain digits.

**What you should know.**
- **Not a data reconstruction.** There are no hourly values, units or absolute levels in it. Up and down are only relative positions in the plot.
- **Inferred settings.** The day length (4.83 px) and the angle where the daily high sits are my own estimates from the image. The phase error is small, but it does exist.
- **Uneven density.** Outer turns are longer, so their marks are spread more thinly than the inner ones. This comes from the spiral, not the data.
- **No clock time.** The work deliberately doesn't say which side of the disc is night. I changed the caption from "night after night" to "day after day" for that reason.

**My view of what was measured, which is inference only.** I think this is a station near the ground over land with vegetation, in a growing season. The evidence:
- a strong, regular 24-hour cycle in CO2;
- a fairly flat low floor for part of each day, which fits daytime uptake by plants and a well-mixed air layer;
- one-sided upward build-ups and spikes once a day, which fit CO2 collecting overnight under a shallow, still layer of air near the ground;
- a slower drift of several days to weeks in the baseline, lower in the middle-to-late part of the record, which fits changing weather systems.

A remote clean-air site would show almost no daily cycle, so this is not one. The file can't tell me the location, the season or the absolute levels.
