I made a single still image, "Sixty-One Breaths": one line wound into a spiral that makes one turn per day, starting at the centre and working outward for 61 turns. I could only preview it in a PIL re-drawing of the same points, because no browser or SVG renderer was available.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at the image, then read it pixel by pixel and printed it as ASCII to see where the dots actually sit. The data occupies columns 2 to 297 and roughly rows 27 to 84. That is about 4.95 values per pixel column, and the dots overlap, so the 1,464 values cannot be recovered exactly.

**How I got to the form.**
- I measured the ink-weighted height of each column and checked whether the curve repeats. It does: there is a very strong repeat every 4.8 to 4.9 pixels, which is exactly 24 hours at this scale (correlation about 0.7 at whole-day lags). The daily rhythm is the dominant structure in the record.
- I built an approximate hourly series. Each hour gets the pixel column it falls in. Within a column, the dot heights (as quantiles of the ink) are handed out to that column's 4 or 5 hours, matched by rank to the average daily profile.
  - The commissioner should know this step is partly circular. It slightly enforces the daily shape inside each block of about 5 hours. Anything finer than about 5 hours is my reconstruction, not something read from the file.
- I lightly smoothed the series and split it into a slow part (24-hour running mean) and a daily part.
- In the spiral, the same hour of each day falls at the same angle. The radius gets a small push from the slow part and a larger one from the daily part. Line thickness and darkness grow with the value.
- Result: the 61 nightly rises stack on one side (lower right) into a heavy, off-centre bulge, and the opposite side stays pale and thin. Multi-day drift and the occasional spikes show up as rings that wander and cross.
- The line is redrawn smoothly between hourly points, so it should not be read as exact. The page has no units or numbers, because the file has none.

**What I think was measured.** CO2 at a station near the ground over land with active vegetation, during the growing season. This is an inference; I looked nothing up. It rests on three things in the file:
1. **A large daily cycle.** Its peak-to-trough is about 12 px against a total spread of about 57 px. A remote background site such as a mountain, ocean or polar station would be nearly flat. A big daily swing points to plants drawing CO2 down by day, and to respiration building up under a shallow night-time layer of air.
2. **A lopsided shape.** The values sit on a dense, flat floor, with taller, sparse excursions upward. That fits a clean daytime level plus nightly build-ups and local plumes.
3. **No clear trend over the two months.** The daily means rise and fall without a steady direction.

On timing: in hours counted from the first value, the average daily peak falls around hours 9 to 12 and the trough around hours 20 to 23. If the record starts at local midnight, that peak is late for a night-time build-up, which normally peaks before dawn. So the record probably does not start at local midnight; it may be in UTC for a station west of Greenwich. That part is speculation. I have no way of knowing where or when the record was taken.
