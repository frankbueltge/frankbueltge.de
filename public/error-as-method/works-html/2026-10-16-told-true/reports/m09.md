I made **"Sixty-One Turns"**, a still image in `/tmp/claude-0/s130/mk/m09/work/index.html`. It folds the record into 61 day-rows so that its daily rhythm shows. `NOTE.md` is in the same folder. I opened the page in a headless browser at 1100 × 800 and it renders as intended. The page is self-contained (about 21 KB of inline canvas and JavaScript) and loads no other file.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at it, then dumped it pixel by pixel as a text map of how dark each pixel is. The ink runs from about x = 2.8 to x = 296.5. With 1,464 hours over roughly 294 pixels, each pixel column covers about 5 hours and holds about 5 overlapping dots. So the individual hourly values cannot be recovered exactly.

**How I got to the form.**
1. I took the darkness-weighted average height of each column. I then checked which repeat periods explain its variation.
   - One period stands far above everything else: **24.0 hours**, with a peak about 30 times stronger than any other.
   - There is no 7-day cycle.
   - It is not the tidal 24.84-hour period.
2. I picked out the 1,206 distinguishable dots, each with its time in hours, its height and its darkness.
3. From those dots I estimated a value for every hour, smoothed over about ±2 hours to match the 5-hour column width.
4. I drew the result as a double-plotted actogram, the chart chronobiologists use for daily rhythms. Each of the 61 rows shows one day of 24 hourly bars, with the next day repeated beside it.
   - Bars grow with the value. Hours below the record's 30th percentile shrink to a hairline.
   - A dot standing far above its hour's estimate (about 12 cases) is drawn as a thin red pin at its actual height.
   - A small title line sits at the bottom.

My first two versions plotted the raw dots and soft smears. They produced a diagonal pattern that came from the 5-hour columns, not from the data, so I dropped them.

**What the commissioner should know.**
- The bars are estimates at about 5-hour resolution, not the 1,464 true readings. The hour-to-hour detail inside each day comes from smoothing.
- "Hour 0" is simply the first sample. Its clock time is unknown, so the tick marks are relative, every 6 hours.
- The vertical scale has no units. The red pins are single dots read from a compressed JPEG. Most are probably real, but one or two could be compression artefacts.

**My view of what was measured (tentative).** Probably a natural quantity driven by the sun, most plausibly an outdoor weather variable such as near-surface air temperature, or something closely tied to it. This rests on four things in the file:
- **The rhythm:** the period is 24.0 hours, with no weekly cycle that would point to human activity and no tidal period.
- **Uneven halves of the day:** the low phase lasts longer and its dots pile up, while the high phase is shorter and its values change faster. That fits flat nights and sharper daytime peaks.
- **Day-to-day changes:** the overall level and the size of the daily swing shift over several days. There is a quiet stretch in the first third, and around day 28 there is a day that stays high through the night. That looks like weather.
- **Rare spikes:** a handful of isolated readings sit far above the rest. These could be gusts, glitches or short events. They make me less sure it is temperature, and wind speed is a possible alternative.
