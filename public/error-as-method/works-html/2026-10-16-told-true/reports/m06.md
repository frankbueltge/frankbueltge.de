I made "Sixty-One Breaths": the 1,464 hourly values wound into a spiral of 61 turns, one turn per day. It's a single still image drawn with canvas in a page that loads nothing else. The finished work is `/tmp/claude-0/s130/mk/m06/work/index.html` and the note is `/tmp/claude-0/s130/mk/m06/work/NOTE.md`. Working files are in `/tmp/claude-0/s130/mk/m06/scratch`. I rendered the page at 1100×800 in headless Chromium: it draws fully, with no script errors and no network requests.

**What I did first.** I looked at the image, then read its pixels with Python (PIL) and pulled out the dark pixels column by column. Only x≈2–297 has ink. Each pixel column holds about 5 hours, so this file can't give back the 1,464 individual values. What it does give is the spread of values in each column, at about 5-hour resolution.

**How I got to the form.**
1. **Finding the day.** The strongest repeat in the column medians is 4.8 px, which is one day.
2. **Fixing a drift.** My first pixel-to-hour mapping made the daily phase drift by about 7 hours across the record, and the daily pattern vanished when I wound it into turns. Fitting the plot's width to about 294.3 px (≈4.975 hours per pixel) gave a phase that stays put, and that mapping is what the work uses.
3. **The hourly series.** I placed each column's median (darker pixels counting more) at the hour that column covers, joined them with straight lines into 1,464 hourly values, and scaled the colors to the 2nd–98th percentile.
4. **The jumps.** Of the 296 columns, 38 have a top dot well above the median (14 px or more). These are drawn as black points.
5. **The spiral.** It runs at exactly 24 hours per turn, from pale (less) to dark (more), with the first hour at the centre. Because every day lands on the same spokes, the disc has one dark side and one pale side. Slower changes over several days show as rings.

**Things you should know:**
- **Interpolated, not traced.** The colors come from a line joining column medians, so anything shorter than about 5 hours is smoothed away. The black points are the only marks of single sharp events.
- **Hour positions are fitted.** Where each hour falls rests on the fitted ~294 px width, which I inferred from the daily rhythm, not read from the file.
- **No clock time.** The record has no clock, so the spiral starts at its own first hour. The wall text says so and doesn't name day or night.

**My view of what was measured.** The file shows near-surface CO₂ at a station strongly shaped by local land, probably vegetated, rather than a clean background site. This rests on three things in the file:
- **A large daily cycle with a stable phase.** Averaged over all days, values are highest around record hour 12 and lowest around hours 23–1. The swing is about half of the 2nd–98th-percentile range.
- **Rare upward jumps.** Sharp spikes go up, sometimes far above the band, and almost never down. That fits overnight build-up in a shallow layer of still air, or a nearby source.
- **Shifts over several days.** Daily averages move a lot. Days 10–27 run lower, and around day 28 there is a sudden jump to the highest level in the record. That looks like a change of air mass (weather), not a trend.

If the record begins at midnight local time, a peak around noon would be unusual for this kind of site, where CO₂ normally peaks before dawn. A record kept in UTC at a station roughly 6–8 hours west of Greenwich, such as in the Americas, would put the peak near dawn and the low in the late afternoon. That is the classic pattern of plants breathing out at night and drawing CO₂ down in daylight, but it is speculation. I can't tell the season or the absolute concentrations from the file.
