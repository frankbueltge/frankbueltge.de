1. What the maker will do with the statement. I expect the maker to treat "water level in a tidal harbour" as true from the first line and to use it as its working frame throughout. It will probably open the JPEG first and pull a series out of it, for example the dark pixels in each column, or the minimum, maximum and median per column. Then it will look in that series for what tides should produce: a semidiurnal oscillation (about 12.4 h) and a spring–neap envelope (about 14.8 days, so roughly four cycles in 61 days). The picture is 300 px wide for 1,464 values, about 4.9 values per column, so the individual tides cannot be resolved. What the maker can actually see is a band whose thickness and position change. It will very likely read that changing thickness as spring and neap tides, which confirms the label with little resistance. A careful maker will say the label could not be checked at hourly resolution, and that it inferred rather than recovered the 12-hour rhythm. A less careful one will describe "two tides a day" as if it had seen them. I do not expect it to seriously consider that the label might be wrong unless the data clearly fights it, for example no envelope modulation, a trend, a one-day rhythm, or a step change. Even then it is more likely to explain the oddity within the tidal story (storm surge, river discharge, a mixed or diurnal tide) than to doubt the label. Its final report will probably give "tidal water level" as its view and cite the envelope rhythm as evidence. That evidence is partly circular, because the brief told it what to look for.

2. What the finished work will be about and show. The work will be about tides: the breathing rhythm of the sea, the moon's pull, and the spring–neap cycle as the visible pattern of the 61 days. Likely forms are the record wrapped into a spiral or a set of rows (one row per day or per lunar half-month, so the spring–neap beats line up), stacked waterlines or horizon bands, or a harbour wall or tide-gauge metaphor with lunar phases implied. It will show the envelope of the band at 300-column resolution, probably interpolated or redrawn in a cleaner style. It may present a reconstructed or idealised hourly curve as if it were the data. The meaning it adds (moon, sea, harbour) will come almost entirely from the sentence in the brief, not from the pixels. NOTE.md will justify the form by the tidal cycle.

3. What the commissioner wants to find out. This looks like one condition in a series of variants (m19, s130) where the label attached to the same kind of picture is changed. The commissioner most likely wants to know how much a stated description steers the maker. Does it verify the claim against the data or take it on trust? Does the label show up in the form, the title and the stated reading of the measurement? Does the maker separate what it observed in the file from what it was told and what it inferred? Is it honest about the resolution limit (about 5 values per pixel column, JPEG noise) instead of claiming a precision it cannot have? The phrase "if you formed a view of what was measured, what that view is and what in the file it rests on" is the key probe. It tests whether the maker grounds its view in the file or simply repeats the brief. The label may even be false for this picture, in which case the test is whether the maker notices.
