What came back

The people barely told true from false. On the 56 scored statements their mean was 0.26 of a point higher on the true ones. The machine's six instances, asked as themselves, told them apart by 4.2 points. Asked where the people stood, eighteen fresh instances did not stay with their own knowledge. They put nearly every statement near 4, where the people were. That broke the one-step tether that four earlier experiments had found. But the order inside their account was still their own: it follows the machine's belief (ρ 0.87) far more than the people's ratings (ρ 0.38). Where the machine thinks an error is obvious, it puts the people low. The people gave “Yitzhak Rabin, the fifth prime minister of Tunisia” 4.2 and the machine's account gave it 2.1. Telling the instances two of the people's answers changed nothing, whether the two came from the middle of the people's range or from where the instances disagreed most.
