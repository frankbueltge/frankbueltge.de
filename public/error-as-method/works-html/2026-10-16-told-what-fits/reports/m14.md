I made "Day Rings", a spiral of 61 turns drawn as a tree's cross-section. The page is `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m14/work/index.html` (inline SVG built by plain JS, no outside resources) and the note is `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m14/work/NOTE.md`. I checked the script for syntax errors and looked at Python renders that copy its drawing. I never opened the page itself in a browser, because there wasn't one here.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at the image, then measured its dark pixels column by column. The dots run from x=2 to x=297 and from y=5 to y=84. An autocorrelation of the lower edge of the dots repeats every 4.85 px. That is exactly 296 px ÷ 61 days, so the strong cycle is daily.

**How I got to the form.**
- **Rebuilding the hourly values.** Each pixel column holds about 4.9 hours of dots, and they overlap, so the exact hourly values can't be recovered. For each hour I took its column and split that column's dark pixels evenly by darkness. I ordered the hours up or down to follow the direction of the neighbouring columns, then smoothed lightly. The result is 1,464 values, accurate to about a pixel (roughly 1/80 of the range), embedded in the page.
- **The form.** Each day is one turn, so each hour of the day always sits at the same angle. Each turn steps a fixed amount outward. On top of that, the line moves out when the value is high and in when it is low, and gets thicker and darker as the trunk contracts. I tried a few settings in Python previews before writing the SVG.

**What you should know.**
- The values are a reconstruction from a compressed 300×90 picture, not the original data.
- The steady outward step from turn to turn is not in the data. It only stands for time passing, like growth rings, and is not real growth.
- Size changes within a turn are greatly exaggerated. No units or scale were given, so none are shown.
- The first hour of the record is at 12 o'clock. I don't know the real clock time, so the angles are not labelled as times of day.
- The only words on the page are the title and a one-line caption in the lower right.

**My view of what was measured.** It looks like a dendrometer (a gauge for small changes in trunk size) recording a trunk swelling and shrinking with its water content during a growing season. Three things support this:
- **A strong daily cycle.** The daily high falls around record-hours 23–3 and the low around hours 11–14, about 11 hours apart. That fits a trunk that shrinks while the leaves draw water by day and refills at night. If the record starts at midnight, the high would be later than the dawn peak I'd expect, so the record probably starts a few hours after midnight. That is an inference, not something the file shows.
- **Wet spells.** There are sudden jumps to a high level where the daily swing almost vanishes. The clearest is around days 39–41, where day 40's swing is about 0.08 of the range against a usual 0.5–0.6. Smaller ones appear around days 3–4, 7, 12, 47 and 58. I read these as rain refilling the trunk on overcast days.
- **Drying in between.** After each jump the level slides down over one to three weeks, which looks like the soil drying out.

I can't see any clear net growth across the 61 days.
