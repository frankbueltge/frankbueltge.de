**What I did first.** I looked at the image, then printed each pixel column's dark pixels as text and made enlarged crops. The dots run from x 2 to 296 and y 5 to 84. One pixel column holds about 4.9 hours, so each day is only about 5 pixels wide. That is why the days look like ladders of dots: the value swings a long way within one or two pixels of width.

**From the file to the form.**
- **Rebuilding the hours.** The JPEG does not hold the 1,464 values, so I rebuilt them. I traced one continuous path through the dark pixels, one point per hour at its known x position, with a penalty for sharp changes in direction. When I re-plotted the result it looked very close to the original. (A first, simpler trace jumped about and did not.) The values are only approximate. They come in whole pixel rows of the source, 77 distinct levels, and you can see that stepping in the strokes.
- **A form I dropped.** I tried a spiral with one turn per day. The 24 hours became 24 radial spokes that buried the record, so I dropped it.
- **The final form.** One column per day, with 24 short strokes at each hour's height. Each stroke is coloured by its hour within the 24-hour block: night-blue at hours 0 and 23, amber at hour 12.
- **What's in the data.** The page holds my rebuilt series, not the picture. The only text is a title, a three-line caption and a small colour key for the hours.

**Things you should know.**
- The values have no units. The source had no scale, so the page has none either, and the heights show only relative change.
- The colours rest on an assumption: that the first value was taken at about local midnight. Nothing in the file says so. I inferred it because the lowest value fell at hours 11–12 of the block on almost every day, which fits midnight best. If the start time is different, the colours are shifted in time, though the rhythm itself is unchanged.

**My view of what was measured.** I think this is a station over land with plants growing nearby, during the growing season, probably summer. It rests on three things in the file:
- **A strong, steady daily cycle.** When I lined up the 61 days, values stay high from about hour 22 to hour 4 and fall to a low at hours 11–12 on almost every day. The daily swing is roughly a third to half of the whole range. That matches carbon dioxide building up at night from plant and soil respiration in still air, then being drawn down by photosynthesis and daytime mixing.
- **Three sudden lifts.** Around days 3–4, 40 and 59, the whole level jumps up and the daily swing nearly disappears for a day or two. At day 40 it is only about 4 pixel rows. I'd read these as changes in the weather, such as a new air mass or overcast, windy days.
- **Slow sinking.** After each lift, the level slowly falls over one to three weeks while the daily swing grows again. That fits fair weather and plants actively taking up carbon.

I can't tell where or when the station is. Since the picture has no scale, I can't say whether the values are high or low in absolute terms.
