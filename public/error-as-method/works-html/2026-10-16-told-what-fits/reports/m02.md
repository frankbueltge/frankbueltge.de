I finished the work. It's called **Each Day a Little Lower**. The page is `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m02/work/index.html` and the note is `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m02/work/NOTE.md`. The page is a single static SVG with the data built into it. It uses no script and loads nothing from outside. I had no browser to check it in, so I checked it with a raster preview drawn from the same geometry. That preview doesn't draw the text, so I haven't seen the title, caption or scale line at 1100 × 800.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at it, then enlarged it and read every column of pixels to find where the dark dots sit. The dots run from x = 2 to x = 296, which is about 4.96 hours per pixel column. The lowest dot in each column reaches a peak every 4.85 columns or so (59 times). That works out to 24 hours, so there is a clear daily cycle. Each day is mostly spent near its high value, with one dip lasting a few hours. The daily dip is missing on two days, and those are the two days with the biggest sudden rises.

**How I got to the form.** At this resolution there is less than one pixel per hour, so the 1,464 hourly values can't be recovered exactly. I estimated them in `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m02/scratch/recon.py`:
- Each column's dot heights are spread over the hours that fall in that column.
- Within a column, the values are put in rising or falling order, whichever joins up more smoothly with the columns on either side.

I then cut the record into 61 days and stacked them down the page, one line per day running across 24 hours. Each line has paper fill below it, so a day sitting higher than earlier days covers them. Lining the days up this way puts all the daily dips into one valley down the middle of the page. That valley gets deeper during each slow decline. Days whose highest value beat the day before by a set margin are drawn in red. In the picture they read as flattened lids over the rows above them.

**What you should know.**
- The shape of each line within a day is my reconstruction, not the measured values. The level of each day and the timing of its dip are reliable.
- The days start at whatever hour the record begins, not at midnight. I have no clock time.
- The red marks a rule I chose, nothing more. Nine days meet it, though some red lines are partly hidden under later ones.
- Working files are in `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m02/scratch` (`build.py`, `vals.json`, `preview.png`).

**My view of what was measured.** I think it is most likely a water level or flow: a small stream's discharge or stage, or a shallow water table. The daily dip would come from plants drawing water during the day. This is a guess, based on four things in the file:
- The record has a sawtooth shape. There are sudden rises around days 4, 24–25, 40 and 59–60, each followed by a long, smooth fall over one to two weeks. That fits rain events followed by drainage.
- There is one dip every 24 hours, and it disappears on the days with sudden rises.
- The dip gets deeper the longer it has been since the last rise, which fits drier, sunnier spells.
- The rises are fast and the falls are slow. Physical storage that fills quickly and drains slowly behaves like this; temperature-like quantities usually don't.

Soil moisture, or a reservoir with a daily draw, would also fit. I can't rule them out.
