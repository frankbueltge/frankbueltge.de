I made **Sixty-One Rings**: the 61 days of the record drawn as the growth rings of a cut trunk, seen end-on. The files are `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m05/work/index.html` (a single still canvas image with the data inside it and no outside resources) and `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m05/work/NOTE.md`.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at it, then enlarged it and printed each column's pixel darkness as text. A cycle is plain: roughly one sawtooth every 4.9 pixels, which is one per day (300 px ÷ 61 days). Each column holds about 5 hours of values.

**How I got from it to the form.**
- **Rebuilding the hourly values failed.** I tried to trace the 1,464 hourly values back out of the picture with a path-tracing method (Viterbi). It smoothed away the daily lows, so I dropped it and did not use it anywhere.
- **What I used instead.** I cut the ink into 61 equal-width day strips. For each day I took two things: the darkness down the strip (where that day's dots lay), and its highest and lowest inked rows (the day's largest and smallest radius).
- **How the rings are drawn.**
  - Each ring is as wide as that day's swing between smallest and largest.
  - Inside the ring, a smoothed version of the day's darkness becomes the grain: dark where the hours lay close together, pale where the trunk moved fast. The day's largest radius is at the ring's outer edge.
  - Days whose middle value sits higher are tinted warmer.
  - The first day is at the pith and the last at the bark. A small red dot on the bark stands for the point where the instrument sat.
- **Checking it.** No browser was available, so I ran the page's own drawing code in Node and looked at the image it produced. The red dot and the caption weren't in that test image, so I haven't seen them on screen.

**What you should know.**
- The days are fixed 24-hour cuts starting at the first value. The time of day at that start is unknown, so a ring boundary may not fall at midnight.
- The highs and lows come from a small JPEG and are only good to a pixel or two. There are no units, so ring widths show relative size only.
- There is a small caption in the lower left with the title and one sentence. Remove it if the work should be wordless.
- My working files, including the preview image, are in `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m05/scratch`.

**My view of what was measured.** I think this is a band or sensor on the trunk recording the daily "breathing" of the stem, over about two months of mostly dry weather broken by rain. Three things in the file support this:
- **A daily cycle in every one of the 61 days.** The trunk swells to a peak, falls quickly through several hours (the widely spaced dots), then recovers slowly (the tightly packed dots). That fits a trunk shrinking in daytime as the tree draws water and refilling overnight.
- **Steady drying punctuated by sudden rises.** Between rises the level drifts down for weeks while the daily swing deepens, to about 50 px by the driest days against about 25 px just after a rise. That fits drying soil. There are sharp jumps around days 4, 8 and 13, a smaller one around day 23, the largest around days 39–40, and another around day 58. At its peak the daily swing nearly vanishes (about 6 px on day 40). Those look like rain soaking the trunk.
- **No upward trend.** The level does not rise over the two months, so there is little or no growth showing, only water going in and out.

I can't say where or when it was recorded.
