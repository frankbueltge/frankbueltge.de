**What I did first with the file**
I looked at it, then read it pixel by pixel. The ink runs from column 2 to 296, so each pixel column holds about 4.9 hourly readings. The individual values can't be read off directly; they have to be estimated.

**From the file to the form**
1. **Recovering the values.** I gave each of the 1,464 readings its x position across the ink. For a first guess I spread the readings in each pixel column over that column's dark pixels, so dense clumps got more readings. Then I adjusted all 1,464 heights together until a re-drawn set of dots matched the JPEG closely. Within one pixel column the order of the readings can't be seen, so I put each column's values in whichever order joined up best with its neighbours. Scripts and the recovered values are in `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m01/scratch` (`fit2.py`, `order.py`, `vals.json`).
2. **Checking it.** The recovered series repeats every 24 readings far more strongly than at any nearby lag. That confirms one dot per hour.
3. **The form.** Angle is the hour of the day and distance from the centre is the radius, so the thread winds once around the circle per day. The 61 days stack up like rings in a cut trunk. Each reading is a dot, and the dots line up into 24 spokes, one per hour, that look like the rays in wood. The line is rust where the trunk is shrinking and blue where it is swelling. A short caption names the work and says what the circle shows.

**What the commissioner should know**
- **Values are relative, not real.** There are no axes, so values run 0 to 1 from the lowest to the highest point in the picture. There is no real unit or size.
- **Hour-level detail is estimated.** The two-month shape and each day's swing are solid. Single readings have about one pixel of error, and their order within a 5-hour column is a best guess, which is why some daily turns look a little wiggly. The smooth line between hours is drawn in; the dots are the data.
- **"First hour" at the top is an assumption.** I assumed the first reading was taken at midnight. Nothing in the file proves that.

**What I think was measured**
I think the tree is taking water in and giving it out, rather than visibly growing. The daily swing covers about half the full height of the plot, and the trunk ends only slightly larger than it started. Three things in the file point this way:
- **The daily pattern.** Counting from the first reading, the radius peaks around hours 3 to 6, falls quickly to its lowest around hours 12 to 15, and refills overnight. With a midnight start, that is the usual pattern of a trunk shrinking while leaves draw water by day and refilling at night.
- **Days that broke the pattern.** About seven days barely shrink at all and jump upward instead (around days 4, 8, 13, 24, 40, 48 and 59, counting from 1). Day 40 hardly moves, with a swing about a tenth of the usual one, right after the largest rise in the record. I read these as rainy or overcast days when the tree took up water.
- **Slow sag between them.** In the long stretches between those jumps, each day's peak sits a little lower than the last. That suggests the soil was drying out, perhaps in summer. I can't tell the place or the year from the file.
