The page loads nothing from outside: all 1,464 values are written into it and drawn on a canvas. There was no browser here, so I never saw the page itself. I checked the shape with a PIL picture built on the same geometry, which joins the hours with straight lines where the page uses curves.

**What I did first.** I looked at the picture, then read its pixels. The dots run from column 2 to column 296, so each pixel column holds about five hours. One day is about 4.8 px wide, and the 90 px height gives only about 90 distinct value levels.

**From the file to the form.**
- **Getting the values back.** I gave each of the 1,464 hours its own column, spacing them evenly from column 2 to 296. Within a column I put the dark pixels in whichever order, top-down or bottom-up, joined on best to the previous column. Then I stretched or squeezed that list to the number of hours that column should hold.
- **How good that is.** The result is approximate. The order within a column is a guess, overlapping dots can't be told apart, and values are only as fine as the pixel rows. It is good enough to show the daily cycle clearly: averaged over the 61 days, the highest hour is hour 5 of the record and the lowest is hour 15.
- **The form.** Each day is one turn of a spiral, starting at the top and running clockwise. The radius on the page is the measured radius, scaled up a lot and placed around a fixed pale core. The 61 days pile up like rings in a cross-section of the trunk. Because the trunk shrinks by day and swells by night, the pile forms a lopsided ring: wider before dawn, pinched in the afternoon. Lines darken from the first day to the last, an open circle marks the first hour and a filled one the last. There is a short caption.

**Things you should know.**
- **Where the clock starts is arbitrary.** I don't know what time of day the record begins, so "top of the ring" means the first hour of the record, not midnight.
- **Only the shape is real, not the sizes.** Both the size of the core and the scaling are my choice, and the original scale is unknown.
- **The fine detail comes from the picture, not the measurements.** Small clusters along 24 spokes come from the pixel steps and from how I rebuilt the values.

**What I think was measured.** It looks like a stem shrinking by day and swelling by night during a dry spell in the growing season, broken by a few rain events. It rests on these things in the file:
- **A strong daily sawtooth.** There is a slow climb to a peak and a faster fall, with the low point about 10 hours after the peak. That fits water being drawn out of the bark while the tree transpires by day and refilling at night. If the record starts near midnight, the peak falls around dawn and the low around mid-afternoon.
- **The daily swing is large.** It is about 40–60% of the whole 61-day range, so most of the movement is water, not growth.
- **Slow sinking between sudden jumps.** For weeks the daily average drifts down, which looks like the soil drying. Then it jumps up abruptly: near day 3, around days 39–40 and near day 58. Around day 40 the daily swing almost disappears, which fits a wet, overcast day.
- **No lasting growth.** The end is not clearly above the start, as you would expect late in the season or under drought.
- **The season is a guess.** Sixty-one days suggests roughly two summer months, but nothing in the file says when.
