I made "Sixty-one Rings": the 61-day record wound into a spiral, one turn per day, so it reads like the cross-section of a tree trunk.

Files:
- /tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m06/work/index.html (about 710 KB): one static inline SVG with no script, no animation and nothing loaded from outside. The only URL in it is the SVG namespace.
- /tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m06/work/NOTE.md: the title and one paragraph.
- Working scripts are in /tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m06/scratch (`ex.py`, `rec.py`, `make3.py`, which writes `index.html`).

**What I did first.** I looked at the image, then printed it as a character grid and pulled out the dark-dot positions in each of the 300 pixel columns. The dots run from x=2 to x=297, so each column holds about 4.9 hours and each day about 4.85 px. The daily low points line up with that spacing: a straight-line fit to them leaves an average error of about 0.7 px, roughly 3.5 hours.

**From the file to the form.**
- **Rebuilding the series.** In each column I ordered the dots so they continue smoothly from the previous column, then spread them out to the right number of hours. That gave back 1,464 values, scaled 0 to 1, with light smoothing.
- **The spiral.** The radius grows steadily, one turn per 24 hours. Each hour's value pushes the line outward or pulls it in.
- **The tint.** The band between one turn and the next is pale where values are high and dark where they are low.
- **What it shows.** The long declines pack the rings together into dark bands. The rapid recoveries leave wide pale rings. The daily swing appears as a small waver in every turn.
- **Checking it.** No browser was available, so I checked the layout with PNG previews drawn in Python, not the page itself. The title and two lines of caption sit to the right of the spiral.

**Things the person who commissioned this should know.**
- **Timing is approximate.** The data was traced back out of a 300-px JPEG, so where events fall within a day is only good to about ±3 hours. Where several hours fell on one pixel, their dots merged, and the order and spacing inside each column are my reconstruction. Treat the series as faithful at the scale of the daily rhythm and the overall drift, not hour by hour.
- **The clock position is arbitrary.** The first value starts at 12 o'clock, but the actual time of day of the first value is unknown.
- **The low point drifts a little.** The daily low moves slightly from turn to turn. That is most likely error from my reconstruction, not something in the data.

**What I think was measured.** The record has a daily cycle that drops fast over a few hours and then climbs back slowly over the rest of the day. On top of that, it declines slowly over weeks, with the daily swing getting bigger as it goes. Three times (near the start, around day 40, and around day 58) the daily cycle stops for about two days while the value climbs smoothly back to near its highest level.

This looks most like a tree-stem dendrometer in summer: the stem shrinks while it loses water through the day, refills at night, shrinks further over weeks of drought, and swells back during rainy spells. The same daily and drying pattern could also be a small stream's discharge or a shallow groundwater level near trees. The smooth two-day rises with no daily cycle in them fit a swelling stem better than a stream, which would jump suddenly in rain. I would call this view moderately confident, not certain.
