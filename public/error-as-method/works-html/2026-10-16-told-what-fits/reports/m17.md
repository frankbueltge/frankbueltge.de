I made "Sixty-One Breaths", a still canvas drawing in `index.html`. It is a spiral with one turn per day: the first day is at the centre and the last at the rim. The page loads nothing from outside, and the 1,464 hourly values are embedded in it.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at the picture, then read its pixels column by column. There were 1,638 dark pixels for 1,464 values, so each dark pixel is about one value. The dots form a sawtooth that repeats about every 4.8 pixels, which is 24 values at this scale.

**How I got from the file to the form.**
- **Rebuilding the numbers:** in each column I ordered the dark pixels along the local trend and joined them into one path. I then sampled that path at 1,464 evenly spaced points across the inked width. The ink runs from pixel 2 to 296, not the full 300; mapping onto the full width smeared out the daily shape.
- **Checking the rhythm:** the autocorrelation of the rebuilt series peaks at exactly 24 hours.
- **Choosing the form:** I tried three spirals as PNG previews in the scratch folder.
  - Dots at the full value gave 24 rigid spokes.
  - A plain smooth line read like tree rings but hid the daily cycle.
  - The final version splits the two time scales. Each hour's departure from its 24-hour running mean pushes the line outward or pulls it inward, so every ring leans the same way and the spiral sits off-centre. The running mean itself sets the line's darkness and weight, so the multi-day spells show as dark and pale bands, like wood grain.
- **Checks:** no browser was available, so I never saw the page itself in a browser. I ran the script in Node against a stub canvas: it draws all 1,464 hours without errors. I also rendered a Python preview with the same settings (`final_preview.png`).

**What you should know.**
- **Approximate values:** they come from a 300 × 90 JPEG, so they are rough. Ordering within a column is a guess, and sub-pixel detail is gone.
- **Relative scale:** the values are in picture pixels. Nothing says what the real CO2 levels are, so the work claims no levels.
- **Phase:** the spiral starts at the top with the first hour of the record. The time of day that hour falls at is unknown.
- **Layout:** the page scales to the window and is set out for 1100 × 800. A small caption sits at the bottom left.

**My view of what was measured.** I think this is a station over land with plants growing around it, recorded in the growing season, rather than a clean background site such as an ocean or mountain station. Three things in the file point that way:
- **The daily swing is huge:** often 40 to 50 of the 90 pixels, which is about half the plot's height.
- **The shape is lopsided:** about 14 hours of slow climbing, then a fall of about 10 hours down to the low. That fits CO2 building up at night from plant respiration under calm air, then being drawn down in daylight as the air mixes and plants photosynthesise.
- **The base level shifts over several days:** the clearest case is a sharp jump around day 40 (near x ≈ 190). One day there has almost no daily swing, about 9 pixels against the usual 40. Changes like that suggest weather systems passing, for example a front or overcast, windy days.

If the low comes in mid-afternoon, as it usually does at such sites, the record would start near local midnight. That is a guess, not something the file shows.
