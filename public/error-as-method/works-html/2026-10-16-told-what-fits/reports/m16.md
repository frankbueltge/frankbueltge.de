I made "Breath Rings", a still image that winds the 61-day record into a spiral like a tree's cross-section, with one turn per day. It's in `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m16/work/index.html` (inline SVG, about 716 KB, no scripts and no outside resources), with the title and paragraph in `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m16/work/NOTE.md`. I never saw the page in a real browser. My preview was drawn with my own Python script, which leaves out the caption text, so the caption's placement in the bottom-left corner is set but has not been seen.

**What I did first with the file:** I looked at it, then printed its dark pixels as a text grid to see the dot layout. The dots fill x 2–297 and y 5–85. There are about 4.9 dots per pixel column, which fits 1,464 values across 295 px. The dots also bunch into near-vertical streaks every ~4.8 px, which is 24 hours. So the strongest pattern in the file is a daily cycle.

**How I got to the form:**
- **Rebuilding the hourly values.** Each pixel column holds about five hours. I took the centres of the dark blobs in each column and ordered them so they continue on from the previous column, then spread that column's hours over them. Redrawing the result matches the original closely. Still, the values are only approximate: about ±1 px on an 80 px range, and the hour order inside a column is partly guessed. I smoothed them lightly over three hours.
- **Reading the cycle.** Averaging by hour shows a high plateau from about hour 22 to hour 5, a steep fall from hour 5 to the low at hour 12, then a slow climb back.
- **Building the spiral.** The first day is at the centre and the 61st at the outer edge. Each hour is a dot at its own hour-angle, and its distance from the centre follows the value. A fill shows whether the value is above the 61-day mean (brown, where the ring bulges out) or below it (green, where it draws in).
- **What the spiral shows.** Each day is high on one side and low on the other, so every turn bulges one way and the whole spiral grows off-centre, like a tree trunk. Spells lasting several days show as dark bands (around days 5–15 and a sharp rise near day 40) and pale green stretches. The fixed hour-angles line the dots up into 24 spokes, which I kept.

**What you should know:**
- The values are a reconstruction from a compressed image, not the original data. There are no units or ppm figures because the file gives no scale.
- The rotation is my choice. Hour 0 of the record is at the top, so the average daily low points down. Nothing in the file says what local time hour 0 is. If the low is really in mid-afternoon, which is typical, the record would start around 3–4 a.m. local time or be kept in UTC, but that is a guess.
- The size comes from drawing the fill as one small shape per 10 minutes. It could be made much smaller with a canvas script if that matters.

**My view of what was measured:** carbon dioxide close to the ground at a station with plants around it, during the growing season. It rests on three things in the file:
1. The daily swing is large, often more than half the whole range, and it is the strongest pattern in the record. That points to plants and the low-lying night air, not well-mixed background air.
2. The shape fits that: the value builds up through the night, drops sharply in the morning to a midday low, and recovers slowly. Plants take carbon dioxide in by day, and at night the still air near the ground traps what they and the soil give off.
3. On top of that there are slower swings of several days: sudden jumps followed by declines over one to two weeks, which looks like changes in the weather.
