I made "Sixty-One Breaths", a single still SVG image. It draws the 61 days of trunk-radius readings as 61 overlapping daily rings, like a cross-section of the trunk.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at the picture, then read every pixel column into a list of where the dark dots fall. The ink runs from column 2 to column 296. With 1,464 values across about 295 pixels, each column holds about 4.9 hours and each day is about 4.8 columns wide. The daily sawtooth was visible straight away.

**From the file to the form.**
- **Rebuilding the hourly values.** In each column I put the dots in order so they follow on from the previous column. I spread that column's ~4.9 hours along them and smoothed lightly over three hours. This gives an *approximate* series of 1,464 values in pixel units (about 6.6 to 83.3). I drew it back out flat and it matches the source closely.
- **The form.** Each turn around the centre is one day of 24 hours, and the distance from the centre is radius = 62 + 3.45 × value. The hourly points are joined with smooth curves. Early days are pale amber and the last days are dark brown, and there is a faint dot for every hour, as in the original. A dotted line from the centre marks the first hour of each record day.
- **Files.** The data is embedded in the page and it loads nothing from outside. My scripts, the rebuilt series and a preview are in /tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m11/scratch (recon.py, build.py, series.json, preview.png).

**What the commissioner should know.**
- **The values are a reconstruction from a compressed picture, not the original data.** Where dots overlapped (the slow overnight recovery), the order of the hours inside a column is my estimate. The daily highs, lows and timing are reliable; single hours are not.
- **The scale is invented.** The source has no units and I don't know the real size of the trunk. The daily change is shown hugely enlarged against an arbitrary centre, and the rings say nothing about the trunk's real girth.
- **The clock position is not real time.** The top of the circle is the record's own first hour, not a known time of day.
- **I couldn't open it in a browser here.** I checked the geometry with a Python copy of the drawing and ran the page's script outside a browser without errors. I have not seen it in a real browser at 1100 × 800.

**My view of what was measured.** I think this is a dendrometer on a living tree's stem, recording a dry season broken by a few rain events.
- **Daily cycle.** Nearly every day the radius peaks around hour 7–8 of the record day and bottoms out around hour 16–19. If the record starts near midnight, that is the usual pattern of a stem drawing water and shrinking by day, then refilling overnight. That timing is only my inference.
- **Dry spells.** Over runs of days the daily high creeps down and the daily dip deepens. That looks like a tree drying out.
- **Wet days.** About five times (around days 3, 12, 23, 39–40 and 58) the radius jumps up sharply and the daily dip almost disappears. On day 40 the swing is about 6 units against a usual 40 or more. Those look like rain or overcast wet days: the stem swells back and barely shrinks.
- **Growth.** There is no clear long-term growth. Day 41 is the highest reading, but the general level wanders rather than climbing.
