I've made the work, "The Long Exhale". It is a still, single-file SVG page that loads nothing from outside, with its NOTE.md next to it.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at the picture, then read its pixels with Python (PIL and numpy). The dots run from x=2 to x=296, which is about 4.82 pixels per day. That means about five hourly values share each pixel column, so single hours can't be read off directly. A frequency check on the picture puts the main cycle at 4.82 px. That is exactly 24 hours, not the 24.84-hour tidal day, which would be 4.99 px.

**How I got from it to the form.**
- **Rebuilding the values.** I rebuilt the 1,464 hourly values from the picture. In each pixel column I found the dark dot positions and gave them to that column's hours in rising or falling order, depending on the trend in the neighbouring columns. Then I removed single-hour spikes with a 3-point median. Drawing the result back out matched the original closely. The values are only relative heights on a 90-pixel scale, and individual hours are an estimate.
- **What the values show.** On average, values are highest from hour 0 to 5, fall fast from hour 6 to 12, bottom out around hour 13 and climb slowly back through the evening. Over the weeks the record makes a sawtooth: the level jumps up suddenly, then sinks slowly over days to weeks while the daily swing grows. On the jump days the daily cycle almost disappears (for example days 4–5, 8–9, 13, 24, 41–42, 48 and 59).
- **The form.** I chose a stack of 61 lines, one per day, running from hour 0 to hour 24, each day hanging just below the day before. Where a line rises, it covers the days above it. The midday dip becomes a valley running down the page and deepening, and the quiet jump days are drawn heavier, as near-straight lines that cut across it. It is ink on warm paper, with a short side text and only light labels (day 1/61, hour 0/12/24) and no value scale.

**What the commissioner should know.**
- The data in the page is my estimate read back from a 300×90 JPEG, not the original numbers, and the page says so.
- "Hour 0" is the record's first hour. I don't know the clock time it starts at.
- The side text uses mood words ("sag", "lifts") but doesn't name what was measured.
- I couldn't render the HTML in a browser here. I checked the line drawing with a Python version of the same shapes, but not the text placement in a real browser. It is worth one look at 1100×800 before showing it.

**My view of what was measured (a guess).** I think it is something weather-driven at a dry, sunny site, broken up by occasional rain or overcast. Relative humidity is my best guess; fuel or surface moisture would fit the same way. It rests on four things in the file:
- **Solar day:** the cycle is a strict 24 hours.
- **Daily shape:** a fast morning drop, a low around hour 13 and a slow recovery overnight. That is the classic shape of relative humidity, if hour 0 is near midnight.
- **Jump days:** sudden level jumps on days when the daily swing almost vanishes, which is what a wet, overcast day looks like.
- **Drying:** a slow decline over the following days with a growing midday dip, which looks like drying out.

The biggest jump (days 40–41) packs together near the top of the scale, which fits a saturation ceiling. Against this, the nightly highs also drift down between rain events, where humidity usually comes back near 100% at night. So a moisture store that dries out (soil or fuel) is also possible. A stream level driven by plant water use seems unlikely, because the daily swing is too big compared with the overall range.
