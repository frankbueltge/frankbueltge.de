I made **Drawing In**: the 61 days drawn as 61 loops around a centre, so the trunk's radius is shown as a radius.

Files:
- /tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m03/work/index.html (one self-contained SVG page with the data embedded, no external resources)
- /tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m03/work/NOTE.md
- Working scripts are in /tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m03/scratch (`rec2.py` reads the values back from the image; `prev.js` and `rast.py` make a preview)

**What I did first.** I looked at the image, then read its pixels. The dots run from x=2 to 296 and from y=5 to 83, so about 4.9 hourly values share each pixel column. That means you can't read off hours by simple column lookup. In the image you can see a strong daily sawtooth, a slow drift over weeks, and three sharp rises (around days 3, 39 and 58).

**How I got to the form.**
- I rebuilt the 1,464 values by tracing a smooth path through the dark pixels, one point per hour, and checked it by drawing it in red over a 4× enlargement of the image. The first tries kept skipping the sparse dots at each day's low point. I fixed that by giving extra weight to the highest and lowest dots in each 24-hour window.
- Then I wrapped time into a circle, one turn per day, with distance from the centre equal to the value plus a fixed inner radius. Because each day is exactly 24 samples, the same hour falls at the same angle every day, so the daily shrinking stacks up into one sector where all the loops pull inward.
- The ink goes from pale to dark over the two months. A pale disc in the middle stands for the rest of the wood. A small straight strip of all 1,464 hours sits beside the circle as a key.
- I couldn't open the page in a browser here (no browser available). I checked the layout with my own preview renderer, so lines may look somewhat darker in a real browser.

**What the person who commissioned this should know.**
- **The hourly values are reconstructed, not read exactly.** The highs and lows of each day and the trend over weeks are reliable. The exact shape within a day is not: timing is only good to about ±2 hours, because one pixel spans about 5 hours. I smoothed lightly over 3 hours.
- **No real units.** The values are in source-pixel units and the magnification is arbitrary, so nothing in the work is to scale.
- **The angle is not clock time.** The top of the circle is simply the first value. I don't know what hour the record started, so I didn't label any hours.
- The page text says the round loops are "most likely wet days". That is my inference, not something the file shows.

**My view of what was measured.** I think it is a point dendrometer on a living tree during a warm season, possibly a fairly dry one. Here is what that rests on:
- **Daily rhythm.** Every day has a long high stretch, a fast drop over about 3–4 hours, a low of several hours, then a slower recovery. That fits a trunk shrinking while the tree loses water by day and refilling at night. The lowest point comes about 10–13 hours after the record's start, and the highest about 0–4 hours after.
- **Size of the swing.** The daily swing (median about 44 of the roughly 78-pixel total range) is as large as the whole two-month drift, and there's little net upward growth. So water movement in the bark, not wood growth, drives most of the signal.
- **Rain-like events.** On three occasions the radius jumps up within a day or so. Each time the daily swing then almost disappears for a day or two (down to about 8–25 against the usual 40–55), and afterwards the baseline slowly falls again over a week or more. This looks like rain refilling the trunk under cloudy skies, followed by drying out.
