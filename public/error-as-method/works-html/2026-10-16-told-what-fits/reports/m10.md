I made "Sixty-One Drawdowns": `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m10/work/index.html`, with `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m10/work/NOTE.md` beside it. It is a single still canvas image at 1100 × 800 that loads no other file. I couldn't view it in a browser because there is none here. Its script runs without errors under Node, and I checked the composition in Python mock-ups of the same geometry. The text column on the right is the least certain part: a mock-up checked its edges, but the text itself was never drawn, and the longest line should end about 25px inside the right edge.

**First, with the file:** I looked at it, then enlarged it and printed the darkness of every pixel column. It showed about 61 teeth, one per day. Each pixel column holds about five hourly dots. On each day the dots climb slowly and close together near the top, then a sparse line of widely spaced dots drops far down. A lone dot is about one pixel across.

**From the file to the form:**
- **Recovering the numbers.** I treated the 1,464 values as unknowns, placed hour *t* at a fixed x position, re-drew dots of the measured size, and adjusted the heights until the redrawn picture matched the original. The difference between the two is only JPEG noise, and the x-scale I assumed (columns 2.8 to 296.6) fit better than nearby alternatives. A smoothness penalty was needed: without it the hours inside a column came out shuffled.
- **Testing the cycle.** Folding the series at 24 hours gives a strong pattern; folding at 22, 23, 25 or 26 hours gives almost none.
- **A dial I dropped.** I first tried a 24-hour dial with the days overlaid. The days' levels differ about as much as each day's swing, so it read as a blurred ring.
- **The form I kept.** I cut the record into 61 rows of 24 hours, stacked them, and drew each day in front of the days after it, so its valley hangs over them. The 1,464 dots stay on the lines. A short text column on the right says what is shown and what isn't known.

**What you should know:**
- The values are read back from a 300 × 90 picture, so heights are relative, not ppm, and there are no real units.
- The order of hours inside each pixel column (about five hours) is a reconstruction, made smooth on purpose. Individual hours could be off by an hour or two; the daily shape and longer patterns are solid.
- "Hour 0" is the first hour of the record, not midnight. I added no place, date or clock time.
- My working files (the recovery scripts and preview images) are in `/tmp/claude-0/s131/mk/m10/scratch`.

**What I think was measured:** CO₂ near the ground at a site surrounded by growing plants, recorded in the growing season. This rests on four things in the file:
1. **One rise and fall every 24 hours.** It is large: up to about 70% of the record's whole vertical range.
2. **Its shape.** On average the value is highest around hours 0 to 2 of each record-day. It falls fast over about five hours to a low at hours 10 to 12, then rises more slowly through the remaining hours. That fits plants taking up CO₂ by day while daytime air mixing dilutes it, and CO₂ building up overnight in still air near the ground.
3. **Weather.** Four times (near days 3, 23, 39 and 58) the whole level jumps and that day's swing nearly disappears; on day 40 it is about a tenth of normal. This looks like a change of air mass or overcast, windy weather.
4. **Deepening valleys.** Between jumps, the level sinks for two to three weeks while the daily low gets deeper. That looks like plant uptake dominating.

The low comes about 11 hours after the record's day begins, while a plant-driven low usually falls in mid-afternoon. So if this reading is right, the record probably starts in the early morning, local time, or is kept in a time zone other than the site's.
