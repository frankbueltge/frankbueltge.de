# The Drying

The 1,464 hourly values are cut into their 61 days and stacked as 61 lines, one per day, top to bottom, each running through 24 hours from left to right. Every line is drawn as a ridge that hides the ones before it. This puts the rhythm the original plot hid inside its saw-tooth right in front of you: the same dip comes at the same hour every day and runs as a valley down the middle of the page, and over each dry spell it gets deeper day by day. On the few days just after a sudden rise, the line lifts almost flat and covers the rows above it, like a sheet thrown over the record. I chose a stack over a single strip because the record's real structure has two clocks, the sun's day and the weather's run of days. Putting one clock across and the other down lets you see both at once. The hourly values were rebuilt from the 300-pixel picture, so the shape within each day is close but not exact.
