# Day Rings

The record is wound into a spiral of 61 turns, one turn per day, so that every hour sits at the same clock angle as the same hour on every other day. Each turn sits a fixed step outside the one before. On top of that step, the line moves outward when the measured radius was larger and inward when it was smaller, and it gets thicker and darker the more the trunk had shrunk. What you get looks like a sawn cross-section. The midday shrinking of each day lines up into a dark crescent on one side, like latewood. The days when the trunk swelled and hardly moved within the day show as pale, loose bands. I chose this form because the quantity is itself a radius of a trunk. Giving it back as the face of a trunk lets its daily breathing and its wet and dry spells be seen the way wood is read, ring by ring. But here each ring is one day, not one year.
