# Breath Rings

The record is wound into a spiral like the cross-section of a tree: one turn per day, the first day at the centre, the sixty-first at the bark, each of the 1,464 hours a small dot at its own hour-angle. The distance from the centre follows the measured value, so the ring swells outward (brown) where the air held more carbon dioxide and draws in (green) where it held less. Because each day's high comes in the night hours and its low around midday, every turn bulges on one side and pulls in on the other, so the whole spiral grows off-centre the way wood does. Weeks of stale, rich air show as dark bands and the clear stretches as pale green. I chose the tree ring because it is the way plants keep their own record of time, and the daily rhythm in this record looks very much like the breathing of plants near the instrument: they take carbon dioxide in by day and give it back at night.
