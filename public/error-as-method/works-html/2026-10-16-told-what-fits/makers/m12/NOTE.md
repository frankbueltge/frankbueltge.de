# Sixty-One Days

The record is cut into its 61 days, and each day stands as one column of 24 dots, one dot per hour, 1,464 in all. Inside a day the hours are sorted by level, not by clock time. Hours that sat at about the same level push each other sideways, so each column widens where the air stayed a long time and thins to a single line of dots where it passed through quickly. I chose this because the file shows the daily rhythm clearly but is too small to show hour by hour what happened. Each pixel column holds about five hours, so the order of the hours cannot be read back out. What can be read is how high and how low each day went and where it spent its time. Building the form from those things alone keeps it honest about the source. Laid side by side, the days show the longer shape that the daily sawtooth hides: three or four sudden jumps to a higher level, each followed by a week or two of slow decline in which the daily swing grows deeper.
