# Sixty-one Rounds

The record's 1,464 hourly readings are wound around a single 24-hour circle: angle is the hour of the day, distance from the centre is the trunk's radius, and the thread makes one full turn per day, so the sixty-one days lie on top of each other like the growth rings of a cross-section. Each reading is a small dot (they line up into radial spokes, one per hour, like the rays in wood), and the line between them is rust where the trunk is shrinking and blue where it is swelling. I chose this form because the strongest thing in the record is not its two-month course but its daily pulse — a tree that contracts every morning and refills every night — and a circle lets every day show that breath in the same place, while the days that broke the rhythm (near-round turns that jump outward) stand apart from the rest without any labels.
