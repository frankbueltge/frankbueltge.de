# Sixty-one Nights

The record is cut into its days and the days are set side by side: each of the 62 columns is one day, read downward from one afternoon low to the next, and each of the 1,464 hours is one dot whose size grows with the amount of carbon dioxide. The page becomes a halftone. The nights, when the air fills up, run through the middle as a dark band, and the afternoons, when it empties, fade at the top and bottom edges. A few columns stay dark from top to bottom: days when the level jumped and the afternoon never drew it down. In the dots to their right you can watch the level drain away again over a week or two. I chose this fold because the strongest thing in the record is a daily breath. A single long line hides that breath in its own sawtooth, and stacking the days makes it the subject, while still showing every hour as a dot, as the original did.
