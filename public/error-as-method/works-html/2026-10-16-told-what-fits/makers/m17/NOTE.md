# Sixty-One Breaths

The record is held up mostly by one rhythm: each day the carbon dioxide climbs slowly for most of the day, then falls away fast and climbs again, sixty-one times. So the form winds the 1,464 hours into a spiral with one turn per day, the first day at the centre and the last at the rim, the way a tree lays down rings. The radius at each hour is lifted by how far that hour sits above the day around it (its 24-hour running mean) and pulled in by how far it sits below. Because the daily swing comes back at the same hours, every ring leans the same way and the spiral is pushed off-centre by the daily breath. The slower changes over several days, when the whole air stood higher or lower, set the line's weight and darkness, so they show up as dark and pale bands like early and late wood. I chose this form because it keeps both time scales in one continuous line: you read the day by going round, and the weeks by going outward.
