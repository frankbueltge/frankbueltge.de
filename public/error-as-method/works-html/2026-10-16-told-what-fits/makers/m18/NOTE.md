# Sixty-One Breaths

The record is wound into a spiral, one turn per day, so that each of the 1,464 hourly values sits at its hour on a clock face and at a distance from the centre set by the measured radius (exaggerated, with a fixed pale core standing for the wood that never changed). The 61 days fall on top of one another like the growth rings of a cross-section, and because the trunk shrinks by day and swells again by night, the rings are not round: together they make a lopsided ring, thick and outward where the trunk was fullest before dawn and pinched where it was thinnest in the afternoon. I chose this because a radius belongs in a circle, and because in the flat plot the daily pulse and the slower drying and refilling are tangled in one sawtooth; laid round, the daily rhythm becomes the shape of the ring and the slow changes become how far apart the rings spread. Line weight darkens from the first day to the last, an open ring marks the first hour and a filled dot marks the last.
