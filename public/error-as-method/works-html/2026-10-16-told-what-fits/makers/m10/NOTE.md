# Sixty-One Drawdowns

The record is cut at every 24th hour, and the 61 days are laid one under another like lines on a page. Each day is drawn in front of the days after it, so its valley hangs down over them. What the original plot crowds into a saw-tooth becomes a stack of the same gesture repeated: a fast fall to a midday low and a slower filling-up through the remaining hours, the night. The stack also shows the slower rhythm. Four times the whole level jumps and the valleys flatten, then the level sinks for two or three weeks while the valleys deepen again. I chose this form because the material has two clocks, the day and the weather, and the stacked rows let one form carry both. The 1,464 hourly values were read back from the supplied picture. Their heights are relative, and hour 0 is the start of the record, not a known clock time.
