# Sixty-one Breaths

The record is cut into its sixty-one days, and each day stands as a column of twenty-four short strokes, one per hour, set at the height of that hour's value. Each stroke's colour comes from where its hour falls in the day: deep night-blue at the start and end of each 24-hour block, amber in the middle. I chose this because the strongest thing in the picture is a daily rhythm: carbon dioxide builds up through the night and is drawn down by about midday. In the original plot this was a blur of dotted streaks, five pixels wide per day. Stacking each day's hours on one column turns every day into a single stroke of breath. Blue settles at the top and amber sinks to the bottom, and the length of the column shows how deep that day's breath went. Seen together, the columns show the slower weather of the record: three sudden lifts where the whole column rises and its breath grows shallow, each followed by a long sinking over many days.
