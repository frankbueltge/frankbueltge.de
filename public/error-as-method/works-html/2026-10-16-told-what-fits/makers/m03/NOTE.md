# Drawing In

The record is the radius of a trunk, so the work draws it as a radius: each of the 61 days becomes one loop around a centre, and the distance from the centre at each hour is the measured value, magnified, with the start of the record at the top and time running clockwise from pale to dark ink. Wrapping the hours into a circle stacks every day's same hours on the same angle, so the daily rhythm hidden in the zigzag of the original plot becomes one shape. In one sector, the same every day, the loops are pulled toward the centre, and then they swell back out. The weeks-long drift, which the flat plot shows as its long slopes, shows here as loops that sit wider or narrower; the few that stay wide and nearly round are the days when the dent hardly happens. I chose the cross-section because it hands the number back to the body it was measured on: the trunk you see is the trunk at every hour of those two months, laid on top of itself.
