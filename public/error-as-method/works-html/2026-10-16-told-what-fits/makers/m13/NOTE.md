# Dew Line

The record is cut into its 61 days and the days are stacked as ridges, the first at the back and the last at the front, with the 24 hours of each running from left to right and the value as height. Each ridge is opaque, so a high, flat day hides the days behind it. I chose this form because the record turned out to be mostly one gesture repeated: a long high stretch, a fall into a short low, and a slow climb back, once every 24 hours. Laying the days on top of each other turns that rhythm into one terrain. The days that barely dip show up as level shelves. Between them, the afternoon valley sinks a little further each day until the next shelf arrives. The hourly values are my reconstruction from the 300-pixel picture, read column by column and lightly smoothed, so the work keeps each day's shape but not the exact value of every hour.
