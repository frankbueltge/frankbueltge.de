# The Day Takes

The work cuts the 1,464-hour record into its 61 days and stacks them, first day at the top. Each day is a black band running from that day's first hour to its last, and the band's thickness at each hour is the measured value. Laid out as one long line, the record's strongest feature, a dip that comes once every 24 hours, turns into hatching too fine to read. Stacked, the dips line up into a pale clearing down the middle of the plate. The few days when the dip did not come stand out as solid dark rows, and the slow thickening and thinning over the weeks shows from top to bottom. I chose thickness instead of a drawn curve because overlapping lines tangled into noise, while a band that is full most of the day and pinched for a few hours keeps the pattern of loss and return visible at a glance. The uncut record runs along the foot of the page for comparison. Thickness starts at the bottom edge of the source plot, not at a known zero, and each hour's value was read back from compressed dots, so single hours are approximate. The broad patterns are not.
