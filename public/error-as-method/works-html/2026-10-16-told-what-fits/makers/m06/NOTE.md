# Sixty-one Rings

The record is wound into a spiral, one turn for every day, starting at the centre and moving outward, so the same hour of each day falls on the same bearing. The radius grows steadily with time, and on top of that each hour pushes the line outward when the value is high and pulls it in when the value is low. The band between one turn and the next is tinted pale for high values and dark for low ones. Read this way, the record looks like the cross-section of a trunk. Long stretches of falling values pack the turns close together into dark bands. The two-day rises that interrupt them leave wide, pale rings. The daily swing shows up as the small waver in every turn. I chose the form because the record is ruled by a strong daily rhythm sitting on a slow drift that is broken by sudden recoveries, and a spiral of days keeps all three readable at once. It is also my best guess about what the record is: something like the swelling and shrinking of a living stem. The data was traced back out of a small JPEG, so its shape is faithful only at the scale of a few hours.
