# Sixty-One Rings

The record is cut into its 61 days and laid back into the trunk it came from, as rings on a cross-section: the first day at the pith, the last at the bark, where a small red mark stands for the point the instrument sat. Each ring is as wide as that day's swing between the trunk's smallest and largest radius, and inside the ring the grain is drawn from the plot's own dots: dark where the day's hours lay close together, pale where the trunk moved fast, with the day's largest radius at the ring's outer edge. Wet, swollen days are tinted warmer. A tree already keeps its time as rings, one a year; I chose that form so this faster ledger reads in the tree's own language. Dry spells, when the trunk shrank harder each afternoon, come out as broad pale rings. The days after rain, when it filled up and hardly breathed, come out as tight dark bands, a little like latewood.
