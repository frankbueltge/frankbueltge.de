I made **The Blossom Line** and wrote both files to `/tmp/claude-0/s119/mk/m08/work`: `index.html` and `NOTE.md`. I couldn't look at the rendered image, because a screenshot would have meant writing a third file.

**What I did first with the file:** I checked that every line follows the same sentence pattern (all 838 do) and pulled out the year and the day. Then I looked at the shape of the data:
- Years run in order with no repeats.
- Bloom days run from 84 (2023, the earliest) to 124 (1323, the latest), with an average of about 104.
- There are few records before about 1400 and almost every year after.
- In half-century averages, the bloom day stays around 101–108 until about 1900, then falls to about 99 for 1950–1999 and about 94 for 2000–2026.

**How I got to the form:** The question was what a bloom day is, not just how to plot it. It's the moment the season turns, so I made each year a column covering the spring weeks, from about 21 March at the top to about 10 May at the bottom. The column is dark until the full-bloom day, shows a bright edge on that day, and the pink then fades over the following weeks. Across the 1,215 years from 812 to 2026, about 0.8 pixel per year, those edges join into one horizon. Over the last century the pink climbs higher than at any earlier point.

Years without a record are left dark. That way the gaps in the record are part of the image, and a strip under the field marks which years have a date.

Other elements:
- A thin pale line shows the 51-year average. It's only drawn where at least 8 years in the window have data, so the sparse early record doesn't produce a misleading curve.
- The earliest (2023) and latest (1323) years are labelled.
- The page has a title, the axes and the source credit.

All the data is inlined as a compact string of about 7 KB. The page draws once on a canvas at 1100 × 800, adjusts for screen pixel density, and loads nothing from outside.

**Things you should know:**
- **Not checked by eye:** I didn't render a screenshot. I checked the page by running its script in Node against a stand-in canvas: it runs without errors and parses all 838 years. I also worked out by hand where the labels and notes fall and fixed two overlaps that way. Please give it one look in a browser at 1100 × 800.
- **Approximate dates:** The day numbers ignore leap years, so the calendar dates on the axis are marked with "~" (day 90 is about 31 March).
- **Fonts:** The text uses fonts already on the viewer's computer (a Palatino or Georgia-style serif, and Helvetica or Arial), so the type will look slightly different from machine to machine.
