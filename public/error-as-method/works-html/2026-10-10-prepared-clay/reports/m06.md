I made "Full Bloom, Kyoto". It is a single still SVG at 1100 × 800 in `/tmp/claude-0/s119/mk/m06/work/index.html`, with a short note in `/tmp/claude-0/s119/mk/m06/work/NOTE.md`. Those are the only two files I wrote.

It runs without errors and draws about 8,300 elements, and the labels sit inside the frame without overlapping each other. Whether labels collide with the blossoms, and how the whole thing looks, still needs one look in a real browser.

**What I did first with the file:** I checked that all 838 lines follow the same sentence pattern, which they do, so the file is really 838 pairs of year and day. Then I summarised it:
- **Gaps:** 377 of the 1,215 years have no record, mostly before 1100.
- **Extremes:** the latest bloom is day 124 (1323) and the earliest is day 84 (25 March 2023).
- **Early springs are not new:** 961 had day 87 and 1409 had day 86. What changed is the average. It stayed around day 101–107 for every century until the 1900s, then fell to about 94 since 2000.

**How I got to the form:** that finding decided it. A single trend line would hide the fact that early springs happened before, and a plain scatter would hide the shift in the average. So the page has three layers:
- **Blossoms:** each recorded year is one small five-petal flower, placed by year (across) and bloom day (down). Earlier days sit higher and are a deeper pink.
- **Branch:** a dark line runs through them. It is the average of the records within 25 years either side, and it gets thicker where more years were recorded. The early centuries are therefore thin and less certain, and the line visibly drops at the far right.
- **Archive strip:** a row of ticks underneath marks every year that has a record and leaves pale gaps where none survives.

Three labels mark 1323 (latest), 1409 ("early springs are not new…") and 2023 ("…but no year was earlier"). The data is written into the page, and it loads no other file, library or network resource.

**Things you should know:**
- **Calendar dates are approximate.** The data gives only day numbers, so I converted them as if every year were a non-leap year (day 91 = 1 April). Leap years would be off by a day, and the old dates were presumably already converted by the compilers. The page says this in small print.
- **The 2026 end of the branch is less certain.** Near 2026 the 25-year window can only look backwards, so the last stretch averages just 26 records. Its "now c. 4 April" is a little less settled than the rest of the line.
- **Fonts are not embedded.** The page uses Georgia or the system's serif font, so the type may differ slightly between machines.
