# m07 (arm T) — hand-back report, verbatim

I made "Eight Hundred and Thirty-Eight Aprils", a single still SVG image. The finished files are `/tmp/claude-0/s119/mk/m07/work/index.html` and `/tmp/claude-0/s119/mk/m07/work/NOTE.md`. I rendered the page in a headless browser at 1100 × 800: there are no script errors, and it makes no request other than the page itself.

**What I did first with the file:** I read it and summarised it before deciding anything. It has 838 rows, and the dates run from day 84 to day 124. The records are sparse early on (14 years in the 800s) and continuous only from 1946 on. The century averages stay around days 101–107 until a clear drop in the 1900s and 2000s. Sorting by date gave me the key fact. The earliest bloom recorded before this century is 1409, day 86 (27 March). The only two years earlier than that are 2021 (26 March) and 2023 (25 March). The latest bloom is 1323, day 124 (4 May).

**How I got to the form:** I wanted the record to keep its texture and its gaps rather than shrink to a trend line. So each recorded year is one small five-petal blossom, placed by year from left to right and by bloom day from top to bottom. They sit on a ruled ground with one faint line per day, like a ledger page. Together the 838 blossoms make a long pink drift through mid-April. On top of that there are three things:
- **A thin ink line** showing the slow average (a weighted average over neighbouring years, about 25 years either side).
- **A dotted line** at the 1409 date across all twelve centuries. 2021 and 2023 are the only blossoms below it, and the only ones drawn in red.
- **A strip of tick marks** under the chart, one per year with a surviving record, so the years nobody wrote down show as gaps.

The data is embedded in the page, and the drawing is plain JavaScript building the SVG.

**Things you should know:**
- **Calendar dates are approximate.** Day of year is turned into dates on a non-leap calendar, so a date can be off by one day in leap years. The footer says so.
- **The data runs to 2026** (day 88, 29 March), which I took to be this year's bloom. It is plotted like any other year. It is early, but not below the 1409 line.
- **The average line is less reliable at both ends.** Before about 950 it rests on very few records, and at 2026 there are no later years to balance it.
- **The fonts depend on the viewer's machine.** The page uses system serif fonts only (Palatino or Georgia) because it can't load any, so the type will look slightly different on different machines.
