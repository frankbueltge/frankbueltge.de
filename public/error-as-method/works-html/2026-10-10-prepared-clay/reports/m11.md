# m11 (arm T) — hand-back report, verbatim

I made a still image called "Twelve Hundred Aprils" from the Kyoto cherry blossom data, plus the short note about it. I could not look at the rendered picture because no browser is available here. I ran the drawing code under Node with a stand-in canvas: it runs without errors and every label lands where I meant it to. Someone should still open `index.html` at 1100 × 800 to check the look.

**What I did first:** I read and summarised the CSV. It has 838 rows, with bloom days from 84 to 124. The day of year stays between about 101 and 107 in every century from the 800s to the 1900s. The 2000s average 94.2, and the two earliest dates in the record are 2023 (day 84) and 2021 (day 85). The latest is 1323 (day 124). 377 of the 1215 years between 812 and 2026 have no record, and most of those gaps fall in the early centuries.

**How I got to the form:** The data is one date per year: it stayed put for eleven centuries and then moved. So I chose a plain field with the year across the page and the calendar date down it, late March at the top and early May at the bottom.
- **Blossoms:** each recorded year is drawn as a small, pale five-petalled blossom on a night-dark ground. The blossoms from 1990 on are a little brighter and larger.
- **Mean line:** a thin white line shows the 51-year mean. It is left out wherever fewer than six records fall inside the window.
- **Record strip:** a strip along the bottom has one tick for each year with a record, so the missing years show as gaps instead of being smoothed over.
- **Annotations:** only the earliest year (2023, 25 March) and the latest (1323, 3 May) are labelled.

**Things you should know:**
- **Dates:** the data gives a day of the year, and I turned it into calendar dates as if every year were a non-leap year, so a leap-year date can be off by one day. I also ignored any old-calendar conversion questions. The footer says this.
- **Self-contained:** the page loads nothing from outside. All 838 records are written into the script, and it uses only system fonts, with Georgia and Palatino as fallbacks.
- **Mean label:** the "fifty-year mean" label sits over the blossom cloud with a dark outline behind it to keep it readable. Check that it doesn't look cluttered.
