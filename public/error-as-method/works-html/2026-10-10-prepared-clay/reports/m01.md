# m01 (arm I) — hand-back report, verbatim

I made **838 Springs**. It's a cross-section of a cherry trunk. Each recorded year is a ring, from 812 at the centre to 2026 at the bark. The calendar runs once around the circle, with 1 January at the top, and a pale pink point on each ring marks that year's day of full bloom.

All the blooms fall in one narrow wedge near 3 o'clock, which is mid-April. Across the outer rings the wedge bends back toward late March. The page is static inline SVG with all 838 data points embedded. It loads nothing external, and no script runs when it is viewed.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at the picture, then decoded the PNG pixel by pixel in plain Python (the image library wasn't installed). The scales came from the plot itself: the gridlines set the day axis at about 8.96 px per day, and the tick marks set the year axis at about 0.82 px per year.

**From picture to numbers.** The dots are 4.4 px wide, about five years' worth of width, so in dense stretches they overlap.
- I fitted dots one at a time, allowing at most one per year and only whole days, then refined the fit with small local moves. This recovered 818 dots.
- The other 20 sit completely behind their neighbours. I added them where an extra dot fit the pixels better than leaving the year empty. Seventeen fit clearly. The last three (1945, 1974, 2009) were chosen partly because the modern record is otherwise continuous.
- That gives exactly 838.
- As a check, 2021 and 2023 come out as days 85 and 84 (26 and 25 March). Those match the dates I remember from public reports. The earliest bloom in the series is 25 March 2023 and the latest is 4 May 1323; both are marked on the image.

**Things to know:**
- **The data is read back from a picture, not taken from the source table.** Whole days and years are probably right for most points. The 20 occluded points are inferred: their years are likely right, but their days are best guesses. A few points in the densest clusters could also be one year off. The caption on the work says this. For accuracy, swap in the original Our World in Data figures; the drawing would not change.
- **Missing years have no ring.** So the darker, sparser wood near the centre is the thin early record, not anything the trees did.
- **The thin line is a 51-year average.** It averages the recorded dates within 25 years either side of each year.
- **I could not look at the finished page.** There was no browser here. I checked the geometry with a rough raster preview and checked label placement against the dot positions by calculation, but nobody has seen the final text rendering.
- **It uses no web fonts.** Type falls back to a system serif (Palatino or Georgia, depending on the machine).
- **I briefly wrote two scratch files in the folder** (the extracted data and the preview image) and deleted them. Only `bloom.png`, `index.html` and `NOTE.md` remain.
