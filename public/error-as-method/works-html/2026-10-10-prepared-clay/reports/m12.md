# m12 (arm P) — hand-back report, verbatim

I made "The Branch of Record", a single still SVG image at 1100 × 800, plus NOTE.md. Both are in /tmp/claude-0/s119/mk/m12/work/. I couldn't look at it in a browser, because none is installed here. I tested the drawing code in Node instead: it produces no NaN values or broken coordinates, draws 838 flowers and 9 branch segments, and loads nothing from outside the page.

**What I did first:** I checked every line of bloom.txt against one sentence pattern, including whether the ordinal endings (1st, 2nd, 3rd, 104th) were right. All 838 lines matched. There are no repeated years, the years are in order, and the days run from 84 to 124. Then I worked out the numbers I would need:
- **Old median:** the middle bloom day of all years before 1850 is day 105.
- **Recent years:** all 37 springs since 1990 bloomed before day 105. Their average is about day 95.
- **Extremes:** the earliest is 2023 (day 84) and the latest is 1323 (day 124). Before modern times the earliest was 1409 (day 86).
- **Missing years:** the long gaps (up to 19 missing years in a row) are all in the 800s, 1000s and 1300s. From 1501 on, every year has a date.

**How I got to the form:** The material has three layers: each year's date, the slow change in the average, and how much of the archive survives. A cherry branch could hold all three at once.
- **Flowers:** each recorded year is a small five-petalled flower, placed left to right by year and up and down by bloom day, with earlier days higher.
- **Branch:** an ink branch follows a weighted running average (±14 years).
- **Branch thickness:** it shows how many dates survive nearby. The branch is a thin twig in the 800s and a full limb after 1500.
- **Breaks:** I first meant to break the branch where its thickness dropped too low. Testing showed that never happens with this data, so I changed the rule to something literal: the branch breaks wherever 9 or more years in a row have no date. There are 8 such gaps.
- **Rest of the page:** thin stalks connect each flower to the branch. A faint line marks the old median, and the bottom line says that all 37 springs since 1990 came before it.

**Things you should know:**
- **Calendar labels:** the dates beside the scale ("1 April" and so on) assume a non-leap year, so they can be a day off. I assume the source already converted the old dates to the modern calendar; I couldn't check that from the file alone.
- **A claim I dropped:** an early draft linked 812 to a court flower banquet. I removed it because it isn't in the data, and I didn't add any outside history.
- **"Old median" is my choice:** day 105 is my reference point (all years 812–1849), not something the source defines.
- **Fonts:** the page asks for Iowan Old Style or Palatino and falls back to Georgia, so the type will look a little different on different computers.
- **Overlap:** the median label has a paper-coloured outline so it stays readable, and it slightly covers a few flowers in the sparse early centuries.

No files outside the folder were read or written, and the folder now holds only bloom.txt, index.html and NOTE.md.
