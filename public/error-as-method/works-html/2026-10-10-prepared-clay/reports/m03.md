# m03 (arm P) — hand-back report, verbatim

I made "Thirteen Centuries of Spring". It's a still image at 1100 × 800, drawn as SVG by plain JavaScript, with all 838 records embedded in the page. The page loads no other file, library or network resource. I wrote only `index.html` and `NOTE.md` in `/tmp/claude-0/s119/mk/m03/work`, and read nothing outside that folder.

I haven't seen the image rendered. Opening a headless browser would have written files outside the folder, so I checked the drawing code against a stand-in page instead. I confirmed that the script has no syntax errors, that all 2,153 elements land inside 1100 × 800, and that the century averages match my own counts. Text width was estimated, not measured. The labels at the right edge and the key line at the bottom are a tight fit, so please look at it once in a browser.

**What I did first with the file:** I checked that all 838 lines follow the same sentence pattern (none differ) and parsed out each year and bloom day. Then I looked at the shape of the data:
- Years are unique and in order, from 812 to 2026.
- Bloom days run from 84 to 124, with an average of about 104.
- The record is thin early on: 14 years in the 800s, then 31, 34, 54, and so on, until it is nearly complete from the 1400s.
- Century averages stay between about the 101st and 107th day for a thousand years. The 1900s average falls to about the 101st day, and 2000–2026 to about the 94th. The two earliest blooms on record are 2023 (day 84) and 2021 (day 85).

**How I got to the form:** Two things stood out: the record fills in over time, and the bloom stays in the same three weeks until it moves earlier at the very end. I wanted both visible at once, so I laid it out like a ledger:
- **Rows and columns:** one row per century, one 9px column per year.
- **Inside each column:** a small calendar from 25 March at the top to 4 May at the bottom, one pixel per day.
- **Recorded years:** pale pink fills the column from the bloom day down, with a deeper pink line on the bloom day itself. An early spring makes a tall column.
- **Unrecorded years:** an empty faint outline.
- **Before 812 and after 2026:** left as blank paper. The last row stops at 2026, which leaves most of the 2000s empty.
- **Right edge:** each century's average bloom date and how many of its years have a record. The 2000s row is the only one whose average is in early April: 4 April, across all 27 of its years.

**Worth knowing:**
- **Dates are approximate.** The labels convert day numbers using a non-leap calendar, so a date can be off by one day in leap years. The footer of the image says so.
- **Uneven record.** Early centuries have few records (14 of 88 years in the 800s), so their averages rest on far fewer years than later ones.
