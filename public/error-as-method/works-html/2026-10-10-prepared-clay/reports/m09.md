# m09 (arm I) — hand-back report, verbatim

I made a still image called "Thirteen Rows of Spring" from the bloom plot. Both files are written. I opened it at 1100 × 800 as a rendered preview (ImageMagick, not a browser), and the layout holds with nothing overlapping. The page's script runs cleanly under Node, draws all 838 years and loads nothing from outside.

**What I did first with the file:** I looked at it. It is a plain scatter plot, 1100 × 500 pixels. No imaging library was available, so I decoded the PNG in plain Python. I then calibrated the axes from the gridlines and tick marks to fractions of a pixel: 0.821 px per year and 8.95 px per day.

**From the picture back to numbers:**
- **Days are certain.** Days sit 9 px apart and a dot is about 4.4 px tall, so each dot's day can be read exactly.
- **Years are harder.** Years are only 0.82 px apart, so neighbouring years merge into horizontal clumps. The two ends of each clump can be measured from their soft edges. That gave 771 dots, and no year was claimed twice, which is a good sign the calibration is right.
- **Hidden dots.** The other 67 are inside clumps. I found them by drawing a model of each clump and adding, one at a time, the unused year that best matched the real pixels. I stopped at the stated total of 838.
- **Spot checks hold:** 2021 comes out as day 85 (26 March, the earliest on record), 2023 as day 84, and 1409 as day 86.

**The form:**
- Time is folded into 13 rows, one per century from the 800s to the 2000s, with 100 years across each row.
- Each recorded year is a stem topped with a blossom, and the earlier the bloom, the taller the stem.
- Years with no record are bare ground.
- A faint pink line in each row marks that century's average.
- The 2000s row stops at 2026, and the rest of that century is left as dotted, empty ground.
- The point lands without explanation: all 27 years from 2000 to 2026 are recorded, averaging day 94. Only about 5% of the years before 2000 bloomed that early.

**Things the person who commissioned this should know:**
- **The numbers are rebuilt from a picture, not taken from the original data.** Every day value is reliable. The year of a dot inside a dense clump can be one or two years off.
- **Five dots are essentially guesses about which year they belong to.** They are 1586, 1162, 2009, 1536 and 1799. Four of them would be completely hidden behind their neighbours in the original plot, so the picture alone can't tell where they are. Their days are still right.
- **I said so on the work itself.** The small print at the bottom says the values were read back from a plot.
- **Check before reusing the numbers.** If the embedded data might be reused as a dataset, compare it with the Our World in Data source first.
- **The page uses a fixed paper colour and a system serif font.** It has no dark mode, and the lettering will differ slightly between machines.
