# The Readership Coast

The year is drawn as a coastline on an old-style nautical chart. North is 1 January and each day is one parallel running south to 31 December. On each parallel the sea comes inland, measured from the western edge, as far as that day's human readers of "Moon" took it, so an ordinary day is a short ripple of shore and a day with a sudden crowd cuts a long, narrow inlet into the land. Engraved water-lines follow the shore, soundings in the sea give the readings in thousands, the borders are graduated in months and readers, and a small tide table gives each month's mean. The inlets are named for what pushed the water in: Eclipse Sound (7 Sep), Apollo Inlet (20 Jul), Blood Moon Inlet (14 Mar), Harvest Moon Fjord, Supermoon Cove and Seven Planets Cove, while Solstice Point (21 Jun) marks the year's lowest water. I chose this form because the Moon moves tides, and attention moves the same way: a steady low swell with surges that come in suddenly and go out within a day or two. A chart shows that as a coast you could sail along, and it tells the story without folding the year into lunar phases the way a moon-shaped picture would.
