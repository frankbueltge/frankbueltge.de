# Read by the Moon

The year is not laid out by calendar months but by lunations: each row begins at a new moon, so every column holds the same phase and all the full moons fall into one vertical band down the middle. Each day is drawn as the moon as it looked that night, its size scaled (by area) to how many people read the "Moon" article. I chose this because the article's subject keeps its own calendar, and the question worth asking of the numbers is whether readers follow the sky. Set out this way the answer is visible at a glance: the largest discs (the eclipses of 14 March and 7 September, the October supermoon) gather in the full-moon column, while a few outliers such as 20 July, the anniversary of Apollo 11, land on a thin waning crescent, where something other than the sky must have sent people looking.
