# Read by Moonlight

Each of the 365 days is drawn as the Moon looked on that day, with the right phase and lit part, and the disc's size is set by how many people read the "Moon" article. The days are not laid out by the calendar. Each row is one lunation, from new moon to new moon, and each column is the Moon's age in days, so 2025 becomes thirteen lunar months stacked on top of each other. I chose this form because it lets the subject arrange its own data, and the arrangement shows something a timeline would hide. The year's biggest days do not fall at random: the spikes in March, September, October and November all land in the full-moon column, and the late-February spike sits at a new moon. The pattern shows that people come to the article when the sky gives them a reason.
