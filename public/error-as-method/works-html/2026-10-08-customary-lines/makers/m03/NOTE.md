# Thirteen Lunations

The year is not laid out by the calendar but by the sky: each row is one lunation, from new moon to new moon, so 2025 folds into thirteen rows. Each day sits at its moon's age, so every full moon falls in the same central column. Each day is drawn as the moon as it looked that day, lit from the right as it waxes and from the left as it wanes, and its area is the number of people who read the "Moon" article. I chose this form because the subject sets the measure: a monthly rhythm of readers, if there is one, should line up in a column, and anything that breaks it shows up as a lone large disc. Both total lunar eclipses (14 March, 7 September) land in the full-moon column, while 20 July, the Apollo 11 anniversary, sits apart in the waning crescent: interest that comes from the calendar, not from the sky.
