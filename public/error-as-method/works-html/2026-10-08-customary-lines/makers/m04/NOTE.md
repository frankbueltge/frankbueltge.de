# Lunations

Each of the 365 days of 2025 is drawn as the Moon looked that day: its phase computed from the synodic month, its size set by how many people read the English Wikipedia article "Moon", and its lit face brighter on busier days. The days are arranged by lunation rather than by calendar month. Each row runs from one new moon to the next, so all thirteen full moons line up in a single middle column. I chose this layout because a calendar would hide the pattern, while the Moon's own clock shows it: readership swells by about a fifth around each full moon, as if people look up and then look it up. The two total lunar eclipses (March 14 and September 7, the year's largest day at 17,284 views) are shown in copper. The Apollo 11 anniversary on July 20 stands out as a large disc in the waning-crescent part of its row, a peak with nothing in the sky behind it.
