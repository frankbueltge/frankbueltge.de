# Thirteen Turns

The year is drawn as the Moon's own path, unwound: Earth sits at the centre, the Sun lies below, and each day is placed at the angle the Moon stood from the Sun that day, while its distance from the centre is the date, so 2025 winds outward in thirteen turns, one per lunation, with every new moon at the bottom and every full moon on the line at the top. A line rides above that path, raised and brightened by the number of people who read the "Moon" article that day. I chose a spiral rather than a calendar or a grid because the angle is not a convenience but the real geometry of a phase, and folding the year on it lets the readers' rhythm line up with the sky: the lit bumps gather near the top, the two eclipses and the October supermoon rise from that line, and the Apollo 11 anniversary on 20 July sticks out on the dark left side, where nothing in the sky called for it.
