# High Water

The year runs left to right as it was lived, not folded into lunations: the readers of the "Moon" article are a body of water whose height each day is that day's page views, measured from zero, and above it the night sky brightens and darkens with how much of the Moon was actually lit, with each real full and new moon of 2025 marked at the top and a plumb line dropped from every full moon. I chose a tide because the Moon pulls tides, and the question worth asking is whether it pulls readers the same way. Kept on the ordinary calendar, the answer is still visible without rearranging anything: the water swells under the bright bands, and its highest crests (the eclipses of 14 March and 7 September, the October supermoon) rise straight up the full-moon lines. The one big wave out in the dark, 20 July, came from the Apollo 11 anniversary, not from the sky.
