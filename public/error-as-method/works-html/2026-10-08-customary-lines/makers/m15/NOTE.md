# Core Sample, 2025

The year's readers of the "Moon" article are laid down as sediment: each of the 365 days is one layer in a drilled core, and a layer's thickness is that day's number of readers. The core is cut into four equal lengths of readers rather than of time and laid in a tray. Its ruler counts readers (0 to 1,937,249), not days, so the months fall unevenly along it. Most of the year settles as a steady brown clay of five or six thousand readers a day. Days that stood far above the weeks around them (the two total lunar eclipses, the Apollo 11 anniversary, the October full moon) show as pale bands, like ash layers in a real core. I chose this form because the other makers all let the Moon's phases arrange the data. I wanted the opposite reading: attention as something that piles up and leaves a record, where an event is visible as a deposit of unusual thickness and colour, and the quiet summer weeks show as tightly packed thin layers.
