# Core Sample, Moon 2025

The year is laid down as sediment. Each day of 2025 adds one layer to a drilled core, and the layer's thickness is the number of people who read the "Moon" article that day. Depth is counted in readers, not in days, and the core is cut into eight equal runs of 250,000 readers and laid in a tray the way geologists store Apollo drive tubes. A quiet day leaves a thin, dark, coarse band. A busy day leaves a thick, pale, fine one, like fresh ejecta. So the calendar is stretched by attention: month marks bunch where few people came and spread apart where many did, and the lunar eclipse of 7 September, the Apollo 11 anniversary and the October supermoon stand out as bright bands you can read from across the room. I chose it because the Moon's own history is read from layered regolith, and because a core turns time into a measurable length of material. That fits a year of readers better than a timeline does.
