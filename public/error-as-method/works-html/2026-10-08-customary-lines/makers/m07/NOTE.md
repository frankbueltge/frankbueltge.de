# Thirteen Lunations

The year of readers of Wikipedia's "Moon" article is set out by the Moon's calendar, not the civil one. Each of the thirteen rows is one lunation, from one new moon to the next. Each day of 2025 is a small moon placed at its age in that cycle and drawn in its real phase, so every full moon falls into the same middle column. A disc's size and brightness follow how many people read the article that day. I chose this form because the subject is also the clock: when the year is read along the Moon's own rhythm, you can see whether people look the Moon up when it is full. Mostly they do, a little. They do so dramatically on the two nights in 2025 when the full Moon went into eclipse, 14 March and 7 September, which flare up as the brightest discs in the full-moon column.
