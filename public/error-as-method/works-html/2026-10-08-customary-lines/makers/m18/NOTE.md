# Synodic Year

The 365 days of 2025 are wound onto a spiral that turns once per lunation (29.53 days), so the angle of each day is the Moon's phase that day: new moon at the top, full moon at the foot, the year running outward from the centre. Each day is a small wedge that rises from the spiral by how many people read the English Wikipedia article "Moon", and its brightness is how much of the Moon was lit. I chose the Moon's clock instead of the solar calendar so the data could answer a question a line chart hides: do readers follow the sky? The tallest marks mostly gather on the full-moon ray at the bottom, the two total lunar eclipses (in copper) and the harvest supermoon among them. The Apollo 11 anniversary stands apart near the new moon, a day people remembered rather than one they looked up at.
