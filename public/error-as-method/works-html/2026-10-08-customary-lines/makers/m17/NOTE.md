# The Readers' Moon

The year is re-folded by the Moon's own clock: each row is one lunation, from new moon to new moon, and each of the 365 days sits at its place in that cycle, drawn as a small moon in the phase it actually showed, with its area set by the number of people who read the article that day. I chose this form because the calendar hides what the numbers are doing and the sky reveals it: once the days are aligned by phase, the great spikes fall into the full-moon column (the two total lunar eclipses of March and September, the October and November supermoons), so the readers' attention is shown waxing in step with the thing they are reading about, while the one bright day off that line, 20 July, is a human anniversary rather than a lunar event. The unlit part of each disc is still drawn, faintly, because even at new moon, when there is nothing to see, thousands still come to look.
