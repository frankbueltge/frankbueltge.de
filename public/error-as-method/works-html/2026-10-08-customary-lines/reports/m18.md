I made "Synodic Year", a single still SVG image built with plain JavaScript. I couldn't look at it in a browser because none was installed and ImageMagick failed to convert the SVG. Instead I generated the SVG in node, checked that it is well-formed with xmllint, and worked out the label and edge positions by calculation.

**What the form does:** It winds the 365 days of 2025 onto a spiral that turns once per lunar cycle (29.53 days, using a reference new moon of 2024-12-30 22:27 UTC). So each day's angle is the Moon's phase that day: new moon at the top, full moon at the bottom, with the year running from the centre outward.
- Each day is a small wedge that rises from the spiral by that day's page views.
- A wedge's brightness is how much of the Moon was lit that day.
- The two total lunar eclipses (14 Mar, 7 Sep) are coloured copper.
- Four of the tallest days have small labels: 7 Sep, eclipse, 17,284 views; 20 Jul, Apollo 11 anniversary, 13,576; 14 Mar, eclipse, 11,854; 7 Oct, harvest supermoon, 10,896.
- The title, a short explanation and the source credit sit in the lower-left corner on a dark background, sized for 1100 × 800.

**What the data shows:** The tallest days gather on the full-moon line: both eclipses and the supermoon fall within about half a day of full moon. The Apollo 11 anniversary is the clear exception, landing near the new moon.
