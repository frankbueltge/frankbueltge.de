I made "The Readers' Moon" as a single still canvas image at 1100 × 800. I checked that the script runs and that the labels land in the right places using a stand-in for the canvas, but I never rendered it in a browser. None was available, and taking a screenshot would have meant writing outside the folder. So nobody has looked at the actual image yet.

**What the image does:** the year is laid out by the Moon's cycle instead of the calendar. Each of the 13 rows runs from one new moon to the next, starting 30 Dec 2024 and ending 20 Dec 2025. I typed the new-moon times in by hand, so they come from memory, not from data. Each day is drawn as a small moon in the phase it showed that day, placed by how far through its cycle the day falls. Its area matches that day's page views. The unlit part of each disc is still drawn faintly, and the busiest days get a soft glow. There are guide labels across the top (new, first quarter, full, last quarter), a size key, a source line, and a right-hand column giving each row's most-read day.

**What it shows:** once the days are lined up this way, most of the big spikes fall in the full-moon column:
- 14 Mar: 11,854 views
- 7 Sep: 17,284 views, the year's peak
- 7 Oct: 10,896 views
- 5 Nov: 7,624 views

The one big exception is 20 Jul (13,576 views), which isn't near a full moon.

The notes beside these peaks are my own reading of the dates, not something in the data:
- 14 Mar and 7 Sep are labelled as total lunar eclipses.
- 7 Oct and 5 Nov are labelled as supermoons.
- 20 Jul is labelled as the anniversary of the first Moon landing.

These are worth checking before the work is shown.
