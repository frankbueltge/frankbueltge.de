I made "High Water". It is a single still SVG image at 1100 × 800, built by plain JavaScript from `data.js`.

**Why this form:** all six other works use the same form. They fold the year into thirteen rows, one per lunation, and draw each day as a moon-phase disc. I kept the ordinary calendar instead and treated the readers as a tide under the Moon.

**What it shows:**
- **The water:** daily page views drawn as a filled area that starts at zero, with a pale crest line along the top.
- **The sky:** above the water, the background brightens and darkens day by day with how much of the Moon was lit.
- **The moon markers:** the real 2025 full moons are light discs and the new moons are dark rings. They are placed using hard-coded 2025 dates and approximate times. A faint vertical line runs down from each full moon.
- **What stands out:** the water rises under the bright bands, and the highest peaks sit on the full-moon lines. Six days are labelled: the eclipses of 14 March and 7 September, the full moons of 7 October and 5 November, 28 February (a new moon), and 20 July (Apollo 11 anniversary, a waning crescent).
- **Around the chart:** a title and subtitle, gridlines at 5,000, 10,000 and 15,000, month ticks, a key, and the data credit.

**The pattern in the data:** I checked it before choosing the form. Measured against the median of the surrounding month, views run about 20% higher on the day of the full moon and the day after.

**Checking:** I rendered the page headless at 1100 × 800. The first render failed on a bug in my text helper, which I fixed. I also corrected one moon date (the first new moon is 30 Dec 2024, not 29 Dec) and then enlarged the vertical scale. The final render shows no errors.
