I made "Wound on the Moon's Clock" and wrote both files in `/tmp/claude-0/s116/mk/m14/work/`: `index.html` and `NOTE.md`. I never saw the image itself. There is no browser here, so I couldn't take a screenshot of it. Instead I ran the drawing code against a fake canvas in node. It runs without errors, and the drawing and annotations stay inside 1100 × 800 (the tallest spikes reach about y=46), so the layout is checked by numbers only.

**Why this form:** all six works in `others/` use the same layout. Each row is one lunar month (new moon to new moon) and each day is drawn as a small moon in its phase. I kept their main finding, that readers come to the article when the Moon is full, but gave it a different shape.

**The form:** a single continuous spiral drawn on a canvas.
- **Path:** it starts at the centre on 1 January and makes one full turn every lunar month (29.53 days), so every new moon falls at the bottom and every full moon at the top.
- **Days:** each day is a wedge standing out from the spiral. Its length is that day's views. Its shade runs from dark slate at new moon to bone-white at full moon, following how much of the Moon was lit at noon UTC.
- **What it shows:** the long, bright wedges gather along the top of the spiral. The tallest days are drawn last, so they lie across the turns outside them.
- **Labels:** five days are marked: the total lunar eclipses of 14 March (11,854 views) and 7 September (17,284, the year's highest), the full moon of 7 October (10,896), the Apollo 11 anniversary on 20 July (13,576, on a waning crescent) and 28 February (8,534, at a new moon).
- **Left side:** the title, a short explanation, a key for wedge length, and a small bar chart. The bar chart folds the whole year onto one lunar month. Views average 5,308 a day across the year, and on the day of the full moon they average about 7,800.

`index.html` loads only `data.js` and uses no libraries or network. `NOTE.md` has the title as a heading and one paragraph on what the form does and why I chose a spiral over a grid of lunar months.
