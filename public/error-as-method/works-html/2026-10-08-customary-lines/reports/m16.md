I made "Thirteen Lunations", a still canvas image of the 2025 readers of the Wikipedia article "Moon", plus its NOTE.md. I couldn't look at it: there's no browser here. The script passes a syntax check and runs to the end without errors against a stand-in canvas, but I haven't seen the actual picture.

**What it shows:** The year is laid out by the Moon's month, not by calendar months. Each of the 13 rows runs from one new moon to the next, so every full moon of 2025 falls in roughly the same column, and the columns are labelled new, first quarter, full and last quarter.
- **Each day:** a small moon drawn in that day's phase. Its area matches how many people read the article that day.
- **What stands out:** The biggest days stack in the full-moon column. Those are September 7 (17,284 readers, total lunar eclipse), March 14 (11,854, total lunar eclipse) and October 7 (10,896, supermoon). The one big day away from that column is July 20 (13,576), the Apollo 11 anniversary.
- **Labels:** Those four days carry a date, a reader count and a short note. There is also a size key and a source line (Wikimedia pageviews, CC0).

**Checks for you:**
- **Moon dates:** I typed the new-moon times for Dec 2024 to Jan 2026 into the page from my own knowledge. Days are placed and phases drawn from those times, and the full-moon days come out at about half a cycle (0.49 to 0.53). The dates themselves weren't checked against any source.
- **Event notes:** The eclipse, supermoon and Apollo notes are also from my general knowledge. Nothing in the data says what caused those spikes.
- **Overlap:** The labels have a dark backing, but they still sit over some moons in the row above, so the September 7 and July 20 labels are worth a look when you open it.
