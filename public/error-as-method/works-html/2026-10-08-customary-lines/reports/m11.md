I made "Core Sample, Moon 2025" and wrote the two files: `index.html` and `NOTE.md`.

**How I chose the form:** all six other works use the same form. They fold the year into thirteen lunar months, one per row, and draw each day as a small moon in its phase, sized by readers. So I left out moon discs, phases and the lunar-month grid entirely.

**The work** (one canvas, no animation, loads only `data.js`, about 1100 × 800): the year is drawn as a drilled core of layered lunar soil, set out in a core tray.
- Each day of 2025 adds one layer, and the layer's thickness is that day's readers. Depth is counted in readers, not days: 1,937,249 in total.
- The core is cut into eight equal runs of 250,000 readers, laid in the tray the way Apollo soil cores are stored. The last run is partly empty.
- Quiet days are dark, coarse bands and busy days are pale, fine ones. The tone is drawn as a log scale from 3,777 to 17,284.
- Month boundaries are darker contact lines with month labels. They bunch together where few people came and spread out where many did.
- Four bright bands carry a bracket and a label: 14 Mar (eclipse), 20 Jul (Apollo 11 anniversary), 7 Sep (eclipse, the thickest layer) and 7 Oct (supermoon).
- There are depth figures at the start of each run, a tone key, and the data credit (Wikimedia pageviews, CC0).

**`NOTE.md`** has the title as a heading and one paragraph on what the form does and why I chose it.
