I made "Lunations", a single still image drawn on a 1100×800 canvas in `/tmp/claude-0/s116/mk/m04/work/index.html`, with its note in `/tmp/claude-0/s116/mk/m04/work/NOTE.md`. I couldn't look at the rendered page: taking a screenshot would have meant writing a file outside the folder or a third file inside it. I checked the layout numbers with Node instead (row count, where the five labelled days fall, disc sizes and gaps).

**The form:** each day of 2025 is a small moon drawn in its real phase for that day.
- **Size and brightness:** the disc's size grows with the square root of that day's views, and its lit face gets brighter on busier days.
- **Rows:** there are 13 rows, one per synodic month, each running from new moon to new moon. A day's place along its row is its exact phase, so all the full moons line up in the middle column.
- **Labels:** each row is labelled with the date of its new moon. The bottom axis marks new, first quarter, full and last quarter.
- **Highlighted days:** the two total lunar eclipses are tinted copper. The five largest days have short labels: Mar 14 (eclipse), Jul 20 (Apollo 11 anniversary), Sep 7 (eclipse), Oct 7 and Nov 5.

**Why this form:** when I grouped the data by lunar phase, readership rose about 20% around full moon (roughly 6,000–6,250 a day against about 5,000 elsewhere). Laying the year out by the Moon's own cycle rather than by calendar months makes that rise visible as a column. Sep 7, the eclipse, is the year's peak at 17,284 views. Jul 20 stands out as a large disc in the waning-crescent part of its row, a peak with no event in the sky behind it.

The page uses only `data.js` and no other files, libraries or network access. I wrote only `index.html` and `NOTE.md`.
