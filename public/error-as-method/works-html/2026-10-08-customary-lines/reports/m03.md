I made "Thirteen Lunations" and wrote both files to /tmp/claude-0/s116/mk/m03/work. I couldn't open it in a browser, so I haven't seen the rendered image. I only checked the placement maths in Node.

- **index.html**: a single 1100 × 800 SVG, built with plain JavaScript from `data.js`. It has no animation, no interaction and no libraries.
  - **Rows:** the year is laid out by the moon instead of the calendar. Each row is one lunar cycle, from new moon to new moon. Counting starts from the new moon of 29 Jan 2025 and a cycle length of 29.53 days, which gives 13 rows. Each row is labelled with the date of its new moon.
  - **Columns:** each day sits at the moon's age at noon UTC, so every full moon falls in the same middle column. There are faint guide lines for new, first quarter, full and last quarter.
  - **Each day:** drawn as the moon's shape that day, waxing lit on the right and waning on the left. Disc area is proportional to that day's views, from 3,777 to 17,284.
  - **Labels:** four peaks are named: 14 Mar and 7 Sep (both total lunar eclipses), 20 Jul (Apollo 11 anniversary) and 7 Oct (harvest full moon). There is also a size key and a source line.
- **NOTE.md**: the title as a heading and one paragraph. It says the form lays the year out by the moon so that any monthly pattern lines up in a column and anything that breaks it stands out. The checks show both eclipse days falling in the full-moon column (moon's age 0.49 and 0.48 of a cycle). 20 July falls in the waning crescent (age 0.82), which suggests interest driven by the calendar rather than the sky.

The 13-row count, the full-moon placement of the eclipse days and the label positions all came out as intended. The layout is tight at the bottom: the last row's discs end around y 734, and the size key starts at about y 747.
