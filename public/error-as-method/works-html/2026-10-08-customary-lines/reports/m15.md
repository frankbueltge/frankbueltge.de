I made "Core Sample, 2025" and wrote its two files in `/tmp/claude-0/s116/mk/m15/work`: `index.html` and `NOTE.md`. I rendered the page in headless Chromium at 1100 × 800 and checked it. After one spacing fix, no labels overlap.

**The other six:** they are all the same idea. Each is a dark night-sky grid with one lunation per row, and every day is drawn as a Moon-phase disc sized by readers. I avoided all three parts: no lunation grid, no phase discs, no night palette.

**My work:** the year's readers are drawn as a drilled sediment core, lying on a light, paper-toned tray.
- **Layers:** each day is one layer with slightly wavy edges. A layer's thickness is that day's readers. A quiet day is about 7 px and the busiest, 7 Sep, is about 34 px.
- **Sections:** the core is cut into four sections of equal readers, not equal time, like a real core box. The ruler under each section counts readers, from 0 to 1,937,249. Small tags mark where each month starts, so the months fall unevenly.
- **Colour:** ordinary days are shades of brown clay that drift slowly through the year. Days far above the 31-day median around them turn pale, like volcanic ash layers. The more they stand out, the paler they get.
- **Labels:** four pale bands carry a date, a reader count and a short reason: 14 Mar and 7 Sep (total lunar eclipses), 20 Jul (Apollo 11 anniversary) and 7 Oct (full moon).

**Build:** the page is one canvas in plain JavaScript and loads only `data.js`. It uses no libraries and no network, and needs no animation or interaction.

**What I found in the data:** there is no weekly rhythm (each weekday averages about 5,000–5,450 readers). There is a clear summer low: June averages about 4,280 readers a day, against about 6,480 in March.

`NOTE.md` has the title as a heading and one paragraph. It says the form is meant as the opposite of the moon-phase layout the other six share: attention that piles up and leaves a record.
