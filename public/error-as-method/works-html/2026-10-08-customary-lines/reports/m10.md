I made "Thirteen Turns", a polar spiral of the year. It's in `/tmp/claude-0/s116/mk/m10/work/index.html`, with its note in `/tmp/claude-0/s116/mk/m10/work/NOTE.md`. I rendered it at 1100 × 800 in headless Chromium and checked the picture: there were no script errors and the labels don't overlap.

**Why this form:** all six other works put the days in a lunation grid of phase-drawn discs. I wanted to keep the same lunar idea without repeating that look.

**How it works:**
- **Layout:** Earth is at the centre and the Sun is straight down. Each day's angle is the Moon's angle from the Sun that day, and its distance from the centre is the date. The year winds outward from 1 Jan inside to 31 Dec at the rim, in about 12.4 turns (the thirteen lunations the title counts). New moons fall at the bottom, full moons on the vertical line at the top, and the quarters to the sides.
- **Readers:** a line rides above the faint path. Its height is proportional to that day's views, and it gets brighter and thicker as views rise.
- **Labels:** five days are named on the spiral: 7 Sep (eclipse, 17,284), 14 Mar (eclipse), 7 Oct (supermoon), 20 Jul (Apollo 11 anniversary, on the waning side) and 28 Feb (on a new moon).
- **Side column:** title, how to read it, a scale, and a small chart of average readers by the Moon's age. That chart is computed from the data: about 7,890 on full-moon day against about 5,000 on other days.

**Limits:**
- The phase comes from the mean synodic month counted from the 29 Jan 2025 new moon, so the angles are approximate.
- The monthly full-moon rise is only a few pixels on most turns. It is visible but faint, and the big days carry most of the picture.
- The causes named in the labels (eclipse, supermoon, Apollo anniversary) are my reading of the dates. They don't come from the data.

The page uses plain SVG built with JavaScript and loads only `data.js`. I wrote no other files in the folder.
