I made "The Limb" and wrote its note. Both files are in /tmp/claude-0/s116/mk/m08/work: `index.html` and `NOTE.md`.

**The form:** all six other works folded the year into rows from one new moon to the next, with each day drawn as a phase disc. I kept a plain timeline instead and gave it the subject's shape. The picture is a large Moon rising from the bottom of the frame against a dark sky. The 365 days run left to right along its curved edge, and the edge's height above a thin inner line is that day's page views, measured from zero. Readership becomes a ridge along the Moon's horizon, with each day as one step.

- **Inside the Moon:** month names and ticks sit along the inner line.
- **In the sky:** two dotted arcs mark 10,000 and 15,000 views.
- **Full moons:** a small white dot sits on top of each of the year's twelve.
- **Labelled peaks:**
  - 7 Sep, total lunar eclipse, 17,284 (the year's highest day)
  - 20 Jul, Apollo 11 anniversary, 13,576
  - 14 Mar, total lunar eclipse, 11,854
  - 7 Oct, supermoon, 10,896
  - 5 Nov, largest supermoon, 7,624
  - 28 Feb, planets aligned, 8,534

**What it shows:** an ordinary day is a low, fairly steady ridge, and attention arrives as single spikes. Most spikes carry a full-moon dot. The 20 July and 28 February spikes do not.

**To check:** the reasons on the labels come from my own knowledge, not from the data file. I am least sure of "planets aligned" for 28 Feb, which I took to be the seven-planet alignment that day.

The page is built from inline SVG with plain JavaScript, loads only `data.js`, and has no animation. I rendered it with headless Chromium at 1100 × 800 and adjusted the layout until nothing was clipped or overlapping; the test screenshots went to the session scratchpad.
