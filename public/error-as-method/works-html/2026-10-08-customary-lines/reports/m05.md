I made a work called "Read by Moonlight" and wrote the two files: `/tmp/claude-0/s116/mk/m05/work/index.html` and `/tmp/claude-0/s116/mk/m05/work/NOTE.md`. I never looked at it rendered. Opening a browser would have written files outside the folder, so I only ran the script in Node with a fake canvas: it ran without errors, and I checked the layout numbers by hand.

**The form:** it is a still canvas image at 1100 × 800, loading `data.js` and using no libraries or network.
- **Rows:** each of the 13 rows is one lunar cycle, from new moon to new moon. The rows are computed from a known new-moon date and the length of a lunar cycle.
- **Columns:** each of the 30 columns is how many days old the Moon was.
- **Each day:** drawn as a small Moon in that day's real phase, with the right lit part, waxing or waning. Its size depends on that day's page views (radius grows with the square root of the count).
- **Labels:** quiet guide lines for new, first quarter, full and last quarter; the first date of each row on the left; the date and count above the four biggest days; a size key; the yearly total; and the CC0 source credit.

**What the data shows:** most of the year's biggest days line up in the full-moon column: 14 March (11,854), 7 September (17,284, the year's highest), 6–7 October and 5 November. The late-February spike falls on a new moon. One big day, 20 July (13,576), does not fit the pattern; it falls a few days after last quarter.

`NOTE.md` has the title as a heading and one paragraph saying what the form does and why I chose it: letting the Moon's own cycle arrange the data shows that readers come when something happens in the sky.
