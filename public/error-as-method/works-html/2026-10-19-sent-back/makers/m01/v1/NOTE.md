# One Sky of Orbits

All 6,451 confirmed exoplanets are moved to a single imagined star at the centre. Each planet's distance from the centre is its orbital period on a log scale, from hours to millennia. Its angle is its place in the order of discovery: going clockwise from 1992 at the top, every planet gets an equal slice. Colour shows the discovery method and dot size the planet's radius. A faint ring is drawn for every orbit, and a dashed ring marks Earth's year. Planets with no known period sit on an outer rim. I chose this form because it puts two things in one picture. The first is how the catalogue grew: the early years are slivers, and the bulk releases of 2014 and 2016 open into wide fans. The second is how strange the planets found so far are: transit planets crowd far inside Earth's year, while radial-velocity giants spread outward and the planets found by direct imaging and microlensing drift at the edge.
