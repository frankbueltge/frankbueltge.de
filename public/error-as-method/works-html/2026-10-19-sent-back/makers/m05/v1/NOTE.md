# The Length of Their Years

All 6,451 confirmed exoplanets are laid out on one dial. Going clockwise, each planet sits at the year it was found. How far out it sits from the centre is the length of its year, on a log scale that runs from about two hours to a million days. Colour shows how it was found and dot size shows its radius. Faint rings mark the years of Mercury, Earth, Jupiter and Neptune for scale. Planets whose orbital period is unknown, mostly found by microlensing, sit as loose dust outside the rim. I chose a dial because it puts time and orbit in one picture, and in that picture the methods sort themselves into bands. Transits crowd the inner rings, with the Kepler releases of 2014 and 2016 showing as bright spokes. Radial velocity fills the middle, and imaging and microlensing lie at the edge. So the picture shows what each instrument is able to see as much as it shows what is out there.
