# Soundings

Each confirmed exoplanet is dropped like a sounding line from a surface at the top, which stands for the Sun. It falls into the column for the year it was found and stops at the distance of its star, on a log scale from 1 to 10 000 parsecs. Colour gives the method of discovery and dot size gives the planet's radius, and the few planets with no known distance lie in a strip under the floor. I chose depth over a plain scatter because the archive is really a record of how far we have been able to look. The first radial-velocity finds stay in shallow water within about 100 pc. The Kepler releases of 2014 and 2016 come down as two bright shafts several hundred parsecs deep, microlensing sets a pink seabed near the galactic centre, and Proxima Centauri and Barnard's star sit just under the surface.
