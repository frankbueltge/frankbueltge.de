# Sediment

The 6,451 confirmed exoplanets are laid down as strata, one per discovery year from 1992 at the top to 2026 at the bottom, each layer as thick as that year's haul. Across the page each planet sits at the length of its own year (orbital period, log scale, with Mercury, Earth, Jupiter and Neptune marked), within a layer Earth-size planets float and Jupiter-size ones sink (a small key says so), faint ruled lines separate the layers, and colour gives the method that found it. Planets with no known period collect in a narrow column at the right. I chose a geological section because the archive is a record of how it was deposited, not a map of the sky: you can read the thin early layers of radial-velocity giants on Jupiter-like orbits, the two Kepler floods of short-period transiting worlds in 2014 and 2016, and the gap at long periods that no method has filled yet.
