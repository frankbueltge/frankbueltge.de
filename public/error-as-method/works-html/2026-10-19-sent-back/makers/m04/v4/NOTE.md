# Sediment

The 6,451 confirmed exoplanets are laid down as strata, one per discovery year from 1992 at the top to 2026 at the bottom, each layer a thin floor plus a thickness in proportion to that year's haul, so every year down to the empty 1993 can still be read. Across the page each planet sits at the length of its own year (orbital period, log scale, with Mercury, Earth, Jupiter and Neptune marked), within a layer the planets are graded like sediment by size rank, smallest at the top and largest at the bottom (a small key says so), faint ruled lines separate the layers, and colour gives the method that found it. Planets with no known period collect in a narrow column at the right. I chose a geological section because the archive is a record of how it was deposited, not a map of the sky: you can read the thin early layers of radial-velocity giants on Jupiter-like orbits, the two Kepler floods of short-period transiting worlds in 2014 and 2016, the giants that settle as a bright floor under every layer, and the gap at long periods that no method has filled yet.
