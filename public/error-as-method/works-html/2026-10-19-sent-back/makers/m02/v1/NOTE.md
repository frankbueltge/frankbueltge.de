# The Shape of Looking

Every confirmed planet with a known mass is a point of light, placed by the length of its year (log scale) and by how heavy it is (log scale), coloured by how it was found. Planets with no known orbital period sit in a separate column on the far right. The eight planets of our own system are white rings among them. I chose this plane because it holds both the planets and the instruments that found them. The blue swarm of transits sits at short periods, because a planet has to cross its star often to be caught. The orange cloud of radial-velocity giants sits high, because only heavy planets pull their stars hard enough to measure. The space around Earth is nearly empty, so the picture reads less as a census of the galaxy than as a portrait of how we look.
