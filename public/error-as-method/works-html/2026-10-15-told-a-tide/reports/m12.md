I made **"Folded on the Sun"**. It's in `/tmp/claude-0/s128/mk/m12/work/index.html` and `/tmp/claude-0/s128/mk/m12/work/NOTE.md`. The page stands alone: the whole 300×90 grayscale picture is embedded as hex and drawn with canvas, and it loads nothing else. I rendered it at 1100×800 in headless Chromium with no errors and no network requests. My working scripts and screenshots are in `/tmp/claude-0/s128/mk/m12/scratch`.

**What I did first.** I looked at the image, then dumped it as ASCII art and measured how the ink spreads up and down each column. It didn't look like a tide. A tide spends extra time at both high and low water, so the dots would bunch at the top and bottom of the band. Here they are dense near a ceiling and trail off in a long, sparse tail downward.

**How I got to the form.** Each pixel column holds about 5 hours, so I took a frequency spectrum of the ink's vertical centre, column by column, across the image.
- **Sun:** there is one strong peak at exactly 24.0 h, plus a 12.0 h harmonic.
- **Moon:** at the lunar semidiurnal period (12.42 h), the main constituent of almost every harbour tide, there is nothing (amplitude about 2–6 against 956 for the 24 h peak). The lunar diurnal period (25.8 h) also shows nothing above the background.
- **Folding:** I wrapped the record on 24.00 h and on 24.84 h (the moon's tidal day). On the 24 h fold the average level moves about 9 px, roughly 10% of the image height, around the day. On the lunar fold it moves about 2 px.

That contrast became the work. A large dial winds every speck of ink once per 24 h, with higher values farther out. Dark shows where the record spends more time than usual at that height and hour, ochre where it spends less, and a thin line marks the average. A smaller dial does the same on the tide's 24 h 50 min clock, on the same colour scale. The original strip sits at its own size as the source.

**Things you should know.**
- The image cannot tell me the clock time. The dial's zero is just where the record starts, and the work says so.
- Each pixel blurs about 5 hours, and the dots are about 2 px wide, so I can't recover individual hourly values. The dials show where the ink piles up, not reconstructed values.
- The lunar dial isn't completely blank. Over 61 days its phase only drifts about two full cycles, so weather and leftovers of the daily cycle show through as faint bands. The caption says it is "fainter and keeps no side" rather than claiming it is empty.
- The work openly contradicts the label it was given. That was a deliberate choice.

**My view of what was measured.** I don't think this is a tidal water level, or at least I don't think the moon's tide is visible in it. The record follows the solar day, with no lunar signal. It also has a stable upper ceiling it holds for part of each day, and drops in the other part, sometimes deep, as runs of separate dots. My best guess is a weather variable with a ceiling, most likely relative humidity: near saturation at night, falling in the afternoon, with drier spells as the deep dips. It could also be some other sun-driven quantity. The evidence is the 24.0 h spectral peak and its 12 h harmonic, the missing 12.42 h peak, and the dense-top, long-lower-tail shape that appears in one half of the 24 h fold. If it is water level, the sensor is recording something other than the tide, or the label is wrong.
