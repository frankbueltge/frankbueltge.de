I made "Sixty-One Turns", a still image that winds the whole 61-day record onto one 24-hour dial, with the original record laid out flat beneath it.

Files:
- /tmp/claude-0/s128/mk/m02/work/index.html (about 30 KB, stands alone)
- /tmp/claude-0/s128/mk/m02/work/NOTE.md
- Working scripts and a test screenshot are in /tmp/claude-0/s128/mk/m02/scratch

**What I did first.** I looked at the image, then printed it as text, character by character, to see how the dots land in the pixels. Dots are about 2 px. The data fills only x≈2.5 to 296.5, so there are 4.98 hours per pixel column and one day is about 4.8 px wide. The individual hourly values cannot be recovered: each column merges about five hours. The values sit in a dense upper band, with sparse dots falling well below it.

**How I got to the form.**
- **The daily rhythm.** I measured how strongly each column's profile repeats at different spacings. The strongest repeat is 4.81 px, which is 23.9 h. Folding the image on 24 h gives a daily swing of about 12.7 px. Folding it on 24.84 h (lunar day), 25.82 h, 12.42 h (both tidal periods) or 23.5 h gives only about 2 px, which is noise. So there is one clear rhythm and it follows the sun.
- **A slower swell.** There is also an irregular rise and fall of roughly 9 days (about 43 px), which looks like weather rather than a cycle.
- **The dial.** Reading the record left to right hides the daily cycle at this resolution, so I wound it instead. Each pixel column's ink is laid as a faint arc covering its own ~5 hours on the dial, and distance from the centre is the value. The 61 days stack up like a long exposure, coloured from blue-black at the start to rust at the end. The result is a ring pulled off-centre: dense and tight across most of the day, sinking inward and thinning where each day falls.
- **The strip.** The source pixels are embedded in the page and drawn underneath as the unwound record, with one tick per day.

**Things you should know.**
- **The dial is detrended.** Before winding, I shifted each column by a 24-hour running median so the 9-day swells would not blur the ring. Without this the asymmetry washed out almost completely. The strip shows the record without that step, and NOTE.md says so.
- **The arcs are deliberately blurred.** Each arc spans a ~5-hour window, because that is the true time resolution of the file. They are not invented dot positions.
- **The rotation is arbitrary.** I don't know the clock time of the first sample. I turned the dial so the daily low (about 18 h after the first sample, modulo 24) sits at the bottom. The hours carry no labels.
- **Checks.** I rendered the page in headless Chromium at 1100×800. There were no errors and no network requests.

**My view of what was measured (held loosely).** It is something the sun drives down once a day. It rests high and steady for much of the day, drops for some hours each day by a widely varying amount, and drifts with weather on a scale of about 9 days. My best guess is near-surface air relative humidity: high and flat at night, dipping in the afternoon, deeply on clear days and only a little on overcast ones. The evidence is:
- the 24 h solar period, with lunar and tidal periods clearly ruled out;
- the lopsided distribution, with a dense top band and a long, sparse tail downward;
- the irregular multi-day swell.

One thing goes against the guess. Around day 20 (x≈100–105) there is a short run of values well above the usual top band. That fits poorly with a quantity capped near saturation, unless that band is not actually at the cap. Other things the sun pulls down by day, such as dissolved CO2 in water or a water level drawn down by plants during the day, are possible but less likely.
