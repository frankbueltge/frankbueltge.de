I made "Two Clocks, One Harbour". It is in `/tmp/claude-0/s128/mk/m05/work/index.html`, a single self-contained canvas page of about 9.6 KB with no external loads, and `/tmp/claude-0/s128/mk/m05/work/NOTE.md`. My reading of the file is that it doesn't behave like a harbour tide. Its main rhythm is a once-a-day drop locked to the solar day.

**What I did first with the file**
I looked at it and enlarged it 4×. Then I pulled out every dark pixel, column by column, with PIL. The plotted dots fill columns 2 to 296, which works out to about 5.0 hours per column. That means each column holds about five readings, and their order within the column can't be recovered. So I worked from what each column shows as a whole: its upper edge, its lowest dot and how many dots it has. I did not try to recover the 1,464 single values.

**How I got from it to the form**
- **What I measured:** I ran period scans over the per-column envelopes. The lowest-dot series has a strong peak at 24.06 hours, and it is the same in each half of the record. Depending on exactly where I take the plot's margins, the figure moves between 23.98 and 24.14 hours.
- **The tidal periods:** the main lunar tide (12.42 h) and the lunar day (24.84 h) are weak, as is the daily tidal pair (K1 at 23.93 h, O1 at 25.82 h).
- **Steadiness:** in rolling 6-day windows, the timing of the daily drop stays put across all 61 days. It never fades away on the roughly two-week cycle a tide would show.
- **The form:** the work stacks the 61 days as rows, two days to a row, twice. The left panel uses the moon's day (24 h 50 min), the clock a tide keeps. The right panel uses the sun's day (24 h). Each picture column hangs down from its row by how far its lowest dot fell below the level around it. Its sides fade out, because the picture can't say which of its five hours the drop came in. On the right the drops stack into straight columns down the page. On the left they wander and step sideways.

**Things the commissioner should know**
- **Resolution:** the source holds 1,464 values in 300 pixels, and it is a JPEG. Everything in the work comes from column outlines, not individual readings.
- **A trap in the binning:** five columns come to about 24.9 hours, almost exactly a moon's day. That alone makes the left panel look more ordered than the data justifies. I softened the column shapes so that this artefact doesn't drive the comparison.
- **The faint drift on the right:** the right-panel columns lean slightly, about 4 minutes a day. That is within how precisely I could read the plot's margins.
- **What I didn't use:** I looked nothing up. The scripts, encoded data and screenshots are in `/tmp/claude-0/s128/mk/m05/scratch`.

**My view of what was measured**
I'm told this is the water level in a tidal harbour. That isn't what the file shows:
1. A semidiurnal tide would leave a clear 12 h 25 min signal and its 24 h 50 min multiple. A diurnal tide would come with O1 and fade on the roughly 13.7-day cycle. None of these show.
2. The values bunch near a slowly wandering top level, with a thin tail of low values. A tide sampled every hour bunches at both high and low water.
3. Neither the tidal range nor the depth of the daily drop changes on the 14.8-day spring–neap cycle.
4. The one strong rhythm is a drop, once a day at the same clock hour. It lasts a few hours and gets deeper in the second month.

That points to something driven by the sun or by people's daily routine. Possibilities include a level drawn down once a day by pumping, a lock or a sluice, or a non-water quantity such as relative humidity. Either the label is wrong, or the tide has been removed or isn't visible at this resolution. I can't tell which from the picture, so the work shows the record against both clocks rather than naming it.
