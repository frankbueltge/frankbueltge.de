I made "The Hour It Falls", a still canvas work at `/tmp/claude-0/s128/mk/m03/work/index.html`. It stands alone, with the data embedded and no external files, libraries or network requests. The note is at `/tmp/claude-0/s128/mk/m03/work/NOTE.md`. I rendered the page at 1100 × 800 in headless Chromium and checked it by eye; it draws completely with no interaction.

**What I did first.** I looked at the picture, then enlarged it 4× and decoded it to grayscale. The ink spans pixel columns 3 to 297, so each pixel column holds about 5 readings and one day is about 4.84 px wide. Single hourly values can't be recovered; what the picture does hold is, for each column, where the record rests and how low it reaches. Its main feature is lopsided: a dense band near the top with a sparse tail of lower values hanging under it.

**How I got to the form.**
- **Period:** a periodogram of the tail's depth per column has a strong peak at exactly one day. The best fit is 23.98 h, within calibration error of 24 h. Strength at 24.0 h is 531 against 9 at the lunar-day period of 24.84 h; over 61 days those two periods are about two full cycles apart, so the picture can tell them apart. There is also a 12 h harmonic, which comes from the sharp shape of the drop, not from a second cycle.
- **Phase:** the time of day of the drop stays roughly fixed across the record, drifting by a few hours at most.
- **Form:** because the rhythm is the main finding, I folded the record into a double-plotted actogram: 61 rows, each showing a day and the day after, 48 hours across.
  - Each row has a dark line for where the record rests and a rust-coloured hang for how far the lowest readings fell, drawn as overlapping ridgelines.
  - The original strip is redrawn above the fold.
  - The falls stack into two upright columns, around hours 9 to 14 counted from the first reading. A run of deeper, longer falls stands out around days 37 to 47, where the whole level also sits lower.

**What the commissioner should know.**
- The values are read back from a 300 × 90 JPEG, not from data. Each hang is a smoothed window of about 5 hours, and I interpolated between the column centres for continuity.
- "Hour 0" is the first reading, not a known clock time.
- The ~4 h drift in drop time over the record is within what my x-axis calibration can resolve, so I don't claim it's real.
- My first two density renders smeared the drop away; the ridgeline form replaced them.
- My working folder path contained the phrase "told-a-tide". That is not in my folder, and I did not use it. The file itself argues against a tide.

**My view of what was measured (low confidence).** It is something sun- or clock-driven, not tidal. The evidence:
- The cycle is 24.0 h, not 24.84 h or 12.42 h.
- The shape is lopsided: steady most of the day, then a short, sharp fall once a day.
- The depth of the fall varies from day to day, with spells of deeper falls.
- The resting level wanders over several days at a time.

That fits relative humidity, which rests high and drops in the afternoon, more deeply on sunny dry days, with weather spells behind the slow wander. A daily scheduled drawdown, such as pumping or demand, would fit too. Nothing in the file pins it down. Against humidity: the top of the band shows no hard ceiling near saturation.
