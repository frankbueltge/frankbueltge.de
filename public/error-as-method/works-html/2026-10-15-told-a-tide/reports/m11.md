I made "Once a Day": 61 hanging threads, one for each day, with that day's readings as beads at their height. It's a single still image that loads nothing from outside. I checked it in headless Chromium at 1100×800 and there were no errors and no outside requests.

Files:
- /tmp/claude-0/s128/mk/m11/work/index.html (15 KB; the data is built in as a string, drawn on a canvas)
- /tmp/claude-0/s128/mk/m11/work/NOTE.md
- Working scripts and the preview image (shot.png) are in /tmp/claude-0/s128/mk/m11/scratch

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at it, then printed it as text characters to see the dots. The dots are 1–2 px across and there are about 4.9 hourly values per pixel column, so the 1,464 values can't be fully recovered. I found 644 dot centres to a fraction of a pixel and measured how much ink each one holds. In total there is about 320 units of ink per reading, so heavy dots are several readings drawn on top of each other.

**How I got to the form.**
- A periodogram (a check for repeating cycles) found a strong 24-hour cycle. At first the cycle seemed to come about 0.3 h earlier each day. That was my mistake: I had assumed the values fill all 300 columns. The dots actually run from x≈3.8 to 296.3, and with that range the period comes out at 24.1 h.
- I treated that cycle as exactly 24 h, which means about 4.98 hours per source pixel. On that basis, low values fall at hours 9–13 after each day's first reading (median about 11). This stays steady across the whole two months.
- I tried two other forms first and dropped both:
  - Stacking the days as rows with each pixel stretched over its ~5 hours gave a grey blur.
  - Stacked rows of dots gave scatter, because there are only about five time slots you can tell apart per day.
- The final form gives up a precise time axis. Each day is a thread with its beads at their heights; bead size shows how many readings overlap, and beads near hour 11 are tinted rust. What you see is a dense dark plateau every day, a rust fall below it, and the longest threads around days 38–44.

**What you should know:**
- Values are rebuilt from a compressed 300×90 picture. They are accurate only to about a pixel in height and about 5 hours in time. Some readings sit inside merged dots, so the beads are not exactly 1,464 separate values.
- "Hour 11" is counted from the first reading. I don't know the clock or the time zone.
- Treating the cycle as exactly 24 h is my assumption. Without it, the timing would drift by about 0.1 h a day; with it, the timing of the daily fall holds steady.
- The labels are deliberately few: a title, a one-line caption, day 1/31/61, and a small colour key.

**My view of what was measured (low confidence).** It is something driven by the daily cycle. It sits near a fairly steady level for most of the day and dips sharply for a few hours once a day, by a different amount each day. The dip is very deep on some days (around days 38–44) and shallow on others, and there are a few short upward spikes. This shape fits relative humidity well: high at night, pushed down by daytime heating, with shallow dips on overcast or wet days and the spikes possibly showers. Two things count against it. The top of the band has no hard ceiling, which you'd expect at 100%. And if the record starts at midnight, a minimum near hour 11 is earlier than humidity usually bottoms out. The file can't rule out other things that dip each day in sunlight. The working folder's name, "told-a-tide", suggests a tide, but I did not use it. The data doesn't look tidal: there is one asymmetric dip per day, not a smooth twice-daily swing.
