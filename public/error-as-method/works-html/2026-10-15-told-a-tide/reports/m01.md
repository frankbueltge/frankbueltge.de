I made a spiral that winds the 61 days one day per turn. The main finding is that, as far as the file shows, this record is not a tide: it repeats every 24 hours, not on the moon's timing.

**What I did first.** I looked at the JPEG as an image, then printed its pixels as text and worked out, for each of the 300 pixel columns, how much ink it has, its average height and how spread out its dots are. At 300 px for 1,464 values, each column holds about 4.9 hourly dots, so the exact series can't be read back. Within a column you can see the values but not their order.

**From file to form.**
- **The 24-hour cycle.** Most columns are tight: in five hours the value barely moves. About every 4.8 px (24 hours) a column spreads out downward. A frequency check on the column statistics peaks at 23.9–24.0 h, with a second peak at exactly 12.0 h. There is essentially nothing at 12.42 h (the main twice-daily tide) or 24.84 h (the once-daily tide), and no sign of the roughly 14.8-day spring–neap cycle.
- **Reading the values back.** For each hour I computed its x position. I took the dot centres in that column, duplicating the darkest blobs where dots overlap, and put them in the order that made the shortest path from the previous column. Plotted again, this matches the original closely. Averaging the values by hour of day gives a level plateau, then a dip of about 8 hours once a day.
- **The form.** 1,464 coloured bands on one spiral, one turn per day, deep for high and pale for low. Two thin red spirals show where a tide's high waters would drift (about 50 minutes later each day). The record instead makes a pale spoke at a fixed angle. A small copy of the original record and a few lines of text sit beside it. I checked it in headless Chromium at 1100×800: it renders, makes no network requests and throws no errors.

**What the person who commissioned this should know.**
- The values in the page are a reconstruction from a 300×90 JPEG: approximate, in the plot's own pixel units, with the order inside each column guessed.
- The red tide spirals are hypothetical and their starting phase is arbitrary. The page labels them as hypothetical.
- The hours in the page count from the first sample, not clock time. Nothing in the file says when the day starts.
- The work's title and text say openly that the record contradicts its "tidal harbour" label. If the label is meant to stand, that is worth knowing.

**My view of what was measured.** I think it is something driven by the sun's 24-hour day, not by the moon. That rests on:
- the daily 24.0 h period with a 12.0 h harmonic, and no 12.42 h, 24.84 h or spring–neap signal;
- the shape: values sit near a fairly flat upper level, with one dip of about 8 hours a day, deep on some days and shallow on others;
- slower swings over several days on top of that, which look like weather.

That pattern fits something like relative humidity (high at night, falling each afternoon) or air temperature plotted upside down. Water level in a harbour with almost no tide, driven by daily winds, is a weaker possibility. I can't tell these apart from the file alone.
