# Sixty-One Turns

The record holds one strong rhythm: it repeats every 24 hours, tied to the sun and not the moon. This work winds the whole record onto a single day so that rhythm can be seen. Each pixel column of the source, about five consecutive hours, is laid as a faint arc on a 24-hour dial. Distance from the centre is the value, with a slow running median of about 24 hours removed first, so the 9-day swells do not blur the shape of a day. All 61 days build up on top of each other like a long exposure, coloured from blue-black at the start to rust at the end. Where a day keeps its values high and close together, the ink builds into a dense ring. At the bottom, where every day falls for a few hours and scatters, the ring sinks toward the centre and thins out. The unwound record runs beneath, shown at its true grain, with one tick per day, so the circle can be read against the line it came from. I chose winding because the file's resolution, about five hours per pixel, hides the daily cycle when the record is read left to right, and laying the days over each other brings it out.
