# Sixty-One Ebbs

The record's hours are cut at its own once-a-day beat of about 23.9 hours, and all sixty-one days are laid on top of each other in one frame. Height is the measured value, measured from each day's own slow level (a two-day running edge of the band), so weather and season do not blur the daily shape. Each column of the source picture holds about five hours of dots whose order inside the column cannot be recovered, so each one is spread softly across its own five hours and not set down as a sharp point. What builds up is a dark, steady band where the water stays for most of the turn, and below it, mid-turn, a soft basin where on every day some hours fall far below the rest. I chose to superimpose the days and not lay them end to end because laid end to end the record looks like noise. Stacked on its own beat it shows one gesture, repeated sixty-one times, and the blur is honest about how much the 300-pixel picture can still tell.
