# Two Clocks, One Harbour

The 61 days are cut into rows and stacked, as in an actogram, twice: on the left each row is one lunar day (24 h 50 min), the clock a harbour tide keeps; on the right each row is one solar day (24 h). Each column of the source picture, about five hours of readings, hangs down from its row by how far its lowest dot fell below the level around it, softened at its edges because the picture cannot say which of its five hours the fall came in. When I read the picture, its strongest rhythm was a once-a-day drop locked to 24.0 hours, with no 12 h 25 min tide in it, so the work puts the record against both clocks and lets you see which one it keeps: on the right the falls stack into straight columns down all 61 days, and on the left they slide sideways.
