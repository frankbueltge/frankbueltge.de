# A Shorter Day

The record is cut into rows twice and the two cuttings are shown side by side. On the left every row is a 24-hour day; on the right every row is 23 hours 33 minutes. Each row is double-plotted (it shows its own cycle and the next) and the record's hours are drawn as a thin trace, with the stretches that fall below their usual level filled in ink. I chose this form because the most striking thing in the picture is not any single value but a rhythm: once a cycle the values drop for about eight hours, and that cycle is slightly shorter than a day. Cut at 24 hours, the low hours slide about half an hour earlier each day and make a diagonal across two months. Cut at the record's own period, they stand still as a column. The unfolded record runs along the bottom as it came: one dot per hour.
