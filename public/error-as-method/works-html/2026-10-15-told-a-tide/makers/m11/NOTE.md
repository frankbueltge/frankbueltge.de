# Once a Day

The record is hung as a curtain of sixty-one threads, one for each day. Each thread carries that day's readings as beads at their height, and a bead is heavier where several readings landed on the same spot in the source picture. Beads read around the eleventh hour after each day's first reading are tinted rust; the rest are ink. The one thing the picture shows for certain is a daily rhythm: the value holds near a level for most of the day, then falls once, for a few hours, by a different amount each day. A line chart already exists (it is the source picture), and that picture can only place a reading to within about five hours, so I gave up a precise time axis. Cutting the record into days and letting each day hang shows the two things that are really there: the steady plateau, and the fall below it. The rust beads gather low on almost every thread, and the threads hang longest in the stretch around days 38–44.
