# Told a Tide

The 1,464 hourly readings are wound onto a spiral that makes one turn every twenty-four hours, from the centre outward, so each of the sixty-one days is one turn and each hour of the day always falls in the same direction; a higher reading sits further out, a lower one bites in toward the day before. I chose the spiral because it asks one question of the record: what clock does it keep? A tide keeps the moon's time, 12 h 25 min between lows, so its lows should drift around the dial (the red ticks show where they would land, counted on from the first low in the record), but the large dots, each day's actual lowest hour, stay bunched on one side of the spiral, once a day, which is the sun's time. The fine lines of dots running straight out from the centre show the same thing more quietly: twenty-four readings a day line up with themselves.
