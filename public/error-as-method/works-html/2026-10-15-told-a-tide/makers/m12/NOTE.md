# Folded on the Sun

The work winds the record onto a clock. Every speck of ink in the 300 × 90 picture is laid onto a dial that turns once every 24 hours, so the sixty-one days stack on top of one another and height becomes distance from the centre. Dark marks where the record spends more time than usual at that height and that moment of the day, ochre marks where it spends less, and a thin line traces the mean. A smaller dial does the same on the tide's clock of 24 h 50 min. I chose a fold because the plain strip hides what the record repeats. I was told this was a harbour's tide, but the record repeats on the sun's day, not the moon's: through one half of the day it holds a ceiling, and in the other half it falls away. The lunar dial stays faint and has no one side. The work shows that gap between the label and the picture instead of drawing the tide I was told to expect.
