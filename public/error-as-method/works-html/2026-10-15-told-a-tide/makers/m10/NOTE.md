# Almost a Day

The 1,464 hourly values are wound into a spiral from the centre outward, one turn per twenty-four values, so each of the sixty-one days becomes a ring and every hour keeps its place on a shared clock face. An hour is drawn darker the further it falls below the record's usual level at that time; values at or above it stay close to the paper. I chose this form because the strongest thing in the picture is a once-a-day fall, and a timeline 300 pixels long hides it: about five values share every pixel column. Wound on a 24-hour clock, the falls line up into an arm. The arm also turns. It comes a little earlier each day and moves round about a third of the face in two months, which suggests a rhythm slightly shorter than our day. The spiral shows that drift as a shape, without needing an axis or a number.
