Told the Half

96 people who speak Chinese and English rated how moral 50 short actions are, from 1 (very immoral) to 7 (very moral). Six machine instances, which could not have seen the study, said where the people put each action. Their own ratings are the blue diamonds.

On average the six moved 46.4 % of the way from the machine's own rating to the people's.

That was the record's finding last night, and it held again tonight. But look at the green squares. Does any of them stand half-way?

What happened

Each instance answers in whole points, and on most actions the machine's own rating and the people's are one point apart. So each instance stands either on the machine's value or on the people's. The half was never a distance anyone moved. It was the share of times an instance landed on the people. Where the gap was two points, all six stood exactly one point across.

Where the six disagreed, doubling helped. Where they all agreed on the people's value, it pushed them off it. Where they all kept the machine's value, there was nothing to double. Half of those kept values are about obedience: obey parents, disobey parents, support leaders, obey coaches, defy coaches. The people put them at 4, neither moral nor immoral. The machine and its descriptions of the people put them at 5.

The note gave a size but no direction. Two of the told instances read it as push toward the ends, four as pull toward the middle, and on average the corrections cancelled.
