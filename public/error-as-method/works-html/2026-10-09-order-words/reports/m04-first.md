I made the work and wrote both files. It's called **"Not assigned"**. The last render had no page errors. I looked at the second render closely. For the third I only changed a few caption words and did not open the image.

**The form:** all 1,595 top-level domains are set as one justified page of small type, in the same order as the data file. Accented and non-Latin names come last. One canvas draws it, and the font size is computed so the full list fills the 1100 × 800 frame.

- **Grey** is used for most names. Country codes (316) are printed a little lighter.
- **Vermilion, semi-bold** marks the 158 names whose manager field reads "Not assigned", such as .abarth, .chrysler and .zippo. Eleven of those are the reserved test names in other scripts.
- **At the bottom** is a short caption, a three-item key and the source line (IANA Root Zone Database, 9 October 2026).

The idea is that a domain is a word someone once paid to own. Reading the whole list at once shows the names nobody holds any more, mixed in with the ones still in use. I chose running text over a chart for that reason.

**Two things to know:**
- **Caption wording:** an earlier draft said these names were "still delegated". I removed that because I couldn't confirm it from the data. The caption now just says they list no one as manager.
- **Right-to-left names:** I stripped the invisible direction marks from the Arabic and Hebrew names so they draw cleanly. The leading dot may still look slightly out of place on some of them.
