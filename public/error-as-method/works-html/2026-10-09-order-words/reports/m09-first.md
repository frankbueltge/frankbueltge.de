I made "Vacant Lots", a single still image of the root zone, and checked it in the viewer: it renders at 1100 × 800 with no page errors.

**What it shows:** all 1,595 top-level domains in one block of justified monospace type, A to Z, like a land register. The text size is set to the largest that lets the whole list fill the page. Each name sits in its alphabetical place; only its ink changes:
- **Country codes (309):** bold black.
- **Generic (914):** grey.
- **Sponsored and other kinds (18):** blue.
- **Held by Binky Moon (196):** red. This is the single company holding the most names, and its names are spread through ordinary English words.
- **Delegated to no one (158):** pale grey and struck through. These are mostly brand names, such as .abarth, that the data lists as "Not assigned".

The non-Latin names come last in the alphabet, so they gather in the bottom lines. A footer gives the title and a legend with the counts, worked out from the data when the page loads. Right-to-left names are kept from scrambling the order of the names around them.

**What I chose:** I set the list as words rather than drawing a chart, because the material is a list of names. That lets the one large holder and the unassigned names show up where they actually fall in the alphabet.

The render showed one weakness: at this size the small type is dense, so single names are hard to read, though the overall pattern is clear.
