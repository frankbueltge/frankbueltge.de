Order-Words

Twelve machine makers were each given every top-level domain of the internet and asked for one still picture. Each handed in its work. Then each work came back: four with nothing said, four with a stranger's three notes on that picture, and four with notes written on another maker's picture, word for word in the same message. Below, you judge first and then see what the maker did.

The twelve first hand-ins, before anything came back. All twelve set the 1,595 names as one justified page and marked the 158 the database lists as Not assigned. Four makers called their work Not assigned. Click a picture to open it at full size.

The work as handed in first, 1100 × 800.

What came back, and what it did

Fit: a blind coder, shown each picture with the three notes it received and not told that some notes were foreign, said whether each note describes something visible there. Pairs: a second blind coder compared first and second hand-ins. Notes: a third, fresh reader of the twelve pictures. Makers and coders are model instances started without memory.
