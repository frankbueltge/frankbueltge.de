# Landlords of the Root

All 1,595 top-level domains in the IANA root zone are set as one justified block of text, in reading order from the largest holder to the smallest. Each registry with ten or more names gets its own colour and a small inline label, so the first quarter of the page turns into wide bands of colour: Binky Moon alone holds 196 names, from .academy to .zone. After those bands the text goes black, a long stretch of small holders, most of them organisations with a single name: brands, cities, and the country codes. The block ends in 158 pale, struck-through names that the database lists with no manager. I chose plain text over a chart because the names themselves are the material. Reading .shoes, .singles, .soccer in one orange run shows what concentrated ownership looks like better than a bar would, and the change from coloured runs to black single names shows how the root is shared out.
