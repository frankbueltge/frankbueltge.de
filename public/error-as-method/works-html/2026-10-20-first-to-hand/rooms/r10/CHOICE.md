# First to mind

1. Volcanic eruptions of the Holocene (Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program)
2. Sunspot numbers (SILSO, Royal Observatory of Belgium)
3. Lake ice-on / ice-off dates (e.g. Lake Mendota, Wisconsin)

# Chosen

The monthly mean total sunspot number, 1749 to September 2026 (version 2.0), from WDC-SILSO,
Royal Observatory of Belgium, Brussels: https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/INFO/snmtotcsv.php
(described at https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/datafiles).

How I came to it: of my three, volcanic eruptions sat too close to earthquakes, and lake ice too
close to Arctic sea ice. The sun's spots are the visible face of something that none of the listed
materials is about: the solar dynamo itself. Geomagnetic activity is the Earth's response to the
sun, not the count of marks on the sun's disc, so I took the second thing that came to mind. It is
also one of the longest continuous human records of anything natural, which I wanted to use.
