# The Wait at Old Faithful

**Material.** GeyserTimes (geysertimes.org, a volunteer eruption-logging project), eruption
archive for Old Faithful, `Old_Faithful_eruptions.tsv.gz` —
https://geysertimes.org/archive/geysers/Old_Faithful_eruptions.tsv.gz (listed at
https://geysertimes.org/archive/). Licence as found: "publicly available under the Open Source
Database License"; use in published research should attribute GeyserTimes. Fetched 2026-10-20.
Kept in this folder only as a derived file, `data/intervals.js`: 99,548 waits between two
consecutive, exactly timed eruptions (30–140 min), each with the duration of the eruption
before it where recorded.

Every wait is a grain of light placed by its date (left to right, 1970 to 2026) and by
its length (higher is longer), so the record piles up like steam into columns, one per
summer of observers. The colour is the eruption that came before: blue after a short
eruption, amber after a long one. In the 1970s and 1990s you can see the geyser's two-part
rhythm, with short eruptions followed by short waits and long ones by long waits. You can
also see the whole cloud rising, from about 65 and 80 minutes to about 90 minutes and more,
across the years without records around the 1983 Borah Peak earthquake. The dark gaps are
left as they are, as stretches when no one logged exact pairs.
