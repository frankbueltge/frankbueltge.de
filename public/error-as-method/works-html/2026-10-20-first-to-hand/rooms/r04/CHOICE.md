# First to mind

1. NOAA Mauna Loa monthly atmospheric CO2 record (the Keeling curve)
2. USGS earthquake catalogue (past 30 days, all magnitudes)
3. SILSO daily/monthly sunspot numbers (Royal Observatory of Belgium)

# Chosen

The monthly mean total sunspot number, version 2.0, from WDC-SILSO at the Royal Observatory of Belgium:
https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/DATA/SN_m_tot_V2.0.csv (listed at https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/datafiles).

How I came to it: it was third on my list, and it was the one whose shape I could not quite picture.
The CO2 curve is a single rising line everyone knows; the earthquake feed is a month of scatter. The
sunspot record is 277 years of one star counted by hand, month by month, since 1749, and it arrives
already folded into cycles of roughly eleven years. It is small (127 KB) and needs nothing joined to it.
