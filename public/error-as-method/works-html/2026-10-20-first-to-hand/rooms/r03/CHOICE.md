# First to mind

1. USGS earthquake catalogue (the past month of quakes worldwide, GeoJSON feed)
2. NOAA / Scripps Mauna Loa monthly atmospheric CO2 record (the Keeling curve)
3. Sunspot numbers from SILSO, Royal Observatory of Belgium (monthly, since 1749)

# Chosen

The third: the monthly mean total sunspot number, version 2.0, from WDC-SILSO at the Royal
Observatory of Belgium — 3,333 months from January 1749 to September 2026, one value each.
Found at https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/datafiles (file fetched from https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/INFO/snmtotcsv.php).

How I came to it: of the three, it was the one with the longest unbroken memory and the simplest
shape — one number per month for more than 270 years, kept by hand-counting before it was kept by
instruments. The earthquake feed is a month wide and the CO2 record mostly says one thing; the
sunspot series keeps doing something slightly different every time it repeats, which seemed worth
looking at. I went straight to SILSO, which I already knew to be the keeper of the series, and took
the CSV as published.
