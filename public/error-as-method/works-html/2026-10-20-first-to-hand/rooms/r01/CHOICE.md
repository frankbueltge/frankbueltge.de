# First to mind

1. The USGS earthquake catalogue (the real-time GeoJSON feeds of quakes worldwide).
2. The Mauna Loa monthly atmospheric CO2 record (NOAA GML, the Keeling curve).
3. The Global Volcanism Program's list of Holocene volcanoes (Smithsonian).

# Chosen

The Mauna Loa monthly mean CO2 record, `co2_mm_mlo.csv`, published by NOAA Global Monitoring
Laboratory: https://gml.noaa.gov/webdata/ccgg/trends/co2/co2_mm_mlo.csv
(described at https://gml.noaa.gov/ccgg/trends/).

How I came to it: it was the second thing on the list, and the one I already knew the address of,
so I went straight to it without a search. I took it because it is one continuous line kept by
hand and instrument for nearly seventy years at one place, with a seasonal breath in it, and with
a scar: the months in 2022–23 when lava cut the road and the readings came from Maunakea instead.
