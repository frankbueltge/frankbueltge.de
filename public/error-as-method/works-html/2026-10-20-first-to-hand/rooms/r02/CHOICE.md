# First to mind

1. The NOAA Mauna Loa monthly atmospheric CO2 record (Keeling curve).
2. The USGS earthquake catalogue feed (all earthquakes of the past month).
3. The SILSO international sunspot number series (monthly, since 1749).

# Chosen

The SILSO monthly mean total sunspot number (version 2.0), January 1749 to September 2026,
published by the World Data Center SILSO at the Royal Observatory of Belgium, Brussels.
URL: https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/DATA/SN_m_tot_V2.0.csv (listed at https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/datafiles).

How I came to it: it was the third of the three that came to mind, and the one I kept
returning to. The CO2 curve is so familiar it has almost stopped being looked at, and the
earthquake feed is a month of scatter. The sunspot record is the opposite: nearly three
centuries of people counting dark spots on the Sun, month after month, with a rhythm
(the ~11-year cycle) that never repeats exactly. I went straight to the SILSO data page,
which I knew, and took the monthly file.
