# First to mind

1. The Mauna Loa monthly CO2 record (NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory).
2. The USGS earthquake catalogue (the past month of quakes worldwide, as GeoJSON).
3. The daily sunspot number since 1818 (SILSO, Royal Observatory of Belgium).

# Chosen

The daily total sunspot number, version 2.0, from the World Data Center SILSO at the Royal Observatory of Belgium, Brussels — the third thing on my list.
URL: https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/INFO/sndtotcsv.php (described at https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/infosndtot)

How I came to it: of the three, it was the one with the longest unbroken human habit behind it — someone has looked at the sun and counted spots on almost every day since 1818, and the file records not only the counts but the days nobody looked (-1). The CO2 curve is too familiar a shape, and the earthquake feed is a month-long snapshot; the sunspot record is two centuries of a single daily gesture, small enough (2.9 MB) to keep whole. I went straight to SILSO's data page and took the CSV.
