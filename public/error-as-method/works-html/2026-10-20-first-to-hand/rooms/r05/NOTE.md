# Every Day the Sun Was Counted

**Material:** Daily total sunspot number (version 2.0), 1 January 1818 to 30 September 2026.
Publisher: WDC-SILSO, Royal Observatory of Belgium, Brussels.
URL: https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/INFO/sndtotcsv.php (file `data/SN_d_tot_V2.0.csv`; `data/sunspots.js` is the same column 5 values repacked as a script so the page reads them offline).
Licence as found: CC BY-NC 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/).
Fetched: 20 October 2026.

The page lays all 76,244 days out as a woven ledger: one row for each year from 1818 at the top to 2026 at the bottom, one column for each day of the year, so the image is read the way a calendar is kept. Each day that had spots is a cell of ember colour, from oxblood for a few spots to near-white for hundreds. A day when the sun was looked at and found clean is a thin slate-blue thread instead, and a day when nobody counted is simply left dark. The eleven-year solar cycle shows up without being drawn as a curve: bands of heat alternate with cooler bands shot through with blue, about every eleventh row. The early decades are full of holes from days nobody observed, and the record only closes up around 1849. A small gauge in the right margin gives each year's share of spotless days, and one circle marks the most spots ever counted on a single day: 528, on 26 August 1870.
