# First to mind

1. The NOAA Mauna Loa monthly CO2 record (the Keeling curve).
2. The USGS earthquake catalog (past-month GeoJSON feed).
3. The Meteoritical Society / NASA meteorite landings table.

# Chosen

Full-bloom dates of the cherry trees (*Prunus jamasakura*) in Kyoto, 812–2026, compiled by
Yasuyuki Aono (from court diaries, chronicles and poems, later from observation) and extended by
Genki Katata; downloaded as CSV from Our World in Data:
https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/date-of-the-peak-cherry-tree-blossom-in-kyoto.csv
(original source page: https://cigs.canon/katata_date/260420sakura_katata.html).

How I came to it: all three that came to mind were the usual climate/earth-science feeds, and the
Keeling curve in particular is drawn so often it has become a logo. I wanted a record that was kept
by people looking at something, not by an instrument, and the Kyoto blossom series came next: twelve
centuries of someone noting the day the trees were at their fullest, with gaps wherever nobody wrote
it down. It was one fetch away and small (28 KB). Its newest entry, spring 2026, is one of the
earliest in the whole record.
