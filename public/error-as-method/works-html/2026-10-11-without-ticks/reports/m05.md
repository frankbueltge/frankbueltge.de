I made "One Year, Fifteen Times". The work is in `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m05/work/index.html` and `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m05/work/NOTE.md`. Scripts, the raw pixel dump and screenshots are in `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m05/scratch/`.

**What I did first.** I looked at `births.jpg`. It is 480×220 pixels and shows two bands of dots, with no axes or numbers. I converted it to raw greyscale with ImageMagick and measured where the dark pixels fall. The dots sit in columns 5–474 (470 columns) and rows about 29–193, with a clear gap between the two bands around rows 117–128. That works out to about 11.7 days per pixel column. A column has about 19 dark pixels for its roughly 12 days, so neighbouring dots run together. Individual days can't be recovered from this picture; what it does hold is how much ink sits at each place.

**How I got to the form.**
- I kept the cropped pixel darkness (470×174, after removing faint JPEG noise) and embedded it in the page as base64. The page draws everything on a canvas and loads nothing else.
- I gave each pixel column a date range, assuming the dots run evenly from 1 Jan 2000 at the first column to 31 Dec 2014 at the last.
- I cut the strip at each New Year and laid the 15 years on top of each other across a single January–December width. A column that crosses a New Year is split between the two years. Each year has its own colour, running from teal (2000) through violet (2007) to vermilion (2014), and the layers blend like overlapping inks.
- The overlay shows the late-summer rise and the stray holiday dots near the edges of the year. In the colours, the middle years sit highest. Underneath, the original strip runs end to end with dashed lines where it was cut, so the overlay can be checked against the source.
- I rendered it with headless Chromium at 1100×800. There were no errors and no network requests.

**What the commissioner should know.**
- Every mark is one source pixel stretched to about 11.7 days wide. The coarse, smeared texture is deliberate: it is as precise as the picture allows, and I didn't invent day-level detail.
- No numbers appear anywhere, because the picture contains none. Up means more births only in relative terms, and I assumed the vertical scale is linear.
- Three things are my reading and not facts given in the material:
  - **Band labels:** the "weekdays" and "weekends" labels.
  - **Date mapping:** placing the first and last days at the edges of the dot cloud, evenly spaced in between.
  - **Month marks:** the month labels follow a common 365-day year; leap years are fitted to the same width.
