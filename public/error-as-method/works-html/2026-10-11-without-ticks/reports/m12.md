I've made "Born on a Weekday", a single still image drawn on a canvas. It's in /tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m12/work/index.html, with the note in /tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m12/work/NOTE.md. I rendered it at 1100 × 800 in headless Chromium and looked at the screenshot: there are no script errors, labels don't overlap and nothing is clipped. The page loads no outside files, libraries or fonts.

**What I did first with the file.** I checked it. It has 5,479 rows and no gaps: every row's date and day of the week matches the real calendar from 1 Jan 2000 to 31 Dec 2014. Then I looked at:
- **Day of the week:** about 12,600–13,100 births on a weekday, about 8,560 on Saturday and 7,520 on Sunday.
- **Year totals:** highest in 2007, falling after that.
- **Each date compared with the same weekday in the same month and year:** this was the most useful view.

**The finding that set the form.** The days don't form one cloud. They split into two bands, weekdays and weekends, with a gap between them that ordinary variation almost never crosses. That points to scheduled deliveries. Once the bands are visible, the dates that leave their band speak for themselves:
- **Holidays drop weekday births to weekend levels.** Christmas Day 2011 had 5,728, the fewest of any day; New Year's Day, the Fourth of July and every Thanksgiving fall the same way.
- **Repeating dates rise above everything.** 9.9.09 had 16,081, the most of any day. 8.8.08, 11.11.11 and 12.12.12 are also high, and 7.7.07 and 10.10.10 were on a weekend but still climbed well above the weekend band.
- **6.6.06 went the other way.** It had 11,661, the fewest of any weekday that June.

**From that to the form.** Each day is one dot, placed across the page by its date in the year and up the page by its birth count, with all 15 years laid on top of each other. There are only month names, faint count lines and two band labels; no tick marks. Ten dates that leave their band are circled in red, each with its actual count. The data is built into the page as one list of daily counts.

**Things you should know:**
- **Leap days are kept.** The x-axis uses a 366-day calendar, so 29 February has its own column and the dates after it line up across years.
- **No colour separates weekdays from weekends.** I left that out on purpose, because the two bands appear from the counts alone.
- **These are births registered with Social Security by date, not a census of births.** I kept the wording to "births registered" and didn't use any information from outside the folder.
- **Some patterns I found didn't fit on the image.** Friday the 13th runs about 5% below a normal Friday. Births also surge in the days just before Christmas and just after it.
