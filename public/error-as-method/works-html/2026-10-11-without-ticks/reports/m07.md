I made "One Year, Fifteen Times", a single still image in `index.html` (84 KB). It loads nothing external; the data is embedded. `NOTE.md` holds the title and the paragraph. I couldn't open the page in a browser, so the text and labels are untested on screen.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at it. It is a 1,100×500 picture with two bands of dots: weekdays around 12,000–14,000 and weekends around 7,000–9,000. The individual dates are gone; only the dot positions remain. There was no image library, so I wrote a small PNG decoder and calibrated the axes from the pixels themselves:
- **Time:** 1,000 pixels for 5,479 days, about 5.5 days per pixel column.
- **Births:** 430 pixels for 12,000 births, about 28 births per pixel row.

**How I got from it to the form.**
- **Ink, not days.** Dots overlap too much to recover single days. Instead I measured the "ink" (darkness) in each pixel, removing the gridlines and the axis line.
- **Checking the reading.** The total ink equals about 5,130 dots against 5,479 days, so overlaps hide about one day in twenty.
- **Three layers.** The weekend band splits into two layers of equal weight, about 1,100 births apart, all the way through. I label these Saturday (upper) and Sunday (lower), marked "inferred" in the work.
- **Holidays.** Listing the outliers by date showed repeating patterns. Every Christmas is below about 6,700, under an ordinary Sunday. Weekday dips fall to weekend level in early July, around 31 October, in mid-January and mid-February, and in spring. Early September is the peak, with the single highest day near 16,100 in early September 2009.
- **The form.** I folded the 15 years onto one January-to-December span, so things that repeat line up in place. Each source pixel column becomes a short streak 5.5 days wide, coloured by year (ember for 2000, straw for 2007, blue for 2014), and brighter where years overlap. It's a dark field with a few quiet labels: the three layers, value marks, months, five notes and a colour key.
- **Checking it.** Because there was no browser, the page's drawing code also runs in Node to produce a preview PNG. I checked label positions against that preview with a stub of the page.

**What you should know.**
- The values are read from a picture, not taken from the data. They are accurate to about ±28 births and about ±3 days, and the streaks show that coarseness on purpose.
- The colours suggest births in the 2010s sat lower than in 2006–08, but it's a mixed signal, not a measurement.
- I didn't name the date of the 2009 peak because the picture only narrows it to about 3–14 September. The holidays I named come from where the dips repeat; I didn't look them up.
- Fonts are system fonts (Georgia and Helvetica/Arial), so spacing may shift slightly on different machines.
