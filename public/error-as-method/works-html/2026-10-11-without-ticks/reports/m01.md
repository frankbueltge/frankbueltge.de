I made **"The Year Turns Fifteen Times"**. It folds the 15 years onto one circular year, and the weekly cycle and the holidays come out as rings and a few marked dips.

**What I did first with the file.** I opened it and then measured it. It is 480 × 220 pixels, and the dots run from column 5 to column 474. That puts 5,479 days into 470 columns, so each pixel column holds about 11.7 days. The picture never had individual days: what I got is a blur of about twelve days per column, with no axes, so no count can be recovered. Looking at the picture showed one upper band and a lower band that splits in two. The upper band is the working week, and the lower one is the weekend, with Saturday and Sunday on separate lines. Underneath both is a yearly swell.

**How I got from it to the form.**
- I turned the JPEG into raw greyscale with ImageMagick. Every pixel darker than the background (about 22,000 of them) went into a compact string inside the page, with its x, y and how dark it was.
- I mapped each column to the run of dates it covers, from 1 Jan 2000 to 31 Dec 2014.
- I scanned the space between the two bands for stray dots. They gather at the turn of the year, early July, early September and late November. So holidays drop from the working-week level toward the weekend level.
- The form puts the year on a clock face: angle is the day of the year, starting with January at the top, and further out means more births.
- Each source pixel is drawn as an arc exactly as long as the dates it covers, so the blur stays visible. Colour shows the year.
- All the arcs are added into one density map with a tone curve, so a single stray day still shows where the rings are dense. The page draws everything on a canvas when it loads.
- What the picture shows: an outer ring for weekdays and an inner ring for weekends. The outer ring swells in late summer, and the 2007 years sit at its outer edge. Hairlines mark 1 January, 4 July, Labor Day, Thanksgiving and 25 December.
- On the right are the title, a short explanation, and the source strip redrawn as a key in the same year colours.
- I checked it in headless Chromium at 1100 × 800. The page makes no requests beyond itself.

**What the person who commissioned this should know.**
- **The holiday marks are my labels.** I placed them because the stray dots gather near those dates. They are ordinary calendar knowledge, not something written in the file, and the source can only place a dot to within about ±6 days.
- **The holiday dips are faint.** Twelve-day smearing softens them, so they read as dips into the gap rather than sharp spokes.
- **Holiday text is hedged.** The page says holiday counts fall "toward the weekend", not that they equal weekend levels.
- **The distance from the centre is arbitrary.** The empty middle is not zero births: with no axis there is nothing to anchor it, and the caption says so.
- **The ring thickness mixes two things.** Real day-to-day spread is blended with the year-to-year drift, because all 15 years sit on top of each other.
- **Fonts may vary.** The page uses system fonts and loads no others, so lettering will differ slightly between machines.
