I made "The Week Remains": a single still image on a canvas, in one self-contained file of about 9 KB with no outside resources.
Files: `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m03/work/index.html` and `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m03/work/NOTE.md`. Working scripts and intermediate files are in `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m03/scratch`.

**What I did first with the file**
- I looked at it. It is a 1100×500 scatter plot with two horizontal bands: weekdays at about 11–15k births a day and weekends at about 6.5–9.5k. There is an almost empty strip between them, holding a few dots.
- I then measured the picture in pixels. I located the gridlines (1,000 births ≈ 35.8 px) and the year ticks (66.67 px per year), which let me convert any pixel position to a date and a birth count.

**From picture to form**
- **Recovering the dots.** Each dot is a solid circle about 2.5 px across. I measured how dark a single dot is, found the clusters of touching dots, and estimated how many dots each cluster holds. I then placed that many dots inside each cluster.
- **Hidden days.** The visible ink only accounts for about 4,900 dots. That means roughly 10% of the days are completely hidden behind other dots. I estimated the hidden ones with a correction that assumes randomly scattered dots: the more crowded an area, the more dots are missing there. With this correction the total came to 5,660 before I scaled it to the known 5,479.
- **Checks.** Redrawing the recovered dots reproduces the original closely, judged by eye. After the correction, 3,780 days sit above the gap and 1,699 below. The true number of weekdays in the period is 3,913, and the difference fits with holidays falling into the lower band.
- **The form.** I removed time. Each day keeps its height, slides left along its own row until it meets the others, and is drawn as one small mark coloured by year. The rows are the original's pixel rows, about 28 births each. What is left is two sideways piles, five to two, with a near-empty band between them.
- **What the colours show.** Because the days stay in date order within each row, the colours form layers. The 2006–08 boom years sit at the top of the weekday pile and 2010–14 at its lower edge.
- **The gap.** The days in the gap are labelled from the dates I recovered. They fall around Christmas and New Year, in mid-January and mid-February, around 4 July and at the end of October.

**What the commissioner should know**
- **This is a reconstruction from a picture, not the data.** Each day's count is only accurate to about ±28 births, and its date to about ±3 days.
- **The length of each row is partly estimated.** About 10% of the days are invisible in the source. I added them back by estimate, more where the plot is denser.
- **Holidays are an interpretation.** I matched the gap days to holidays by their approximate dates, using general calendar knowledge; I did not look anything up.
- **"Monday to Friday" and "Saturday and Sunday" are also an interpretation.** The labels rest on the 5:2 split, not on day-of-week information; the picture does not contain any.
- **How I checked it.** The canvas is exactly 1100×800. I checked it in headless Chromium, but in a slightly taller window, so it was not viewed at exactly 1100×800.
