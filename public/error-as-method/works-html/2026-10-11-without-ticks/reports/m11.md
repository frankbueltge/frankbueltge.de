I made "Not on a Sunday", a single still image. It's in `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m11/work/index.html`, with the title and a short paragraph in `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m11/work/NOTE.md`. The page loads nothing from outside: the only "http://" in it is the SVG namespace string. All 5,479 daily counts are embedded in the page as one compact string, and plain JavaScript draws the SVG from them when the page opens. No browser was available here, so I checked it by rendering it through librsvg into a 1100×800 image; it has not been looked at in an actual browser.

**What I did first with the file:** I checked it before drawing anything.
- It has 5,479 rows and no dates are missing.
- The day-of-week column matches the real calendar on every row.
- Daily counts range from 5,728 (Christmas Day 2011, a Sunday) to 16,081 (9 September 2009).
- On average, weekdays have 12,000–13,000 births, Saturdays about 8,600 and Sundays about 7,500.

That weekly gap is larger than any seasonal change, which told me the data is mostly about scheduled births. So I estimated each day's expected count from its year-month average and its weekday (a per-year median factor), then looked at how far each day fell from that. What stood out:
- **Holidays:** Christmas −41%, Christmas Eve, New Year's Day and the Fourth of July about −25%, Halloween −13%, and dips around Thanksgiving and Labor Day.
- **Year end:** births rise by up to about 10% in the last working days of December.
- **The 13th:** births fall about 2.4% on the 13th of every month.
- **Repeating dates:** 9.9.09, 10.10.10 (a Sunday, still about +19%), 11.11.11 and 12.12.12 run 14–22% above an ordinary day of their weekday, while 6.6.06 was avoided (−17%).

**How I got to the form:**
- **Top:** a 15-row calendar with one 2.6px column per day, lined up by date across years and shaded by the number of births. Weekends show as pale stripes that move a day later each year. Fixed holidays line up as pale columns through all fifteen years. The five repeating dates are ringed in red and labelled.
- **Bottom:** the same 366 days with the weekday and month effects removed and averaged over the 15 years. Days above expectation are drawn as ink bars and days below as red bars, with a few labels.

The point of the work is that the calendar shapes the births. I stopped at that level of annotation so the image wouldn't read like a report.

**Things you should know:**
- **The figures depend on my method.** The "expected" baseline is my own simple model (monthly mean × the weekday's median factor), so every percentage on the page depends on that choice.
- **29 February** is averaged over only three years. I did not label it.
- **Fonts:** the page asks for Georgia and Helvetica/Arial. On machines without them the text will look slightly different, and the labels are tuned closely enough that a very different fallback font could make some of them touch.
- **Working files** (exploration script, template, build script, renderer, preview PNG) are in `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m11/scratch`.
