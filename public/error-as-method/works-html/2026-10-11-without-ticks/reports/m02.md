I made "Appointed Days", a still image in one self-contained SVG page: /tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m02/work/index.html, about 380 KB, sized 1100 × 800. It loads no scripts and no outside files. The note is at /tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m02/work/NOTE.md. I couldn't check the finished page in a browser, because none of the usual tools for rendering a page to an image were available.

**What I did first with the file:** I checked it before using it. It has all 5,479 rows, the weekday column matches the real calendar on every row, and yearly totals range from about 4.0 to 4.4 million. Then I looked for structure:
- **The week dominates.** An average Tuesday has about 13,100 births and an average Sunday about 7,500.
- **Holidays are deep dips.** On Christmas Day births fall to about half of what would otherwise be expected.
- **Repeating dates draw births.** The days from 2/2/02 to 12/12/12 run above expectation, reaching +20% on 11/11/11. 9/9/09 (16,081 births) is the busiest day in the whole file.
- **6/6/06 is the exception.** It came in 16% below expectation.
- **Smaller effects:** Fridays the 13th run about 6% low, Halloween about 11% low, and 14 February about 4% high.

**From the file to the form:**
- **Expected value:** for each day I divided births by the median for that weekday in that year. I then divided by a rolling 31-day median to take out the seasons. What remains is the day's own deviation.
- **Layout:** each day is one cell, with a row for each year and a column for each date.
- **Color:** a neutral paper tone near "as expected", fading to dark ink for days below and to vermilion for days above. Small differences of ±3% stay neutral so random variation doesn't read as a pattern.
- **What it shows:** Christmas, New Year and 4 July appear as dark vertical lines. Thanksgiving, Labor Day and Memorial Day appear as dark marks that shift from year to year as their dates move. The busy days before New Year form a red band. The repeating dates make a diagonal of outlined cells, with 6/6/06 marked differently.
- **Text:** there are a few labels, a color key and a short explanation in the page. I generated all of it with Python scripts in the scratch folder.

**What the commissioner should know:**
- **The checks were limited.** I drew only the colored grid to an image to check it. I placed the text labels by calculating positions, so some may sit slightly differently, or overlap, depending on the serif font the viewer's browser uses.
- **The colors show a modeled comparison, not raw counts.** They depend on my choice of expected value (yearly weekday medians and a 31-day rolling median). The note and the page both say this.
- **1/1/01 is an outlier.** It is low because it is New Year's Day, so it is labelled as such rather than shown as an exception to the repeating-date pattern.
- **Blank cells are expected.** The 29 February column is empty in non-leap years.
