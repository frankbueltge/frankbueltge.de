I've made "The Moat", a radial calendar of the births, as a single file at /tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m09/work/index.html with the note in /tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m09/work/NOTE.md. All the values were measured from births.png, so they're approximate. I could separate 4,995 of the 5,479 days, and each date is only good to about three days.

**First step.** I looked at the image. It's an 1,100 × 500 scatter plot with two bands: weekdays at about 11,000–15,000 births and weekends at about 6,500–9,500. There's a nearly empty strip between them. No imaging library was installed, so I wrote a small PNG decoder in plain Python. I then found the gridlines and the axis to set the scales: the y gridlines are about 71.7 px per 2,000 births, and the x-axis runs 1,000 px for 5,479 days, so each pixel column covers about 5.5 days.

**From picture to numbers.** I grouped the dark pixels into blobs. One isolated dot carries a fairly constant amount of ink, so I divided each blob's ink by that to estimate how many dots it held. Blobs with more than one dot were split by weighted clustering. That gave 4,995 points. The other ~480 days are hidden under other dots in the source, mostly in the dense weekday band. Each point was then turned back into a date and a birth count.

**The form.** All 15 years are wound round one calendar circle, with January at the top and the year running clockwise. Distance from the centre is the birth count, measured from zero. The two bands become two rings and the empty strip becomes a moat, shaded faintly between 9,850 and 10,750. Folding the years together brings out what a straight timeline hides: the few weekdays that sink into the moat come back at the same calendar points every year. Most are at Christmas and New Year, and a few are scattered through Feb–Jul. These are marked in red. The rings also bulge towards September and are narrowest in winter. The page draws everything with canvas and plain JavaScript, with the data embedded, and loads nothing else. I checked it in headless Chromium at 1,100 × 800: no errors and no outside requests.

**Things you should know:**
- **Dates are approximate.** A date taken from the picture can't pin the day of the week, so weekday versus weekend is read only from which band a dot sits in. Some streaks along the spokes come from the source image's pixel columns, not from the data.
- **The moat's edges are a threshold I chose.** The real gap shifts with the season: summer Saturdays climb to about 9,800, and in the later years some weekday counts at year's end drop to about 10,800. That's why a few dots near the edges in July and September are left black.
- **On the page I name only Christmas week and New Year**, because that's where the red dots clearly cluster. I didn't name holidays for the scattered ones.
