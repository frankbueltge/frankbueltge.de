I made "The Week Inside the Year", a single still image. It winds all fifteen years of the data onto one turn of the calendar. The page is `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m06/work/index.html` (about 68 KB, with the data inside it and nothing loaded from outside). The title and paragraph are in `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m06/work/NOTE.md`. Working files are in `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m06/scratch`. I rendered the page at 1100 × 800 in headless Chromium: no errors and no network requests.

**What I did first with the file:** I looked at it, then turned it into raw greyscale pixels with ImageMagick (Python's image library wasn't installed). It is 480 × 220 pixels. I counted how much ink falls in each row and column, and enlarged a crop to look at the dots up close. The picture splits into a dense upper band, a sparse gap, and a thinner lower band that is visibly double. The upper band holds about 66% of the ink, close to 5 days in 7, which supports reading it as weekdays and the lower band as the weekend.

**How I got to the form:**
- **Dates across the picture:** the dots run from about x = 6.5 to x = 472.5, so each pixel column holds about 11.8 days (about 31 columns per year).
- **Checking that:** under this mapping the weekday band sits highest in August and September and lowest in winter. The very lowest pixels fall around 17–31 December and early January.
- **Data kept:** I kept every pixel darker than a threshold (15,742 of them), with its position and darkness, and packed them into the page.
- **The drawing:** the angle is the time of year, clockwise from January at the top. The distance from the centre is how high the pixel sat in the picture: further out means more births. Each pixel is drawn as a faint arc as long as the days its column covered, so the work is never finer than its source.

I tried colouring the arcs by year (blue for 2000 to red for 2014). The overlap turned it into muddy purple, so I dropped it for a single ink colour. That means the change across the years is not shown.

**Things you should know:**
- **No numbers:** the picture had no axes, so the work shows no counts, and the empty centre is not zero. The page says so.
- **Assumptions:** I assumed "higher in the picture means more births", and inferred which band is weekdays and which is the weekend from the shapes and the 5:2 ink share. Nothing in the file states either. The text deliberately does not say which weekend strand is Saturday and which is Sunday.
- **Dates are approximate:** any date is only accurate to about ±6 days, plus perhaps one pixel of error in where the plot starts and ends. So "the lowest marks gather at the turn of the year" is a claim about a cluster, not about Christmas Day.
- **Coarse source:** the picture is small and compressed, so the streaky arc texture is honest about how little detail the source holds. It is not a style choice laid over finer data.
