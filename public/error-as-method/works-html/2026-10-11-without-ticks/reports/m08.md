I made a single still image called "Two Floors". The page is `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m08/work/index.html` and the note is `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m08/work/NOTE.md`.

**What I did first:** I checked that the file is complete before drawing anything. It has 5,479 rows with no gaps and no duplicate dates, and every day-of-week value matches its calendar date. Then I looked at averages:
- **Day of the week:** Tuesday to Thursday average about 12,900–13,100 births. Saturday averages about 8,560 and Sunday about 7,520.
- **Years:** the totals peak in 2007 and decline after it.
- **Unusually low or high dates:** I compared each date with the usual level for its weekday in that year. The low ones are all holidays: Christmas, New Year, 4 July, Thanksgiving, Memorial Day and Labor Day. Mid-September is the busiest stretch.
- **Odd dates:** 9.9.09 is the busiest day in the whole file (16,081). 8.8.08 and 7.7.07 are high; 7.7.07 is the busiest Saturday in fifteen years. 6.6.06, a Tuesday, is well below other Tuesdays.

**How I got to the form:** The strongest structure in the data is the working week, so I laid every day on one shared 366-day calendar. Each column is a date and holds fifteen dots, one per year; height is the number of births. Weekdays are dark and weekends are orange. That colouring alone splits the cloud into two floors. Weekday holidays then appear as dark dots sitting on the weekend floor. Christmas falls below both floors: the eight quietest days in the file are all Christmas Days. On the left, the floors are labelled with the average for each weekday. A few short notes mark the holidays, the odd dates and a late-December surge (27–30 Dec). The data is embedded in the page and drawn with plain JavaScript into SVG. The page loads no other files, libraries or network resources.

**What the person who commissioned this should know:**
- **Wording kept to what the data shows:** The captions only describe what is in the data. An early draft said the gap between the floors reflects scheduled births. I removed that because the dataset doesn't show why the gap exists. The closing line, "Births keep office hours", is the only figurative phrase.
- **No browser preview:** None was available here. To check the layout, I ran the page's own script in Node and turned the result into a PNG with a small renderer I installed into the scratch folder (it uses a stand-in font). In a real browser at 1100 × 800 the page uses Georgia, so spacing may differ slightly.
- **Raw counts, not adjusted:** Heights are raw counts, not adjusted for the yearly trend. The 2007 peak therefore makes each floor somewhat thicker.
- **Findings I left out:**
  - Friday the 13th averages about 5% fewer births than other Fridays.
  - Some of the quietest Sundays fall on daylight-saving change days and on Easter. I didn't label these, to keep the image readable.
