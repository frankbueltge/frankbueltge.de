I made **Five and Two**, a single still image in `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m04/work/index.html`, with `NOTE.md` beside it. Each day is one dot, piled over its birth count, with one shelf per year for 2000–2014. Every year splits into two heaps, about 115 days near 7,000–9,000 births and about 250 days near 12,000–14,000, with an almost empty "valley" between them.

**First step with the file.** I looked at `births.png` (1100×500): a scatter plot with two horizontal bands of dots. No image libraries were installed, so I wrote a small PNG decoder in plain Python. With it I read the scale from the picture itself: gridlines every 2,000 births and year ticks every 66.67 px, so 5,479 days span 1,000 px.

**From picture to data.**
- **Dot shape:** from isolated dots, each one is an antialiased disc of radius about 1.3 px.
- **Fitting:** I placed discs one at a time wherever they best explained the remaining ink, at quarter-pixel positions, with overlapping dots darkening the way stacked ink does. Every isolated dot got exactly one disc.
- **Stopping rule:** where the bands are dense, dots hide under other dots, so the count depends on when the fitting stops. I chose the stop so the total came out near the true number. That gave 5,501 dots for 5,479 days. Per-year counts are within ±9 of 365/366 except 2009, which has 22 too many.
- **Conversion:** each dot became a day number and a birth count. The counts are good to about ±15 births. Dates are only good to about ±2 days.

**Choosing the form.** Values are precise and dates are coarse, so the form uses value and year and leaves out the order of days within a year. Piling the days turns the gap between the bands into the subject. The large heap visibly drifts upward to about 2007 and back down afterwards. A ledger on the right gives each year's low and high day counts, and the 47 days in the valley (9,700–10,900 births) or below 6,400 are marked in red. The page is canvas plus plain JavaScript with the reconstructed data embedded (about 69 KB). It loads no other file or network resource and is laid out for 1100×800.

**What the person who commissioned this should know:**
- **The data is a reconstruction from a picture, not the original numbers.** Any single year can be off by a few days, and the footer of the image says so.
- **About 10 days at the start of January 2000 are missing.** Those dots sit under the plot's left axis line, so the 2000 shelf is slightly short.
- **The link to the week is inferred, not shown.** The roughly 5:2 split in every year fits weekdays against weekends (and maybe holidays), but the picture cannot tell me which dot is which weekday. The note and the page present this as a reading of the shape, and I did not impose it on the data. I deliberately did not assign exact dates by forcing a weekday pattern, because that would have made the pattern circular.
- **I checked the layout with my own rough raster preview, not a real browser.** No browser was available here, and the preview drew text as boxes. Text widths are estimated, and the longest footer line comes close to the right edge, so look at it once at 1100×800.
