The work is "Fifteen Turns of the Year". It's in `/tmp/claude-0/s121/mk/m10/work/index.html`, with `NOTE.md` beside it. The page is self-contained, with all the data inline and nothing loaded from outside. I rendered it in a headless browser at 1100×800 to check it.

**What I did first with the file.** I looked at the image (480×220 px) and turned it into raw grayscale with ImageMagick. Then I counted where the ink falls by row and by column. The dots span columns 5 to 474, so one pixel column holds about 11.7 days. The rows show three groups:
- a broad upper band of working days;
- two thin lower lines, the weekends;
- a nearly empty band between them, with a few stray dots in it and below the weekends.

I gave the stray dots dates from their columns. The deepest lows came out at Christmas and New Year in almost every year. The others fell in early July, early September and late November, and the highest points in September and just before Christmas. That confirmed the horizontal calibration. It also told me what the picture can honestly support.

**How I got to the form.** I kept every inked pixel of the source as a point: 15,743 pixels, with their darkness, stored inside the page. Each point is wrapped onto one circle:
- **Angle** is the day of the year, clockwise from 1 January at the top. Leap years are handled.
- **Radius** is the pixel's height in the source, so farther out means more births.
- **Placement in time** is random within the ~12 days its column covers. A fixed random seed means the page looks the same every time it loads. The scatter is the real uncertainty about the date, not decoration.

Laying the fifteen years on top of each other turns the weekday/weekend split into two rings with an empty band between them. Points in that band (source rows 114–131) or below the weekend floor (row 189 and lower) are drawn in red. They gather at the holidays, which I labelled, and the rings bulge toward September.

I first coloured the points by year to show the rise and fall across 2000–2014. It came out muddy and unreadable, so I dropped it for one ink plus red.

**Things the commissioner should know:**
- **No numbers.** The source has no axes, so the work has no scale and its centre is not zero. Only relative heights are real, and the page says this.
- **Dates are approximate.** Each point's date is only known to about ±6 days.
- **The red marks come from fixed row cut-offs.** They are not picked per day, so a few borderline days may be wrong. One example is the high weekend days in September of the peak years.
- **The holiday names are my reading of where the clusters fall.** The data doesn't name them. The same goes for the claim that births are partly scheduled.
- **Two smaller clusters are not labelled:** one around the end of October and one in late winter or early spring.
- **The weekend ring merges.** In the source, Saturday and Sunday are two separate lines. Overlaying fifteen years blurs them into one ring, so the page just calls it "weekends".
- **The rise and fall over the years is not shown.** Folding everything onto one year hides the climb to a peak around 2007 and the decline after it. That trend is clearly visible in the source.
