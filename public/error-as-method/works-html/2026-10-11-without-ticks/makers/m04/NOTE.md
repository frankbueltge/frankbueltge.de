# Five and Two

Each of the 5,479 days becomes one dot, and the dots are piled over the number of births registered that day. Fifteen shelves are stacked one above the other, one for each year from 2000 to 2014. The order of days inside a year is left out on purpose. What remains is the shape of the year: every shelf splits into a small heap near 7,000–9,000 births and a large heap near 12,000–14,000, with an almost empty valley between them. In every year the heaps hold roughly 115 and 250 days, close to two days in seven against five. That ratio points to the week, and probably to how births get scheduled, more than to biology. Going down the shelves, the large heap drifts right towards 2007 and back left afterwards. The few red days sit in the valley or far below it, where something broke the usual week. I chose piles over a timeline because the source picture already is a timeline, and its most striking feature is the gap, which a timeline can only show sideways. Piling the days makes the gap the subject, and it keeps every day countable as one mark.
