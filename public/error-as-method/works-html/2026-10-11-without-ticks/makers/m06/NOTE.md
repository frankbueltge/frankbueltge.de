# The Week Inside the Year

Fifteen years of daily US birth registrations are wound onto one turn of the calendar. The angle is the time of year and the distance from the centre is the number of births, so every year lies on top of the others. Winding the data into a circle makes the annual rhythm into a shape: the ring leans out towards late summer, and the lowest marks gather at the turn of the year. It also shows that what splits the ring is not the calendar but the week: a thick band of weekdays, a thin double strand of weekends, and an open gap between them that never closes. I had a small picture of the data to work from, not the numbers, so every mark is one pixel of that picture laid back onto the calendar, drawn as an arc as long as the eleven or twelve days that pixel stood for. The resolution of the source stays visible, and there are no counts because the picture never had any.
