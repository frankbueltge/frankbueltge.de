# Appointed Days

All 5,479 days sit in one calendar field, a row for each year and a column for each date, and every cell is colored by how many births that day had compared with what its weekday and season would predict. Taking out the weekly beat (about 13,100 births on a Tuesday, 7,500 on a Sunday) and the slow seasonal swell leaves only what the calendar itself did. Holidays show as dark lines running down through every year, with Thanksgiving and Labor Day shifting as their dates move. The days just before New Year fill up. And a diagonal of red cells runs from 2/2/02 to 12/12/12, with one gap at 6/6/06. I chose a plain calendar grid because the finding is about dates: when births can be scheduled, people pick some days and avoid others, and a grid lets you read that choice at the date where it happened.
