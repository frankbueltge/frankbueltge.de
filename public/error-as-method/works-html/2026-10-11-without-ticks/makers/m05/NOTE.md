# One Year, Fifteen Times

The picture of 5,479 days is cut at each New Year and its fifteen pieces are laid over one another on a single January-to-December width, each year in its own colour from deep teal (2000) through violet (2007) to vermilion (2014), blended like overlapping inks. Folding the strip this way lets the repeating shape stand on top of itself: the two bands (busy weekdays above, quiet weekends below), the late-summer swell, the stray holiday dots at the year's edges, and, in the colour stratification, the middle years riding highest. I worked only from the pixels, not from numbers I do not have, so each mark is one pixel of the source, about eleven and a half days wide, and the image keeps that coarseness rather than inventing day-level detail; the original strip runs underneath with its cut lines so the fold can be checked against what it came from.
