# The Week Remains

The work takes the time axis out of the plot. Every one of the 5,479 days stays at its own height, its count of births, but slides left along that row until it meets the days already there. The rows are the plot's own pixel rows, about 28 births each, so what you see is a sideways pile of days. Within each row the days keep their order and are coloured by year. What survives without the calendar is the week: two separate masses, five days to two, with a nearly empty band between them. The only days in that band are holiday weekdays that came out like weekends. Because the colours stay in order, the pile also shows the change across the years. The boom years 2006–2008 sit at the top of the weekday mass, and 2010–2014 slump to its lower edge. I chose this form because the most striking thing in the original picture is that horizontal gap. Removing time showed that the gap was never about time: the week puts it there.
