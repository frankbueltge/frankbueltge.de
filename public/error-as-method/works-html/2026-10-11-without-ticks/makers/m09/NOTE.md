# The Moat

The work winds all fifteen years of daily US births (2000–2014) round a single calendar circle, with January at the top, and sets each day's distance from the centre by its count, measured from zero. The source plot already showed two bands, one for weekdays and one for weekends, with an empty strip between them. Folding time into a circle turns those bands into two concentric rings and the strip into a moat. Days in the same season line up across the years, so the few weekdays that sink into the moat can be read by date. Most of them fall at the turn of the year. I chose the fold because a long timeline hides that the outliers come back every year, and the ring shows it without any extra marking. The values were measured from the picture itself (4,995 of 5,479 dots could be told apart), and the page says so.
