The Moat

Every day of fifteen years of American births, 2000–2014, wound once round the calendar. January is at the top; the year runs clockwise. Distance from the centre is the number of births that day, measured from zero.

The outer ring is the working week, the inner ring the weekend. Between them lies a moat about a thousand births wide that ordinary days almost never enter.

The few that do, marked in red, are weekdays on which births fell nearly to a Saturday's level. Most of them gather at the top, where the year turns: Christmas week and New Year.

Points were recovered from a dot plot image, one dot per day; 4,995 of 5,479 days could be separated, the rest lay under one another. Dates are good to about three days.
