# Fifteen Turns of the Year

The work wraps the fifteen years of daily births around one circle and lays them on top of each other. Angle is the day of the year, clockwise from 1 January at the top; distance from the centre is the count, read off the source picture with no scale and no zero. Folding the years together makes the calendar the subject. The broad outer ring of working days and the inner ring of weekends come apart, with an empty band between them. The few days that land in that band or below the weekends are drawn in red, and they gather at Christmas and New Year, early July, early September and late November. That is where you can see that births are partly scheduled. I chose the circle because the straight timeline in the source hides this: there the holidays are scattered specks fifteen years apart, and here they fall on the same spot.
