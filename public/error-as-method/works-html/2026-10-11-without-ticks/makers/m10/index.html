Fifteen Turns
of the Year

The number of births registered in the United States on each day from 1 January 2000 to 31 December 2014: fifteen years laid on top of one another, each running clockwise from the top.

Farther from the centre means more births, though the centre is not zero. The broad outer ring is the working week; the inner ring is Saturdays and Sundays. Between them lies an empty band that ordinary days do not cross.

The days that do fall into it, or below the weekends, are marked in red. They gather at Christmas and New Year, early July, early September and late November: holidays. Many births are scheduled, and the calendar shows through.

The swell toward September comes back every year.

Read back from a 480×220 pixel picture with no axes and no numbers, so there is no scale here either. One pixel of that picture held about twelve days; each point is placed somewhere inside its twelve, never more exactly.
