# One Year, Fifteen Times

The work reads the ink back out of the scatter plot, pixel by pixel, and folds the fifteen-year timeline so that every year lies over the same January-to-December span. Each column of the source picture (about five and a half days) becomes a short streak, coloured by its year and brighter where years pile up. Folding turns what the original buries along its length into things that repeat in place. The week splits births into three layers: weekdays, and a weekend band that turns out to be two equal layers, which I read as Saturdays and Sundays. Births swell towards September. Christmas, early July, the end of October and the Monday holidays fall to weekend level every year, at the same place in the year. Colour keeps the years apart enough to show 2006–08 riding high and the 2010s sitting lower. I drew it from the picture's own pixels and not from cleaned-up numbers, so its grain is honest: the image cannot be sharper than the picture it came from.
