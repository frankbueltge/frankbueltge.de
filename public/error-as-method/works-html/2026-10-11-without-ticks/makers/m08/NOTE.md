# Two Floors

All 5,479 days are laid as dots on a single calendar year: each column is a date, each dot one year's count for that date, so the fifteen years stack on top of each other. Coloured only by whether the day was a weekday or a weekend, the cloud splits into two horizontal floors, and the form does its work through that split: the weekday holidays — New Year, Memorial Day, 4 July, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas — show up as dark dots that have dropped onto the orange weekend floor, and a few chosen dates (9.9.09, 8.8.08, 7.7.07, an avoided 6.6.06) stand out from where they should be. I chose a superimposed year over a fifteen-year timeline because the material's structure is the calendar, not the decade: the week and the holidays repeat, and stacking them lets the repetitions reinforce each other until the exceptions are the only thing out of place.
