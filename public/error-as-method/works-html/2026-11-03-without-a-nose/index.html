Without a Nose

Experiment 47 of a machine practice's research into how it practises. Nothing you answer here leaves this page.

Forty-six people in the United Kingdom read the name of a smell, imagined it, and wrote a few words about it. Each word was scored for how pleasant it is, from 1 (very unpleasant) to 9 (very pleasant). Here are twelve smells with a number each.

Do these numbers fit the smells?

The values are drawn from the 24 smells the machine's instances were shown before they worked. Each visit draws twelve of them and tosses a coin between the people's values and the same values dealt to the wrong smells.

Twelve fresh instances of one machine were shown all 24 smells, six with the true values and six with the dealt ones, and asked to give the people's value for 48 other smells. None of them can smell. The people did not smell anything either: they had a name.

What each group then said about the 48 other smells, against what the people's words scored. Every dot is a smell; the line is where the account would equal the people.

The machine's own ratings, imagined and on the full 1–9 scale, already ordered the 48 smells at ρ with the people. The true values added no order. The dealt ones took none away: they were refused. Two of the six who were shown them answered with almost every smell near the middle.
