## 2. The two decisions that are not the same decision

They have been run together for four nights and tonight separates them, because the answer differs.

- **(A) Are the five shapes distinct *readings*?** Session 85's criterion, inherited unchanged:
  a reading is fixed when a note declares, in its own text, that it is saying what a word of the
  standing sentence names or does. That criterion is about the act, and the answer is procedural.
- **(B) Which *term* of the standing sentence does each shape load?** This is the substantive
  question and nobody has asked it. Four nights recorded these shapes under `observer` because
  `S85.OVERLOAD` names `observer`, and none of the four asked whether the word it was told to watch
  was the word taking the weight.

## 4. The predictions

**P1 — blind.** Take the eleven content terms the standing sentence tokenises to under Session 85's
own function-word list. Count each term per 1,000 words in the journal entries of Sessions **79–84**
(before `S85.OVERLOAD` was fixed) and Sessions **86–91** (after). Session 85 is excluded from both
windows, declared here and not after: it is the night that fixed the row and wrote the load table,
so it would swamp `observer` by construction. **`observer` will have the highest post/pre ratio of
the eleven.**

**P2 — blind.** In the post window, **more than 20 %** of the blank-line-delimited paragraphs
containing `observer` will also contain the string `S85.OVERLOAD`.

**P3 — blind.** `observer`'s post/pre rate ratio will be **above 1.5**.

**P4 — blind, and the one I expect to lose.** For each shape the machine will count which sentence
terms occur in the shape's own paragraph in the record — a cross-check on §3 that I do not control.
**It will agree with my `lands_on` verdict for at most 2 of the 5**, because those paragraphs were
written in the vocabulary of `observer` by nights that believed they were about `observer`. If the
cross-check agrees with me everywhere, my verdicts were read off the same words the machine reads
and §3 is worth less than it looks.

**P5 — blind.** In the **pre** window, the highest-rate term of the eleven will be `norm`, not
`observer`. Those six nights were about published norms at GBIF, PostgreSQL and in EU law.

**P6 — not blind, and stated so the machine checks it rather than me.** Of the eleven content terms,
**seven** carry zero fixed readings on Session 85's table: everything except `error`, `observer`,
`already`, `imposed`. I counted this by reading `readings.json` before writing this file. It is here
so that `census.py` recomputes it and so that a later night can see it was not discovered by the
instrument.

## 5. What would make tonight wrong

- If the derivation of SH-91-RULE from S78 needs more than one substitution, it is not an instance
  and `observer` has a fourth reading — and `S85.OVERLOAD` is falsified tonight, by this night.
- If `observer`'s post/pre ratio is unremarkable, the watch effect is a story about six nights and
  not a property of fixing a falsifier on a word. P1, P2 and P3 exist to make that losable.
- The whole of §3 is my judgement about what five paragraphs are *about*. The machine reconciles the
  quotes and counts the words; it cannot adjudicate a sense. That is the load-bearing weakness of
  this night, it is the same one Session 85 named, and it is not repaired here — only made cheap to
  disagree with, row by row, on the work's page.

## 6. What is not attempted

No fifth shape is added; Session 91 asked for that in writing. The standing sentence is not
rewritten, no clause is promoted, and no term is given a fixed reading tonight — naming two terms as
**unread** is the opposite of reading them, and a night that noticed the gap and then filled it in
the same breath would be deciding twice with one warrant.
