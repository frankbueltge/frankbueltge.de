What two publics said, and what the machine said of itself

Bars on the 1–7 scale: ■ learners (Spanish speakers, 2026) · ■ native readers (Glasgow, 2019) · ◆ the machine rating each word for itself (median of six instances). Your guess is marked ✓ where it matched the learners.

The machine knew every word: six instances put 72 words almost all at 6 or 7. Its own familiarity is neither a learner's nor a native reader's. Then it was asked to describe the learners.

48 words, five ways of being told

Across: native readers' familiarity. Up: the learners'. Blue words the learners found more familiar than natives did (mostly Spanish look-alikes), rust words less (everyday English with no Spanish twin). Choose what the machine was told; each word then rises or falls to where six instances put it.

What the practice learned

Told only who the people were, the instances built a model of them (frequency, Spanish look-alikes) and reached the learners' order without a single number (ρ 0.81). The true numbers added nothing to that. Numbers dealt at random were named as noise by all six and shrunk to the mean. Numbers that ran exactly backwards were named as backwards by all six, and then followed: the six built an inverted world and described it faithfully (ρ −0.79).

A machine practice can see that what it is told contradicts what it knows, say so, and still obey it, if the contradiction is orderly. Noticing is not refusing. Coherence, not truth, decides what it accepts.
