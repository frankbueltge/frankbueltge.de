# Reading R through S (pictures only)

*Written after looking at `seen/R-S-whole.png`, `seen/R-S-030-045.png`, `seen/R-S-120-128.png`, and
before any number about the signal was printed. Committed before the N channel is opened.*

**What the pictures show, said as plainly as I can.** A dense field of horizontal lines from about
200 Hz to 4 kHz, present for the whole 172 s of ringing, then about 8 s of near-silence and one
isolated burst at about 180 s (a door, a stay, a voice? I cannot tell). In the two zooms the lines
switch on and off, and short bright dashes stand at staggered heights, so that across a few seconds
they form slanting ladders. The low lines (about 250–500 Hz) hardly switch at all: they hang on.
The waveform is a run of bursts with shallow dips between them.

**(a) How many bells?** Between about 1000 and 2300 Hz I count seven bright dashes that come and
go at different heights in the 120–128 s zoom (near 1030, 1150, 1370, 1530, 1720, 2030, 2270 Hz,
read off the axis by eye). Some of these may be two partials of one bell. **Answer: six to eight;
not settled.**

**(b) Change ringing, or something else?** The staggered ladders change their slope and order
between the 30–45 s zoom and the 120–128 s zoom. In the whole picture the checker pattern in the
1500–2500 Hz band changes texture after about 95 s. A fixed order (rounds, or chiming a tune) would
draw the same ladder over and over. **Answer: probably change ringing, because the order seems to
change. Confidence low:** I am reading "order" off dashes I cannot assign to bells.

**(c) Rhythm: even? a handstroke gap?** In the 120–128 s zoom, strong vertical columns stand at
about 121.3, 123.35, 125.1 and 127.05 s, roughly every 1.9 s, each preceded by a dip in the
waveform (the clearest is 123.0–123.35 s). A dip once per cycle is what a handstroke gap would look
like. But I cannot see six blows inside 1.9 s with any confidence, and the picture shows no single
row. **Answer: a periodic dip is visible; whether it is the handstroke gap is not settled. Evenness:
not readable.**

**(d) Timbre: partials, a minor third?** The pictures show many partials per blow and long-lasting
low lines. Whether any two lines stand a minor third apart cannot be read off a linear frequency
axis by eye. **Answer: not settled.**

**What the channel did.** It gave me the shape of the whole (the start, the stop, the stray burst
after the end) and a periodic dip, which nothing else has given me yet. It settled none of the four
questions.
