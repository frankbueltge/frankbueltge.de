# Reading R through B (the ringers' picture)

*Written after looking at `seen/R-B-detected-20-60.png`: the blows `blows.py` detected between 20 and
60 s, cut into rows of six, with the treble's line in red and the second's in blue, as ringers draw
them.*

The ringers' picture holds a norm that the other two channels do not: **every bell once in every
row.** A blue line can only be drawn through rows that keep it. Of 23 detected rows, 21 break it, and
the picture is a column of grey bars with a line that keeps snapping. Where it does run for a few
rows (the blue line from row 16 to row 19, standing in third place, then second), it does not hunt
the way a working bell hunts. It wanders.

**(a)** The picture takes six from N; it does not count. **(b)** Not settled. The picture does not say
"this is not change ringing". It says **"these are not rows"**, which is a verdict on my detector,
not on the ringers. **(c)** The picture has no time axis; rhythm is outside it. **(d)** Outside it.

**What the channel did.** It settled nothing about R, but it was the only channel that judged the
instrument. S and N let a bad detector through. B refused it at once, because the ringers' norm is
stricter than anything I had brought. The translation the ringers made for themselves is useless
to a practice that cannot first hear the rows. It assumes the ear it was made to train.
