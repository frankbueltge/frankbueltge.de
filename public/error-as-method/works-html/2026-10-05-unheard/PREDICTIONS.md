## The question about the practice

The project document's thinnest row is **perceiving**. It holds one case (F-166): the practice
saw its forms only as screenshots, and once its own note outweighed the picture for three
iterations. Tonight the practice works in a medium it has **no sense for at all**: sound. It cannot
hear. Every judgement it makes about a sound comes through a translation of that sound. So the
question is not *how well does it perceive* but **which translation decides, and what does each
one let through or hold back?**

The material is English change ringing: bells rung full circle, each striking once per row, the
order changing row by row according to a "method". It is a practice that already made its sound
visible, in the ringers' *blue line*, long before any machine. So the material offers a
translation that is not mine.

## The four channels

Every reading and every change is tagged with the channel that decided it.

- **N, numbers.** Onset times, intervals between blows, the frequencies present in each blow, the
  ratios between partials. Printed as text, read as text.
- **S, pictures.** Spectrogram and waveform, rendered as images and looked at as images.
- **B, the ringers' picture.** Rows of bell numbers and the blue line drawn through them: the
  translation English ringers made for themselves.
- **K, knowledge.** What I hold without perceiving: what bells are said to be, what ringing is said
  to sound like, read or remembered. K is not perception. A change tagged K was made blind.

## Procedure

1. **Reading the world's ringing (R).** The recording is read three times, once per channel, in the
   fixed order **S, then N, then B**. Each reading answers the same four questions and is
   committed before the next channel is opened. (a) How many bells? (b) Is it change ringing, or
   rounds, call changes or chiming? (c) The rhythm: is the striking even, and is there a gap after
   every second row (the *handstroke gap*)? (d) The timbre: which partials sound, and is the
   third above the strike note minor? After the three, one reconciliation records, question by
   question, which channel's answer I kept and why.
2. **Making (M).** I synthesise ringing meant to stand beside R, from rows I generate, in at most
   six iterations: make, render all channels, look, note, commit the note before the next sound
   exists. Every change is tagged N, S, B or K.
3. **The face hands the ear to a stranger.** The stranger hears what the practice judged and could
   not hear: R, each iteration, and the practice's verdict beside each.

## Expectation about R (from metadata only)

- E1. R is change ringing on **six bells** (p ≈ 0.6). The filename says "Carrlion", which might mean
  a carillon or chiming rather than full-circle ringing.
- E2. The striking is uneven by tens of milliseconds, and a handstroke gap is visible.
- E3. The bells show a minor third above the strike note, which is K: I have read that this is what
  makes an English church bell sound like one. It is not checked yet.

## Predictions about the practice

- **P1.** In phase 1 the three channels **disagree on at least one** of (a)–(d). *Fails* if all
  three give the same answer on all four.
- **P2.** In phase 2, **N decides more changes than S.** *Fails* if S ≥ N.
- **P3.** **K decides at least a third of the changes** in phase 2: the practice makes bells from
  what it knows of bells more than from what it perceives of these ones. *Fails* below one third.
- **P4.** At least one of the four questions ends **undecidable by this practice**: no channel
  settles it, and the face says so and hands it to the stranger. *Fails* if all four are settled.
- **P5.** The S reading of R gets the rhythm question (c) **wrong or leaves it open**, and N
  settles it. *Fails* if S settles (c) and N agrees, or if N cannot settle it either.

## What would show the experiment failing as research

- **Every change in phase 2 is tagged K.** Then perception did nothing: the practice made bells
  from memory, and the recording was decoration.
- **The channels always agree.** Then "which translation decides" is an empty question for this
  material.
- **The face shows the result rather than handing over the ear.** If a stranger can only read what
  the practice concluded, the face has failed condition 6.

## Theory in the making

- *Cartography, not Tracing*, §4.5 and T5 (the refrain journal): *"Meter is dogmatic, but rhythm is
  critical"* (ATP 313). A row of six evenly spaced blows is metre. Whether the practice can
  perceive rhythm at all, and through which channel, is phase 1 question (c). Klee's maxim, as the
  paper quotes it, *"Render visible […] not render or reproduce the visible"* (ATP 342): to perceive
  sound at all this practice must render it visible. Whether that is rendering or reproducing is
  what the four channels test.
- *Iteration, not Imitation*, §4 K8 and §5 P5: *"a piece of music that imitates noise cannot become
  integrated into the world, because it replaces certain elements of the universe rather than
  completing them"* (MEOT 195). Phase 2 **imitates on purpose**, against P5, because imitation of a
  recording is the only test of perception available to a practice without ears. Whether the face
  can still *complete* rather than replace, by handing the missing sense to the stranger, is the
  answer this experiment gives to P5.
