# The Purse, Divided

Each Nobel Prize from 1901 to 2026 is one column in its category's strip. The column's height is the award in today's kronor, and it is cut into stacked pieces, one for each laureate's share, so every piece is the money one person or organisation actually took home. Pieces are dark for men, red for women and blue for organisations. I chose this form because the data holds two quieter stories under the famous names, and stacking shows both together. The purse fell to about a third of its 1901 value by the 1920s and did not recover until the 1990s. Over the same years one prize turned more and more often into thin thirds. The red pieces stay rare until the most recent decades, and the gaps from the war years and the late start of Economics show as empty ground.

## v2

**(a)** The purse's worth and the laureate's share converge in one coordinate: the kronor one person took home, on a single log axis, where every laureate is one mark. **(b)** The obstacle of v1 was that the purse is the same in every category, so the six strips drew one silhouette six times; now that sameness is the means: the one purse curve repeats as four parallel echoes (whole, half, a third, a quarter), and those echoes are the share scale itself, with the thickness of each echo counting how many took that cut in that year. **(c)** Struck: the six category strips, their labels and counts, the stacked columns, and the legend that had to explain the gaps between pieces.

I merge the six strips into one field, x the year, y the money one laureate took home (log), each laureate a short dash coloured man, woman or organisation, the dashes of one year and one share piled around their level. The fall and return of the purse is the shape of every band at once, and the slide from whole prizes to thirds shows as the top band thinning while the low band thickens. Category is given up on purpose: in this data it changes nothing about the money.

## v3

**(a)** The purse and the share converge in one area: each laureate is one piece whose width is the year's purse in today's kronor and whose height is their share of a prize, so the piece's area is exactly the money they took home, and a year's column is everything paid that year. **(b)** The obstacle of v2 was that the four echoes on a log axis were one purse curve drawn four times, so the share added only a fixed offset and splitting showed only as hard-to-compare pile thickness; now the purse is the means that measures time itself: the x axis is cumulative kronor, so the lean 1940s shrink to a sliver and the rich 2000s spread wide, and the share becomes a plain height in which whole prizes, halves, thirds and quarters can be counted. **(c)** Struck: the log money axis, the four echo traces and their labels, the pile-thickness legend, the even calendar axis and the dots for empty years, which now simply have no width.

I turn the field into a money-proportioned stack: x is the purse laid end to end, 1901 to 2026, each year's column as wide as its purse, cut into pieces one per laureate, whole prizes at the bottom and the thinner cuts on top, coloured man, woman or organisation. The one dip that v2 repeated four times is now said once, by the width of the decades, and the slide from whole prizes to thirds is read as the shredding of the top of each column.

## v4

**(a)** The prize count and the category converge in one structure, the row: each of six rows is one category and each cell in it is one prize, as wide as the year's purse and cut by its laureates' shares, so position says which prize and a hole says a prize not given. **(b)** The obstacle of v3 was that pieces were sorted by share, so a missing prize only lowered the column's ragged top and nobody could tell which prize was missing or whose thirds were whose; now the absences are the means: each hole sits in its own row, so the war years, the Peace prizes withheld and the empty Economics row before 1969 draw themselves, and the shredding into thirds and the rare red pieces show where they belong (sciences striped, Literature solid, Peace red and blue). **(c)** Struck: the "N prizes" scale, the sort by share and its legend line, the "1969 adds a sixth prize" note, which the empty row now says.

I keep v3's money-wide years and area-is-money pieces, but fix each prize to its category's row instead of piling the pieces by share; the right margin counts each row's holes.
