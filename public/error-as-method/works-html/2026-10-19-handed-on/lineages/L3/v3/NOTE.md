# The Long Lean

Each of the 1,035 awards that have a birth or founding year becomes a single thread. It runs from that year on an upper rail to the year of the prize on a lower rail. Both rails use the same calendar, so how far a thread leans is how long the laureate lived before the prize. Seen together, the threads make a woven sheet that leans further as the century goes on: the median laureate is 56 in the first quarter-century and 68 in the last. Five heavy black threads show the median wait for each quarter-century, and a small dotted gauge in the empty corner shows what 20 to 80 years look like as a lean. Men are a pale grey mass, women are red and organisations blue. Thread weight shows each laureate's share, and red ticks on the lower rail make the women countable. I chose this form because the most human fact in the table is not who won but how long they waited. A pair of shared timelines turns that wait into a visible angle and keeps every award in the picture. That includes the outliers: Malala at 17 is almost upright, Goodenough at 97 leans far over, and a Quaker council founded in 1647 runs in from off the page.
