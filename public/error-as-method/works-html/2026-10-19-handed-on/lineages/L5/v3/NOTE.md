# The Long Wait

Each of the 1,036 awards is drawn as one half-circle over a shared timeline. It starts at the laureate's year of birth (or an organisation's founding) and ends in the year of the prize, so the width of each arc is the life lived before the prize. Physics, chemistry and medicine rise above the line, while literature, peace and economics hang below it. Together they close into a lens-shaped weave that drifts rightward and widens as laureates grow older: the median age at the prize moved from 56 to 68 over the century. Along the line where every arc lands runs a spine with one small block per laureate in each prize year. It shows the shared prizes thickening, the red of women appearing late, and the gap of 1940–42, when nothing was given. Men are faint ink, women red and organisations blue. I chose the arc because it holds a whole life and its moment of recognition in one stroke, so the waiting itself becomes the shape.
