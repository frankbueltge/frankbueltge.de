# The Long Wait

Each of the 1,036 awards is drawn as one half-circle over a shared timeline. It starts at the laureate's year of birth (or an organisation's founding) and ends in the year of the prize, so the width of each arc is the life lived before the prize. The prizes for physics, chemistry and medicine rise above the line. Literature, peace and economics hang below it. Together they close into a single lens-shaped weave that drifts rightward and widens as laureates grow older. Men are faint ink, women red and organisations blue. That makes visible both how few the women are and how the institutions crowd the underside, where the peace prize lives. A few arcs stand apart: John Goodenough's 97 years, Malala Yousafzai's 17, the Red Cross's three arcs from 1863, and the Friends Service Council's arc from 1647, which falls off the page. I chose the arc because it holds a whole life and its moment of recognition in one stroke, so the waiting itself becomes the shape.
