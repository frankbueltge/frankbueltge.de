# The Long Wait

Each of the 1,036 awards is drawn as one semicircle over a shared timeline. It rises at the laureate's year of birth (or an organisation's founding) and comes down in the year of the prize, so the width of each arc is the life lived before the prize. Men are faint ink, women red, organisations blue. Together the arcs make a dense vault that drifts rightward and widens over the century, because laureates have been getting older. Some arcs stand apart: the Red Cross's three arcs from 1863, Malala Yousafzai's small arc, John Goodenough's 97-year span, and the Friends Service Council's arc from 1647, which runs off the page. Below the line, one mark per laureate tallies each year's prizes, which shows the empty years of the war. I picked the arc because it shows a person's age and the moment of recognition as a single gesture, and the waiting becomes something you can see.
