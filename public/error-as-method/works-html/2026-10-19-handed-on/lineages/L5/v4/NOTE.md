# The Long Wait

Each of the 1,036 awards is drawn as one half-circle over a shared timeline. It starts at the laureate's year of birth (or an organisation's founding) and ends in the year of the prize, so the width of each arc is the life lived before the prize. Its weight is that laureate's own purse, their share times the prize amount in today's kronor. Physics, chemistry and medicine rise above the line, while literature, peace and economics hang below it. The weave drifts rightward and widens as laureates grow older: the median age at the prize rose from 56 to 68. It also breathes with money. The first prizes and the recent ones are drawn heavy, the thin mid-century prizes faintly, and the divided science prizes stay lighter than the undivided literature prizes underneath. Along the line runs a spine of one block per laureate per prize year, broken at 1940–42. Men are ink, women red and organisations blue. I chose the arc because it holds a whole life and its moment of recognition in one stroke, so the waiting itself becomes the shape.
