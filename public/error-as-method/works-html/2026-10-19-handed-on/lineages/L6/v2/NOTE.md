# The Long Wait

Every laureate is one thin horizontal thread, running from the year they were born (or, for an organisation, founded) to the year of their prize. The threads are stacked in order of award from top to bottom, so the right edge becomes a staircase that is simply the calendar, and the ragged left edge shows when the winners came into the world. The distance between the two edges is the life lived before the prize. I chose this form because a prize is a single moment, but the data holds a whole lifetime behind each one. Laid side by side, the threads show that the band pulls away from the calendar over the century, so the wait keeps getting longer. The few red threads, for women, stand out against the pale mass of men, and the blue threads mark organisations.

## v2
The page said "the wait is getting longer" but only in words: against a staircase of ragged threads the eye cannot judge a drift of twelve years. So v2 draws the claim into the picture. A bright line traces the median birth year of the people awarded around each year (an eleven-year window), and a thin dashed line shows where that median would sit if laureates had kept the age they had before 1950; the widening wedge between the two is the lengthening wait. I also give the annotations a dark halo so they stay legible where they sit on threads, and say in the legend that one organisation (the National Dialogue Quartet) has no founding year and so no thread.
