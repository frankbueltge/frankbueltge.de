# The Heap

Every meteorite in the list is drawn as a stone at one shared scale, its width growing with the cube root of its mass the way a real stone's does, and let fall in order onto a ground line at the year it was recorded, settling at the lowest place where it touches nothing; the ones with no year are poured onto a small heap of their own at the right, and the darker stones are those whose place of fall is also known. I chose a pile over a chart because mass is the one thing every entry has, and a heap shows it physically: a few giants like Hoba and Campo del Cielo hold most of the 217 tonnes, while hundreds of gram-sized fragments are barely specks of grit. The time axis, in turn, shows how the record itself has grown, with a thin scatter over four centuries and a tall mound from the last few decades of finding and cataloguing.
