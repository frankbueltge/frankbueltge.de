# The Whole Heap

All 568 meteorites are dropped onto one floor at a single scale. Each one is a disc as wide as a ball of the same density would be, so its width follows the cube root of its mass, and they are laid largest first, each settling into the lowest place it can find. I chose a heap over a chart because the facts here are about weight and how unevenly it is shared. Five stones (Hoba, Campo del Cielo, Gancedo, Agpalilik, El Ali) make up the ground and hold 81% of the 217 tonnes. Hundreds of gram-sized finds end up as a crust of near-dust over them, and the half of the record that weighs under 422 g looks like the grit it really is. The shade of each disc gives the year on record, darker for older and grey where there is none, so the heap also shows when the record was made without becoming a timeline.
