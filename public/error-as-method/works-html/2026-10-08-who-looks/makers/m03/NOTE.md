# Sediment

Each of the 568 meteorites is drawn as a stone at its true size relative to the others: the radius follows the cube root of the mass, so the stones read as solid bodies of one density. They are dropped onto a floor one at a time, in the order of the year Wikidata gives them, at that year's place on a timeline, and each one rolls until it settles. Stones with no year form their own heap off to the side, and stones whose location is recorded are dark while those without one are pale. I chose to show the data as sediment because it shows two things a chart would flatten. The early centuries hold only a few stones with wide gaps between them, and the recent ones pile up in a dune. Most of the record, by count, is gravel that settles into the gaps between a handful of giants. In some places, like Hoba under El Ali, an older giant ends up buried under the ones recorded after it.
