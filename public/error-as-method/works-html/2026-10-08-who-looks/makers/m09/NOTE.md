# Everything Weighed

Every one of the 568 meteorites is drawn as a sphere of the same density, all at one scale, so its size on the page follows the cube root of its mass. Then the stones are dropped one at a time onto a single ground in the order they were found. Each one rolls to the lowest stable place it can reach, either on the ground or cradled between two stones. The result is a heap. The 1576 Campo del Cielo mass sits at its base, the 60-tonne Hoba leans on its flank, and the recent desert and Antarctic finds settle over everything as pale grit. Undated stones fall last and are drawn as empty outlines. I chose a heap over a chart because mass is the one thing every item in this set states. Showing it as physical bulk makes the real proportions plain: a few giants make up nearly all the weight, and hundreds of gram-sized finds are specks. Stacking the stones by year adds a rough stratigraphy of when people found and weighed them.
