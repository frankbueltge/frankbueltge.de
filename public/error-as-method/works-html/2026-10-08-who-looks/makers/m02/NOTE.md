# Deposit

Every meteorite in the file is drawn as a disc at one shared scale and one assumed density, so its size follows the cube root of its mass, and then all 568 are dropped into a single trench in the order of their recorded year, each settling into the lowest spot still open from above. The result is a cross-section of sediment: a few heavy stones from the sixteenth to nineteenth centuries at the bottom, the twentieth century over them, the thin, crowded layers of the 2000s and 2010s near the surface with the recent giants (Gancedo, El Ali) resting on top, and the undated stones as hollow outlines on the very top. Darker means earlier, rust means the record gives a place. I chose a deposit rather than a map or a timeline because what stands out in the material is how unevenly mass and record are spread: five stones hold four fifths of the total weight, while nearly two hundred finds weigh under 100 grams each. Stacking the stones lets you see both at once, with Hoba and Campo del Cielo as boulders and the grams as grit around them, and the layers show the record piling up faster toward the present.
