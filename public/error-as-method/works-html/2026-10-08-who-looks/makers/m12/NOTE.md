# What Came Down

Each meteorite is a disc whose area is its mass. The disc rests on a single ground line at the year Wikidata records for it, so the stones overlap as they pile up in time. The few with no year hang from a thread above, not yet set down. I chose true area over a log scale because the material is lopsided: Hoba and Campo del Cielo alone make up about half of the 217 tonnes. At that scale a kilogram is less than a pixel, and 349 of the 568 stones are lighter than that. They are not dropped and not inflated to a visible dot. They are written as names beneath the ground, heaviest first down to the single gram, with gauge marks where the weight passes round numbers. The image shows the record two ways at once: the weight sits above the line, and most of the count is under it.
