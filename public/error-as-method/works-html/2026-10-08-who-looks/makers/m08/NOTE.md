# Where They Came to Rest

All 568 meteorites are drawn as stones at one common scale. Each stone's width grows with the cube root of its mass, which is how a real stone of uniform density would scale, so a 60-tonne iron is about four hundred times as wide as a one-gram crumb, not sixty million times. Each stone is dropped above its year on a time ruler, in date order. It rolls off whatever it lands on until it is cradled, so the record settles into a heap of scree. Campo del Cielo lies almost alone at the far left. Hoba ends up half-buried under the stones counted after it. Most of the record is a slope of gravel from the last fifty years, which piles up against the present, and the stones with no year form a separate cairn. I chose a pile rather than a chart because mass is the one thing every item has, and weight and the order of arrival are what make things heap up. The image shows both at once: how lopsided the record is, and how recent.
