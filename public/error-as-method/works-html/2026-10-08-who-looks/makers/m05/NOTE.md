# The Pile

Every one of the 568 meteorites is drawn as a stone at true physical scale (one density assumed for all, 54 px to the metre, a person standing in for a ruler), dropped heaviest first above the year it fell or was found, and left to roll until it rests against the others. Masses run from a gram to sixty tonnes, and a chart on a log axis would make them all look alike; a single physical scale keeps the real disproportion, with a handful of boulders carrying most of the 217 tonnes and hundreds of finds reduced to grit. Piling them by year makes the record's shape into a landform: an empty plain for centuries, then a slope climbing steeply toward the present, when desert and Antarctic searches turned up most of what Wikidata lists. Stones with no recorded year make their own small heap behind a fence.
