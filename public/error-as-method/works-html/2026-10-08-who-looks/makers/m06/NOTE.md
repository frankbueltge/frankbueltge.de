# The Heap

Every meteorite in the file is drawn at one shared scale, as if all were made of the same stuff, so each stone's diameter follows the cube root of its mass; then the stones are let fall, one by one in the order of their year, each to the lowest place it can rest. What comes out is a sediment: Campo del Cielo (1576) and the early finds lie at the bottom, while the gravel of recent desert and Antarctic finds settles in the gaps and on top, under a few late giants such as Gancedo and El Ali. Stones with a recorded place are rust, the others graphite, and the 38 undated ones are set aside in their own pile. I chose a heap over a map or a timeline because most of these stones have no place, and the data's real drama is its scale: a handful of multi-tonne irons hold almost all the volume, the median stone weighs 422 g and is a pebble, and the lightest is a speck of dust. Piling them at true relative size makes that disproportion visible in one image, and the order of falling turns the years into layers rather than an axis.
