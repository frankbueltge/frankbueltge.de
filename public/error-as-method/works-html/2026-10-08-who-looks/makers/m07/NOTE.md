# Count and Weight

The same 568 meteorites are laid out twice across the width of the page, and each one is joined to itself by a band. Above, every stone gets an equal sliver, in the order its year was recorded, with a stroke whose height is the logarithm of its mass. That is the usual way a catalogue looks: many entries, all seeming to count about the same. Below, each stone gets only the share of the width its mass claims of the 217 tonnes in all. Between the two rows the bands either swell or close up: Hoba, Campo del Cielo, Gancedo and a few others spread into wide rust-coloured fans, and more than five hundred threads narrow into hairlines that come down on slivers of the bar thinner than a pixel. I chose this form because the most striking fact in the data is how far apart these two ways of counting are. Ten stones hold 93% of the weight. A plot on a log scale hides that, and a linear plot leaves almost every stone out of sight, so the image keeps both and draws every connection from one to the other.
