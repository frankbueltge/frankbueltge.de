I made a still image called "Count and Weight" and wrote its note. Both files are in the work folder. The page renders with no errors in the viewer; the final version is `renders/02.png`.

**How it works:** the 568 meteorites appear twice across the width, and each one is joined to itself by a curved band.
- **Top row:** every stone gets an equal slice, ordered by the year it was recorded, with the stones that have no year at the end. Each stroke's height is the log of its mass, so this row reads like an ordinary catalogue where every entry seems to count about the same.
- **Bottom row:** a bar of the 217 tonnes in all, where each stone gets only the share of the width its mass claims.
- **The bands between them:** the ten heaviest stones (Hoba, Campo del Cielo, Gancedo, Agpalilik, El Ali and five others) spread into wide rust-coloured fans and are labelled with mass and year. The other 558 narrow into faint threads that end on slivers of the bar too thin to see.

**Why this form:** the data's strongest fact is how far apart the two ways of counting are. The ten heaviest stones hold 93% of the mass, and 527 of the 568 would be less than half a pixel wide on a linear scale. A log plot hides that, and a linear plot hides almost every stone, so the image shows both and draws every connection between them.

**Remaining flaws:**
- Two leader lines in the second row of labels run through first-row text: Vaca Muerta's through "Bendegó", and Gancedo's under "Al Haggounia 001".
- The "1 g" scale mark sits tight against the left edge.

**Something I noticed in the data:** some coordinates look like where a stone is kept, not where it fell. Agpalilik, which fell in Greenland, has coordinates in Copenhagen. Only 106 stones have a place at all, so the image doesn't use location.
