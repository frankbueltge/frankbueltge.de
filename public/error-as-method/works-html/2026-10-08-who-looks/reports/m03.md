I made "Sediment", a single still image drawn on a canvas at 1100 × 800. Each of the 568 meteorites is a stone drawn at its true size relative to the others. Each stone is dropped onto a floor in the order of its Wikidata year, at that year's point on a timeline from 1570 to 2025, and rolls until it settles. The 38 stones with no year get their own heap at the right edge. The 106 stones with a recorded place are dark and the 462 without one are pale.

**What the picture shows:**
- **Sizes:** the radius goes with the cube root of mass, so every stone is drawn at the same density. Hoba (60 t) is the largest. The 1 g Revelstoke is ringed so it can be found. The median stone (about 420 g) is barely a grain.
- **Timeline:** the early centuries hold a few stones with wide gaps between them. After 1950 a dune of small grains builds up around a handful of giants. Hoba ends up buried under El Ali and other stones recorded after it.
- **Text:** the nine heaviest stones are labelled with name, mass and year. A short footer gives the 217 t total and notes that the two largest hold 51% of it. It also gives the size rule and the source.

I rendered it four times with the viewer; the last is `renders/04.png`, with no page errors. Two things are still rough in that render. The labels are tightly stacked over the crowded post-1950 area, and some leader lines cross stones.

Two oddities in the data, neither of which the image corrects:
- Agpalilik's coordinates point to Copenhagen, where it is held, rather than to Greenland, where it was found. So "a place is recorded" partly means a place where the stone is kept.
- One stone, Q65130721 (7 t, 2018), has no name in Wikidata. Its label shows the ID and says it is unnamed.
