I made "Everything Weighed", a still image of all 568 meteorites as one heap of stones. It loads `data.js` and uses no libraries or network. I ran the viewer four times, and the last render (`renders/04.png`) shows the full heap with no page errors.

**How it works:**
- **Size:** every meteorite is drawn as a sphere of the same density, all at one scale, so its diameter follows the cube root of its mass. Hoba (60 t) is about 235 px across and the 1 g Revelstoke is a speck.
- **Order:** stones are dropped onto one ground line in order of year found. Each rolls to the lowest stable spot within reach, either on the ground or resting between two stones below it, and bigger stones roll further. The result is one deterministic mound.
- **What you see:**
  - Campo del Cielo (1576, 50 t) sits at the base.
  - Hoba leans against the right flank.
  - Gancedo and El Ali, both recent, lie near the top.
  - The many small recent finds collect as pale grit.
  - Stones with no year fall last and are drawn as empty outlines. Verkhne Tschirskaia, 7.5 t, is the largest of these.
- **Colour:** stones run from dark umber for old finds to pale bone for recent ones.
- **Text:** a small title and caption, and labels on the six heaviest named stones. Wikidata item Q65130721 (7 t) has no name, so it is left unlabelled. There is also a year key, a line naming the lightest stone, and the data source.

The first two renders failed: one stacked the stones into towers, the next had Hoba balanced on a column of pebbles and running off the top of the frame. Rolling the big stones further fixed both. The "oldest at the bottom, newest on top" layering holds only roughly, because big stones roll down to wherever they fit.

`NOTE.md` has the title and one paragraph on what the form does and why I chose it. Apart from the viewer's own `renders/` folder, these are the only files I wrote.
