I finished "Every Gram Counted", a still canvas image at 1100 × 800, and wrote its NOTE.md. I rendered it twice with the viewer; the second render (`renders/02.png`) shows the final version, and the viewer reported no page errors.

**What the picture shows:** each of the 568 meteorites is a slightly irregular stone, and its area on the page equals its mass, all at one scale. The stones are packed heaviest-first around a centre. Colour shows the year the stone entered the record: dark rust for the oldest, back to 1750, through to pale ash for 2024, and grey where no year is given. The large stones are labelled with name, mass and year, and the text column on the right gives the title, a short explanation, a colour key and size keys from 1 kg to 1 tonne.

**What the data shows:**
- All the stones together weigh 217 tonnes.
- Hoba (60 t) and Campo del Cielo (50 t) make up about half of that, and the eight largest stones carry nine tenths.
- The lighter half of the record, 284 stones, weighs 25 kg in all.

**One departure from strict scale:** at this scale most of the light stones would be smaller than a pixel. Those are drawn as single faint grains scattered around the boulders, so the dust stays visible. The caption on the page says this.

**Data issues I noticed but did not change:**
- Agpalilik's coordinates point to Copenhagen, probably where the stone is kept rather than where it was found.
- One item has no name, only the ID "Q65130721"; it is labelled "unnamed".
- A few items are Mars finds, such as Aeolis Mons and Meridiani Planum.

The work uses no libraries or network and loads only `data.js`.
