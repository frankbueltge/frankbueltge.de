I made "The Heap", a single still image drawn on a 1100 × 800 canvas. It loads `data.js` through a script tag and uses no libraries or network. I rendered it once in a headless browser: there were no errors, and the layout looks the way I intended.

**What the image shows:**
- **Size:** all 568 meteorites are drawn at one scale, as if they all had the same density. Each stone's diameter follows the cube root of its mass, so the 60-tonne Hoba is about 300 px across and the 1 g stone is a speck.
- **Order:** the stones fall one by one in order of year, and each settles at the lowest point it can reach. Campo del Cielo (1576) and the other early finds end up at the bottom, small recent finds fill the gaps and the top, and late giants like Gancedo (2016) and El Ali (2020) sit on top.
- **Color:** stones with a recorded place are rust-colored and the rest are graphite.
- **Undated stones:** the 38 with no year sit in a separate small pile on the right.
- **Labels:** the largest stones carry their name, mass and year. The left edge marks roughly where each era settled. A footer gives the source, the lightest and heaviest masses, and the median of 422 g.

One thing about the data: some recorded places seem to be where a stone is kept, not where it fell. Agpalilik, for example, has Copenhagen's coordinates. In the image, rust means only "Wikidata records a place".
