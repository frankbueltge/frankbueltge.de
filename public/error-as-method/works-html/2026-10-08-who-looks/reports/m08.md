The work is finished: **"Where They Came to Rest"**. It renders with no page errors. The last check was the viewer's render 09, at 1100 × 800.

**What the image shows:** all 568 meteorites appear as stones drawn at one common scale.
- **Size:** each stone's width grows with the cube root of its mass, so the stones are drawn as stones of equal density would be. Hoba (60 t) is about 165 px across and a 1 g stone is about 1 px.
- **Placement:** stones are dropped in date order above their year on a ruler running from 1560 to 2030. Each one rolls off whatever it lands on until it rests on the ground, against a wall, or cradled between two stones below it. The result is a heap of scree.
- **What you see in it:** Campo del Cielo (1576) lies almost alone at the far left. Hoba is half-buried under later stones. The stones from the last fifty years form a steep slope against the present. The 38 stones without a year form their own small heap at the right.
- **Labels:** stones above about 6 t have their name, mass and year written on them in pale ink. One of them, Wikidata item Q65130721, has no label, so it is marked "unnamed". At the bottom there is a scale key (1 g, 1 kg, 1 t) and a source line.
- **Look:** the sky is pale paper with faint parallel streaks, one per stone, showing its fall path. The stones are dark and pitted.

Because stones roll, some lie up to a few decades away from their year on the ruler; Bendegó (1784), for example, ended up near 1800. The year in each label is the recorded one.
