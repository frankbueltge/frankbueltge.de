I made "The Pile", a single still image drawn on a canvas, and checked it in four viewer renders. The last one, `renders/04.png`, shows no page errors and nothing clipped.

**What it shows:** all 568 meteorites are drawn as stones at one physical scale, 54 px to the metre. Each size comes from its mass, treating every stone as a sphere of the same density (3.5 g/cm³). The heaviest are dropped first, each above the x-position of its year, and they roll and settle against each other. A drawing of a person 1.75 m tall stands in for a ruler.

The result reads like a landform:
- An empty plain until about 1750, with Campo del Cielo (1576) alone on the left.
- A steep slope rising toward the present: Hoba, Gancedo, Agpalilik and El Ali form its base, and hundreds of finds under a kilogram sit on top as grit.
- The 38 stones with no recorded year form their own small heap behind a dashed fence on the right.

Stones with a radius of 34 px or more are labelled inside with name, mass and year. Item Q65130721 has no name in the data, so its stone reads "unnamed". The caption gives the totals worked out from the data: 217 tonnes, and 349 stones under 1 kg.

**Two things to know:**
- Using one density for all stones is a simplification. Iron meteorites like Hoba are really about twice as dense, so they would be smaller than drawn. The caption and the footer both say what was assumed.
- A stone's position is its year only roughly. Where stones crowd together they roll away from their year, especially in the recent decades.
