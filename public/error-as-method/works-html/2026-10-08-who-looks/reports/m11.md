I made "The Heap": all 568 meteorites drawn as stones at one shared scale, piled on a ground line at the year each one was recorded. It renders without errors as a still 1100 × 800 SVG. I never saw a picture of it: I only measured the layout numerically in a headless browser, because saving a screenshot would have meant writing a file outside the two you allowed.

Files are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user/8e7fe88b-dd11-5136-b7f9-9982ffeca570/scratchpad/s117/m11/work/`:
- `index.html`: loads `data.js` (`window.FALLS.items`) with plain JavaScript. No libraries, no network, no animation, no interaction.
- `NOTE.md`: the title and one paragraph.

**How the form works:**
- **Size:** each stone's width grows with the cube root of its mass, the way a real stone's does. That brings 1 g to 60 t (almost eight orders of magnitude) onto one page. Hoba, the heaviest, is about 124 px across; a 1 g stone is a third of a pixel, so the smallest are drawn at a minimum radius of 0.55 px.
- **Pile:** stones are dropped in date order at their year and settle at the lowest spot where they touch nothing. Dates run from 1576 to 2026.
- **Undated stones:** the 38 with no year go on a separate small heap at the right, marked "no year".
- **Shade:** darker stones are the 106 whose place of fall is also recorded. The two shades are close, so this may be hard to see.

**What it shows:** a few giants (Campo del Cielo, Hoba, Agpalilik, Gancedo, El Ali) hold most of the 217 tonnes, while hundreds of gram-sized pieces are specks. Records are a thin scatter over four centuries and then a tall mound since about 1950.

**Labels:** the five heaviest are labelled with name, tonnes and year. Gancedo and El Ali land almost on the same spot, so their labels sit on either side with bent leader lines. The page also has a title, a two-line caption, a size key (1 kg, 100 kg, 1 t, 10 t), year ticks and a source line.
