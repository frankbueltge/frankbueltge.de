**What the image shows** (a single still canvas, 1100 × 800, loading only `data.js`):
- **The ground line:** each meteorite is a disc whose area matches its mass. Hoba, at 60 t, has a radius of 140 px. Every disc sits on one ground line at its recorded year, with ticks for 1600–2000. The discs are only faintly filled, so where they overlap you can still see them pile up in time. The heaviest ones are labelled with name, mass and year, and the labels are moved so they don't collide.
- **No year:** the 22 stones with no year hang from a dotted thread at upper right, "not yet set down".
- **Below the ground:** at this scale a kilogram is less than a pixel, so the 349 stones under 1 kg are not drawn. They are written below the ground as names, heaviest first down to 1 g, and fade as they get lighter. Marks such as "— under 100 g —" show where the weight passes round numbers.
- **Text:** the title, a one-line summary (568 meteorites, 217 tonnes), a four-line caption and the Wikidata source line are drawn on the canvas.

**Points to know:**
- Hoba and Campo del Cielo together are about half of the total mass, which is why I used true area rather than a log scale.
- The names below the ground come out at about 7 px. The size is picked automatically so the text fills the space down to the bottom edge.
- One item has no label in Wikidata and appears as "Q65130721" (7 t, 2018).
- The place data (latitude and longitude) is not used. The work is about mass and time.
