I made the work, **"Cairn"**, and wrote its note. Both files are in the work folder. The last render (`renders/04.png`) shows no page errors and nothing overlaps.

**What it shows:** each of the 568 meteorites is a disc whose area is its mass. Hoba is the largest disc, with a radius of 126 px, and every other disc uses the same scale. The discs are dropped one at a time onto a single heap on a ground line, in order of recorded year, with the undated ones last. Each disc settles at the lowest resting point, with a pull toward the centre so the pile forms a mound. Darker tone means an older year. So Campo del Cielo (1576) is the dark stone at the base and Hoba (1920) sits on top of it.

**Labels and text:**
- Stones with a radius of 29 px or more are labelled inside the disc with name, mass and year.
- Smaller stones are drawn at a minimum size with low opacity, so the lightest ones show only as faint grain on the slopes.
- Beneath the heap are a short text, a year-tone key, and reference discs for one tonne and one kilogram. Wikidata is credited as the source.

**Numbers from the data that the text uses:**
- Total mass is 217 tonnes, and the five heaviest stones hold 81% of it.
- The lighter half of the record (284 stones) weighs about 25 kg in total.
- 522 of the 568 stones together come to under 1% of the mass. The lightest, Revelstoke, weighs 1 g.

**Data quirks to know about:**
- Some years are when the stone was found, not when it fell (for example Gancedo, 2016). The footer says so.
- One item has no name and appears as "Q65130721 (no name)".
- Some coordinates point to where a stone is kept rather than where it landed: Agpalilik's lat/lon is Copenhagen. I didn't use location in the work.
