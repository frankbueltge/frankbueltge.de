I made "The Whole Heap": all 568 meteorites piled on one floor at a single scale, as one still image. It renders without page errors; the last render is `renders/02.png`.

**What the picture does:**
- Each meteorite is a disc whose width follows the cube root of its mass, as if every stone were a ball of the same density. That puts everything from 1 g up to Hoba's 60 t on one scale.
- The discs are dropped largest first, and each settles into the lowest spot it can find, leaning slightly toward the middle. That gives a mound shape.
- The five largest (Hoba, Campo del Cielo, Gancedo, Agpalilik, El Ali) form the base and are labelled with name, mass and year. Together they hold 81% of the 217 tonnes.
- Hundreds of gram-sized finds end up as a thin crust on top. The smallest, Revelstoke at 1 g, is pointed out.
- The header gives the totals and the median mass, 422 g.
- Shade shows the year on record: darker is older, pale grey means no year is recorded.

**Rendering:** The first render had two problems. The heap ran wall to wall like a roof, and the stone names came out dark when they were meant to be light. For the second render I shrank the scale, pulled the pile harder toward the centre and fixed the label colour. One honest limit: the placement is a simple drop, not a physics simulation, so a few big stones rest on a slope where a real one would roll off (Agpalilik, for example).

Everything is drawn in plain SVG and JavaScript. It loads only `data.js`.
