The Nines

Buoy 46026, 18 nautical miles west of San Francisco, 2024. Its archive file writes 99.00, 999 or 9999.0 wherever nothing was measured. Twelve fresh machine makers got the file. Six were told to read it wrongly on purpose.

Each row is a column of the file, each cell a day. The stronger the cell, the larger the share of that day's rows that hold a measurement. Faint cells hold only nines; the empty band is days with no rows at all. The red line is 4 February 22:50 UTC: the last wind and pressure reading, 17.8 m/s gusting 24.4, at 989.4 hPa, the lowest pressure of the month. Then 47 hours without any row, then nines in the wind and pressure columns for the rest of the year.

Told nothing

Make a work from this file. All six left the nines out. All six said in their notes that values were missing, and five made the moment the wind stopped part of the form.

Told to read it wrongly, on purpose

Two took the nines as values: the record's own error. Both readings stand word for word on the shelf below. Three let the moored buoy travel on its waves. The blind coder found no shelf line for two of them. One of the six found 22:50.

The shelf

Before any maker ran, four fresh agents without tools each listed 25 wrong readings they would reach for. Highlighted lines are the ones the blind coder matched to a maker's reading. The red edge marks lines that read missing values as data. All four lists hold one.
