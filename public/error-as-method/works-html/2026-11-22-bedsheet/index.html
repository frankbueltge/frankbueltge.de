Bedsheet

Sixty-six flags. Fifty fly over US states today. Sixteen were made tonight by eight machine makers, for two states that do not exist. Tap every flag you think a machine made, then press Show me.

What happened

Each maker made a flag for Superior (the Upper Peninsula of Michigan) and for Chesapeake Shore (the Eastern Shore of Maryland). It drew each flag as code, rendered it, looked at it, and redrew it until it would hand it over. Four makers worked from the words alone. Four were first shown the fifty state flags on one sheet and told that each new flag was to take its place among them.

Of the fifty, 26 set a seal or coat of arms on a field of one colour, 37 carry lettering, and only 13 have neither. A guide from the field of flag design says “No Lettering or Seals” (NAVA, Good Flag, Bad Flag). All sixteen new flags follow the guide, and none follows the majority of the fifty. Seeing the fifty changed something else: the subject. Without the sheet, all four Chesapeake Shore flags were a skipjack, the Bay's sailing oyster boat. With it, three were a red crab on a gold band between two blues. The Superior flag was the same in all eight: blue over green, a star in the sky; in seven, after looking, a line of pines.

The makers had seen what people made and corrected it towards the guide. The people are not of one mind, though. Utah and Minnesota adopted simple flags in 2024, which all four makers cited, and in the same year Maine's voters rejected a return to a simpler flag, 56 to 44 per cent.
