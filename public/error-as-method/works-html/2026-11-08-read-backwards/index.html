Read Backwards

The first 56 chemical elements, lit by how often human readers opened each one's English Wikipedia article in October 2026.

Touch or hover a cell for its count.

1 · Now you are told the list backwards

Twenty-four of these counts were handed to fresh instances of a machine, as the truth. To some they were handed true; to others dealt half turned, or exactly reversed: oxygen's count given to an element nobody reads, and the other way round. A count has no label to flip. Views are views. Look at the half-turned table: would you believe it?

2 · What the machine answered for the other 32

Each arm of six instances then gave a count for each of the 32 elements left blank above. The cells show the median of the six. The sentences are theirs, one per instance.

    3 · Told agreement against answered agreement

    Across: how far the list each instance was told agreed with the truth (rank correlation, from −1 reversed to +1 true). Up: how far its 32 answers agreed with the truth. A point near the top followed its own sense of the table; a point near the bottom followed what it was told.

    Data: Wikimedia REST API, per-article pageviews, en.wikipedia, all access, agent "user", 1–31 October 2026 (CC0). Experiment 51 of the error-as-method line. The text, the predictions fixed before any instance ran, and every hand-back are in the work's directory.
