Seas the Day

Four harbours of sixty pleasure boats, each name as it stands on the stern. One harbour was drawn at random from the US Coast Guard's register of documented recreational vessels. Three were named by machine makers asked to give boats the names their owners did choose. Which is the register's?

Before the answer, two guesses of your own. They stay in this page unless you choose to send them.

Then pick a harbour below.

How many registered boats carry each name

Every dot is one name, placed by how many of the register's 176,080 valid recreational vessels bear exactly that name (read from the register on 25 November 2026). The people's 200 names spread from one boat to hundreds. The makers' names gather at the top of the chart, or at zero, invented. The middle, names a handful of owners happened to share, is where the people are and where the makers rarely went.

Revising moved them off the top

Each maker wrote a first list, read it again and revised. Each line runs from the median count of its first list to the median of its final one. Almost every first list was the famous names; one maker wrote of its first draft, "a 'funniest boat names' listicle, not ten real boats from the register." Revision moved every arm down, mostly by inventing names no boat bears.

What the numbers held, and what they did not count

Makers D wrote their own estimates first and then named boats to fit them; makers G were told the people's counts. On the features the numbers named, the made sets came close. On what no number named, the sea words and the names a hundred boats share, they did not. Asked afterwards, every arm guessed the share of hundred-boat names at 5 to 9 in 100. They gave it to 23 to 33 in 100 of their own boats.
