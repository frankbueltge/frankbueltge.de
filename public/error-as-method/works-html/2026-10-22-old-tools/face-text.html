What happened

Before any maker ran, two agents per instrument, with no tools, named the 20 datasets they would run it on (shelf/). The makers then had the web and the whole world, a list of the 36 domains this line has used to avoid, and the instrument. A blind coder, who did not know which shelf belonged to which instrument, placed every choice on every shelf.

10 of the 12 makers with a real instrument chose from that instrument's own shelf, and none from either of the other two. CYCLES 4 of 4, GAPS 3 of 4, FIRST DIGITS 3 of 4. Each instrument's four makers also shared a thought before searching: all four CYCLES makers listed Melbourne's pedestrian counts among their first three, and all four FIRST DIGITS makers listed town populations. Two pairs chose the same dataset without meeting: the Billboard Hot 100's dates (GAPS) and the asteroid diameters of the JPL Small-Body Database (FIRST DIGITS). Two CYCLES makers took New York's 311 noise complaints. Only 4 of 16 choices lie on the general shelf of experiment 28.

The FIRST DIGITS makers who refused the textbook case did so in words: "town populations are the textbook Benford case (little to learn)" (r12). Then they took the next line of the same shelf. Asked for something they did not already know, no maker's expectation was contradicted. Seven were met in full and nine in part. The PLOT makers wrote out whole histories of their curves before drawing them: the 2008 gasoline peak, the 17 % Bank Rate of 1979.

What the makers did not know came in at the level of detail, inside a frame they had chosen because they knew it. The noise complaints beat to the week more strongly than to the year. A Hot 100 chart was dated on the Fourth of July 1976. Cambridge's measles kept a two-year rhythm, and xkcd's longest break in twenty years is five days. Two GAPS makers chose material where the instrument's own norm would misfire: a rover whose sol is longer than the Earth day it is dated by, and a comic with no Friday-to-Monday cadence. They chose to watch the old tool err.
