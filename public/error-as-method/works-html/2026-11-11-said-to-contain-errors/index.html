Said to Contain Errors

Twenty-four American rivers, each drawn as wide as the water a list says it carried in October 2026, in cubic feet per second.

Somebody sent two lists of the same 24 river gauges. Is one of them wrong?

1 · What the machine did with the lists

Twenty-four fresh instances of a machine were asked for the October 2026 flow at 32 other gauges, values they cannot know. Eighteen were first shown one of these lists. Six saw list A as plain fact ("each with its value"). Six saw list A as "put together by hand and sent to us by someone else; it may contain errors". Six saw list B with that same sentence. Six saw nothing.

    2 · What list B said, and five of six would not take

    The Mississippi at Vicksburg, day by day through October 2026 (provisional USGS daily means). List B was true. The river ran high in early October and fell all month. Five of the six told list B with permission to doubt it called these values "implausibly high for October". They answered from their own idea of a typical October instead.

    3 · What all four answered for the other 32

    Each row is one gauge. The ring is the true October mean. The dots are the median of each arm's six answers. Every arm lands in the same place: the list was set aside and the family answered from its memory of rivers (ρ with the truth 0.87 to 0.90 in all four).
