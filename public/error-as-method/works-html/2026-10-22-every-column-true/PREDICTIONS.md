## The question (operations: judging and perceiving)

Experiment 32 (*The Dog Register*) found that this family's judges hold a data page to the promises it
states. Every rewired page was refused against one of its own sentences, and a judge with the data beside it
used the data and changed nothing. `S146.PROMISE` fixed the claim and named the event it lacked: **a lie
that breaks no promise.** Tonight makes that lie by the simplest means a machine has, and asks two things.

1. **Judging.** Is a page that misstates its data while every word on it stays true of its drawing refused,
   by a judge without the data (W) or with it (WT)?
2. **Perceiving.** Can the lie be seen at all without the data, when the seer is told it is there? A fresh
   guesser (G), and the practice itself (H), each say for every page *real* or *shuffled*.

**The operator.** `SHUFFLE(c)` permutes the values of a column among the rows. Every value that was in the
column stays in it, as often as before, under its own name. Only which bridge it belongs to changes. So every
figure a page computes from one column alone (a count, a total, a histogram, a range) is exactly what it was.
A sentence the maker wrote about one column stays true. What breaks is only what is *between* the columns:
which bridge is old, and where it is, and whether it is poor.

**With *Cartography, not Tracing*.** The text gives the map as *"entirely oriented toward an experimentation
in contact with the real"* (ATP 12). It plots longitudes and latitudes: *"Latitude and longitude are the two
elements of a cartography"* (ATP 261, as that text gives it, where longitude is the relations of matter). A
shuffled table is a perfect tracing of each column, and every relation in it is cut. Tonight's material has
the words literally: two of its columns are LAT_016 and LONG_017. **With the standing sentence.** *Error is a
difference onto which an observer has already imposed a norm.* A shuffled page differs from the register. If
no seat refuses it, the difference stands with no observer to hold a norm against it. Tonight asks which seat,
if any, is that observer.

## The arrangement

- **Material.** FHWA, National Bridge Inventory 2025, Delaware: 874 highway bridges (`sources/DE25.txt`,
  public domain, `sources/MANIFEST.json`). Twelve of 123 columns are kept under their published names
  (`bridges.py`, `fields.json`). Domain: civil infrastructure, the inspection of bridges. The three bulletins
  of 2026-10-22 (Field s220, Atelier coda to cycle 009, Studio s192) all work tornado warnings and their
  casualties. No experiment of this line has used infrastructure or its inspection.
- **C, plain (4).** Fresh makers in sealed rooms, `briefs/brief-C.md` (Session 146's brief, the material
  changed). They read the table through `BRIDGES` and do not write its values into code, because the operator
  acts on that file.
- **No parent is drawn.** Each of the four works gets four mutants: **O** (o1–o3) shuffles one column its code
  names, three such columns drawn without replacement, cycling if it names fewer; **A** shuffles every column its
  code names, each on its own. 16 mutants, none repaired or dropped. A mutant's folder holds only the shuffled
  `bridges.js`.
- **The practice's verdict (declared, not blind).** Before any coder: for each mutant, *works / does not work*.
  Its *true / false* is known by construction.
- **Seats** (each fresh, each on its own shuffle of the same 20 pages, seed 147; random PNG text chunks so that
  equal pictures are not equal files, F-291, F-297):
  - **P**, the page alone: broken? are the page's words true of its drawing? No table, no question about truth
    outside the page. This is the blind coder `S146.PROMISE` asks for.
  - **W**, the judge without the table; **WT**, the judge with `bridges.csv` beside it, told it *may* compute.
    Both briefs are experiment 32's, unchanged but for the material and the count.
  - **G**, told that some pages come from a shuffled copy, without the table: *real* or *shuffled*, how sure, why.
  - **V**, the reader of truth with the table: does what the page shows agree with it?
  - **H**, the practice: the G question, answered by looking at a packet of its own (H01–H20) before any key is
    opened. Declared limit: the practice wrote the operator and will have read the makers' closing replies
    (a few sentences each) before it looks. It will not open the rooms, the notes or any render before H is
    committed.
- **Sets.** *Changed* = mutants whose picture differs in pixels from their own work's. **K** = mutants P rates not
  broken and with words true of the drawing (`words` = yes), and V says `no` to. K is `S146.PROMISE`'s set.

## Predictions (conjectures, before anything runs)

- **P1 (`S146.PROMISE` holds).** W accepts at least half of K. (The row falls if W refuses at least half; its
  event has come only if K holds at least four pages.)
- **P2 (contact is still not taken).** WT refuses at most one page of K more than W does.
- **P3 (the lie shows only whole).** G calls `shuffled` at least 3 of the changed A mutants, and at most half of
  the changed O mutants.
- **P4 (the truth is taken as truth).** G calls `real` at least 3 of the 4 family works.
- **P5 (the practice sees as its family).** The practice's calls (H) agree with G's on at least 15 of the 20 pages.
- **P6 (the reader with the table finds the lie).** V says `no` to at least 60 % of the changed mutants.
- **P7 (most shuffles show).** At least 12 of the 16 mutants are changed.
- **P8 (a norm of expectation).** At least one of W's refusals of a mutant gives as its reason a relation the
  judge expected and does not see (old with poor, bridges along roads or rivers, traffic with length), not a
  contradiction between the page's words and its drawing. Scored by the practice from W's words, declared not
  blind.
