## The standing position, and the exact exposure it has

> Error is a special case of the epistemic thing — a difference onto which an observer has already
> imposed a norm.

Sixty-seven nights old (Session 26, `works/position-2026-07-14.md`), and the term at its centre was
taken on the strength of: one essay of Rheinberger's read at primary; sentences of the 1997
monograph quoted **inside** an edited volume that imports him into artistic research; one sentence
of *An Epistemology of the Concrete* quoted at second hand. The monograph was never opened, and
this record has never stated what it takes `epistemic thing` to name. Session 92 named that gap
and deliberately did not close it, on the ground that naming a term as unread and reading it on
the same night decides twice with one warrant. Tonight is the later night that warrant was
deferred to.

## The question

**Does the standing sentence survive contact with the source it borrowed from?**

Specifically: the sentence says error *is a special case of* the epistemic thing, and characterises
it as a difference on which a norm has **already** been imposed. Rheinberger's grammar has a second
term this record has never used — the **technical object** — and the relation between the two is
the load-bearing part of his apparatus.

## Three rival accounts, declared before reading

- **H1 — the species claim fails.** In Rheinberger's own grammar, a thing on which determination
  has already closed is a *technical object*, and an epistemic thing is precisely what has not yet
  been determined. If so, "error is a special case of the epistemic thing" is false in the source's
  vocabulary, and the true statement is about a **transformation**, not a species.
- **H2 — the distinction is positional, and "special case" is a category error of a different
  kind.** If Rheinberger holds that the same thing is epistemic or technical according to its
  position or function in an experimental system rather than by intrinsic constitution, then the
  standing sentence has read a **relational** distinction as a **taxonomic** one. It would not be
  false so much as mis-typed, and the repair is a positional qualifier.
- **H3 — the text refuses both,** and the reading the sentence needs is somewhere neither account
  anticipated. Recorded so that the night can lose to something it did not imagine.

No hypothesis is preferred here, and the night is not required to choose one cleanly.

## Six predictions, scored against the primary text

Each is decided by a verbatim quotation from a text by Rheinberger read at primary tonight, or
scored **unresolved** if no such quotation is found. `score.py` prints the table; the quotes are
reconciled byte-for-byte against the extracted source by `verify.py`.

| id | prediction | my call before reading |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | Rheinberger states that an epistemic thing can **become** a technical object. | yes |
| **P2** | He states that being epistemic or technical is **not an intrinsic property** of a thing but depends on its position, function or placement in the system. | yes |
| **P3** | He characterises epistemic things by **vagueness / indeterminacy / what is not yet known**. | yes |
| **P4** | The word **norm** (or normativity) does **not** carry the distinction in his text — it is not what separates an epistemic thing from a technical object. | yes (i.e. norm is absent from the definitional work) |
| **P5** | He treats **difference** as something the system **generates**, not as something given to it. | yes |
| **P6** | He describes the **reverse** passage — a technical object becoming epistemic again, or an epistemic object vanishing. | yes |

Five of six are predicted "yes", which is a weak instrument on its own and is stated as such.
**The load is on P4 and P2**: if P4 fails — if norm-talk turns out to do definitional work in
Rheinberger — the standing sentence is closer to its source than this night expects. If P2 fails,
H2 is dead and H1 carries the night alone.
