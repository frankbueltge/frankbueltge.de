## v0

The work plays the record one day at a time (about two and a half minutes for 48 years): a white, ragged disc whose area is that day's ice extent breathes out each winter and in each summer, while a dotted ring holds the 1979–1990 average for the same calendar day, so whatever is missing shows as open water between the two, and beneath it a thin line writes the difference down as it happens, drawing a slow descent that is not there until you have lived through it. I chose area-to-scale and real-time pacing because a number like 5.96 means little to a stranger, but watching the ice fail to reach its old edge, season after season, against a fixed memory of what it used to be, makes the imbalance felt rather than read; pausing and dragging the line lets the viewer stop at any day and weigh it.

## v1

**(a)** The disc (the felt magnitude), the strip (the record and the way through time) and the year's clock converge in one dial: angle is the day of the year, radius is the square root of that day's extent, so a year swept around the centre is itself the disc, area to scale, and every finished year stays behind as a closed contour, the record now lives inside the figure instead of under it. **(b)** The seasonal cycle was the obstacle: it made the disc breathe by ten million km² while the loss was a hairline ring nobody could see; now the season *is* the shape (the angular form of the loop), and the deficit appears at every angle as a rust band between the year being drawn and the dotted 1979–1990 year, whose area is the year's mean shortfall, the balance, read out as a running figure. **(c)** Struck: the anomaly strip below, the invented ragged edge (the edge is now the real daily record), the breathing whole-disc, and the separate scrubber — you wind the dial itself backwards or forwards by dragging around it.

## v2

**(a)** The reading and the ice converge in one surface: the year, the day, today's extent and the running balance are no longer a column beside the dial but are written at its centre, on the ice itself, inked dark only where this year's ice has already been swept; and each finished contour now carries its own year's balance as its tint, so the record of shapes and the ledger of shortfalls are one line per year, and the long descent that v1 lost with the strip returns as a drift from grey-blue to rust, inward. **(b)** The obstacle was the white wedge: a great opaque sector that dominated the figure, covered its centre and left the numbers to crowd the month labels on the left; now the wedge is the page, and its incompleteness is the means: the year's name and figures are dim until the ice reaches them, so the inscription fills in at the pace of the year. **(c)** Struck: the left column of three large figures, the "today against 1979–1990" figure (the band at the hand already says it), the four-line legend reduced to a short key, and the off-centre placement that made the dial collide with its own text.
