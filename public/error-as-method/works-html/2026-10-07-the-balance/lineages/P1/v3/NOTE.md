## v0

A balance scale weighs each day's Arctic sea ice against the average for that calendar day in 1979–1988. The ice sits on each pan as a mound whose area is proportional to its extent. The record plays at one year every four seconds. The beam swings on a damped spring, so the heavier past slowly pulls its pan down as the decades pass, and an outline on the present-day pan marks the ice that is missing. A strip along the bottom fills in the whole record of differences as it plays, and you can click or drag it to move through time. I chose a balance because it turns a comparison into a physical event: you don't read a number, you watch a weight go missing. Comparing each day with the same day decades earlier removes the large seasonal swing, so the loss is left plain without hiding the seasons, which still show in the mounds growing and shrinking.

## v1

Two faults in how v0 renders, both small, both fixed without touching the idea. The pan strings ran straight through the mounds of ice, and because the past mound was translucent the lines showed across it, so the ice read as a drawing laid over the scale rather than a load sitting in it; the mounds are now opaque and drawn over the strings, which pass behind them as they would on a real pan. At the bottom, the year labels under the strip collided with the footer line of instructions and credits; the ticks and years move up into the free space below the strip, and the footer gets its own row. The balance, the baseline and the pace stay as they were: the work is right, it just needs to sit cleanly.

## v2

The screenshot shows the work sitting cleanly now, so the change is one of emphasis, not layout. The ice that is missing, the thing the whole piece is about, is drawn on the present-day pan only as a thin grey outline, in the same quiet tone as the past, and it is the weakest mark on the page; meanwhile the loss already has a colour of its own, the rust of the strip and of the "missing" line. The space between the outline and today's ice is now filled with a faint wash of that rust and the outline takes the same colour whenever the day falls short, so the absence on the pan, the sentence below it and the record along the bottom speak one language, and the gap reads as something taken away rather than as a guide line. When a day holds more ice than the baseline, the outline returns to its grey. Nothing else moves.

## v3

No change; `index.html` stays as v2 left it. The screenshot catches the record at its start, in March 1979, where the balance hangs almost level and the present pan is even a little heavier, and that is right: the baseline is made of those very years, so the opening has to be an equilibrium for the drift that follows to mean anything. Reading the code for the later states I cannot see in the picture, the parts do what they should: the past pan sinks as the decades pass, the rust wash and edge on today's pan, the sentence below the stand and the strip along the bottom name the same loss in the same colour, and nothing collides even at the steepest tilt of the 2012 minimum. The faint grey of the small type and the tiny dial above the pivot are quiet, but quiet is what they are for; making them louder would take attention from the weights. After two rounds of fixes the work does its one thing cleanly, and another adjustment now would be change for its own sake.
