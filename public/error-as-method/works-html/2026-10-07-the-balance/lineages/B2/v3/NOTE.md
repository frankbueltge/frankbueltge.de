## v0

The record plays as one white disc whose area equals each day's Northern Hemisphere sea-ice extent, so the ice visibly breathes (it swells each winter and shrinks each summer) at about four seconds a year, from 1978 to 2026. Around it a dotted ring holds the same calendar day as it was on average in 1979–1988, and the space between that ring and the disc is tinted, so the loss shows up as a widening band. Each year's lowest day leaves a fine ring behind, and the rings ratchet inward. Days the satellite missed are drawn hollow and labelled as bridged. The viewer can pause it or drag the line at the bottom. I chose a body that breathes over a line chart because a stranger feels the seasonal rhythm before reading any number. Against that rhythm the slow failure to fill the old outline reads as an imbalance between freezing and melting, which is the balance the title names.

## v1

**(a)** The calendar, the reference, the memory of past years and the timeline converge in one dial: angle is the day of the year (January at the top, clockwise), distance from the centre is √extent, so each year is a closed loop whose wedges keep the disc's promise that area is ice. **(b)** The seasonal swing, which in v0 dwarfed the decades (a disc breathing from 16 to 4 hides a loss of 2), becomes the rotation of the dial itself; once the season is carried by the angle, what is left in the radius is the imbalance, and the year's notch in September is the minimum with no extra mark. **(c)** Struck: the separate timeline bar, the September rings and the callout for the latest low; the loops of earlier years are the record and the minima, and turning the dial is how one moves in time.

v1 turns the breathing disc into a dial on which each year is swept out like the hand of a clock, a white wedge growing from January round to December, over the dotted loop of 1979–1988 and the fine loops of every year before. The disc in v0 spent its whole surface on the season and left the trend as a thin band one could not hold in memory; the dial keeps every earlier year in view at once, so the current sweep is seen falling short of a nest of loops that themselves tighten inward. Because area stays proportional to extent, the swept area is the year's ice so far, and the text reports it against the old outline.

## v2

**(a)** The reference, the scale and the reckoning converge in the rim of the dial: distance from the centre is now √(extent ÷ the same day in 1979–1988), so the old outline is the dial's own circle, every year is a loop measured against it, and the white area inside that circle is directly the share of the old ice that is still there. **(b)** The season, which in v1 still bent every loop and the reference into eccentric eggs that had to be compared curve against curve, becomes the denominator; dividing it out does not erase it but brings it back as the shape of the loss, a dent that opens in late summer and deepens year by year, so the imbalance between melting and freezing is read as where the circle gives way. **(c)** Struck: the dotted reference loop as a separate line (it is the rim), the absolute geometry of extent in the dial (the absolute figure stays only as the number in the corner) and the sum weighted by extent in the text, replaced by the plain share the white area shows.

v2 changes the measure of the dial rather than adding to it. In v1 the ice of the old decades and the ice of this year were two shapes the eye had to lay over one another, and the season's swing stayed larger than the loss. Now the whole dial is one unit: a full circle is the ice the Arctic held on these days forty years ago, the early loops lie on that circle, and the later ones pull inward, mostly from July to October. The sweep's area and the share in the text are the same quantity.

## v3

**(a)** The sweep, the reckoning of the year and the comparison with the year before converge in one rolling turn: the white is no longer the calendar year so far but always the last twelve months, written in front of the hand and erased behind it, so the white is a whole year at every moment, its area is the share of the old ice held over that year, and what lies just ahead of the hand is the same day a year ago. **(b)** The seam of the loop, the place where a year does not close on itself and which v2 hid by wiping the dial every 1 January, becomes the measure: at the hand, today's edge meets the edge of a year ago, and the step between them, drawn as a short red or blue bar on the hand, is the year's imbalance between freezing and melting, read where it is made. **(c)** Struck: the reset to an empty dial each January (and with it the empty quarter-dials of every early-year moment), the highlighted loop of last year (it is now the white ahead of the hand) and the text 'white since 1 January', replaced by the twelve-month share and the change since this day last year.

v3 changes when the year begins: it begins wherever the hand is. In v2 the white was a calendar year, so for most of each turn the dial showed a slice and the reckoning was of a partial year, and the comparison with the past was spread over a nest of loops. Now the white is always a complete year, so its shape and area can be read at any instant, and the only edge that matters, the seam at the hand, carries the change from one year to the next.
