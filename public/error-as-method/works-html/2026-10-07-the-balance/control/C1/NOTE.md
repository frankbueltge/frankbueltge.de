## v0

The work plays the record as a breathing disc seen from above the pole: a white circle whose area always equals that day's sea-ice extent swells each winter and shrinks each summer, while a clock hand sweeps one year per turn (January at the top) and leaves the year's outline behind as a loop, so forty-eight loops pile up and the September side visibly caves in toward the centre, past a dotted ring marking the 1979 minimum. A side column fills in each year's smallest extent as the years go by, and a scrubber lets the viewer stop and travel. I chose an area-true polar form because the seasonal cycle is the loudest thing in the data and a circle makes it felt as breathing rather than read as a zigzag; drawing the long decline as accumulated loops makes it visible without a separate chart.
