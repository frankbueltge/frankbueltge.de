# One Day, Sixty-One Times

The record has one strong rhythm, and it repeats exactly 61 times in 1,464 values, so once every 24 values. The work cuts the record at each of those turns and lays the 61 pieces on top of one another. Below is the record as it came, re-drawn from the picture's own pixels, with a hairline at every cut. Above are the pieces stacked: each dot is placed by where it falls within its piece, and its height is taken relative to that day's own level. Colour runs from amber for the first days, through madder, to indigo for the last, so the stacked dots can be traced back to the strip. Read one way, the record is a long and fairly restless line. Stacked, one arch appears, and it is lopsided: it rises over about two-fifths of the cycle and falls over the remaining three-fifths. I chose this form because the arch is the main thing the picture holds. You cannot see it in any single day, because at this resolution each day is only about five pixels wide. It only shows once all 61 days are added together.
