# Sixty-one turns

The record rises and falls once roughly every 4.84 pixels of the source picture, which at 1,464 values across about 297 pixels is about twenty-four readings per turn, and 1,464 is exactly 61 × 24. So the work cuts the record at the low point of each turn and lays all the turns over one another in one wide panel, each printed in translucent ink that shifts from blue-black for the earliest to red-brown for the latest; where many turns agree, the ink builds up. What the original strings out as a jagged line becomes one shape seen sixty-one times: a slow low stretch, a climb, a narrower crest, a fall, with a pale hollow under the crest where only the flattest turns pass. Underneath, the same turns stand in order as sixty-one short spans, so their changing size stays readable, including the quiet run about a third of the way through where the swing almost stops. I chose folding because the repetition is the strongest thing in the material, and stacking shows both the rhythm and how it varies. The panel is drawn from the picture's own pixels, not from numbers taken out of it, because the picture is too small and too blurred by compression for the 1,464 separate values to be recovered.
