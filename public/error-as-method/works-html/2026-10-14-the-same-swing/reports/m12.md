I made a work called "1,464 into 40": a single still image in `/tmp/claude-0/s124/mk/m12/work/index.html`, with its title and a short explanation in `/tmp/claude-0/s124/mk/m12/work/NOTE.md`. The page draws with canvas and plain JavaScript and loads no other files. The only data in it is the source's 40 × 12 grid of grey values.

**What I did first:** I read the raw pixel values. The key fact is that the image is 40 pixels wide for 1,464 values, so each pixel column holds about 36.6 hours. Those hours sit on top of each other inside the column. You can't recover single readings, their order, or the daily cycle from this picture. What each column still shows is how high and how low the values reached during that day and a half, plus some JPEG blur.

**From file to form:** I worked out the dark range in each column and saved the 480 grey values (scripts and data are in `/tmp/claude-0/s124/mk/m12/scratch`). I chose not to invent a smooth curve. Instead the work shows the loss itself:
- **Bottom:** a ruler with one tick per hour and a longer tick at the start of each of the 61 days.
- **Middle:** from each tick, a hairline curves up and gathers with its neighbours into one of 40 narrow stems.
- **Top:** each stem feeds a tall column drawn from that column's 12 actual grey values. They are enlarged and blended only between neighbouring pixels in the same column.
- **Text:** three short captions in muted serif type.

**Worth knowing:**
- No browser was available here, so I haven't seen the final page rendered. I checked the JavaScript with node and drew a close version with PIL to check the layout; the real page draws the hairlines as curves rather than straight lines.
- The light grey patches above and below the dark bands come from the JPEG compression, not from the measurements. I left them in rather than clean up the source.
- A few sharp jumps in range fall exactly where JPEG's 8-pixel blocks meet (between columns 8 and 9, and 31 and 32). They may be partly compression, not change in what was measured.

**What I think was measured (a guess):** In every column the values spread over 4 to 10 of the 12 rows. So within any 36 hours the quantity swings by a large share of its whole two-month range. That points to a strong cycle shorter than a day and a half, most likely daily. The spread is wide for days 1–14, narrow around days 18–26, wide again around days 33–43, and middling after that. That suggests changing weather. My best guess is hourly air temperature (or something driven by sunlight, like relative humidity) at one place over two months. The wide-then-narrow pattern roughly every 30 days also fits a tide gauge with a monthly cycle. I can't tell the two apart from this file, and nothing in it shows the units, the place or the season.
