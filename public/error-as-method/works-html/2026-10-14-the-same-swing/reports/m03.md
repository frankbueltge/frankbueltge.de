I made "Hours Without Order". It's at /tmp/claude-0/s124/mk/m03/work/index.html, with the note at /tmp/claude-0/s124/mk/m03/work/NOTE.md. The page is a single SVG built by inline JavaScript. It loads no other file, library or network resource, it is a still image, and it is laid out for 1100 × 800.

**What I did first:** I read the JPEG and printed all 480 grey values (40 × 12) as numbers. That told me the most important fact: the 1,464 values can't be recovered from it. Each pixel column holds 36 or 37 consecutive hours (24 columns hold 37, 16 hold 36). A column only records how much dark ink fell at each of its 12 heights. The order of the hours inside a column is gone completely. JPEG blur and ringing also smear the edges of the dark band, and a fully black pixel looks the same whether one dot or ten landed on it.

**How I got to the form:** I decided the honest subject was the gap between what the record held and what the picture kept.
- **The dots:** every one of the 1,464 hours gets a dot again. Within each column the dots sit on one vertical line, spaced by the column's ink: dot k sits at the height where (k+0.5)/n of that column's ink lies above it. So each dot keeps roughly where its value was but none of when.
- **The background:** the original 40 × 12 pixels are drawn enlarged and faint, so the source stays visible.
- **The foot:** along the bottom runs one hairline for each hour in its true order, with a longer one every 24th. Small marks group them into the 40 columns that absorbed them.

The pixel array is embedded in the page and the layout is computed there. I checked the script in Node with a stub DOM: it draws exactly 1,464 dots. I also rendered a preview of the dot layer, but I had no browser, so the full page with its text has not been seen rendered.

**What the person who commissioned this should know:**
- The dot heights are an interpretation of ink density. They are not measured values.
- I treated anything lighter than grey level 225 as no ink, to suppress JPEG noise. A few stray dots at the band's lower edge, and possibly at its top edge, are compression ringing rather than data.
- The work deliberately shows no trend line, because the picture can't support one at hour level.
- My scripts and the pixel dump are in /tmp/claude-0/s124/mk/m03/scratch.

**What I think was measured (a guess, held loosely):** every column's dark band is wide, spanning about half or more of the full height. Each column covers about 1.5 days, so a wide band means a strong cycle of about a day or shorter. Over the 61 days, the band's overall height and its width both change slowly:
- In the first sixth or so the band is wide.
- Around columns 9–17 its top drops while its bottom rises, so it gets narrower.
- Around columns 24–27 the whole band moves upward.
- Near the end it is wide again.

That pattern looks like hourly air temperature: a daily swing, compressed on what could be overcast stretches, on top of weather lasting several days. I considered sea level, but the narrowing and widening don't repeat at the roughly 15-day spring–neap tide cycle (about 10 columns), and the band sometimes moves up or down as a whole. Nothing in the file shows place, season or units.
