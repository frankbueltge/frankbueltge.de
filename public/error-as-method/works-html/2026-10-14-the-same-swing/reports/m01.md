**What I did first:** I printed the grey level of every one of the 480 pixels. The file is not what the brief describes. There are no separate dots on white. Each of the 40 columns holds a dark run between about 4 and 10 pixels tall, and together the runs form one thick band with a white margin above and below. Forty values drawn as dots cannot cover over half the picture. So either many values share each pixel column and overlap, or the dots were drawn much larger than a pixel. Either way, individual values can't be recovered from this file.

**How I got to the form:** For each column I found where the darkness begins and ends, between whole pixels, at the halfway grey level. I also measured the band's height and centre. Those two edges are the only firm facts left. The page has:
- **Main field:** the picture redrawn as it is, one soft grey strip per column built from that column's own 12 grey values (all 480 are embedded in the page). Red ticks and lines mark each column's upper and lower edge.
- **Lower strip:** the height of the band alone, column by column.

It is a single still SVG with no outside resources, and it includes no invented data points.

**For the commissioner:** The material doesn't match its description, and the work is built around that gap rather than hiding it. If a dot-per-value picture exists at a larger size, it would support a different and more exact piece.

**What I think was measured:** a guess, not a finding. The band's height rises and falls fairly regularly:
- **Widest:** around columns 0–8, 18–20 and 28–31.
- **Narrowest:** around columns 12–15, 24 and 38–39.

That is a cycle of roughly 12 columns. Meanwhile the band's centre stays around rows 5–7. That pattern fits a quantity that swings quickly up and down, with the size of the swing waxing and waning more slowly. Tide height over several weeks is one example, where the twice-daily tides merge into a band and the spring and neap tides make it wider and narrower. Any similar oscillation with a beat would fit just as well. Nothing in the file pins down the units, the time span or the place.
