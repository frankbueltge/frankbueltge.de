The work is done. It's called "Sixty-one turns". `index.html` is one file of about 39 KB that loads nothing from outside, and `NOTE.md` has the title and the paragraph.

**What I did first with the file:** I looked at the picture, then dumped its pixels as text and tried to pick out each dot. That only found 551 dots out of 1,464. At about 4.9 values per pixel column, with roughly 2-pixel dots and JPEG blur, many dots overlap, so the individual values can't be recovered from this picture. I didn't make any up.

**How I got to the form:**
- I averaged the darkness in each pixel column and looked for repeats in the result. It repeats very strongly every 4.84 pixels. Nothing shows up at half that length or at about 34 pixels.
- The data runs across about 297 pixels, so 4.84 pixels is about 24 readings, and 1,464 is exactly 61 × 24.
- So I cut the record at the average low point of each turn, which gives 62 pieces: 60 whole turns plus a broken one at each end. I laid them all over one another in one wide panel.
- The panel is drawn on a canvas straight from the picture's own pixels, which are embedded in the page. Each turn is printed as translucent ink that shifts from blue-black for the earliest to red-brown for the latest, and the ink builds up where turns agree.
- The result is one shape seen many times: a long low stretch, a climb, a narrower crest and a fall, with a pale gap under the crest that only the flattest turns cross.
- Below the panel, each turn is shown in order as a vertical span from its low to its high, with a mark at its middle. That keeps the changing size of the swing readable.
- I couldn't open a browser here, so I checked the layout with a Node stand-in for the canvas. Its preview didn't draw the text, so the title and caption haven't been seen in place.

**What the person who commissioned this should know:**
- The panel stretches each turn of about 5 pixels across 920 pixels on screen, so it looks soft and streaked sideways. That is the real resolution of the material, not a style choice.
- The cut point (start offset −0.48 px) and the turn length (4.84 px) are my estimates from the picture. If the true values differ, the turns drift a little against each other across the record.
- The spans are measured with a darkness cutoff on blurred pixels, so they are approximate.

**My view of what was measured, which is an inference:** most likely air temperature near the ground, read once an hour for 61 days, about two months. It rests on these things in the file:
- The cycle comes to about 24 readings, which fits a solar day sampled hourly better than the 24.8-hour tidal day. There is almost nothing at 12 or 12.4 hours, so it isn't a mostly twice-daily tide.
- There's no sign of a 7-day pattern, which argues against human activity such as power use.
- The size of the daily swing changes a lot. There is a quiet run of several days about a third of the way in where the swing nearly disappears, and stretches of large swings elsewhere. That looks like clear weather alternating with overcast weather.
- The baseline wanders slowly over several days, like passing weather systems.
- The lows are not flat, so it is probably not sunlight, which drops to zero at night.

I can't say where or when it was measured.
