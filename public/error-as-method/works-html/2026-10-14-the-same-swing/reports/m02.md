I made "Sixty-one Swings". It splits the record into its 61 days and hangs each day from one pivot as an arc of a pendulum's sweep. It's done and checked in a 1100×800 render.

**What I did first.** I looked at the image, then printed it as text pixel by pixel. That showed the extent of the ink: about x = 2.6 to 296.4. So there are about 0.2 px per value, or about 5 values per pixel column. The dots are about 2 px across, so each column holds marks from around 10 neighbouring values.

**From the file to the form.**
- I tried rebuilding the sequence value by value with a path-tracing fit. It reached about 90% of the ink and missed some of the extreme highs.
- That trace was still useful. Its strongest period is 24.1 values. A clean stretch near x = 88–118, where the dots form a single wave, independently measures about 4.8 px per cycle, which is 24 values. And 1,464 = 61 × 24.
- The order of values inside a column can't be read from the picture, so I didn't build the work on the reconstruction. The work uses every dark pixel of the source as it is (3,773 of them, darkness kept as opacity):
  - each pixel's column gives its time, so its day and where in the day it falls;
  - its row gives an angle, with the record's middle row hanging straight down and higher values swinging right;
  - each day is one ring, the first nearest the pivot;
  - a pale arc behind each ring shows that day's full reach.
- The result looks like a hanging fan. Most days swing wide, and two spells pull in to narrow waists.

**Worth knowing.**
- The cut into days is my inference from the 24-value period. Where each day starts is set by the first value, not by any known midnight.
- A source pixel covers about 5 values, so marks near day boundaries can land in the next ring.
- The angle scale (1.7° per source pixel) and the "middle hangs down" convention are design choices, not data.
- The title and the one-line caption are drawn on the canvas.

**What I think was measured.** Probably an hourly reading of air or surface temperature over two months, at a dry or continental site with a large daily range. This is an inference, not something the file proves. It rests on four things:
1. The 24-value period, and 61 × 24 = 1,464, suggests hourly samples for 61 days.
2. The average day is lopsided: it rises in about 10 samples and falls over about 14. In the quiet spell the rise is sharp and the decline long. That is the shape of a sun-driven daily cycle.
3. The daily swing is huge on most days and nearly vanishes in two spells: roughly days 18–26 (x ≈ 85–130) and days 48–54 (x ≈ 235–262). In those spells the level also sits lower, with highs cut much more than lows were raised. That is what overcast or rainy days do to temperature.
4. The envelope does not follow the regular roughly 14-day beat a tide would have, which argues against tides.

Something else tied to the sun (humidity inverted, ozone, a sensor in the sun) can't be ruled out, and the units and scale are unknowable.
