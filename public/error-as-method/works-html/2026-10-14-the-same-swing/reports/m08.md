I made "Sixty-One Turns", a still image built from the record. The record repeats every 24 values almost exactly, so I wound it into a spiral of 61 turns, 24 values to a turn, starting at the centre.

**What I did first.** I looked at the picture, then dumped its pixels as text and measured them column by column. The dots are about 2 px across and overlap heavily: there are about 4.9 values per pixel column, and only 472 separate blobs are visible against the 1,464 values. So the picture can't simply be read back value by value. The averages of each column already showed a steady wave about 4.9 px long, which is about 24 values. Two stretches (around x 86–120 and 205–235) show the wave cleanly at a small amplitude.

**From file to form.**
- **Reading the values back:** I recovered an estimated value for each of the 1,464 points by drawing candidate dots and comparing them with the picture. Each value sits at a known x, from x 2.5 to 297.3. Its height was tuned until a rendered dot pattern matched the JPEG, with a mild penalty against sharp bends.
- **The period:** a frequency check on the recovered series peaks at exactly 24.0 values. The next strongest periods are all within about 0.6 of it (23.8 to 24.6).
- **The spiral:** larger values sit further out. I rotated it so the usual daily high points upward.
  - Days with a large swing pull their ring off-centre, always towards the same side.
  - Quiet stretches lie as nearly true circles, so the record reads like eccentric growth rings.
  - The 1,464 dots fall on 24 spokes, which keeps the count visible.
- **The page:** the original JPEG is embedded at small size as "the record as it came", with a short caption. I checked it in headless Chromium at 1100×800.
- **Standalone:** everything is inline (the data as a JS array, the JPEG as a data URI) and the page loads nothing external.

**What the person who commissioned this should know.**
- **The values are an estimate.** They are reconstructed from a 300×90 compressed picture. The overall shape of each 24-value cycle, its size and the slow drift should be roughly right, but individual values could be off by a few pixels of the original. Because of the smoothing penalty, peaks and troughs are probably a little rounded.
- **No units or start time.** Units are source pixels and the starting hour is unknown, so where the spiral starts is arbitrary. I rotated it to put the typical high at the top.
- **The spread is exaggerated.** The record's full range is drawn as 110 px of outward movement on top of about 3.8 px of growth per turn, so rings cross. That is deliberate.

**My view of what was measured.** Most likely hourly near-surface air temperature, or something closely driven by it, over 61 days (1,464 = 61 × 24). It rests on:
- **A 24-value period that is exact.** That is a daily cycle sampled hourly. It is not tidal: the tidal periods of 12.42 or 24.84 hours don't fit.
- **The size of the daily swing changes in spells of several days.** Days 17–23 are almost flat, at about 15% of the largest swings. That fits cloudy or rainy spells against clear ones.
- **The daily average wanders slowly over days,** as it does when the weather changes.
- **The rise looks a little shorter than the fall,** about 11 values against 13, in the direction typical of temperature. This is weak evidence, since it depends on the reconstruction.

Humidity, soil or shallow-water temperature, or some other sun-driven quantity can't be ruled out, and nothing in the file says where or when it was recorded.
