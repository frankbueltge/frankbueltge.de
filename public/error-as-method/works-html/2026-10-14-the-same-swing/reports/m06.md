I made a still work called "Somewhere in Here" in `/tmp/claude-0/s124/mk/m06/work/index.html`, with `NOTE.md` beside it. The page stands alone: the only material it carries is the picture's 40 × 12 grey values, written into the page as numbers. Its script passes a syntax check, but no browser was available, so I never rendered the page itself. I checked the layout with a Python render that copies the drawing step by step (`scratch/preview2.png`, 1100 × 800); its text is placeholder, so the page's real title and captions were never seen.

**What I did first.** I read every grey value of the picture and printed it as numbers, then looked at an enlarged copy. That settled the main fact: 1,464 hours drawn 40 pixels wide means each pixel column holds about 36.6 hours. The dots also blur together in the compression. No single value can be recovered. What survives in each column is how high and how low its dots reach (the spread), and how dark it is at each height.

**How I got to the form.** I made that loss the subject instead of drawing a smooth line the file can't support:
- **One thread per hour.** Each of the 1,464 hours is a thin vertical thread. It is drawn at every height its column's dots covered, with darkness taken straight from the picture's grey values.
- **No blending across columns.** I smooth only up and down. A first version also blended from column to column, and it hid the 36.6-hour blocks, which are the honest unit.
- **Layout.** The threads sit in four bands of 366 hours on warm paper. Ticks above each band mark the 40 columns. Ticks below mark the 61 midnights, with a longer tick every seventh day, so you can see that days and columns fall out of step.
- **Text on the page.** A small title and two lines of caption say this.

**What you should know.**
- The work shows nothing finer than about a day and a half. Any rhythm inside a day, such as a daily cycle, is invisible in the file and therefore in the work. That limit is the point of the piece.
- The page draws at a fixed 1100 × 800, the stated viewing size.
- The page is always light, even when the viewer's system is set to dark mode.

**My view of what was measured (tentative).** Each column's spread is wide: about 5–8 of the 12 rows. The midline barely moves, staying around rows 5.5–6.5 across the 61 days. So most of the variation happens inside each 36-hour window, which suggests a strong regular cycle shorter than a day and a half, sitting on a fairly steady level. The width of that swing changes over the period:
- wide in roughly days 0–14
- narrow around columns 12–15 (about days 18–23)
- wide again around columns 18–20 and 28–31

Two readings fit that:
- **Hourly air temperature in a season with a stable average,** where cloudy spells narrow the daily range.
- **A tide gauge,** where the swing widens and narrows over about two weeks. But the wide and narrow stretches don't repeat at the clean roughly 14.8-day rhythm I'd expect from tides.

The file can't tell these apart, and I wouldn't claim either.
