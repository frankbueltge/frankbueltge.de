I made **Forty Strings**: `/tmp/claude-0/s124/mk/m04/work/index.html`, a single self-contained canvas page at 1100×800, with `/tmp/claude-0/s124/mk/m04/work/NOTE.md` beside it. I couldn't look at the finished page because there's no browser here. I only checked that the script parses and worked out the layout by hand, so it needs one look at 1100×800 before you rely on it.

**What I did first.** I read the JPEG's 480 grey values and printed them as a grid. That showed the limit of the material straight away: each pixel column holds about 36.6 hourly values (1,464 ÷ 40), and each row is a twelfth of the value range. The individual dots and their order in time can't be recovered. What survives is, for each 1.5-day column, how far its values spread and where they bunch up. Rows 0 and 11 are almost empty, so the record nearly fills the frame.

**From the file to the form.** For each column I turned darkness into a distribution of heights (script in `/tmp/claude-0/s124/mk/m04/scratch/ext.py`). I then placed that column's true count of values, 36 or 37 for a total of exactly 1,464, at evenly spaced quantiles of that distribution. Each column becomes a vertical string of beads, with the beads bunched where the picture is dark. The enlarged source pixels sit faintly behind the strings, the original image appears at actual size in the corner, and a two-line caption says plainly what was lost. The bead count is exact. The bead heights are an estimate, and their order within a string means nothing.

**What you should know:**
- The bead heights depend on my choice of what counts as white background (225).
- JPEG compresses in 8-pixel blocks. Some of the changes in spread fall on block edges (columns 8/9, 16, 24, 32), so treat sharp steps there with caution.
- There is a small caption and title text inside the image. Remove them if the work must be wordless.

**My view of what was measured, held loosely.** Every column is a solid dark band, which means the quantity swings fully up and down within each 36-hour column. So it has a strong cycle shorter than a day and a half, most likely daily or twice daily. Over the weeks, the spread of each column grows and shrinks:
- wide around days 6–12, about 28–33 and about 41–48;
- narrow around days 18–24 and from about day 51 onward.

When the spread widens, the top rises and the bottom falls together, while the column average stays roughly level (0.4–0.55 of the frame). That even swelling and narrowing around a steady middle points to sea level and its tides, with large and small tidal ranges alternating over the month. The wide spells are irregularly spaced (about 21 and 15 days apart, not a steady ~15), so near-surface air temperature with daily swings shaped by the weather is a real alternative. I lean towards tide, but not confidently.
