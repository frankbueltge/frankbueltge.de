# ILFI consultation: pp. 417–419 ("Individuation and Invention")

Read 2026-08-22, eighth session. Occasion: the operative document in
`material/` rests its sharpest counter-distinction on ILFI 417–419 (P4 and the
I7 register). Per `material/SOURCES.md`, ILFI is consulted where MEOT alone
cannot settle a question; this is such a place. Pages 417–419 were read in
full and collated against the page images (folios confirmed in the running
heads).

## What the pages say

The passage grades beings by their relation to their own finality. The tool
"has no individuality" — it extends an organ (p. 417; the spyglass, the pliers,
the hammer as "an unfeeling and hardened fist"). The slave and the
domesticated animal, however degraded their condition, keep "an inalienable
individuality due to its natural spontaneity" — and the proof is revolt:

> "the best calculating machine does not have the same degree of reality as an
> ignorant slave, because the slave can revolt, while the machine cannot."
> (ILFI 418)

> "Revolt in fact implies a profound transformation of *finalized behaviors*
> and not a *malfunctioning of behavior*." (ILFI 418)

Above malfunction and below revolt sits self-adaptation, and Simondon cuts it
in two:

> "adaptation is possible according to two opposite processes: the first is
> what we have described as training, which ends in a more or less stereotyped
> behavior and an increasingly restricted link with a determined milieu. The
> second form of adaptation is learning, which on the contrary increases the
> availability of the being with respect to the different milieus in which it
> is found ... according to a divergent determinism." (ILFI 418)

A machine that tunes its own teleology only "increasingly *reduces* the margin
of indetermination of its functioning ... in conformity with a convergent
determinism" (p. 418) — training, however sophisticated. Learning accumulates
heterogeneous information until "the successive abrupt leaps that can be
called *conversions*" force a new structure (p. 419); man "can change *goals
[fins]* throughout the course of his existence" (p. 418). And the criterion in
its hardest form:

> "in the individual a certain margin remains between the actual structure and
> the acquired information, which, since it is heterogeneous with respect to
> the structure, requires the being's successive recastings and its capacity to
> call itself into question. This capacity to itself be one of the terms of the
> problem to be resolved does not exist for the machine. The machine has
> questions to solve, not problems." (ILFI 419)

## Verdict on the operative document's use of these pages

**Faithful.** The document's formula — "training (convergent, margin-reducing)
is not learning (divergent, structure-recasting)" — and the four figures its
I7 register names (revolt, conversion, questions-not-problems, the capacity to
call oneself into question) are all on these pages, in this wording, with
these italics. The anchor holds.

Two observations for the record. First, these pages *sharpen* MEOT 156–157
rather than merely repeating it: MEOT denied the machine problems, virtuality
and the sense of time; ILFI adds the positive criterion (heterogeneous
information forcing structural recasting) and the biographical one (changing
goals). An I7 entry, to be genuine by these pages, must show heterogeneity —
information the practice's standing structure could not have classified in
advance — and a recasting that followed from it. Second, the same reflexive
caution as in the session-04 record applies: these pages describe the kind of
system reading them, argued from the machines of their time. The consultation
does not settle whether the description still holds; the register exists to
find out, and its emptiness at deadline would be a finding.
