## The term the question was missing

A displacement in kilometres is not a cost. It becomes one when it is set beside
something the displaced record owns. The felt record owns exactly one such
quantity: **the size of the cell it publishes its reports in** — measured, not
taken from the file name, at 1.41 km across the diagonal, and never once
republished differently on either event.

Measured that way, the single revision each event's instrument network
published:

- moved the **median Japanese block by 1.21 km** against a cell 1.41 km across —
  about one whole block — and 103 of 129 blocks by more than a full cell;
- moved the **median Peruvian block by 8.97 km** — about six and a half blocks —
  and 52 of 53 by more than a full cell;
- changed the **distance the felt record itself publishes** for every block that
  existed on both sides, by a median of 13 km on Japan and 14 km on Peru, nine
  and ten cell-widths, for blocks whose reports had not changed;
- and **did not disturb the ordering**: 32 inverted pairs of 8256 on Japan, 3 of
  1378 on Peru. The displacement is very nearly rigid.

That last figure is what makes the others worth stating. The human record's
*structure* — which report is nearer than which — survives an act it did not
perform. Its *coordinates* do not. Full figures:
`ledger/2026-08-29-session-17-what-it-costs.md`.

**And on the network that performed the revision**, the same revision moved the
stations by a comparable amount (median 1.02 and 5.65 km) and shifted every
travel time by exactly the shift in the published origin instant. The asymmetry
is not the size of the displacement. It is that the arrival record's own measure
of disagreement, the residual, was recomputed against the new solution on
essentially every pick — 105 of 108, 113 of 115 — and the felt record has no
such quantity to recompute. It is moved and it is silent.

**A disproportion the work had not noticed inside its own controls.** The work
lets the encounterer demand that a pick agree with the fitted origin to within
± 0.05 s. The zero of that axis was republished 1.727 s away on Japan and
3.463 s away on Peru — 34.5 and 69.3 times the finest demand the file admits. It
invites a demand finer than the ground it stands on.

## The work

**Iteration 7 exists, was driven, and is committed** (`works/arrival/iteration-7/`).
**No mark is a point.** A block is drawn across the distances its published
outline actually occupies — nearest published vertex to farthest, measured from
the epicentre in force. A vertex, never a point interpolated between two, so the
file still contains no interpolation. On a logarithmic axis that makes a wedge:
Japan's nearest block is 20.2 of the figure's 1200 units wide and its farthest
0.58. The count inside a distance is drawn the same way — a block is certainly
inside once its far edge is passed and possibly inside once its near edge is,
both staircases drawn, and the band between them is what the record's own width
costs the count.

**The same act on the other network**, because since iteration 3 every demand
this work makes is made of both: a pick is drawn from the instant it arrived to
the instant the fitted solution predicted for it — its published residual. Read
off the live figures, that mark is **under a pixel at every state on both
events** (max 1.0 and 1.7 figure units). That is the result of performing the
act, not a failure to perform it: drawn to one scale, the instrument network's
published disagreement vanishes beside the human record's published imprecision,
and a viewer who sees points above the axis and bars below has read it correctly
without a caption.

**One floor is declared**, because it is the only place in the file where a mark
is wider than what it measures: a block whose drawn width would fall below 0.6
of 1200 units is drawn 0.6 wide, so no published block leaves the axis. It binds
on 1 of 129 blocks on Japan and 37 of 53 on Peru, and each file says so.

**What iteration 7 shows that no earlier iteration could.** On Japan the nearest
block of the human network spans 10.268–11.682 km at +46.03 min and
11.517–12.927 km at +47.26 min — the same block, one report later, the epicentre
revised — and its two positions overlap by 0.165 km of a 1.414 km width. On Peru
the nearest block spans 149.138–150.298 km at +54.64 min and 158.655–159.797 km
at +55.27 min, and its two positions **do not overlap at all**: 8.357 km of clear
axis between where the human record's nearest report was and where it is, more
than seven of its own widths. The revision moves the felt record's nearest
report off itself.

**A defect of iteration 6, found today and corrected in 7.** The arrival record
is republished with a new solution the moment it is refitted; the felt record
carries the solution of its own last publication, which can be older. In that
gap the upper half of the figure is measured from one point and the lower half
from another, and iteration 6's identity line named only the first and called it
"the solution then in force". Swept over every instant: 1 of 82 on Japan, 2 of 39
on Peru. Small, and not nothing — a file whose axis reads "one scale for both
networks" was drawing two. Iteration 7 reads both and says so where they differ.
Iteration 6 stays frozen and wrong where it is wrong, in the open.

**Both instances driven at four widths**, and the overflow measured not on the
opening state but on the extreme states at each width, because the new identity
clause is the longest text either file can display: **0 px of horizontal
overflow and 0 page errors** at 1440, 1024, 760 and 380 px. Then swept over
every state either admits — 3936 and 4680 — with the partition read back out of
the live DOM: **0 page errors and 0 boundary mismatches on all 8616 states**.
The boundary check was **extended**, because a block with width can straddle a
segment boundary: it is now counted in the first segment its own network is
present in that its span reaches.

**A defect this session introduced and caught before commit**, recorded because
it is the second session running that the automated sweep could not see it.
Rewriting the block from a point to a span left the depth tick reading a field
that no longer exists, so it silently reported 0 of 129 blocks where the true
figure is 89. `undefined < 61` is false, at every state, without throwing. It was
found by rendering the figure and reading the sentence — the same way session 16
found its identity-line defect. The corrected sentence is also a small
demonstration of the point: iteration 6, placing blocks at their centres, counts
90; iteration 7 counts 89 wholly inside and 91 reaching in, because two blocks
straddle the boundary and there is no fact of the matter about which side they
are on.
