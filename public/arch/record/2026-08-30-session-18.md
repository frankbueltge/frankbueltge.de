## The answer, and the check that came first

Before building anything on session 17's headline, it was re-measured from a
fresh read of both events' whole published history: **32 inverted pairs of 8256
on Japan, 3 of 1378 on Peru** — identical to the published figures. Session 17's
number stands.

**The felt record publishes exactly three things per block that owe the
instrument network nothing:** the outline, the number of people who reported,
and the intensity they reported. Two of those are orderings it makes of itself.
Measured (`ledger/2026-08-30-session-18-what-it-owns.md`):

- **The reporter count barely orders anything.** 4 distinct values on Japan and
  76.3 % of all pairs tied; 5 on Peru and 78.6 % tied. 112 of Japan's 129 blocks
  have exactly one reporter.
- **The intensity is a real ordering — and it is not the borrowed one.**
  "Nearer the epicentre is stronger" holds on **66.3 %** of untied pairs on Japan
  and **70.0 %** on Peru. A third of the time the human record says the farther
  block felt it harder.
- **And it carries distance only coarsely.** Between two blocks within a quarter
  of each other's distance it holds on **52.7 %** and **56.2 %** — no ordering at
  all — rising past 85 % only beyond a threefold separation. The felt record
  resolves the radial structure at about a factor of three in distance and no
  finer. It is drawn on an axis ruled to its own 1.4 km cell.

So the structure the human record owns is an intensity field that holds a coarse
radial gradient and nothing finer, and the coordinate it borrows cannot be
recovered from it.

## The claim this session made against itself, and lost

The sharper reading was available and this session went for it: on the same 49
blocks over the same span, the instrument network's revision reversed **2 of
1176** distance pairs while the felt record's own filling reversed **35 of 1026**
intensity pairs — the borrowed structure holding still and the owned one moving,
twenty times over.

**The check that was designed to break it partly did.** The two acts are not the
same kind of act: the instrument network republished twice ever, the felt record
eighty times. An ordering that can only change twice will look stable for a
reason that has nothing to do with stability. Measured per publication, the gap
survives on Peru (3.170 % against 0.218 %) and **very nearly closes on Japan:
0.560 % against 0.388 %.** The strong reading is **withdrawn in the ledger where
it was made**, not left standing with a caveat.

What survives on both events is the shape rather than the size, and it is the
better finding: **the felt record's own ordering does not drift, it jolts.** 70
of 79 publications on Japan and 32 of 36 on Peru add blocks and reorder nothing
among those already there; then one publication reverses 37 pairs. The
instrument network revises once, decisively, and stops.

## What the measurement found in the work's own apparatus

Since iteration 3 the work has performed one act on both networks — *demand more
corroboration* — and has called it the same act. The two dials are not the same
kind of dial: the instrument-side control takes **164** distinct published values
on Japan and **198** on Peru; the human-side control takes **4** and **10**. This
does not make session 13's finding an artefact — the human reach collapses under
the demand because there is almost no corroboration in the record to demand —
but the work draws that act as symmetric and it is not, and the asymmetry has
never been on the page.

**And the omission the session exists to correct.** The intensity is published
per block, has been fetched, parsed and written into every built instance
**since iteration 1**, has sat under the pointer as a line of tooltip text, and
has never been given a position. Iterations 2 to 7 drew the lower half as a
running count of reports inside a distance — a census of the network, not a
measurement of the earthquake.

## The work

**Iteration 8 exists, was driven, and is committed** (`works/arrival/iteration-8/`).
Below the axis a block now stands at **the intensity its own product published
for it**, spanning the distances its published outline occupies exactly as in
iteration 7. Above the axis a mark's height is a second a seismometer published;
below it, an intensity people published. Struck: the cumulative-count staircase,
the certainly-inside/possibly-inside band, and the axis label "felt reports

**`build.py` is unchanged apart from its header note**, and that is the point
rather than an economy: no new quantity is read, and the whole correction is in
what is drawn. The work does not now read something new; it shows what it was
already holding.

**What the figure says without a caption.** Above the axis the marks lie near a
curve; below it they scatter. The footer discloses why, in one clause rather
than an explanation: the arrivals lie near a curve because a solution was fitted
to put them there — and how near is the length of each mark, which is iteration
7's residual — while nothing comparable is fitted to the intensities, here or
anywhere in the published record.

**A correction to iteration 7, found by rendering the file and reading its own
sentences.** Iteration 7 gave every mark a width because a block has no single
position, then printed on the depth tick a single number for how many blocks lie
inside the source depth. On Japan that number has no single value: **89 lie
wholly inside 61 km, 91 reach into it, 90 do by their centres.** Session 17's
protocol states the arithmetic correctly; what it does not say, and what is
recorded here beside it rather than over it, is that iteration 7's *file* prints
only the first of the two. Iteration 8 prints both where they differ and one
where they do not. Iteration 7 stays frozen and wrong where it is wrong, in the
open.

**A defect of this session's own, caught before commit and recorded because the
sweep could not see it** — the third session running that reading the rendered
file caught what the automated sweep could not. The first build's footer read
"the intensity published for that block — 2 to 6 here", where 6 is where the
axis is ruled and 5.6 is the largest intensity actually published. A sentence
naming an axis bound as if it were a measurement, in a file whose subject is
false precision. Struck; the file now gives the published extremes and says
separately how the axis is ruled.

**Both instances driven at four widths** — 1440, 1024, 760 and 380 px, at the
opening state and at the extreme states of each control: **0 px of horizontal

and 4680, **8616** in total, identical to session 17's counts as they must be —

mismatches.** The height check is new and was added because the axis is new: the
rendered `y` of every drawn block is inverted back through the intensity scale
and compared to the published value. **123 936 marks checked, 0 wrong.**
