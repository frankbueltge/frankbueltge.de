## What this session did

**The zone-with-no-instrument, finally taken, and it is not a curiosity.** The
150 most recent M ≥ 5 events were walked; 58 carry both products and were
measured. **49 of the 58 have at least one felt block nearer the epicentre than
their nearest seismometer, and 32 of the 58 have their entire human record
inside that radius.** The median event of this population is perceived by
bodies, at every distance it is perceived at, in a place where no instrument
stands. Session 12 had read Peru as the exception that broke iteration 1's
picture; on 58 events there is no rule for it to be an exception to. Full
figures: `ledger/2026-08-26-session-15-two-origins.md` §1.

**On the way to that, a defect in this work's central claim.** The survey
compared each block's published distance with the distance to the block's own
published outline, as a check on the survey's own arithmetic. The check failed,
and it failed in a way that scales with depth. **The `dyfi` product publishes a
felt block's distance to the hypocentre; the `phase-data` product publishes a
pick's distance to the epicentre.** Iterations 2, 3 and 4 divide the first by
111.195 km per degree and draw it beside the second, and call the result "one
shared axis — the only coordinate they share". It was not shared.

The claim was checked on both halves rather than asserted on one. Against the
hypocentral prediction the published distance agrees to a median of half a
kilometre on a one-kilometre published cell; against the epicentral one it
disagrees by up to 121 km. And QuakeML's epicentral convention was verified
rather than quoted, by comparing 31 picks' published distances with the station
positions a public station service publishes: median difference 0.03°, on
events whose depth is 0.9°.

**What the defect cost, and where.** All of it in the near field, which is
where this work's finding lives. Japan's human network begins at **0.079°**,
not the 0.549° four iterations have drawn — seven times too far out — and 90 of
its 129 blocks lie nearer the epicentre than the source is deep. On the
logarithmic axis the work uses, the human network is more than four times wider
than it has ever been shown. The far edge, from which every headline figure was
taken, moves by under 2 %.

**A generalisation of sessions 13 and 14 overturned, not merely corrected.**
Both concluded that the demand for corroboration leaves the instrument reach
almost unmoved while the human reach collapses — session 14 sharpening it to
"the two networks shed their far fields at opposite ends of the demand". Both
measured only the outer edge, and the outer edge is the half of the instrument
network that does not move. Its **inner** edge moves further than anything else
in this work: Japan's goes 1.627° → 4.906° → 31.861° → 44.010° as the residual
demand tightens, a factor of 27, while it keeps 94 % of its outer reach. The
mechanism is in the published data: median |residual| is 2.01 s inside 5° and
0.35 s beyond 60°, so a tight residual demand takes the near field first. The
earlier figures are right about what they measured; they were offered as
statements about a network, and they are narrowed here, dated, beside them.

**Iteration 5 exists, was driven, and is committed** (`works/arrival/iteration-5/`).
Each block is placed at the epicentral distance computed from the outline its
own product publishes for it; its published distance travels with it and can be
read on it. On the corrected axis each network is an **interval**, and the
shared axis itself carries a strip saying who was present at each distance —
above the line the machines, below it the people, both filled where both stood,
outlined and empty where neither did. Nothing about that strip is coded: it
reuses the figure's own top-and-bottom convention, so it needs no legend.

**And it has something to show that no earlier iteration could.** The same act
— demand more corroboration — takes the human network's **far** edge (Japan's
2.414° is held entirely by single-person blocks; at two reporters per block the
human network ends at 0.498°) and the instrument network's **near** edge. The
two retreat from each other. Of the 48 threshold states Japan admits, **3 keep
a distance at which both networks are present**; of Peru's 120, 14. Past that
there is a band — 2.414° to 4.906° at ± 1.5 s — in which this earthquake stands
in no record at all, and the work names it *neither*. The zone-with-no-
instrument is no longer a curiosity the work displays; it is one band of a
partition the encounterer's own hand moves.

**Struck between iterations 4 and 5:** the mixed axis; the reach **ratio** the
work has carried as its headline since iteration 2; and the "reaches / of its
full reach" table, which measured only outer edges and thereby hid the finding.

**Two rendering defects found by reading the driven output, and fixed before
commit** — both of the kind this work exists to refuse, a picture saying
something its own data denies. The partition counted an item lying exactly on a
segment boundary in both segments, so the band named "instruments only"
reported a felt report inside it and "people only" a pick. And the table printed
teleseismic distances in kilometres — "10901 km = 98.0°" — an arc length nobody
reports. Details in the ledger.

**Both instances driven at four widths.** `us6000tmta.html` and
`us6000tm81.html`, at 1440, 1024, 760 and 380 px: no page errors and **0 px of
horizontal overflow** on either instance at any width, and no page error across
a sweep of 48 and 120 threshold states with each state's partition read back
from the live DOM.

**All four frozen predecessors confirmed to still run (I8).** Each was rebuilt
from a scratch copy outside the repository and each produced a complete
instance. No committed file was touched.

## The instruments, run against iteration 5

- **I2 (concretization balance) — live, and this is the strongest of the
  lineage's four schema claims.** (a) *Functions converging.* The shared axis
  now does three things in one structure: it is the common coordinate, it is
  the readout of who was present, and it is the boundary between the two
  half-planes that gives the readout its meaning without a legend. Three things
  that were separate in iteration 4 — an axis, a ratio, and a table of reaches
  and percentages — collapse into one strip whose top belongs to one network
  and whose bottom belongs to the other. (b) *Obstacle become a means.* The
  obstacle is that the two products measure from two different points. Iteration
  5 does not hide the correction: the axis is corrected, the published distance
  travels on each block, and the bracket under the strip says how many blocks
  lie nearer the epicentre than the source is deep — so the defect's own cause,
  the depth, becomes a mark on the axis it broke. (c) *What was struck:* the
  ratio, the reach table, and the mixed axis. **The doubt, stated:** unlike
  iteration 4, whose change was almost entirely in the template, this one
  changes what is measured before it is drawn — a felt block's position is a
  different quantity than it was, computed from a datum (the published outline)
  the work had never opened. That is a change of "which measurements are given"
  on any reading of I2's level discipline. The failure criterion — three
  successive iterations with no schema change — has not fired: 1→2 was the
  shared axis, 2→3 the symmetric controls, 3→4 the two populations shown, 4→5

   — the data decided it.
- **I4 (advantage probe) — the claim stays withdrawn, and this session gives
  the first evidence about the half of it that had none.** Could a person with
