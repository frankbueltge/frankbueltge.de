## What this session did

It took the lead session 10 left — the apparatus at the key-point, not the
feed — and went to the apparatus.

For one event (`us6000tmta`, M 5.8, Toride, Japan, origin
2026-08-22T17:00:39.472Z) the USGS detail record publishes both of the things
that matter here, and both were pulled and parsed: the `phase-data` product —
**123 instrument arrivals**, each with a station, an absolute instant to 10 ms,
an epicentral distance and a residual — and the `dyfi` product — **97 human
felt reports in 86 geocoded blocks**, each with a distance and an intensity.
Everything requested, everything returned, and the derived figures are in
`ledger/2026-08-23-session-11-two-perceptions.md`.

Two measurements came out of that, neither anticipated when the products were
requested:

1. **The travel-time curve bends, measurably.** Least-squares slope by
   quartile of the 123 stations falls monotonically: 11.19 → 7.82 → 6.69 →
   5.14 s/deg from 1.6° to 98.0°. Distant rays bottom deeper, in faster
   material, and gain on nearer ones.
2. **The two perceiving networks have reaches that differ by a factor of
   41.7.** Instruments registered the event out to 98.04°; human bodies
   reported being touched by it out to 261 km = 2.35°. Beyond that sliver,
   this earthquake happened to no one — only to the apparatus.

## What was decided

**The problem is found, and it is not the one session 10 handed on.** Session
10 asked which technical object at this key-point the practice could engage.
The answer is that there is no single object to engage: what sits at the
key-point is the *relation between two networks that perceive the same event*
and disagree about its extent by a factor of 42. That is a change in the form
of the problem, not progress on it, it issued from the practice's own contact
with the material and from nothing said in `CHANNEL.md`, and it is entered as
the first entry in `registers/i7-virtuality-register.md`, with its ground set
out so it can be disputed.

**A work candidate exists.** `works/arrival/` — a generator (`build.py`,
standard library only) and one built instance (`us6000tmta.html`): a
self-contained page, no libraries, no network request at encounter time, that
draws the 123 arrivals against distance, carries the felt reports in a
magnification wedge out of the 2.3% of the axis they occupy, and lets whoever
opens it **click any two seismometers** and be told the apparent speed across
the surface between them, with each measurement kept on screen beside the
last. Rendered and exercised before commit: near pair 8.75 km/s, far pair
23.40 km/s, no page errors. The rising number is the bend, found by the
encounterer's own two clicks rather than announced to them.

Two defects were found in that rendering and fixed before commit: the inset's
leader lines read as a fitted trend through the data, and its captions
collided with the curve. Recorded because the fix was a real change and
because P3 asks that failed gestures stay in the record.
