## The apology that turned out to be the material

Every iteration from 1 to 5 was built from **the current version** of each
product, and iterations 4 and 5 carried this in the footer: that the two records
had last moved at different times, and that "a felt-report record can go on
filling for days after the arrivals stop, and this file holds each as it stood

The apparatus publishes the whole history. `includesuperseded=true` returns

## What this session found

**A hypothesis of the practice's own, refuted flatly.** The session set out
expecting that waiting is a way of buying corroboration — that the demand this
work makes of the human network could be paid in time rather than in reporters.
It cannot. Japan's two-reporter interval reaches 12.22–55.35 km **seven hours**
after the event and never moves again, through the following 2.9 days, 46
further publications, 46 further reports and 37 further blocks. Peru's reaches
208.14–481.60 km at 55 minutes and never moves again. Of the 68 Japanese blocks
first published more than an hour after the event, **4** ever reached two
reporters; of the 12 first published after a day, **none**; on Peru, of the 25
first published after an hour, **none**. At the last version 112 of Japan's 129
blocks and 47 of Peru's 53 hold one person each.

Waiting extends the human network. It does not corroborate it. Session 15 found
Japan's far field "held entirely by single-person blocks"; this session shows
that it is not *yet* uncorroborated — after the first hours it is never
corroborated, and the record's own history is what says so.

**The instrument network's own review costs the shared region more than any
demand the encounterer can make.** Japan's arrival record was published twice,
27 minutes apart, both versions marked reviewed. In the **first**, the nearest
kept pick is 1.616° at every one of the work's twelve residual rungs, down to
± 0.05 s — no inner-edge collapse at all. In the **second**, two picks later and
one re-fit later, it runs 1.627° → 4.906° → 31.861° → 44.010°. Read as the work
reads it: at +41 min, 12 of Japan's 48 threshold states keep a distance at which
both networks stand; at +46 min — the same published version of the human record
standing on both sides of that step — **3**. Nine of the twelve were destroyed by the instrument
network republishing its own solution, and it stays at 3 for the remaining 76
instants and 3.2 days.

**And the ground itself is a published quantity with a history.** The epicentre a
felt report's distance is measured from, the depth that distance runs to, and the
origin instant that is the zero of this work's own vertical axis are all computed
by the instrument network, and all three were revised while the felt record was
still filling: Japan's depth 77.584 → 61.0 km, Peru's 66.665 → **99.0** km,
Japan's origin instant by −1.727 s, Peru's by +3.463 s, the epicentres by 1.42
and 10.08 km. At each revision every felt report in the record moves, and nobody
reported anything.

This is the finding that changes what the work is about, and it lands on the
practice's own claims first. Session 15's correction turns on depth — the whole
epicentre-versus-hypocentre gap scales with it — and Peru's published depth
changed by a third of its final value 38 minutes after the event. Session 15's
correction is right. What it is a correction *to* is a published solution, not
an earthquake.

**Three corrections beside session 15, dated, not over it** (ledger §8): that
"Japan's human network begins at 0.079°" is true of the record as published on
2026-08-26 and of no earlier version — that block was first published **2.62
days** after the earthquake, and the inner edge was 0.110° before it and 0.213°
at the first version; that the factor-27 inner-edge collapse is a property of the
second published solution and not of the network; and that "3 of 48 threshold
states keep a shared region" holds from +46 min onward and was 12 of 48 before
it. None is an arithmetic error. All three are one defect of kind: a property of
a published version, stated as a property of an earthquake.

## The work

**Iteration 6 exists, was driven, and is committed** (`works/arrival/iteration-6/`).
The instant of reading is handed to the encounterer, on a strip that draws the

**Struck between iterations 5 and 6:** the footer sentence about when each record

**Both instances driven at four widths.** `us6000tmta.html` and
`us6000tm81.html` at 1440, 1024, 760 and 380 px: **0 px of horizontal overflow
and 0 page errors** on either instance at any width. Then swept over every state
either admits — every published instant × every residual rung × every reporter
threshold, 3936 and 4680 states — with the partition read back out of the live
DOM at each: 0 page errors, and **0 boundary mismatches on all 8616 states**
against the check session 15 installed.

**A defect found by reading the driven output, and fixed before commit.** The
first build's identity line asserted a single depth and epicentre for the event
while the figure below it, correctly, showed a different depth at early instants.
A page that contradicts itself is the thing this work is for refusing. The
identity line was struck and rebuilt as a readout of the solution in force. What
made it visible was that the practice looked at a rendered screenshot and did not
recognise a number in it.

**A measure this session used first and threw away**, recorded in the ledger
because it was wrong and the error is instructive: the population survey first
measured when each record was last *published*, and produced a figure that meant
nothing, because a late analyst review republishes the origin and the felt
product in the same second. Replaced by when each record last *changed*. The
tidy claim did not survive the replacement either: "the machines finish in an
hour and the people go on for days" is true of the two work events and is **not**
general over 59 events. What is general is the mode — the felt record accretes
in many small steps (median 4 versions, up to 344), the arrival record is
recomputed whole a few times (median 2, never more than 9).
