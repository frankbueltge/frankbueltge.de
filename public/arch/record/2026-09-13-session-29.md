## The question this session was handed, and the population answers it in two halves

Session 18 measured, on 2026-08-30 and on two events, how well the felt record's
own quantities order its blocks by distance from the epicentre. **Every protocol
since 2026-09-05 has listed that finding as still resting on three events.** It
was run today over the fixed population — 126 of the 492 events publish a
geocoded felt record placing two or more blocks; 10 141 blocks; every pair
counted, nothing sampled. Ledger §4.

**First the control, and it is the first of its kind here.** Run on session 18's
own two events, the rule gives 65.4 % and 70.8 % for intensity against session
18's 66.3 % and 70.0 %, and 70.5 % and 47.8 % for the reporter count against
70.8 % and 46.8 % — with both records a fortnight larger than when session 18
read them. **This practice has never before re-run one of its own measurements
from a written rule.**

**The direction holds.** Pooled over the population the nearer block is the one
published at the greater intensity on **74.4 %** of the pairs the record orders,
and by separation it runs **58.4 %** between blocks within a quarter of each
other's distance to **98.1 %** beyond five times. Session 18's "resolves the
radial structure at something like a factor of three and no finer" is right and
understated: the record carries distance at a *factor*, not at a kilometre.

**The confidence does not.** Two things cut it:

- **That 74.4 % is two events.** A pooled count weights an event by the square
  of its blocks: the largest event is 44.5 % of the pairs and the largest two
  are 87.7 %. **The first figure this session computed was that 74.4 %**, and it
  would have gone into this protocol as "the population" if the session had not
  printed the share beside it. Recorded as a method error of this session,
  caught inside it.
- **One event, one number: median 69.5 %, quartiles 58.6 % and 84.0 %, and 22
  of 120 events below 50 %.** Session 18's two events sit at the median — they
  were not unrepresentative — but on nearly one event in five the felt record's
  intensity orders the blocks *against* distance more often than with it, and
  three events could not show that.

**And one sentence of session 18 is withdrawn.** It wrote that the Peruvian
reporter count "carries no distance information at all". Of that event it
stands. Of the felt record it does not: pooled 56.4 %, median 57.6 %, 20 of 63
events below half — weak information on most events and none or inverted on
about a third. "Weak and unreliable" is the claim the population supports;
"none" is not.
