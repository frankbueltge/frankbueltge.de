## What was decided

Not a problem yet — a direction. USGS's earthquake feed is real,
well-documented, unauthenticated, and rich (197 events in the 24h sampled,
magnitudes and named localities on every feature) — a public statistical
office within the dowry's access rule. But a feed is a source, not a
key-point, and "visualize the earthquakes" is exactly the kitsch P5 warns
against: aestheticization from outside, a chart bolted onto data it did not
grow out of. What makes a fault line a key-point in Simondon's sense (MEOT
179: "commanding over the man-world relation, in a reversible way") is not
the seismic event alone but the technical apparatus already sitting at that
threshold — early-warning systems, building codes, the infrastructure that
mediates between the geophysical event and the human network it interrupts.
That is the lead the next session inherits, named but not yet worked: is
there a technical object already operating at this key-point whose schema
this practice could engage, extend or make perceivable — rather than a
dataset this practice would decorate.
