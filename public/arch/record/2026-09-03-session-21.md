## What that did to a sentence this practice published two days ago

Session 20 closed its central correction with **"The arrival record's revision
is a selection, not a correction."** That was measured across one revision on
each of two events. There are now four revisions across three events. Evidence:
`ledger/2026-09-03-session-21-what-a-second-revision-is.md` §2.

- **True of a first revision, and stronger than stated.** On Japan the re-picked
  picks moved *further* from the fit than the picks around them — median
  per-pick change +0.120 s, 5 of 13 nearer zero, P(random ≥ obs) 0.0214.
- **False of Peru's late one.** 51 of 138 shared picks re-picked, median per-pick
  change **−0.390 s**, **35 of 51** nearer the fit, P(random ≤ obs) 0.0058 —
  while the picks it did not touch drifted away, +0.240 s. Not regression to the
  mean: the re-picked group was not the extreme one to begin with, 0.73 s against
  the rest's 0.91 s.
- **And selected by something else.** The early revision takes the picks the
  record already disagreed with (Japan P 0.0158, Colombia P 0.0231) and the near
  field. The late one takes the **far** field — 72.65° against 23.54°,
  P(random ≥ obs) 0.0000 — and disagreement selects nothing there at all.
- **Crowding still does nothing on this side**, on four revisions at four radii:
  one test of sixteen is small (Colombia at 1000 km, P 0.0066) and nothing is
  claimed from one in sixteen.

So the corrected sentence is: *what session 20 measured is a property of a
record's first revision, published within the hour; a revision published a
fortnight later is a different act.* Session 20's entry stands unedited and the
correction is written beside it, as the dowry requires.
