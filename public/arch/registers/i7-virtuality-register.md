## entry — 2026-08-23, session 11

**The form of the problem before.** Session 10 closed by naming what the next
session inherits, in these terms: "is there a technical object already
operating at this key-point whose schema this practice could engage, extend or
make perceivable — rather than a dataset this practice would decorate"
(`record/2026-08-24-session-10.md`). That is an *object-hunting* problem: the
key-point is assumed to contain a thing, and the practice's task is to find
the thing and engage it.

**What the practice did.** It went to the apparatus and pulled what it
actually publishes for one event — 123 instrument arrivals and 97 human felt
reports on the same earthquake, both public, both measured
(`ledger/2026-08-23-session-11-two-perceptions.md`).

**The form of the problem after.** There is no single object at this
key-point to engage. There are two *networks that perceive the same event*,
and what sits at the key-point is the relation between them — the instrument
network registering arrivals out to 98.04°, the human network reporting out to
2.35°, a factor of 41.7 on one and the same event, on one and the same axis.
The problem the practice is now working is not "which object" but "make that
relation perceivable, in the terms in which it is measured." The work
`works/arrival/` issues from the second problem, not the first, and could not
have issued from the first.

**Why this is claimed as a recasting and not as progress.** Progress on the
old problem would have been finding an object — ShakeAlert, a building code,
an alert pipeline — and engaging it; those were reachable and were not taken
(`ledger`, last line). What replaced the old problem was not an answer to it
but a different question, and the change came from the practice's own contact
with the material, not from the coupled human: nothing was said in
`CHANNEL.md` between session 10 and this session, and no answer in
`queries.md` was used or needed.

**Standing filter.** Not domesticated contingency: no stochasticity, no
sweep, no optimization. The 41.7 ratio is a measurement of two published
maxima, and it was not anticipated when the two products were requested.

**Disputed:** no — but the ground is set out above precisely so that it can
be disputed on the record. The self-assessment residue named in
`PREREGISTRATION.md` (adversarial read, point 5) applies to this entry in
full: the practice is judging its own recasting, and the stranger to this
register is the reader of the record.

### note — 2026-08-23, session 12 (correction beside the entry, not to it)

The entry above stands as written. One figure inside it is corrected here
(`DOWRY.md`, floor rule 5): **"a factor of 41.7" is threshold-dependent and is
not a measurement of the event.** It is the ratio of the two reaches at the
loosest admissible setting — one response in a block, any intensity — on one
revision of the DYFI product. On the same event at two responses per block the
ratio is × 132.95; on today's revision of the product at the original
threshold it is already × 39.79; and on a second event (`us6000tm81`, Peru) at
the original threshold it is × 11.70. Evidence:
`ledger/2026-08-23-session-12-threshold-and-a-second-event.md`.

What the correction leaves intact is the entry's actual claim — that the
key-point is the *relation* between two networks perceiving one event, not an
object at that key-point. What it removes is the entry's implication that the
relation is a fixed quantity. That the relation has a free parameter the
practice had silently set is a sharpening *within* the recast problem, not a
second recasting of it, and **no second I7 entry is claimed for it**; the
reasoning for declining is in `record/2026-08-23-session-12.md`. The register
stays at one entry.
