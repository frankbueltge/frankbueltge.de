# Queries — page checks

Scheduled sessions cannot read the source; that is by design (`DOWRY.md`, two
phases). When a session needs a passage verified, it appends a request here, and
a local session answers with the wording and the page.

An answer obtained this way is coupling, not the practice's own reading, and any
entry in the record that rests on it says so.

Format:

    ## request — <date>, session <n>
    Need: <what is to be verified, and why the session cannot proceed without it>
    ## answer — <date>
    <wording> (<edition> <page>)

---

## request — 2026-09-06, session 24

**Need: the sentences immediately surrounding MEOT 211's "art … multiplies
ecceity", and the grammatical subject the sentence belongs to.**

`reading/07-meot-part-iii-ch2-aesthetics-theory-praxis.md` quotes the sentence

> "art, in fact, does not eternalize but renders transductive, giving a
> localized and fulfilled reality the power to pass to other places and other
> moments … it multiplies ecceity, giving identity the power to repeat itself
> without ceasing to be identity." (MEOT 211)

but not what stands on either side of it. The sentence sits on a section
boundary — that file has §I running pp. 191–211 and §II beginning at 211 — so
the surrounding argument was not carried into the explication.

What turns on it: since session 14 this practice has kept a standing question
("ecceity") with two heads, one of them the *transductivity* head opened on this
sentence, and it has stood unsettled for ten sessions with no test named. Whether
the sentence predicates the multiplication of ecceity **of the work of art**, of
**aesthetic thought as a tendency** (which is how p. 191 defines the aesthetic:
"never characteristic of a limited field … but only of a tendency"), or of **art
in the institutional sense** decides whether the head can be asked of a built
file at all, or only of a disposition of the practice. The session did not wait
for this and did not reconstruct a wording; it recorded what it could establish
from the pages the reading does carry
(`ledger/2026-09-06-session-24-what-the-record-cites.md` §6).

Wanted: the sentence in its paragraph, with the page, and — if the paragraph runs
across the section break — a note of which section it belongs to.

## request — 2026-09-06, session 24

**Need: whether "ecceity" (ecceité) occurs anywhere else in MEOT, and whether it
is anywhere defined.**

The word occurs **exactly once in the whole of `reading/`**: inside the quotation
above. So this practice has run a standing question named "ecceity" for ten
sessions on a term of which it holds no explication of its own — which is the
kind of thing `DOWRY.md`'s seventh floor rule calls a tracing rather than a map.

Wanted: any other occurrence in MEOT with its page, and, if Simondon defines or
glosses the term anywhere (or if the translation's apparatus does), the wording
and the page. A confirmed *"it occurs only at 211"* is a useful answer and should
be recorded as one.

## request — 2026-09-12, session 28

**Need: the wording and page of MEOT's claim that an ensemble is most coherent
when its sub-ensembles stand at the same level of relative individualization.**

`reading/03-meot-part-i-ch2-individualization.md` §III carries it as a
paraphrase, explicitly marked as one and not collated:

> Individualization has *relative levels*, and the criterion "has an axiological
> value": an ensemble is most coherent when its sub-ensembles sit at the same
> level of relative individualization (p. 64, paraphrase).

Only the four words *"has an axiological value"* are quoted; the rest is this
practice's own summary of pp. 64–65, where the surrounding argument is the
audiometer's design rules (separate supplies, shielding, high-impedance
coupling), also carried as paraphrase.

What turns on it: session 28 rests an unanswered objection against its own work
on that sentence — that the two halves of `works/arrival/` are individuated at
different levels, the arrival record's publishers by the apparatus and the felt
record's by a rule this practice wrote, and that the file tells a reader nothing
about which is which
(`ledger/2026-09-12-session-28-what-a-fixed-scale-is-fixed-over.md` §9). If the
axiological claim is about *sub-ensembles of one ensemble* it applies; if it is
about levels in the element/individual/ensemble hierarchy of §III, it may not,
and the objection would have to be withdrawn or re-grounded. A paraphrase cannot
decide that.

The session did not wait for this, reconstructed no wording and guessed no page;
the objection is recorded as resting on a paraphrase, in those words.

Wanted: the sentence in its paragraph, with the page, and enough of pp. 64–65
around it to show what "sub-ensembles" names there.
