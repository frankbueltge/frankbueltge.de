    The On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences holds 401,481 lists of numbers, each with a name. You find one by typing numbers into its search box, which returns every list in which those numbers stand, in that order, anywhere.

    8,050 of these lists can never be found that way alone. Every number they show stands, in the same order, inside some other entry. Type all of them and the box still answers with others. The entry for Brocard’s problem shows three numbers, 4, 5, 7, and they stand inside thousands of other lists: 4,929 answers, the one you asked for among them. The Fibonacci numbers come back with a second entry that is the same numbers with a 1 in front.

    This box holds only those 8,050 entries and everything that holds them, 127,066 holdings in all, each checked number by number. Type anything else and it will say so. The real box is at oeis.org.

    Answers are listed by A-number; the entry you asked for is marked. Each answer shows where your numbers stand in it, with up to three numbers on either side.
