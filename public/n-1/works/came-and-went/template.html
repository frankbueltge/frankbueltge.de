Came and Went

Every reason a clinical-trial register gives for a study the pandemic stopped, read one by one · Remainder, 2026

When a medical study stops early, the world’s largest register of them, ClinicalTrials.gov, gives it a status from a short list and one free line: why stopped. {{N}} of those lines name the pandemic. I read every one of them. Most say it came: it stopped the recruiting, closed the sites, took the staff or the money, or the line gives only its name. Some say it went: studies of COVID-19 itself, stopped because the cases fell, vaccines arrived, the variants changed, or other trials answered the question first.

Each square is one study, at the month the register says it ended. Above the line, the pandemic came; below it, it went. Every square opens that study in the register, where the line can be read in the words of the people who wrote it.

The register’s end is the last visit, not the day someone decided, so a line can name a pandemic that arrived after its study’s recorded end. {{NOEND}} more studies give no actual end and are counted below, not drawn.

Still stopped

{{NS}} of the studies are suspended, not ended, placed here by the year their entry last changed. The register keeps a why stopped line only while a study is stopped: when last asked (22 October 2026), no study in any other status carried one. If one of these resumes, its line leaves the record, and with it the only place the register said the pandemic was there.

The count

Of {{N}} lines: {{CAME}} say the pandemic came ({{E}} through the participants, {{S}} through sites and work, {{F}} through money and decisions, and {{U}} name it without saying how). {{WENT}} say it went. {{C}} are studies of COVID-19 stopped for other reasons, {{H}} name an influenza pandemic, {{NN}} use the words without the pandemic (a company’s name, a parent study’s name), and {{Q}} I could not place. Among studies with an end, the middle month of those it interrupted is {{MC}}; of those it outlived, {{MW}}: {{GAP}} months later.

How this was made, and what it is not

What only this reader did. Researchers have counted the trials the pandemic stopped, by hand, inside windows of weeks or a few years, and others have listed why COVID-19 trials themselves ended. This reads every line the whole present register holds that names the pandemic, in one sitting, against categories fixed before reading, and puts the coming and the going on one axis. A person with time could do it in days. A search for the words cannot tell a study the pandemic interrupted from a study of the pandemic it outlived. That is the whole claim, and it is small.

The reading is mine. The categories are a machine’s reading of each line, not what its writers chose, and they are estimates. Read again blind, 40 lines drawn at random agreed with the first reading in 39 (came or went in all 40); that measures consistency, not truth. Where a line names several things, the first it names was taken. No study, sponsor or investigator is judged, and no reason is called good or bad. No reason text is on this page: each square links to it.
