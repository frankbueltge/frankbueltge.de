# Came and Went — the work

*Declared 2026-10-24 (Berlin), night 86 (record 112), project 16 (Why stopped), session 3 of three to five, by the rule registered in
`projects/why-stopped/SESSION3.md` (committed f131a5b before any record was fetched). The page is `index.html` in this directory, built by
`build.py` from one committed file (`projects/why-stopped/session3.json`). The study it grew from, `projects/why-stopped/`, stands
unchanged as history.*

## Problem

Found in the material, not chosen (`projects/why-stopped/AUDIT.md`, sessions 1-3): a register with a closed list of states keeps one free
line for why a study stopped, and an event it has no field for, the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic, entered that line and nowhere else. Read for how
much (session 1) and for its own dates (session 2), the line held more than the list: the decision, and its own time of writing. **The
question the work asks: what did the pandemic do, in that line, to the studies it stopped, and does the line tell apart the studies it
interrupted when it came from the studies of it that stopped because it went?**

## Form

One page. Every stopped study whose reason names the pandemic and whose end the register gives is one square at its end month (2,013;
earlier ends in one column): above the axis the line says the pandemic came (by what it did to participants, sites and work, money and
decisions, or without saying how), below it says it went, with the rest beneath in grey. Each square links to the study's page in the
register, where the line can be read in its writers' words. A strip holds the 198 suspended studies by the year their entry last
changed, because their line lasts only as long as the stop. Below, the count and a table by year, readable without the picture. Nothing is
fetched while it is looked at except a register page a visitor opens.

## Neighbours and daylight

Searched in the Atlas of Data Art (sha256 `4765ce73…`, unchanged since session 1, where no entry worked a clinical-trial register;
tonight's grep script failed to parse the feed and was not repaired, so session 1's search stands) and outside
(`projects/why-stopped/NEIGHBOURS.md`).
- **Counts of trials the pandemic stopped** (TranspariMED 2020; Asaad et al., *Annals of Surgery* 2020; a medRxiv cohort, 2026): windows
  of weeks to three years, partly by hand, the record as it stood then. **They read the coming.**
- **Analyses of why COVID-19 trials ended** (GlobalData via Clinical Trials Arena; a PLOS One study of 4,441 COVID-19 trials). **They read
  the going.**
- **This work reads both, line by line, from the whole present register, on one axis,** and leaves the words in the register.

## The advantage — stated, and small

Registered so it could fail: one reader reads every flagged line of the present register in one sitting, against nine categories fixed
before reading, and checks itself blind. It did not fail: all 2,643 lines read (`readings3.json`), 39 of 40 on re-reading (`result3.json`).
What it does not show: that a person with time could not do the same; the page says so. The rule alone cannot tell came from went.

## Reception

The first paragraph asks the visitor to know only that medical studies can stop early; the axis and the colours carry the rest, and the
squares open the register itself. On a phone the field is a texture and the table carries the count. Untested on a stranger.

## Rights

ClinicalTrials.gov's terms (attribute, display the processing date, state every modification, keep current) are met on the page except the
last, which a dated reading cannot meet; the page says it is dated. Reason texts may be third parties' and none is on the page or in the
repository: only NCT numbers, text hashes, months and my readings. No person is named, no sponsor ranked, no reason judged. Affected
publics: the people enrolled in these studies, and those who would have been, are in no datum, and the page does not claim to show them.
