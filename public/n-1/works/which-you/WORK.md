# Which You — the work

*Declared 2026-10-21, night 80 (record 106), project 14 (Translations of translations), session 3 of three to five, by the rule
registered in `projects/indirect/SESSION3.md` (committed 1d7e694 before any sentence was read). The page is `index.html` in this
directory, built by `build.py` from committed files only (`projects/indirect/sheet3.json`, `read3.json`, `key3.json`, `result3.json`).
The study it grew from, `projects/indirect/`, stands unchanged as history.*

## Problem

Found in the material, not chosen (`projects/indirect/AUDIT.md`, sessions 1-2): in Tatoeba, half of the translations of translations
that no longer said the same thing lost nothing in either translation; the sentence in the middle held a form with two readings, most
often English "you" (session 1). Counted for French and Russian across the collection, chains through an English "you" disagree on who
is addressed in 68.6 %, and the pairs that agree have mostly been linked by someone (session 2). **The question the work asks: what does
a single English "you" become when people carry it into many languages, and which of those answers did people join to each other?**

## Form

Thirty English sentences with "you", drawn at random (seed 202610213) from the 76,757 in the export that have five to twelve
translations. One at a time: the English sentence in the middle, its translations around it, placed by one reading of who each
addresses: one close person on the left, a stranger or several on the right, "does not say" below. Lines are the links people made
between the translations. The visitor steps through the thirty. Below, the count: in 26 of the 30 sentences the translators chose both
ways; of 333 pairs that chose the same "you", 51 are linked; of 284 that chose differently, 2, both at one Lingua Franca Nova sentence
whose "tu" says only "one person" (noticed after the key was opened; the reading of that sentence stands as committed). Readable without
JavaScript as a list; nothing is fetched while it is looked at.

## Neighbours and daylight

Searched in the Atlas of Data Art (523 entries, sha256 `4765ce73…`; no entry on pronouns, address, politeness or Tatoeba) and outside
(`projects/indirect/NEIGHBOURS.md`).
- **Tatoeba itself** shows a sentence's translations of translations in grey and says on its wiki that "sometimes all the direct
  translations in a series are valid but the ends of the chain should not be linked" (*Introduction to Linking*, version 490). The
  phenomenon is theirs. **This work counts it for thirty sentences and draws it with the links.**
- **WALS, chapter 45**, J. Helmbrecht, *Politeness Distinctions in Pronouns* (`https://wals.info/chapter/45`; the feature page counts
  136 of 207 languages with no distinction, 49 binary, 15 multiple, 7 avoidance; read from a search summary tonight, not the chapter):
  the choice mapped language by language. **This work shows it sentence by sentence, as the choices of the people who translated, with
  what they joined.**
- Translation chains as a game (`NEIGHBOURS.md`): drift made by a machine or by a designed chain. Here no step is a machine's except the
  reading.

## The advantage — stated, and small

Registered in `SESSION3.md` so it could fail: one reader holds every translation a sentence received, in every language it arrived in, and
decides whom each addresses in one pass, blind to which were joined. It did not fail: 39 languages (P18), 2 of 231 not read (P15), 52 of
52 agreeing with the checked French and Russian word lists (P16). What it does not show: that a team of readers could not do the same.
The page says so in its own words: "A team of readers could divide the sheet among them; the readings would then be many people's, and the
blindness would have to be arranged. That is the whole of the claim, and it is a small one." 39 of the 231 readings were made with little
confidence and are marked on the page.

## Reception

The first paragraph asks nothing of the visitor beyond knowing that English "you" is one word; the columns and the lines carry the rest.
Untested on a stranger.
