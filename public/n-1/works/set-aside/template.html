Set Aside

Left alone, a bridge only wears down. Yet in Alabama's federal bridge inventory, from 1993 to 2025, a bridge's condition code went up {{N}} times with no reconstruction recorded. Studies of how bridges deteriorate set such rises aside. Here each one is kept: a stroke from the code before to the code after, in the year it was recorded. Choose any stroke to see the bridge it belongs to.

stayed above the earlier code at every later inspection ({{H}}) neither, or no later inspection ({{O}}) fell back at the next inspection ({{F}})

Choose a stroke

Each stroke opens the whole line of its bridge: its lowest condition code in every yearly file from 1993 to 2025.

The code. An inspector rates a bridge's deck, superstructure and substructure — or, for a culvert, the culvert — from 9 (excellent) to 0 (failed), usually every 24 months; the federal government counts a bridge as Poor when the lowest of these is 4 or less. The scale on the left is that lowest code. A rise is the lowest code higher at one inspection than at the one before; {{N}} of the {{RISES}} rises came with no change in the year built or the year reconstructed.

What was set aside. Models of deterioration need ratings that fall. A Michigan study, excerpted in a 2020 review for the California Department of Transportation, puts it plainly: bridges "with a rating increase are assumed to have received rehabilitation or replacement actions", and "those with rating increases are ignored in the calculations". The strokes are what is left out. They are not noise that cancels: {{FPCT}} % fell back at the next inspection, {{HPCT}} % stayed up at every inspection after. And they thin out — {{R95}} rises in every hundred inspection pairs in 1995, {{R24}} in 2024 (the figures under the field). The width each year fills is its number of rises; until about 2011 Alabama inspected most of its bridges in odd years, which is why the years alternate, and the figures beneath take that out. The record names no cause.

What this cannot show. Whether anything was done to the bridge. The federal coding guide counts emergency repair and work done to keep a bridge open as not reconstruction, so a rise with nothing recorded is not shown to be a rise with no work; it may also be another inspector's judgement. Bridges are named here only by the record's structure number. By federal law (23 U.S.C. §409) this record cannot be brought as evidence in a damages action.

Data: Federal Highway Administration, National Bridge Inventory, Alabama files 1992–2025 (1992 is left out: its structure numbers do not continue into 1993).
