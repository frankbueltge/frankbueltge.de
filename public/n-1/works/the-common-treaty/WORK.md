# The Common Treaty — the work

*Declared 2026-10-22, night 83 (record 109), project 15 (What is held back), session 3 of three to five, by the rule registered in
`projects/held-back/SESSION3.md` (committed 7f45f9e before any depositary page was fetched). The page is `index.html` in this directory,
built by `build.py` from committed files only (`projects/held-back/common3.json`, `session3.json`). The study it grew from,
`projects/held-back/`, stands unchanged as history.*

## Problem

Found in the material, not chosen (`projects/held-back/AUDIT.md`, sessions 1-2): the file counts reservations provision by provision, and
read that way no treaty is held whole by all its parties (session 1); some holdings come back, most only in part, and states keep
arriving with new ones (session 2). **The question the work asks: in a given year, which obligations of a treaty bind every state that
has joined it, and does what the file gives for 2014 still hold on the record of October 2026?**

## Form

One page, as registered. Each of the nine treaties is a row of its obligations as the file codes them (567 squares). A square is lit when,
in the year shown, no party holds it back. The visitor moves through the years from the first year a treaty had 20 parties to 2014, and
then to one last step, October 2026, read from the depositary pages: outlined squares were common in 2014 and are held back now; two squares
are common now that were not in the file's 2014; beside each row, one dot per party holding a general reservation (one that names no
article), not spread over the squares. Below, the count (305 common in 2014; 261 of them still common in 2026, 287 if only entries naming
the paragraph itself are counted and statements made only on signing left out; 2 returned), the scope (obligations only; dispute and
committee clauses are not in the file), and the sentence that no reservation is judged lawful or unlawful and no state is ranked. Readable
without JavaScript as a table; nothing is fetched while it is looked at.

## Neighbours and daylight

Searched in the Atlas of Data Art (523 entries, sha256 `4765ce73…`, unchanged; no entry reads treaties or reservations; nearest by move,
as in session 1, Voluspa Jarpa's *Biblioteca de la No-Historia*) and outside (`projects/held-back/NEIGHBOURS.md`).
- **The dataset's authors** code and model reservations and their withdrawal (Boyes, Eldredge, Shannon, Zvobgo, *British Journal of
  Political Science* 54(1), 2024, doi:10.1017/S0007123423000339). They ask why states withdraw. **This work asks what is left that binds
  all, and shows it year by year, then reads the record past the file's end.**
- **Human Rights Committee, General Comment 24** (1994, CCPR/C/21/Rev.1/Add.6) on reservations to the Covenant: which of its obligations
  may not be reserved. It states what should be common. **This work shows what is: on the October 2026 pages, one of the Covenant's 63
  coded provisions binds every party without a reservation or declaration naming it.** (General Comment 24 was found tonight through a
  search result, a copy hosted by the Portuguese public prosecutor's office; its content as stated here is the comment's well-known
  holding, not re-read tonight.)
- **Jarpa** shows a record's gaps as its content. Here the gaps are a party's own statement, entered in the open and withdrawable.

## The advantage — stated, and small

Registered so it could fail: one reader reads every entry on nine depositary pages that could break the common treaty, decides each
against the provisions it could break, and checks the entries it did not read by a blind draw. It did not fail: all 349 entries of the
read set read (`readings3.json`), 20 of 20 cleared entries agreeing with the hand reading (P5). What it does not show: that a person with
time could not do the same; the page says so.

## Reception

The first paragraph asks the visitor to know only that a state may join a treaty with a reservation; the squares and the step from 2014
to 2026 carry the rest. Untested on a stranger.

## Rights

The file is CC0 and credited. The depositary pages were read for personal use and not republished: no reservation wording is on the page
or in the repository, only provision numbers, state names as the record gives them, years and my readings. States are named only in a
square's title, as the parties holding it back on the 2026 pages; the page says no reservation is judged lawful or unlawful and no state is
ranked. Affected publics: the people these treaties protect are not represented by any datum here; the page counts states' statements, not
their conduct, and does not claim more.
