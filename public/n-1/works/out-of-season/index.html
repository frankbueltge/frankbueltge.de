Out of Season

In 2025, 206 storm overflows in England discharged sewage for longer than half of the overflows whose count reached the investigation trigger of 30 spills a year — and each of them was counted fewer than 30. Each small calendar below is one of them, laid out as a year planner: a row for each month, a square for each day.

ink: hours of discharge that day (full = 24 h) the bathing season, 15 May – 30 September a day on which the official rule began counting one spill

All 206 together

66,219 hours of discharge; 1,982 of them in the bathing season (3.0 %), which is 38 % of the year. Choose any calendar below to open it here.

The count. England's Environment Agency counts storm-overflow spills by a 12/24 rule: from the first discharge, any discharge in the first 12 hours is one spill, and each following 24-hour block in which the overflow discharges is one more. An overflow counted 30 times or more in a year (or 20 a year on average over two) meets the threshold for an investigation; the annual return enters every overflow here as "High spill threshold not met" for 2025. The red dots are the counted spills, placed on the days they began; under each calendar the number of dots is the number the overflow was given. It does not say how long the overflow ran.

The season. The Bathing Water Regulations 2013 set the bathing season in England from 15 May to the end of 30 September. Of these overflows' 66,219 hours, 1,982 fall inside it; 83 of them did not discharge in it at all. Their hours fall in winter — in a year the Agency called "significantly drier than 2024 across large parts of England". The count and the season are two calendars over the same year; neither knows the other, and these discharges pass under both.

What this cannot show. Whether the discharge reached a beach, a shellfish bed or a swimmer, how far downstream, or what it held. The water named under each calendar is the receiving water as the company's annual return gives it; 19 of these overflows carry a bathing-water monitoring requirement and 8 a shellfish-water one. Calendars are in the order of the record's identifiers; nothing here ranks a company.
