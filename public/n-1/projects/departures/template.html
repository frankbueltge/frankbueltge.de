Where each game leaves

In January 2013, {{N}} rated games of chess were played on Lichess, a free, non-profit chess server that publishes every game it hosts (database.lichess.org, CC0). Every game starts from the same position, and for its first moves almost every game is a copy of others. This page lays the whole month over itself and asks, for each game: at which move does it stop being anyone else's game? From that move on, nobody else played it that month.

Half of the games had left all the others by the ninth half-move (White's fifth move). Every game that left at all had left by the 37th. {{NEVER}} games ({{NEVERPCT}} %) never left: they were the whole beginning of another game, or equal to one.

The month as one figure

Every game of the month is a line, from left to right, one step per half-move. The games are stacked in the order of their moves, as a dictionary orders words, so games that began alike lie side by side. A line is blue while some other game of the month has played exactly the same moves so far, and amber from the move at which no one else did. It fades where games have ended.

The blue edge is the month's shore of copying. Of the {{PLIES}} half-moves played that January, {{SHAREPCT}} % were moves that some other game had also played at the same point; the rest, {{OWNPCT}} %, belonged to one game alone. The edge reaches furthest where many games walked a long book line together. The four rows that stay blue longest begin with Ruy Lopez moves (Lichess's tag most often there: the Marshall Attack; the row's middle game is still shared until ply {{LATEST}}), the Italian Game's Greco Gambit, the Two Knights Defense (most often tagged the Fried Liver Attack) and the Najdorf Sicilian.

Whether a stranger reads this figure as meant is untested. Rows are bins of adjacent games, so a row's colour at each ply is a mixture; no single game is drawn on its own.

Leaving the moves is not leaving the board

A game can leave all the others' move orders and still reach a position another game reached by a different order of moves (a transposition). In {{LATER}} % of the games that left, the position stayed shared for longer than the moves did. Counted by position, the middle of the month leaves at ply {{PMED}}, not {{MED}}.

How this was made

The file: lichess_db_standard_rated_2013-01.pgn.zst, checked against Lichess's published checksum (fetch_log.json). Moves are compared as written; {{ODD}} moves in the file are written with more disambiguation than usual (for example "Rd1d2"), and comparing them as board moves instead changes no number on this page (explore1.json). Positions are compared by piece placement, side to move, castling rights and a capturable en-passant square, hashed to 64 bits (chance of a false match: about one in 250,000, an estimate).

The players. No account name, game link, date or time appears here or in the committed files. A sequence of moves is shown only when two or more games played it. No move is called good or bad.

Scripts and numbers: lib.py (the definitions, tried on invented games in test_lib.py), session1.py → session1.json, sample.json, explore1b.json; the figure: session2.py → session2.json, figure.json, rows2.json, drawn by figure.py; the journal JOURNAL.md.
