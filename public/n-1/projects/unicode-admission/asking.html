Before anyone asked

Some languages waited years after their writing system entered Unicode before their last letter was given a code point. Unicode’s committee keeps a public register of every paper filed with it. For each language that waited, this page finds the first paper in that register that asks for the missing letter by name. Most of the years of waiting fall before that paper. Once someone asked, the letter usually arrived within one to three years.

the script is in the standard, no paper in the register asks for this letter yet a paper has asked; the letter is not yet assigned no asking found

One row per language (language, the last letter it needed, +n more letters from the same release). The dot is the first paper found; an orange dot is a paper from a later stage (a draft chart or a ballot), so the real asking was earlier than drawn. Hover a dot for its number and title.

The count

34 languages waited for at least one letter. For 22 of them a paper naming the letter was found. Across those 22, the years before the first paper add up to 186 and the years after it to 42: median 9 years before, 1 after. Of the 13 that waited ten years or more, every one waited longer before the paper than after it. Bangla’s khanda ta was first named in L2/03-084 (2003-03-03) and assigned in 2005. The longest wait after asking is Blin’s (Ethiopic): a 1998 proposal, before the script itself entered, assigned in 2005.

What this does not say

The register cannot say why nobody asked sooner, or why anybody asked when they did: it holds titles and dates, not reasons, and no claim is made about any person’s or committee’s intent. “First paper” means the first paper in the committee’s register whose text uses the letter’s final name or final code point. Papers that asked under another name or another code point are missed, and so are papers filed only with the ISO working group; both errors move the asking later than it was and make the time after asking look shorter than it was. A looser search for seven of the letters under informal names found no earlier asking. Three code-point matches named a different character and were struck by reading them; the registered run before that correction gave the same verdicts. Twelve languages have no paper found, among them both Latin cases (the search stopped at its cap of 60 Latin papers, 54 of them early and with no text online) and Persian and Pashto, whose missing mark was assigned in 1999. Years are release years. Registered predictions, all held: a paper found for at least 60 % of the 28 admission events (21); median time after asking at most two years (1); for most long waits the larger part before asking (13 of 13); khanda ta asked for by 2003.
