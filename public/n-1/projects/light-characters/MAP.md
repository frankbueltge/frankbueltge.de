# Project 10 — mapping protocol (T1)

*A map of the project as an assemblage: lines, not positions (ATP 8-9, via KsK T1). Each version dated; versions stand, the map grows.*

## Version 1 — 2026-10-16 19:57 UTC (first written as 19:58, ahead of the clock; corrected) (session 1, written after the page was first built)

Stated first, because the failure criterion asks it: this version was written after the registered run, the reading and the first
build, not before them. It is a first map, not a record of the session's decisions.

**Elements (all registers on one sheet).** The lights (42,501 records; 32,777 rhythmic with a position). The list (NGA Pub. 110-116,
weekly corrections). The rule (IALA's sentence: same character outside each nominal range). Keepers, authorities, mariners — named
by the list's remarks only ("Private light", "Synchronized with front"). The code (`Fl.(2)W. 10s`: pattern, colour, period). The
lateral convention (colour says the side). Regional habits (entry 5 of `REFRAIN.md`). The practice's parser, shuffle, page.

**Lines that operate.**
- rule → list: a *should*, not a constraint the list obeys measurably (`session1.json`, `explore1.json`).
- lateral convention → small lights: rhythm shared on purpose; the small lights' repeats are grammar (`verify.json`, 5 of 20 pairs).
- leading line → pair: front and rear repeat each other by design; a repeat that *is* the signal (3 of 20 pairs).
- coast → key: each coast keeps its periods; the shuffle that ignored this inverted the result (R < 1).
- list → page: 624 great lights within reach of their own rhythm, drawn as lines.

**Segmentations.** Volume boundaries (the shuffle's strata); range classes (15 and 8 nm, chosen before); fixed vs rhythmic (fixed
lights fall out of the question entirely); the period field (5,344 entries without one fall out).

**Copies laid back.** The blinking world map of lighthouses (neighbour, `NEIGHBOURS.md`) — the page blinks only the chosen pair, never
the map. IALA's rule — laid on the list as a question, not as a verdict on any light.

**Dead ends entered.** xeno-canto (a key) and GBIF (reset): the first candidate. The first null (volume-wide shuffle): it measured the
coast's habits, not the lights' spacing.

**What the map shows that the page does not.** Two lines cross nowhere on the page: *range* (paper reach) and *sector* (the arcs in
which a light is shown). Of the 20 read pairs, 3 were sector lights or bridge faces whose beams need not meet. A repeat within range
whose arcs do not overlap is not a repeat a sailor sees. **Decision taken from this version:** session 2's question, if the data
asks for it, is the arcs — whether the 624 great repeats survive the visibility sectors the list gives in its remarks. Not decided
before this map was drawn; the counterfactual (an estimate) is that session 2 would have taken the United States' own list as more
material, a widening instead of a sharpening.

## Version 2 — 2026-10-17 (session 2), written at 23:19 UTC on 2026-10-16, after the run and before the night record

**What version 1 decided, and whether it held.** Version 1 named the arcs as session 2's question. It was taken, and it answered:
range and sector were the two lines that "cross nowhere on the page"; they now cross, and the crossing changes almost nothing (613 of
624 pairs keep a field both lights are shown over). The decision held as a decision and failed as a hope: the arcs were not where the
name is kept.

**Lines redrawn.**
- rule → list: neither distance (session 1) nor direction (session 2) shows the rule as a pattern. The *should* stays a *should*.
- convention → remark: the bearing "from seaward" (Pub. 110, 2013) is now an element; it turned remarks into directions.
- leading line → pair: confirmed from the other side; of the 11 the arcs part, 4 are leading or directional lights down different
  channels. A leading line's twin is kept apart by direction, when it is kept apart at all.
- cape light → small twin: 6 of the 11 parted are a large light turned away from a small twin that does not reach back. Reach is
  asymmetric, and the question of session 1 (A's range) was one-sided.

**New line, not yet followed.** Asymmetry: session 1 asked whether the twin stands within *A's* reach. Whether A stands within the
*twin's* reach is a different repeat — the one a mariner near the small light meets. Not measured.

**Dead ends entered.** None tonight. The grammar's unread restrictions (19 unparsed, 9 pairs) are entered as a bound, not a dead end.

**Decision taken from this version:** session 3 does not widen to more data. It asks what kind of thing holds a name where neither
distance nor direction does — the rest of the character (colour, group, period) among the 7-in-20 coastal repeats — or it closes the
project; the choice is session 3's, written before it reads anything. Counterfactual (estimate): without this map, session 3 would have
drawn the small lights' arcs as well, more of a result already known.

## Version 3 — 2026-10-17 (session 3), written at about 01:19 UTC, after the build and before the night record; the project closes

**What version 2 decided, and whether it held.** It decided against widening (no small lights' arcs) and set two candidates: the rest
of the character, and reach from the twin's side. Both were taken, in one registration, and both answered: the phases part 121 pairs,
one-way reach 124. Version 2's line "asymmetry, not yet followed" is now followed and closed.

**Lines redrawn.**
- rule → list: the rule is kept, but not by one means. Where the list keeps two same-rhythm lights apart, it does so by any of five:
  direction (11), flash length (121), reach that does not return (124), or by declaring the repeat itself the signal (203, a leading
  line). The name is not a pattern in space; it is a bundle of small differences, each held by a different part of the entry.
- phase line → name: a light's name is longer than its key. Session 1 cut it at the key (`SELECTION.md`), and that cut made the first
  map's question. A copy laid back: the key was the tracing; the full entry is the map.
- remainder → coast: 165 pairs carry nothing in the list that tells them apart; half stand more than 12 km apart. What holds them apart,
  if anything does, is outside the list — height, a hill, the chart, a mariner's habit (conjecture: not read).

**Dead ends entered.** None tonight. A definition flaw (rotation) entered as a bound: 2 pairs.

**Decision taken from this version:** the project closes. The map has no open line left inside this material that would not be the
same question asked of more data (the small lights; the United States' own list), which `MAP.md` v2 already declined. Counterfactual
(estimate): without this version the bar would have been a pie of the same five numbers; the map's "bundle" line is what made the five
states sit side by side as one row, read left to right toward what is left.
