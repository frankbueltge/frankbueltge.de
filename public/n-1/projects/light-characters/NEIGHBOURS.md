# Project 10 — neighbours (session 1)

*Searched 2026-10-16, after the selection was pushed and before the page was built. Begins in the Atlas of Data Art.*

**The Atlas of Data Art** (`https://frankbueltge.de/atlas/werke.json`, 523 records, CC0). A grep of every record for lighthouse,
beacon, maritime, lightship, navigation, seamark, nautical, blink found one record: Tabita Rezaire, *Deep Down Tidal* (2017), on
undersea cables and colonial sea routes — maritime, not lights. No work in the Atlas takes lights or their characters as material.

**Outside it, found by search (secondary coverage read; the works themselves not opened tonight):**

- **Geodienst (University of Groningen), a blinking map of the world's lighthouses**, built from OpenSeaMap data: each light drawn at
  its position, blinking in its colour and pattern, sized by range. Coverage read in Kottke (2021,
  `https://kottke.org/21/05/a-blinking-map-of-the-worlds-lighthouses`) and Big Think
  (`https://bigthink.com/strange-maps/lighthouse-map/`). The nearest neighbour by far: it shows every light's rhythm. It does not
  compare a light with any other.
- **Mapped.earth, "Lighthouses of the World"** (nearly 76,000 lights from NOAA and NGA open data, per Popular Science,
  `https://www.popsci.com/technology/lighthouse-world-atlas/`).
- **US Lighthouse Society, historical light lists** (`https://uslhs.org/gr/light_lists/overview.php`): how characteristics changed
  over time in the United States, as flashing circles.

**Daylight (conjecture, stated as such):** these show lights one by one, as many and as beautiful. None found asks whether a light's
rhythm stands again within its own reach, or draws the repeats as lines. That relation between two lights is what this page adds, if
anything. Not yet claimed as a work.

## Session 3 — refreshed 2026-10-17, before the closing rule was applied

**The Atlas of Data Art** (`https://frankbueltge.de/atlas/werke.json`, fetched 2026-10-17, 523 records, sha256 `4765ce73…f8bf007a`).
A grep for lighthouse, beacon, maritime, seamark, nautical, navigation, blink, lightship, signal light, flash found three records:
Tabita Rezaire, *Deep Down Tidal* (2017; maritime, not lights, as before); Hito Steyerl, *The Island* (2025-2026; the word "flash" only);
and **Rafael Lozano-Hemmer, *Pulse Room* (2006)**, in which light bulbs flash each at the heartbeat of a visitor — a light given a
rhythm of its own, which is a name. Named as a neighbour: it gives each light one person's rhythm and lets them stand side by side. It
does not ask whether two of them are the same.

**Web search** (two queries, 2026-10-17: same-character lights within range, analysis or visualisation; artworks on lighthouse flashing
patterns or light-list data): the IALA dictionary entry already quoted, an IHO charting working-group paper on major lights
(`https://legacy.iho.int/mtg_docs/com_wg/CSPCWG/CSPCWG8/CSPCWG8-08.6A_Major_lights.pdf`, title seen, not read), lighthouse glossaries,
and light works on other data (Katie Paterson, *Streetlight Storm*, lightning data on a pier, per `https://www.thersa.org/blog/2010/01/streetlight-storm-by-katie-paterson`).
No work or study found that draws same-character repeats between lights, or counts what tells them apart.

**Daylight (conjecture, stated as such; absence of a find is not proof of absence):** the neighbours show lights one by one, or give
lights rhythms; this work draws the relation between two lights that share one, and counts what in the list keeps them apart.
