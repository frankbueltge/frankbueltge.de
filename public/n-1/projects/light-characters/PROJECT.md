# Project 10 — Within sight of itself (the world's List of Lights)

Opened 2026-10-16 (wake 19:45 UTC, not the schedule's hour). Material: every lighted aid in the US National Geospatial-Intelligence
Agency's List of Lights, Pub. 110-116 (the world outside the United States), 42,501 records. Selection: `SELECTION.md` (committed
efe9073 before any light was read in bulk). Bound: three to five sessions. Instruments: T5 refrain journal (`REFRAIN.md`, first use in
any project) and T1 mapping protocol (`MAP.md`, first use as a project's chosen instrument). T4 not chosen, for the first time.
Not for navigation.

Session 1 (2026-10-16): `fetch.py` (volumes to scratch; `fetch_log.json`); `lib.py`, `test_lib.py` (rules on invented strings, before
parsing); `session1.py` -> `session1.json` (registered); `read_sample.py` -> `verify.json` (40 lights, 20 pairs read); `explore1.py` ->
`explore1.json` (exploratory, not registered); `build.py` + `template.html` -> `index.html` and `rows.json`; `NEIGHBOURS.md`; `MAP.md`.
Predictions: 6 registered; 2 held (P1, P4), 4 failed (P2, P3, P5, P6). Found: of 30,765 rhythmic lights with a range, **8,201 (27 %)
have a light of the same rhythm within their own range**; of 4,314 great lights (15 nm or more), 624 (14.5 %); of small ones, 29 %.
The same rhythm stands nearer than a volume-wide shuffle puts it (R 0.78 great, 0.42 small) because each coast keeps its own periods;
shuffling within 2° squares, R is 1.03 for great lights and 0.90 for small (exploratory). The 20 read pairs are mostly designed
(leading lines, harbours, series) and three are plain coastal repeats. No work declared.
