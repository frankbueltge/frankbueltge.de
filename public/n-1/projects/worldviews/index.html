Depending on who's counting

Natural Earth is a free world map, in the public domain, that many other maps are built on. Its map of countries comes in one default edition, which "shows de facto boundaries by default according to who controls the territory", and in 33 more, which "show the worldview for several dozen countries according to de jure boundaries (as prescribed by the home country's law and/or local conventions)": "between 246 and 258 countries, depending on who's counting" (its download page). This page lays all 34 editions over one another and keeps only the land on which they do not agree.

10,477,761 km² of land are drawn in a different country, or in none, depending on which of the 34 editions is opened: 7.14 % of the land the default draws, in 161 pieces.

Most of it is one edition's. The edition drawn for Taiwan's point of view has no country called China: one unit, named Taiwan, covers Taiwan and the whole of the mainland, 9,483,217 km². In that edition 9,373,406 km² the default gives to China lie in "Taiwan". Leave that one drawing aside and about 1.1 million km² remain on which the editions disagree (an estimate: the two overlap in places). About 352,432 km² have three or more different owners across the editions, and a few small islands in the South China Sea have five.

How much each edition redraws

Measured against the default, because the default is the edition the publisher puts first, not because it is the true one. Dark: land the edition leaves in no country at all.

The edition for Taiwan redraws most (9,686,569 km²); the ISO edition, next, leaves most of what it redraws in no country; the edition for France redraws least (311,869 km²).

What this page does not do

It draws no border and judges no claim. Every piece is named by what each edition calls it, and no edition is called right. The editions are Natural Earth's reading of each country's law, at one version (5.1.1), not the countries' own statements; other publishers draw otherwise. The pieces are the land of real places where people live, and a count does not say what living there is like.

How it was counted

Each edition's countries were laid over the default's on an equal-area projection; a country is matched across editions by its code, and a country split into parts is not counted as moved. Land in a different country, or in none, is counted; areas are estimates at the map's 1:10 million detail. Seven predictions were written down before the editions were read in bulk (selection). One error was found after the first run and corrected: a country drawn mostly on land the default gives another (an edition's Western Sahara, for one) had been matched to that other, and its move hidden. Both runs are kept. Of 30 pieces read by hand, 22 lie inside places Natural Earth itself lists as disputed or breakaway; seven of the other eight are parts of mainland China and its islands drawn as Taiwan in one edition, and one is Ashmore and Cartier Islands, drawn as part of Australia in the ISO edition. None was a slip of the drawing.
