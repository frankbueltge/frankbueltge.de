Each by its own map

Natural Earth is a free world map, in the public domain, that many other maps are built on. Its map of the world's countries is published in 34 editions: a default one, which "shows de facto boundaries … according to who controls the territory", and 33 more, which "show the worldview for several dozen countries according to de jure boundaries (as prescribed by the home country's law and/or local conventions)" (its download page). Thirty-one of them are drawn for one country's point of view each. All 34 are made by the same publisher: each edition is Natural Earth's reading of a country's law, not that country's own statement.

Here each of those 31 countries is drawn as its own edition draws it, over the same country as most of the other 33 editions draw it.

15 of the 31 are larger in their own edition. The other 16 are drawn by their own edition exactly as most of the others draw them. None is smaller, and no country's own edition leaves out any land that most of the others give it.

One drawing, two names

The edition for China's point of view draws China at 9,483,217 km², with Taiwan in it. The edition for Taiwan's point of view draws Taiwan at 9,483,217 km², with the mainland in it, and has no country called China. It is the same polygon, point for point, under two names. Most of the other editions draw China at 9,373,401 km² and Taiwan at 36,281 km²; 8 of them draw no Taiwan at all.

The others larger in their own edition

Each panel is centred on the land only its own edition (or a few editions) give the country; small pieces are ringed so they can be found. "What most others call it" is read at one point of the largest such piece. Areas are estimates at the map's 1:10 million detail.

Drawn by their own edition as most others draw them

Bangladesh, Egypt, France, Germany, Greece, Indonesia, Italy, the Netherlands, Poland, South Korea, Spain, Sweden, Turkey, Ukraine, the United Kingdom, the United States.

What this does not say

It does not say any border is right, or that any country claims anything: the editions are one publisher's readings, at one version (5.1.1), and other publishers draw otherwise. "Most of the other 33" is a count of that publisher's editions, not a fact about the land. Two places do not fit the pattern and are kept here: land the default draws as Israel, which four editions draw as Palestine and Palestine's own edition does not; and Clipperton Island, which only the ISO edition draws in France and France's own edition draws apart. The land in these pictures is where people live, and a count does not say what living there is like.

Data: Natural Earth, Admin 0 – Countries and point-of-view variants, 1:10m, version 5.1.1, public domain.
