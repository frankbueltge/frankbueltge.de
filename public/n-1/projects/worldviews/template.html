Depending on who's counting

Natural Earth is a free world map, in the public domain, that many other maps are built on. Its map of countries comes in one default edition, which "shows de facto boundaries by default according to who controls the territory", and in 33 more, which "show the worldview for several dozen countries according to de jure boundaries (as prescribed by the home country's law and/or local conventions)": "between 246 and 258 countries, depending on who's counting" (its download page). This page lays all 34 editions over one another and keeps only the land on which they do not agree.

Most of it is one edition's. The edition drawn for Taiwan's point of view has no country called China: one unit, named Taiwan, covers Taiwan and the whole of the mainland, {{TWN_UNIT}} km². Named by no one

A map can also decline to name. An edition can leave land in no unit at all, a hole; it can draw a polygon there and leave its name and code blank; or it can draw and name a unit and give it the type the publisher calls "Indeterminate". The largest such land is Antarctica: {{ATA}} km², typed "Indeterminate" in all 34 editions. Leave it aside and {{NL}} km² remain that at least one edition draws in one of these three ways, in {{NLP}} pieces (the map shows those of 1 km² or more).

Two editions write the hole otherwise. The editions for Palestine's and Russia's points of view hold {{UN_PSE}} and {{UN_RUS}} units with no name and no code: {{UN_KM}} km² together. Each keeps only its own edition's code, and those codes are the ones Natural Earth gives, in its file of disputed and breakaway areas, to places most of the other editions leave empty. In the Russian edition, two of them lie on land all 33 other editions draw as Ukraine, and one on land all 33 draw as Azerbaijan. This page does not supply the names those units were not given; tap a piece to see what each edition calls the land.

Bir Tawil and Scarborough Reef (under 1 km², too small for the map below) are the only land on which all 34 editions name no country: each draws it in no unit, or in a unit of its own typed "Indeterminate". Where most editions name no one, the few that do are mostly the editions of the country they name: the Southern Patagonian Ice Field is Argentina's only in Argentina's edition, and the Spratly pieces are China's, Taiwan's or Vietnam's only in the editions for each (a reading of the pieces, not a tested rule).

Each by its own map

Thirty-one of the editions are drawn for a country's point of view. Session 3 compared each of those countries as its own edition draws it with the same country as the other 33 editions draw it. {{LARGER}} of the 31 are larger in their own edition than in the median of the other 33; the other {{EQUAL}} are drawn by their own edition exactly as the median draws them; none is smaller, and no country's own edition leaves out land that most of the others give it. The largest difference after Taiwan's is Morocco's, {{MAR}} km², the land 22 editions draw as Western Sahara. China's edition draws China at {{CHN}} km²; Taiwan's edition draws Taiwan at the same {{CHN}} km²: one polygon under two names. These are Natural Earth's readings of each country's law, not the countries' statements. The work drawn from this is Each by Its Own Map.

Of {{RARE}} places in the contested land that a country with an edition is named for by only one to five editions, {{SELF}} are named so by that country's own edition. The two that are not: land the default draws as Israel ({{PSI}} km²), which the editions for Bangladesh, Indonesia, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia draw as Palestine and Palestine's own edition draws as Israel; and Clipperton Island, which only the ISO edition draws in France.

What this page does not do

It draws no border and judges no claim. Every piece is named by what each edition calls it, and no edition is called right. The editions are Natural Earth's reading of each country's law, at one version (5.1.1), not the countries' own statements; other publishers draw otherwise. The pieces are the land of real places where people live, and a count does not say what living there is like.

How it was counted

Each edition's countries were laid over the default's on an equal-area projection; a country is matched across editions by its code, and a country split into parts is not counted as moved. Land in a different country, or in none, is counted; areas are estimates at the map's 1:10 million detail. Seven predictions were written down before the editions were read in bulk (selection). One error was found after the first run and corrected: a country drawn mostly on land the default gives another (an edition's Western Sahara, for one) had been matched to that other, and its move hidden. Both runs are kept. Of 30 pieces read by hand, {{N_VER_IN}} lie inside places Natural Earth itself lists as disputed or breakaway; seven of the other eight are parts of mainland China and its islands drawn as Taiwan in one edition, and one is Ashmore and Cartier Islands, drawn as part of Australia in the ISO edition. None was a slip of the drawing.
