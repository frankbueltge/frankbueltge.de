Counted as One

When a sewer in England fills with rain, a storm overflow lets sewage and rainwater out into a river or the sea, and a monitor records when it starts and stops. The public is told one number per overflow, its spill count, and at 30 counted spills in a year the overflow is to be investigated, unless the year's rainfall was exceptional. In 2025 the monitors recorded 1,120,341 discharges at 12,007 overflows, 1,876,994 hours in all. The counting rule made them 293,036 spills — and a spill so counted held anything from under a minute to a full day. One overflow that discharged for 1 hour 16 minutes is counted 44 spills, over the trigger; another that discharged for 567 hours is counted 27, under it.

Every overflow with at least one counted spill in 2025 (12,007), placed by its recorded hours (across) and its counted spills (up), both on log scales; darker squares hold more overflows; hover a square for its count. Dashed: the one-year trigger. Counts are this study's, made by the rule below from the companies' start/stop records, and equal the companies' own returned counts for 89.4 % of overflows.

1. The rule

The Environment Agency's storm overflow assessment framework 2025 (Appendix A) fixes how a spill is counted:

“Counting starts when the first discharge occurs. Any discharges in the first 12-hour block are counted as one spill. Any discharges in the next, and subsequent, 24-hour blocks are each counted as one additional spill per block. Counting continues until there is a 24-hour block without a discharge.”

So ten short discharges inside twelve hours are one spill; a discharge that runs for four days is five. The rule turns a stretch of time into a number of events. The same framework sends an overflow to investigation at 30 counted spills in one year, or 20 a year on average over two, leaving out years of exceptional rainfall.

2. What one counted spill holds

Of 293,036 counted spills, 99,094 (33.8 %) hold less than an hour of discharge, and 65,003 (22.2 %) hold twelve hours or more. 674 hold under a minute; 25,837 hold a full 24 hours. The median counted spill holds 2 h 28 min; one in ten holds 23.3 hours or more. On average one counted spill folds 3.8 separately recorded discharges; one folds 1,077. Overflows counted 1 to 10 times discharged a median 1.1 hours per counted spill; those counted over 60 times, 7.9. The longest unbroken count, at SBB01043, ran from 1 January to 12 August without a discharge-free day: 224 counted spills in one run. That overflow recorded 21 discharges in the year, 5,446 hours, 235 counted spills.

3. The trigger does not sort the hours

3,622 overflows were returned with 30 or more counted spills. Their median discharge was 242 hours. Below the trigger, 206 overflows discharged for longer than that. Among the 687 overflows returned with 28 to 32 counted spills — at the trigger — the hours run from 5.1 to 564 (median 105). The rule reads frequency, as it says it does; what it hands the public as one number is frequency and duration folded together, and at the line where action begins the two come apart.

What this cannot say. The record is the companies' own, not verified by the Environment Agency (its catalogue says so for the start/stop data). A recorded activation is the monitor's reading that the overflow discharged; how much flowed, and what was in it, is not in the record. Monitors that worked for only part of the year undercount; the annual return gives each monitor's operating share, not used tonight. Discharges of 2025 that no monitor recorded are not here.
