Project 19, session 1 · a study in the making · 27 October 2026

On the grid

Every number on a U.S. Nutrition Facts panel is rounded by law. Calories go to the nearest 5 up to 50 and to the nearest 10 above it; fat to the nearest half gram below 5 grams and the nearest gram above; sodium to the nearest 5 or 10 milligrams (21 CFR 101.9). So a panel can only say certain numbers: 80 calories, never 80.1.

The U.S. Department of Agriculture keeps the panels of branded foods in a public database, FoodData Central. Its release of April 2026 holds 455,458 of them. This page reads every number they declare per serving, and asks how many of them are numbers a panel could say.

Only 71.5 % of the declared calorie values are on the law's grid; 79.0 % of the fat values, 68.3 % of the sodium values. Most of the rest sit a hair beside it: 5,523 foods declare 80.1 calories and 4,620 declare 89.9, numbers no label prints.

Where the near-misses come from

The database also keeps each food per 100 grams, its calories as a whole number, and it keeps the per-serving numbers to three digits. Send a panel's 80 calories on a 28-gram serving to 100 grams (286) and back, keep three digits, and it returns as 80.1; send 150 calories on a 145-gram serving there (103) and back, and it returns as 149. Tested on every off-grid calorie value (128,204): 103,607 of them (80.8 %) are a number on the grid that went to 100 grams and came back with three digits. Counting those as the panel's own number, 94.5 % of all declared calories trace back to a number a label may print. 20,822 fit the round trip but no grid number stands behind their per-100 g value; only 2,267 fit neither.

So the record does not keep the panel. It keeps what the panel became after one conversion and back, and the law's grid shows through it blurred by a tenth, or by a whole calorie once the number passes 100. How the database converts is read here from the numbers alone, not from its documentation: the fit is measured, the mechanism is an inference. Fiber suffers most: only 49.6 % of declared fiber values are whole grams. Trans fat suffers least, 98.1 % on the grid, because almost every panel declares it as zero and zero survives every conversion.

Calories against the grams

Protein and carbohydrate give about 4 calories a gram, fat about 9. For 354,389 foods declaring at least 50 calories and all three, the declared calories minus 4, 4 and 9 times the declared grams. 62 % of them land within 5 calories, 86 % within 10; only 3.3 % land exactly on it. The law allows this gap: calories are computed before rounding, and by more than one method.

Five-calorie bins from -60 to +45; hover for counts. The two red bins hold -5 to +5.
