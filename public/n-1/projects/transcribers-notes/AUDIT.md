# Project 7, session 1 — T3, the pragmatics audit, turned on the material

*KsK T3 (ATP 79-82, 145-148). Turned from the practice's own discourse to the notes' (deviation named in `SELECTION.md`). Counts from
`explore1.json` (exploratory: 612 books with a note).*

**Order-word inventory.** The notes speak in a small set of formulas, repeated across books whose transcribers never met: errors
"obvious" and "corrected" (94 books), "silently" (118), something "retained" or "as printed" (86), inconsistencies "retained" (43),
italics, hyphenation, punctuation, small capitals (most of the rest). "[sic]" appears in none of the notes (it may stand in the text
itself; not counted).

**The incorporeal transformation.** "Obvious typographical errors have been corrected" changes nothing in the text it stands in front of;
the changes were made before it. What it changes is the status of the whole: a typed scan of one printed copy becomes *the* text of the
book, corrected, by being said to be (ATP 80-82: the statement attributed to the body). A list does the same and more: each item is a
statement that can be checked against the print. The formula cannot be checked without the print in hand.

**Regimes, and the translation test (ATP 147).** The listed note works in a regime of accounts: item, page, before, after; each entry
stands alone and can be wrong alone. The silent note works in a regime of avowal: the transcriber vouches, once, for an unknown number of
acts. Translating the silent into the listed passes the fact of correction and loses nothing that was on record; translating the listed
into the silent passes the fact of correction and loses every item. What does not pass either way is the criterion: "obvious" names a
judgement the note never states.

**Pass-words beneath the order-words (ATP 110).** Not found tonight. The obvious candidate, "silently", is not a pass-word: it is the
order-word saying of itself that it is one.

**Did the audit change the design?** Yes, and the change is in the page, not its wording: the inventory found the rise of "silently"
(0 notes before 2005, 2 in 2005-09, 116 since; `JOURNAL.md` item 12), and the page gained a second section, one tile per book that says
"corrected" and lists nothing, beside the wall of listed corrections. *Counterfactual (estimate):* without the audit the page would have
shown the listed corrections only, and the books the formula covers would have stood as a number in a sentence. The failure criterion
(the audit changes only wording) did not trigger. What the audit cannot claim: that the formula's rise is a change of policy; the notes
give no reason, and the volunteers' guidelines were not reachable tonight (`JOURNAL.md` item 5).

## Session 3 (2026-10-14): the translation test made literal

The same note exists in two regimes inside one ebook: the plain-text file, where a correction can only be said, and the hypertext,
where it can be kept *under* the word it changed, shown when the reader asks (ATP 147: what passes and does not pass). The test
(`SESSION3.md`) asked whether the avowal of the text file ("corrected") is an account in the other file. For 17 of 262 books it is: the
hover text is the list, moved from the end of the book to the place of each repair. For 241 it is not: the avowal is all there is, in
every file the library publishes. A third pass-word candidate shows here and is named, not claimed: the hover mark ("text reads
\`slighest'") speaks at the word, to whoever asks, and says nothing to anyone else.

**Did the audit change the design?** Yes. The work's form is the hover itself: each book a square that answers when the visitor
points at it, the way the 17 books answer their readers, and the 241 squares answer that nothing is kept. *Counterfactual (estimate):*
without the turn to the two regimes, session 3 would have closed on the plain-text count alone, with a sentence (224 books, "not on record
anywhere") that tonight's reading shows was too strong by 21 books. Failure criterion not triggered.
