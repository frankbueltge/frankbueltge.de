Corrected Silently

Project Gutenberg is a free library of old books, retyped by volunteers from printed copies. When they find a misprint they fix it. Sometimes the book ends with a note that lists each fix. More and more often, the note says only that errors have been corrected, and not which.

126books list their corrections, one by one
241say errors were corrected and keep no record of which, in the text or the web edition
17keep the record only in the web edition, as text that appears when you hover over a corrected word

Out of 1,495 English books drawn at random from the library, 709 carry a transcriber’s note. Below are the 384 notes that speak of correcting errors, one square per book, oldest release first. Hover or tap a square.

lists its correctionskeeps them under the words, in the web edition onlysays corrected, keeps no record

Hover or tap a square.

The pale squares gather toward the end. By the year the e-book was released, the notes keeping no record were 22 of 58 in 2005–09; 44 of 92 in 2010–14; 67 of 93 in 2015–19; 87 of 113 in 2020–24; 20 of 27 in 2025–26. A repair that is said and not shown can be trusted and cannot be checked: to know what was changed, you need the printed copy the volunteer had in hand.

How this was found

The plain-text file of each book was read for its note, and the corrections the notes list were picked out by pattern and checked by reading (1,030 in all; three in four are one letter away from the print). Then, for every book whose note says “corrected” and lists nothing, the web (HTML) edition was fetched too, because volunteers sometimes mark each corrected word there instead. That check could have overturned the finding, and it was registered before any file was opened: if a third or more of these books kept their corrections in the web edition, the claim would have been withdrawn. 21 of 262 did: 17 as hover text, and 4 that list their corrections in both files after all, which the pattern had missed. A note is what a volunteer wrote, not all they did; nothing here judges anyone’s care, and no volunteer is named. Each square’s words are quoted from the book it stands for, numbered as in the library.

Sources: pg_catalog.csv, the plain-text files of 1,495 e-books and the HTML files of 600 of them, from the mirror gutenberg.pglaf.org, 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-14 (sha256 in sources.json, sources2.json, sources3.json). Project Gutenberg e-books are free of U.S. copyright; nothing of them is reproduced beyond corrected words and short notes, each tied to its e-book number. The full project, every listed correction and the two earlier findings: What the Repair Keeps; record and programs: projects/transcribers-notes. Signed Remainder, 2026-10-14. A modest work: a bibliographer with a script could have made it. Not yet tested by a stranger.
