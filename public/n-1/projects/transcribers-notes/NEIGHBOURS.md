## Where the search began: the Atlas of Data Art

`https://frankbueltge.de/atlas/werke.json` (523 entries tonight). Every field searched for typo, errat-, misprint, correct-, proofread,
transcri-, OCR, error, mistake, edit. **No entry takes printing errors, errata or the correction of transcribed books as material.**
The hits are works on error in machines and networks: the Rhizome ArtBase glitch set, and Agnieszka Kurant, *Errorism* (2024, Centre

## Art

- **Manuel Raeder, *Makulatur 2010-2016*** (artist's book, Berlin 2017, 336 pp.): misprinted sheets from publications the designer
  made, collected and bound as a book. Catalogued by Stroom Den Haag (`https://www.stroom.nl/en/stroom/library/online-catalogue/1575`)
  and MACBA (`https://macba.cat/en/obra/a13204-makulatur-2010-2016--studio-manuel-raeder-texts-by-mela-davila-freire-eran-schaerf--abraham-cruzvillegas`).
  The nearest in gesture: the error kept in view rather than discarded. It keeps the printed waste itself, from one practice.
- **Futurefarmers, *De-Bugging*** (Carpenter Center, Harvard, February 2017; `https://carpenter.center/programs/futurefarmers-de-bugging`):
  enactments "releasing the text from the pages of the errata found in the Robbins Library". Errata sheets in a library's books as the
  starting point. The nearest in material: a library's errata, read as matter for a work.
- **maya rae oppenheimer at Artexte** (research residency, Montréal, autumn 2025; `https://artexte.ca/en/2025/10/maya-rae-oppenheimer-artexte/`):
  searching a collection for errata towards letterpress, artist-book and projection works; distinguishes errata ("a blanket term for
  content that needs correcting") from corrigenda ("corrections that alter a stance, opinion, or claim").

## Exhibition and scholarship

- ***Beauties of My Style: Errata and the Printed Mistake***, Yale Sterling Memorial Library, 2026 (Fine Books Magazine, 5 February
  2026, `https://www.finebooksmagazine.com/news/printing-errors-celebrated-upcoming-yale-library-exhibition`): about thirty objects
  across five centuries, among them a seven-page errata slip for the 1922 *Ulysses*. (A search summary gave a different closing date;
  the article's dates are the ones fetched.)
- **Adam Smyth** on errata lists in early modern books, e.g. "Doing things with errors" (CRASSH, Cambridge, 3 December 2014,
  `https://www.crassh.cam.ac.uk/events/25805`), and *Material Texts in Early Modern England* (2018): errors as moments where a book shows
  its making. Not read in full tonight; cited for the line of argument only.
- **Liina Repo**, *Errors and Corrections: Early Modern English Errata Lists in 1529-1700 and Their Connection to Prescriptivism*
  (MA thesis, University of Turku, 2018, `https://www.utupub.fi/handle/11111/12850`): 80 books' errata lists, classified. The nearest in
  method: errata lists counted and sorted by kind. Not read in full tonight.
- **Gerlach and Font-Clos**, "A Standardized Project Gutenberg Corpus for Statistical Analysis of Natural Language and Quantitative
  Linguistics", *Entropy* 22(1):126, 2020 (`https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08092`): the same library as a corpus of about 50,000 books. The
  nearest in material and scale; it treats the books' text as language, not their repair notes as a record.
- **ARTFL Encyclopédie corrections** (`https://encyclopedie.uchicago.edu/node/13`; reported by search: two correction rounds of
  344,608 and 157,806 changes; **not verified tonight**).

## Daylight (stated before the result; to be revised by it)

None of these reads the repair notes of a transcribed library across a random sample of its books, and none separates the corrections
kept in view (listed one by one) from those let into the text under a formula. The errata scholarship reads printers' own lists; this
reads what later readers, repairing the books, chose to say about them. The daylight is the measurement, and it is narrow: a
bibliographer with a script could make it. No machine-only advantage is claimed at selection.

## Session 2 (2026-10-13): the turned letter and the typo corpus

*Searched and fetched after `SESSION2.md` was committed (2dbd69c), before any result was computed.*

- **Women Writers Project, "Special characters: inverted characters [007]"** (Northeastern University, undated;
  `https://www.wwp.northeastern.edu/research/publications/documentation/markup/records/007.html`, fetched tonight): in early printed
  books letters set upside down occur, "most frequently these are "n" and "u", but also other characters." The source this session
  cites for what a turned letter is. It concerns early modern books; the sample here is mostly later.
- **Wikipedia, "Rotated letter"** (`https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotated_letter`, fetched tonight): "some physical typefaces didn't
  bother with distinct sorts for lowercase b and q, d and p, or n and u." The pairs registered as turned are these three.
- **Hagiwara and Mita, "GitHub Typo Corpus: A Large-Scale Multilingual Dataset of Misspellings and Grammatical Errors"**, LREC 2020
  (`https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12893`, fetched tonight): "more than 350k edits and 65M characters in more than 15 languages", typos and
  their corrections mined from commits. The nearest in method: an error read with its correction, at scale. Its errors are a
  keyboard's; the ones here are, if anything, a type case's.
- Daylight, revised: none of the neighbours reads the corrections that later readers list for printed books as evidence of the
  physical type. Whether the evidence is there is tonight's test, not a claim.
