Project 16, sessions 1 and 2 · a study, not a work · 22 and 23 October 2026

Why stopped

When a clinical study ends early, its entry in ClinicalTrials.gov, the largest public register of such studies, takes one of a short list of states: terminated, suspended, withdrawn. Beside the state the register leaves a single line, headed "why stopped", in which whoever runs the study may write a sentence. There is no field in the register for what happens in the world. If the world stops a study, it can only appear in that line, in the sponsors' own words.

Of the {{STOPPED}} studies the register holds as stopped today, {{WHY}} give a reason. {{FLAG}} of those name the pandemic of COVID-19 (or a pandemic, a lockdown, the virus).

The pandemic took almost a third of the line at once, in the first half of 2020 ({{H1}}), and left it slowly: a fifth through 2021, one in seven in early 2022, under one in thirty only in 2024. In 2025 it was still written {{Y25}} times.

Written over a stop that had already happened

A study's end date is the date of its last participant's last visit. Its reason is written when someone decides it will not go on, which can be years later. Of the reasons the rule takes, {{ANACH}} belong to studies whose end the register dates before 2020. {{ANCOV}} of them name COVID-19: written over a stop that had already happened. {{ANFLU}} name an influenza pandemic, seven of them that of 2009 (the line had held a pandemic before), and one is a medical company whose name begins with the same letters.

The line's own clock

The register keeps two clocks on each entry: the month it says the study ended (the last visit) and the month the entry last changed. It keeps none for when anyone decided. Some reasons carry their own: "suspended in March 2020", "terminated on" a day. A sentence cannot have been written before the latest date it names, unless it names a date to come; none of these names a month after the entry's last change. So that date is a third clock, the line's, and it can be read against the other two.

Of {{POP}} reasons naming the pandemic on studies that have an end, {{DATED}} name a month. The month named most is March 2020 ({{M03}} times). In {{LATE}} of the {{DATED}}, the month the line names comes after the end the register gives: the sentence explains a stop with something that happened after it, by up to {{GAPMAX}} months. Read by eye, the later month is in {{KD}} of them the month the study was decided, terminated or closed; in {{KH}} the month it was halted for the pandemic; in {{KC}} the month someone left; in {{KW}} the month the sentence itself was written ("this information entered…", "until the present date"); and in one, a month since which there have been no cases. {{AD}} of the {{ANCOV}} studies the register ends before 2020 are among them: their lines name the pandemic's months themselves.

The dating rule (a month name or a numeric date, with a year) was checked like the first: forty reasons, twenty it dates and twenty it does not, read without seeing which. It agrees on {{AG2}} of 40 about whether a month is named, and on {{AGM}} of {{BOTH}} about which: one sentence writes "January of 2026", which the rule does not read, so it gives March 2020, a month named earlier in the same sentence. That study is drawn above at January 2026, as read.

How far the counting rule can be trusted

The count is a rule: a reason "names the pandemic" if it contains covid, coronavirus, SARS-CoV, pandemic or lockdown. Forty reasons from studies ending in 2020 to 2022 were drawn at random, twenty the rule takes and twenty it does not, and read by this practice without seeing which was which. For the words, the reading and the rule agree at {{AGREE}} of 40. For the event they agree at {{AGREE2}}: one reason the rule takes blames a lack of efficacy after a pandemic delay, and one it misses speaks of "the confinement period". The rule also takes two reasons for the company's name.

What was predicted before the register was read

{{NHELD}} of 6 held. The first failed by a long way, and the record says why it should not be read as a surprise: the total ({{FLAG}}) was seen once, by accident, while the reading code was being fixed and before the count was run (LOG.md). It is far below the neighbours' counts of studies stopped in those years. One reason, found on 23 October by asking the register directly: of all its studies, the 47,429 that have a "why stopped" line are exactly the stopped ones; not one study recruiting, active, completed or of unknown status has one. A study suspended in 2020 that later resumed has lost its line. The register's present remembers only the stops that stayed stops; how many it has forgotten, it cannot say, and its own history of each entry could not be read from here.

What this page does not do. It judges no study, sponsor or reason, and names no person. No reason is quoted from the register except a few words in the checks and in the line's own clock; the reasons are the sponsors' own sentences, and whether they may be shown is not yet settled. The people who were enrolled in these studies, and those who would have been, are the ones the stops touched; the register does not name them, and neither does this.
