Project 16, session 1 · a study, not a work · 22 October 2026

Why stopped

When a clinical study ends early, its entry in ClinicalTrials.gov, the largest public register of such studies, takes one of a short list of states: terminated, suspended, withdrawn. Beside the state the register leaves a single line, headed "why stopped", in which whoever runs the study may write a sentence. There is no field in the register for what happens in the world. If the world stops a study, it can only appear in that line, in the sponsors' own words.

Of the 53,251 studies the register holds as stopped today, 47,429 give a reason. 2,642 of those name the pandemic of COVID-19 (or a pandemic, a lockdown, the virus).

The pandemic took almost a third of the line at once, in the first half of 2020 (32 %), and left it slowly: a fifth through 2021, one in seven in early 2022, under one in thirty only in 2024. In 2025 it was still written 30 times.

studies stopped that month, with a reason…whose reason names the pandemic

Each column is one month (a year label stands at its January), by the date the register gives for the study's end (its actual completion date), for terminated and withdrawn studies; suspended studies have no end and are not drawn. Hover a column for its counts. The register as processed on 22 October 2026, 09:00 UTC; studies stopped in recent months are still being entered.

Written over a stop that had already happened

A study's end date is the date of its last participant's last visit. Its reason is written when someone decides it will not go on, which can be years later. Of the reasons the rule takes, 95 belong to studies whose end the register dates before 2020. 86 of them name COVID-19: written over a stop that had already happened. 8 name an influenza pandemic, seven of them that of 2009 (the line had held a pandemic before), and one is a medical company whose name begins with the same letters.

a study whose reason names COVID-19…an influenza pandemic

Each line runs from the month the register says the study ended (the dot) to the month its entry was last changed: the reason was written somewhere on that line, and for COVID-19 not before the shaded band, which begins in January 2020. The median line is 41 months long. One study, read in the check below, gives an end in February 2020 and a reason that dates its halt to 18 March 2020.

How far the counting rule can be trusted

The count is a rule: a reason "names the pandemic" if it contains covid, coronavirus, SARS-CoV, pandemic or lockdown. Forty reasons from studies ending in 2020 to 2022 were drawn at random, twenty the rule takes and twenty it does not, and read by this practice without seeing which was which. For the words, the reading and the rule agree at 39 of 40. For the event they agree at 38: one reason the rule takes blames a lack of efficacy after a pandemic delay, and one it misses speaks of "the confinement period". The rule also takes two reasons for the company's name.

What was predicted before the register was read

  • Failed, P1: >= 4000 flagged stated reasons.
  • Held, P2: most-flagged stop month is 2020-03 or 2020-04.
  • Held, P3: >= 30 % flagged in 2020H1 (terminated, withdrawn).
  • Held, P4: >= 1 % flagged in stop year 2025.
  • Held, P5: >= 10 anachronisms.
  • Held, P6: rule agrees with the reading for the words at >= 36 of those told.

5 of 6 held. The first failed by a long way, and the record says why it should not be read as a surprise: the total (2,642) was seen once, by accident, while the reading code was being fixed and before the count was run (LOG.md). It is far below the neighbours' counts of studies stopped in those years. One reason, not yet tested: a study suspended in 2020 that later resumed and finished is no longer stopped, and the register's present record no longer holds the stop.

What this page does not do. It judges no study, sponsor or reason, and names no person. No reason is quoted from the register except a few words in the check; the reasons are the sponsors' own sentences, and whether they may be shown is not yet settled. The people who were enrolled in these studies, and those who would have been, are the ones the stops touched; the register does not name them, and neither does this.
