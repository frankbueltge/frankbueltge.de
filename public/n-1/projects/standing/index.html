Standing

Every plant has a name, and many have had several. Of the 1,047,905 species names of vascular plants published since 1753, 35 % stand today as the name of a plant. The rest were undone by a later statement — and the plant did not change.

Sections can be read in any order; each says which others it connects to.

1. A name, and a verdict on it

A species name is made once, in print, in a year: a genus and an epithet, Rosa canina. Botanical names count from 1753. Later botanists may decide that the plant belongs in another genus — the epithet is carried over and the old name becomes a synonym, "moved" here — or that it is the same plant as one named earlier, and the later name is sunk into the older one, "merged" here. Some names are void under the rules of naming. Kew's World Checklist of Vascular Plants records, for every published name, the verdict of the field as Kew reads it: 1,448,984 names, 1,048,616 of them of species. This study reads them by the year each name was made.

Connects to 2 (how much stands) and 4 (in what form).

2. How much of each year stands

The share standing climbs from under one in five for the names of the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries to 76 % for the names of the 2020s. The lowest decade is the 1790s (15 %). The year 1753 itself is an exception: 52 % of its 5,537 species names stand. The recent names stand partly because they are good and partly because no one has yet looked again: the right end of the curve is untested, not proven.

3. Two books the field refused

The two deepest years are each one publication. In 1891, Revis. Gen. Pl. made 27,102 species names, most of them new combinations under older genus names; 712 stand (2.6 %). Without it, the 1890s stand at 26 % instead of 18 %. In 1796, Prodr. Stirp. Chap. Allerton made 1,594; 13 stand and 732 are declared illegitimate. The tall bar of 1891 in the figure is almost entirely blue and red. (Exploratory: found after the registered count.)

Connects to 2, and to 4: the 1891 names were mostly moved, not merged.

4. Moved or merged

A name that was moved is not wholly gone: its epithet lives on inside another name. A name that was merged is. Of the undone names of 1753, 75 % were moved. Through the nineteenth century the share falls to about a third: those names fell mostly by being found to be plants already named. Then it rises again, to 84 % for the undone names of the 2020s. The names of the long middle, from the 1760s to the 1940s, were undone mostly by doubling; those of 1753 and of recent decades mostly by regrouping. (Exploratory: the registered count expected the flag spelled TRUE, and it is spelled T; see 7.)

5. The epithet that waits

Of the 365,813 standing species names, 28 % stand on an epithet first published under another name — moved names, returning. For those, the standing name came a median 56 years after the epithet (half between 22 and 105 years).

Connects to 4.

6. What this cannot tell

Whether the verdicts are right. A name "stands" here in Kew's view on 4 June 2026; other checklists disagree, and some of today's standing names will fall. Why a name fell: the argument is in a monograph, not in a column. Anything about the plants themselves. The registered check against Kew's public website could not run (the site answered with a bot challenge, which was not circumvented); against Kew's own copy of the checklist on GBIF, 11 of 12 sampled names carry the same verdict (the twelfth, illegitimate here, is a synonym there, whose scheme has no class for it). Nearest neighbours: NEIGHBOURS.md.

8. Three and a half years of verdicts

Kew keeps its earlier releases. Seven of them, from December 2022 to June 2026, hold the same 1,023,761 species names under the same ids, and 39,436 of them (3.9 %) carry a different verdict at the end than at the start. 9,789 names stopped standing and 4,418 began to; the share standing still rose a little (34.6 % to 34.9 %), because new names arrive standing. Not one plant changed.

The verdict moves on old names and new ones alike, but not equally: about one standing name in seventy from the eighteenth century was undone in these three and a half years, one in twenty-five of the 2020s. The newest names are where the field is still deciding — in both directions: 5.9 % of the 2020s' undone names stand again.

The largest single flow is not a verdict on a plant. Of the 13,837 names that went from merged to moved, 9,739 point to the same accepted name before and after: the synonym was not re-placed, it was re-described — the record now says the name shares its type with the accepted one, where before it said nothing. In all, at least 13,555 of the 39,436 changes (34 % or more; the exploratory file keeps the thirty largest classes, which hold all but 58) keep the same accepted name; the record corrected its own grammar. 9. The court still sits

Session 3 split every change of the six steps into what it does. A verdict touches a plant's standing or where a name points: 10,570 names stopped standing, 4,960 began to, 6,949 synonyms were sent to another plant, 4,503 names were judged for the first time and 447 had a judgment withdrawn. A re-description keeps the same accepted name and changes only the record's wording: 14,186 (34 %), three in four of them in the first two steps. In every step a verdict fell on a name of 1753; recent names are judged about twice as often per name as the eighteenth century's. The work draws each verdict as a dot at the year its name was published, and the re-descriptions apart: The Court Still Sits.
