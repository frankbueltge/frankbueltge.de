**Result.** Session 1: of a random 3,000 of the 26,519 named minor planets, 2,337 carry a citation; 1,352 honour a person alive when
named who is given a birth year, 383 someone already dead. The commonest five-year age at naming is 15-19, nearly all finalists of
school science competitions; their teachers are named too. Session 2: the date a citation carries is that of the citation that stands
now; of 604 citations dated by a WGSBN bulletin (2021-2026), 156 are later corrections or re-issues, mostly copy-edits, standing a
median 15 years after the first publication; finalists were named within a year of their competition through 2018, and the last ones
in the sample four years late. Session 3: the 1,733 older citations dated by a batch of the Minor Planet Circulars, each batch opened
(178): 1,570 stand under NEW NAMES in the batch named, none under a correction, none pointing to an earlier page; 109 are not in the
named batch, and where they were first published was not found. Nothing re-dated. Final: 1,340 living honourees with an age (the
15-19 band 295, the next 113), 379 dead (middle wait 43 years).

the headline held through every correction this project could make (and, exploratory, with every unconfirmed date set aside), and the
neighbour search found no work or study drawing the age at naming. No machine-only advantage is claimed (`JOURNAL2.md` item 17).
