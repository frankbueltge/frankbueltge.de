Named into the sky

When a minor planet (an asteroid) is given a name, the International Astronomical Union publishes a short citation saying who or what it is named after. Most names honour a person, and the citation often gives the year that person was born. A body that will circle the Sun for longer than any person will remember them is named, on a given day, after someone of a given age.

How old? Of 3,000 named minor planets drawn at random from the 26,519 named so far, 2337 carry a citation. 1352 of them honour a person who was alive when named and give a birth year; 383 honour someone already dead. Each is one dot below. The commonest age is not that of an astronomer at the end of a career. It is fifteen to nineteen: 294 people, nearly all of them finalists of school science competitions, named in batches. Around them: astronomers, colleagues and benefactors at every age from twenty to over ninety; a man named for his forthcoming 107th birthday; and the children and grandchildren of discoverers, named as infants and young children.

Alive when named: age at naming, in five-year bands

Each dot is one person, tap or hover for what the record says. No exact age is shown under 25, and no number or link for anyone under 18 when named: they did not ask to be counted, and the page does not lead to them. For everyone else the number leads to the record of the Solar System Dynamics group at JPL, where the name and the citation stand.

Dead when named: years between death and naming

The last band holds everyone named 200 years or more after death. A quarter of the dead were named within six years of dying (94 of 383 within five); the median is 44 years; a quarter waited more than 117.

When the finalists were named

Citations per year in the sample, 1990-2026 (the year of the citation that stands now). Orange: finalists of school competitions; violet: their teachers. Finalists were 27 % of all citations of 2002-2012 and 7.6 % since 2020.

How this was read

FrameThe Minor Planet Center's list of named minor planets, fetched 2026-10-15 (26,519 numbers); a random 3,000 (seed 20261015). Citations from JPL's Small-Body Database API. Registered before any citation was read: SELECTION.md.
YearsThe first pair of years in the citation, "(1913-2002)", or a birth year, "(b. 1996)", decides; the year of the citation comes from its publication reference. 60 citations read in full: 60 right.
FinalistsA citation naming a school competition or school. 30 read: 8 wrong, 7 of them teachers who mentored a finalist; teachers were then counted apart. One remaining error is not corrected (an architect who went to a junior high school).
Predictions6 registered; 2 held as registered (the largest band is 15-19; most objects carry a citation), 3 after the teacher correction (finalists were a quarter of the citations of 2002-2012 and under a tenth since 2020). The ones that failed: the share 19 or under (26 %, predicted 30 %); the median age of the others (54.5, predicted at least 55); the median wait after death (44 years, predicted over 50). JOURNAL.md.
LimitsWhere a citation was later corrected, its year is the correction's, so an age can be too high: one living age of 105 is such a case and stays in, as registered. Citations without a year (most places, things and many people) are not drawn. Not tested by a stranger.
