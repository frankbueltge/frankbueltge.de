# The Elbe at low water — pragmatics audit (T3)

*KsK ch. 6, T3, first use in this practice. Begun session 1, night 39,
2026-09-29, after the prospect had read the material. The paper applies T3 to
the project's own core proposition; the order-word inventory of the material's
own statements is an extension, marked as extrapolation.*

## Session 1

**The core proposition, as the session would first have written it.** "The
project shows the Elbe's two memories of low water: the gauges and the hunger
stones."

**Regime** (ATP 147, via KsK T3). Signifying: it refers everything to a centre
("the project shows") and asks to be interpreted as a comparison. It is the
sentence of a wall text.

**Translation test.** Moved into a presignifying, collective form — the
sentence is replaced by a situation instead of being asserted: *the stones'
dates can be read only while the river is low enough to read them.* What
passes: the two memories, the river, the dates. What does not pass: "shows".
In the prospect's material the stones are not shown; they are exposed, by the
river, at the only time they can also be added to (finding 4: carving
continued to 2015 and 2016). What remains irreducible is the condition.

**What the test changes in the design, not only the wording.** The first
sentence implies a picture: gauges and stones set side by side. The translated
one implies a work that is conditional — that gives its reader the stones'
dates only under the river's own condition, read from the gauge, and withholds
them otherwise. That is a different project: its form is a rule, not a
chart. Taken as the working direction for session 2; not yet a form.

**New statements generated** (ATP 147: "create new, as yet unknown statements
for that proposition"):
- A record that can be read only in the state it records.
- The gauge's public service forgets in 31 days and can always be read; the stones keep six
  centuries and can be read only when it is dry.
- To read the stone is to stand where the next line would be cut.

**Order-word inventory of the material** (extrapolation). The stones'
statements are addressed to a future reader:
- "Wenn du mich siehst, dann weine" ("If you see me, then weep", Děčín, dated
  19th century by the Saxon table). An order-word in the strict sense (ATP 76,
  via KsK §4.2): it commands. Its condition of being read is its referent — it
  can be seen only when the drought it warns of is present. It is true whenever
  it is read.
- The Czech line of probably 1938 on the same stone, which the table gives in
  German as „Mädchen, weine und klage nicht, wenn es trocken ist, spritze das
  Feld“ ("Girl, do not weep and complain; when it is dry, water the field",
  translated here).
  It answers the first line and turns weeping into irrigation. A candidate for
  what the paper calls transforming "the compositions of order into components
  of passage" (ATP 110, via KsK §4.2). Marked as a reading, not a finding.
- "29.08.1904: (-)47cm" (Schönebeck slab, row assigned by text order) and the
  2016 wave: the gauge's own statement cut into the stone's register. Here the
  two memories are not side by side; one is written inside the other.
- The waterways office's move of the Schönebeck stone (2011) is not a statement
  but an act on a body that changes when a statement is made. Occupied as a
  work by the neighbour in `NEIGHBOURS.md` §1.

**Failure criterion, provisional.** Not triggered: the translation test changed
the design direction (a conditional work instead of a comparison), not only the
sentence. Whether that change holds is session 2's to find.

## Session 2

**Core proposition of the study, as first written:** "Dates are legible only
while the river is low." Regime: signifying again — an illustration of a known
fact ("the press says it") with a rule the study invented (MNW as the line).

**Translation test.** Strip the invented line; what passes is the descent: the
river falls, a person cuts a mark, the river falls further. New statement
generated: *the mark records a minimum it cannot know is final* (the record's
own lowest reading, 50 cm, came the day after the first prospect, below that prospect's 52). It is
carried from the data (running minimum) and from Elleder et al. (marks are the
year's lowest stage). It is stated on the page in one line and is the study's
only part that is not an illustration.

**Failure criterion** (the translation changes only the wording): **not
triggered** — the test moved the design's centre from the blur to the cut.
Whether that holds is session 3's to find.

## Session 3

**Core proposition of the candidate work, as first written:** "The Cut lets a visitor mark the
river's lowest level." Regime: signifying (a demonstration with a score).

**Translation test.** Strip the score; what passes is the one irreversible act under uncertainty.
New statement generated: *the mark is cut at a level the gauge may revise and the river may undercut
— the record's own recent past is provisional* (36 held readings rewritten upward by 1–5 cm). The
design changed in substance, not wording: the build counts revisions, the page states them, and its
verdict reads "lowest held", never "lowest".

**Failure criterion** (translation changes only the wording): **not triggered.**
