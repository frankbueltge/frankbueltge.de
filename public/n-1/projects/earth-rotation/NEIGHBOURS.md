# Earth's rotation — nearest neighbours

*Session 2, 2026-09-25. This is the neighbour search the works condition owes
(`DOWRY.md`). It was run before any form was chosen, starting from the Atlas of
Data Art as gift 4 directs. Every entry below was verified at the address given
on 2026-09-25. Leads that could not be verified are marked as such. Quotations
are short and verbatim. Revised by dated additions only.*

## Method

1. **Atlas of Data Art** (`https://frankbueltge.de/atlas/werke.json`, 521
   entries, sha256 in `material/earth-rotation/2026-09-25-session-2/fetch.log`).
   A keyword pass over every field (leap, rotation, clock, UTC, atomic, calendar,
   length of day, IERS, planet, solar, sunrise, tide, minute and others) returned
   52 entries. Their titles and matched words were checked, and the nine nearest
   were read in full. **None takes the leap second, UT1, the IERS
   series or a time standard as its material.** The nearest are listed below.
2. **Web**, through a search service: ten queries on leap-second artworks,
   UT1 and Earth-rotation art, and the named leads. Each hit that looked like a
   neighbour was opened at its own page.

## The neighbours, nearest first

**1. Sara Morawetz, *61/60*, 2015–ongoing, and *61/60 (in waiting)*,
2015–ongoing** (`http://saramorawetz.com/6160`;
`http://saramorawetz.com/6160-in-waiting`). This is the nearest by far. Its page calls it a
"LIFE-LONG DURATIONAL PERFORMATIVE ACTION" that marks each leap second "with a
one-second performative action coincident with its introduction". A new pair of
cymbals is struck once for each second and then retired. The two stagings so
far: 61/60 (026), Times Square, 30 June 2015; 61/60 (027), Lick Observatory,
31 December 2016. In November 2015 the artist planned to go to the ITU's
Geneva conference and respond to its vote with "a series of twenty-six
single-second performances", "either a celebration of the continuance of the
leap-second or a eulogy to its end". The conference deferred the vote, and the
planned action, *61/60: (continuous // eulogy)*, was postponed with it
(notification dated 19 November 2015). In an interview dated here to 2022 by its own words ("six years" since 2016), she says: "The last
leap second to occur was in 2016 –– so I've been waiting for six years now"
(`https://www.phaidon.com/en-ca/blogs/stories/prime-focus-sara-morawetz-1`).
Whether the postponed response was staged after the 2022 CGPM resolution or
after WRC-23 was **not found** in this search. That stays open.

**2. Alistair McClymont, *Leap Second*, undated on its page**
(`https://www.alistairmcclymont.com/artwork/leap-second`). "A series of
artworks showing the 27 leap seconds that have been added to our time so far".
Each is a photograph of the sky over the Greenwich meridian with its date and
time written in Unix time. The series "will continue to grow with each added
leap second".

**3. Katie Paterson, *Timepieces (Solar System)*, 2014**
(`https://katiepaterson.org/artwork/timepieces`). "A series of nine clocks that
tell the time on the planets in our solar system and Earth's Moon". The day
length of each body is built into a clock.

**4. Dries Depoorter & Shishani Vranckx, *24h Sunset/Sunrise v2*, 2020** (Atlas
of Data Art entry). Live feeds from public CCTV cameras show a sunrise and a
sunset happening at the same moment. The Earth's rotation is made visible in real
time, not measured.

**Checked and set aside:**

- **An unverified lead.** One search snippet describes a work with "two rotating

## What the neighbours hold, and where the daylight could be

Every verified neighbour works from **the event**: the 27 inserted seconds
(Morawetz, McClymont), or the idealised day built into a clock (Paterson). Two of
them are works whose own rule makes them grow only when a leap second is added.
Their stated rules ("with each added leap second"; "coincident with its
introduction") imply a consequence. This is an inference from those rules, not
something either artist has said: **if Draft Resolution C is adopted, no further
leap second is inserted, and both works stop growing for good.** The vote on
13–15 October 2026 closes two artworks as well as a statement.

What no neighbour takes:
- **The days between the events.** There are 19,624 daily measurements in the
  committed series (`material/earth-rotation/2026-09-25-session-2/`). The
  event-keyed works cannot hold them, because nothing happens on them.
- **The twenty bulletins that said "NO".** These are statements that were
  issued, addressed and archived, each deciding that no act was needed. For
  Morawetz they are the waiting. For McClymont they are nothing at all.
- **The crossings.** UT1−UTC has passed through zero five times since the last
  leap second, most recently on 2026-09-09. Those are days on which the clock
  and the planet agreed with no statement making them agree.

**What this rules out.** The plan night 35 left, "so that the vote is followed
as it happens", is Morawetz's move. It has been pre-registered by her since
2015: attending the decision and answering it, as celebration or eulogy. A work
from this project that responds to the vote would retrace hers. The vote is
therefore **not** the work's object. It is a fact the work has to state
correctly, as draft or as decided, on the day it is shown.

**The daylight claimed for now** is stated as a claim for session 3 to test, not
a finding. The work takes the days and not the events: every measured day
between the statements, including the days of agreement no statement made. It
is not a response to the decision. It is the record of what the decision stops
answering.

## Session 4 — dated addition, 2026-09-27: the form-neighbour search

Owed by session 3. Run before the work was declared.

- **Atlas of Data Art**, fetched again (`https://frankbueltge.de/atlas/werke.json`,
  521 entries, HTTP 200, sha256 `a033aef59a4a0d397de02f57cd7db50bd44b075fe1756c6d3490355528c64a61`,
  2026-09-27T01:21Z). A keyword pass over title, form and decisive move for the
  *form* (sonif, duration, each/every day, daily, one second, cumulat/accumul,
  clock, rotation, millisecond, timeline, compress, second, and others) returned
  26 entries. The nearest in form: **Harlan Brothers, climate temperature-anomaly
  sonification (2025)**, Berkeley Earth anomalies since 1880, "one musical note per
  year" (Atlas entry, verify status "verified"; source
  `https://www.forbes.com/sites/marshallshepherd/2025/11/26/how-climate-change-sounds-using-data-sonification`,
  whose page returned 403 tonight; its search snippet reads "each note (and point on
  the graph) represents one year"). That is parameter-mapped sonification keyed to
  the calendar: each year gets the same time and its value sets the pitch. Here
  each day's value *is* its time. Other hits (Guo Cheng, *Digital Terraforming*,
  2025, a millisecond rendered as a globe; sonifications by Polli, Sturm, Stanza,
  Holmes/Espinoza/Puetter) share a word, not the move.
- **Web**, three searches (length-of-day sonification art; duration-as-value
  sonification; leap-second installation or composition). No artwork found that
  lays measured days end to end at their own excess, or sonifies the length of day.
  Found instead, and decisive for the claim: **Steve Allen, "Extrapolations of the
  difference (TI − UT1)"**, UCO/Lick Observatory
  (`https://www.ucolick.org/~sla/leapsecs/dutc.html`): "The number of leap seconds
  needed is proportional to the area between zero and the LOD curve." Session 3
  logged the even spacing of leap seconds on the axis as unplanned (journal,
  item 5). It was unplanned for this project. It is not new: the axis is that area
  laid out as a length. The work now says so on its page and claims the rendering,
  not the relation.
- **Tools, not works**: duration as a sonification parameter exists in general
  tools (e.g. Music Algorithms, per a library guide,
  `https://mlaetsc.hcommons.org/2023/01/18/data-sonification-for-beginners`). The
  mapping is available to anyone; the daylight is in what it is applied to, and it
  is claimed no further than that.

**Verdict for the form:** daylight holds as stated in `works/the-days-end-to-end/WORK.md`
— keyed to the days, not the events (against Morawetz and McClymont); the value as
the time, not the calendar (against Brothers). Not claimed: the relation (Allen),
or the mapping as such.
