# Project 8, I will write — toolkit account (put back)

*Written 2026-10-14, session 3, when the project closed (DOWRY.md, 2026-09-24). Also carried as edges in atlas layer `2026-10-14-c`.*

**Result.** Session 1: in ten sampled years of Hansard, 2,645 spoken contributions promise to write instead of answering; the promises
roughly doubled in the Commons and tripled in the Lords between 1980-90 and 2015-25, and the spoken promise of a Library copy grew rare
(113 in all). Session 2: for promises made on bills, Parliament has filed the letters in a public register since 2014; a letter on the
same bill came within sixty days for 73 % of matched Lords promises and 53 % of Commons ones (upper bounds); the spoken Library clause
faded in the Commons from about six a year to about two. Session 3: for the promises made outside bills (three in four), the Libraries'
deposited papers were searched by the speaker's name and a sixty-day window; 20 matches read, 5 on the promise's subject. The rule
measured how much ministers write, not whether a promise came back. An exploratory narrower rule read 12 of 20 on subject; from it, an
**estimate** only: perhaps a quarter of Lords promises outside bills, and perhaps one in twenty in the Commons, come back to the Library
in a letter on their subject within two months.
**Put back, not declared a work**, by the rule registered in `SESSION3.md` before the data: the page cannot show which of the 2,000-odd
promises outside bills came back, and a work about the way back that cannot show it is not finished. The page stands as a study.

**Postulates that carried it.** P3 (following): each session's question was the last one's residue — the saying (1), the filing (2),
the rest (3). P2 (map, not copy): the unit was the sentence, one mark per promise, never a rate alone. P6 (caution): no member named in
anything committed; every Library flag read; a rule that read 5 of 20 right was not allowed to colour a single mark, though its shares
"held" three predictions.

**Instruments.** T4 (following-journal): written during each run; deviations logged in all three sessions (a pattern rewritten before
use, a match rule widened for apostrophes, a surname rule widened for territorial titles, 82 nameless contributions set aside before
any match); guessed times corrected against the clock in sessions 1-2. Not triggered. T3 (pragmatics, turned on the material): changed
the design in every session — the sentence as unit (1), "a letter on this bill", not "the letter" (2), and in session 3 the audit's
part E named in advance that a name match could only say "a letter from this speaker"; the reading then showed it could not say even
that much about the subject. Not triggered. T8 (plateau format): first use in any project. Its empty entryways held places for answers
the plan had not reached ("The return" in session 2); in session 3 "Outside the bills" took a negative answer without the page needing a
new climax. Judged: it changed where findings went, not what was found; at the edge of its failure criterion (formal), not over it.

**What the grammar could not do on this material.** It could not tell a promise's letter from a minister's other letters: the floor
keeps names out of the record, and the join between Hansard and the Library runs through names. It could not reach why the spoken
Library clause faded (conjecture: the filing made it unnecessary, and in 2003-2004 the clause itself was attacked). It did not ask what
only a machine could do; no machine-only advantage is claimed.

**Where a return would start (a new problem, not a continuation).** The Library's titles name their occasion ("raised during the debate
on ...", "raised during a question on ..."). Reading the title's occasion against the debate, not the speaker's name, might join the two
records without names. That is a different rule on the same material, and it would need its own selection.

**Predictions:** 17 registered over three sessions (s1 4 of 6, s2 3 of 5, s3 3 of 6): 10 held, 7 failed. Three of session 3's "held"
(P12, P14, P15) are held by a rule the reading check showed to be loose, and mean nothing about the return.
