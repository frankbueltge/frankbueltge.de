# Project 8 — neighbours (session 1, in progress)

*Searched 2026-10-14, beginning in the Atlas of Data Art (`https://frankbueltge.de/atlas/werke.json`, 523 entries tonight), over every
field for parliament, hansard, politician, minister, letter(s), promise, congress, bundestag, speech, deferr-, unanswered, postal, mail.*

**In the Atlas.**
- **Angelica Mesiti, *ASSEMBLY*** (2019; Venice Biennale, Australian Pavilion; the atlas marks it "toVerify"). Follows the stenographic
  keyboard of parliamentary record-keeping as its data is translated into word, code, music and choreography. Nearest in material (the
  parliamentary record as made), far in move: it stages the recording, not what the record lets go.
- **Dries Depoorter, *The Flemish Scrollers*** (2021-2026, ongoing; `https://driesdepoorter.be/theflemishscrollers/`, atlas
  "verified"). Machine vision on the Flemish parliament's livestream, posting each politician caught on a phone. Near in subject (a
  legislature watched by a machine), opposite in ethic: it names and tags persons; this project names none.
- **Giorgia Lupi & Stefanie Posavec, *Dear Data*** (2014-2015). Letters as the form of data. Near only in the letter.

**Outside the Atlas.**
- **Denis Beaubois, *Indivisible*** (Tin Sheds Gallery, University of Sydney, 20 February - 4 April 2025; page fetched 2026-10-14:
  `https://www.sydney.edu.au/architecture/about/tin-sheds-gallery/past-exhibitions/indivisible.html`). "An examination of
  Parliamentary language since Federation", with historic Hansard records credited to Tim Sherratt; includes *To fill the air (House of
  Reps)*. The nearest found: a body of work that mines Hansard. Daylight: it reads what parliament says of a nation; this reads the one
  sentence by which parliament stops saying anything in public.
- **The Hansard record itself names the move.** A Lords question of 2005-01-20 (contribution `be17c1fb-ed96-4d8c-98bd-dc18dd6d26c2`)
  says that ministers answer by saying "that they will write to the honourable Member, which more or less buries the question". The
  suspicion is old and in the record; what it lacks is a count.
- **The Bills API register of "will write letters"** (publication type 44, `https://bills-api.parliament.uk/api/v1/PublicationTypes`).
  Parliament's own return path for promises made in bill debates. Not a work; the institution's answer to the same problem.

Daylight, after session 1: no work found counts the promise to write, or reads each one for whether it says the answer will come back. The
nearest (Beaubois) mines what parliament says; this reads the sentence by which an answer leaves the public record. No machine-only
advantage is claimed yet.
