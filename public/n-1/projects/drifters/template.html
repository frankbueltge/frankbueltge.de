After the drogue

A drifter is a float put into the sea to be carried by it. Below it, on a tether, hangs a drogue, a sea anchor of nylon cloth: "The drogue is centered at a depth of 15 meters", and it is "designed to move with the near-surface ocean currents", so the float goes where the water goes. Each drifter reports its position by satellite for months or years; the programme that puts them out, NOAA's Global Drifter Program, has kept them in the water since 1979.

Drogues come off. The float goes on reporting, but "if the drogue is lost at sea, the wind and waves play a greater role in moving the drifter", "much as a ping pong ball or beach ball is pushed across the surface of a pool" (the programme's own answers to frequent questions). It is no longer following the water; it is being carried. The programme writes down, for each drifter, the day its drogue came off, and has re-estimated many of those days later, from how the float moved. This page asks how much of the record of following the sea was made after the following had stopped.

Re-dated: following, re-stated as carried

The day a drogue came off is not seen; it is worked out afterwards, from how the float behaved: whether it rode under, whether it slid downwind, how its signal came and went (the programme's own list). Between February 2012 and June 2013 the programme re-worked it for {{RE_FILE}} drifters of 1992 to 2010, first by a method and then by a person, drifter by drifter, and published each version (droguedetect, after Lumpkin and others, 2013). For {{RE_N}} of them the loss moved earlier. {{FC_M}} million drifter-days that the record had counted as following the water were re-stated as carried, against {{CF_K}} thousand moved the other way. Half of the re-dated drifters had been carried for more than {{MED}} days while counted as following; the longest, for six years. The programme kept every version public, which is the only reason this can be counted: the re-dating is a correction made in the open, not a fault found here.

Where the carried ones end

The longest-carried drifters went on for seven or eight years after their drogues came off. All ten were last heard far out in an ocean (three of them are still reporting), near 30° north or south: where the great subtropical gyres turn, and where, as oceanographers report, floating things gather (that last is not checked here: conjecture). What this is, and is not

Data: NOAA Atlantic Oceanographic and Meteorological Laboratory, Global Drifter Program, the four drifter directory files (dirfl_*.dat), fetched {{FETCHED}}. Counted from the directory only: start, end, drogue-off date and death code per drifter; no path is drawn, so the map shows where drifters began and stopped, not where they went. Drogue dates are the programme's own estimates and are revised. A lost drogue is wear, not fault, and an undrogued drifter's record is still used (with care) by oceanographers; "carried" here names what the float was doing, not a judgment of the data. Of {{N_UNK}} drifters the drogue record cannot be read. Read against the raw rows: 40 random drifters and the 10 longest carried (no error found). The re-dating: AOML's drogue-detection files, the version of 2013-06-30 and its seventeen predecessors, fetched {{FETCHED2}}; 30 random rows and the 10 largest shifts read against the raw lines (15 rows without an automatic date were dropped by the registered rule and restored; the predictions did not change).
