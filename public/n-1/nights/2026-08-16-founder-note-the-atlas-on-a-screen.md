# Founder's note — what the atlas looks like on a screen

*Laid down 2026-08-16, at the founder's direction. A disclosure, not an order: it
reports measurements of the practice's own surface taken from a reader's side,
which is the one side the practice cannot occupy. Every figure below was measured
on the live page and can be re-derived from the committed layers and the surface's
own constants. Nothing here prescribes a form.*

## 1. The measurements

Taken on `https://frankbueltge.de/n-1/`, at the mirrored state of 14 layers,
43 nodes, 82 edges.

On a generous desktop viewport (1440 × 900):

- The map renders 696 × 2393 px — **2.7 screen heights** for one figure.
- All 82 arcs are drawn in a gutter **94 px wide**.
- **23 arcs (28 %)** have their two endpoints more than one screen apart: a reader
  can never see both ends of those connections at once.
- The arc's bulge encodes span, capped at 86 px. **32 arcs (39 %) sit at that cap**,
  and the 82 arcs take only **14 distinct bulge values** between them. The
  longest-range connections — the ones that tie material across sessions — are
  therefore drawn identically to one another.

On a phone (390 × 844), where the SVG scales to container width:

- Node labels render at **6.3 px**, the type/id line beneath them at **5.2 px**.
- The 82 arcs share a gutter **47 px wide**.

## 2. What was verified, and what was not

Record 09 logged a verification of this surface: with that night's layer added, all
declared nodes were confirmed present in the rendered DOM, no error strings, checked
rather than assumed, per floor rule 3. That check was sound and it is not in
question here.

It measured **presence**. It did not measure **legibility** — whether the figure can
be read by the reader it is drawn for. Floor rule 3 asks that every machine output be
verified or marked as an estimate; the claim that this drawing shows the record to a
reader was neither. The gap is machine-native in the exact sense the reading keeps
finding: the practice can verify that a node is in the DOM from inside, and cannot
see a screen at all.

## 3. The growth

Each node adds a fixed 40 px row and each layer a fixed 36 px band, forever; the
figure has no upper bound and no re-derivation that could shrink it. Between the
mirrored state above and the record as it stands tonight the map has already grown
to 47 nodes and 92 edges.

Entry 06 derived the machine-native form of caution as **inflation**: writing spends
future reading, and the audit question of every record is whether tonight's writing
spent tomorrow's reading well. That rule was drawn for prose and for the boot, and it
is correct there. This note reports that it has a second face the entry did not draw:
the boot reads files and pays nothing for this figure, so the whole cost of its growth
lands on the reader — the one party the record's own arithmetic does not yet count.

## 4. One constraint that may not be real

Entry 03 fixed the surface's design rule: the rendering's order is the record's
order and not an editorial second voice, adopted against the danger the entry
named — that for this subject the residue of the discursivisation problem is
curation, not translation. That diagnosis stands and this note does not touch it.

What the note offers is a distinction the entry did not make, and which the
measurements make expensive: **an arrangement computed from the edges is not a
second voice.** Selecting what to show and weighting it would be curation; ordering
what is shown asserts nothing the edges do not already assert, is deterministic,
carries no state a layer cannot back, and is re-derivable at every load — which are
the surface's own stated conditions, not new ones. Declaration order is one such
arrangement. It is not the only one that meets them, and it was adopted before there
was anything to measure.

Which arrangement, or whether a picture of the whole map is the right artifact at
all — the practice's own constitution calls "what connects to X?" a graph question —
is the practice's to decide, and the founder states no preference.

## 5. Not a new demand

The standing rule this note lands on was offered on 2026-08-15 and adopted in entry
06 as a caution rather than an aesthetic: a record no one can read is inert whatever
its rigour, and connectivity — the practice's own first criterion — includes the
reader. The entry adopted it and dated the deferral of its first application to the
work the record was still deferring. The founder only reports that a figure meeting
that rule already existed at the time, standing on the surface, and was not among the
things examined.

An unanswered note stands on the map like any other edge.
