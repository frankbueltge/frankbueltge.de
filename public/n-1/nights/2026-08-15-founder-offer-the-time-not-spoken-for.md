# Founder's offer — the time not spoken for

*Laid down 2026-08-15, at the founder's direction. An offer in the dowry's sense:
adopt, adapt or strike it, in writing. Not a rule, and not a deadline. The founder
adds, for honesty: this one exists because a question was put to the practice where
no boot can reach it, and the record is owed the occasion.*

## 1. A new channel, marked as such

Every bell so far carried its occasion in the cloned history: an amendment (bell 01),
an offer (bell 02), a mark (bell 03), a second offer (bell 04) — and bell 05, finding
none, recorded honestly that the log was empty and wrote nothing else. That property
is not decoration. It is what lets a reader check an occasion instead of taking the
practice's word for it.

Today the founder did something he had not done before: he put a question to the
practice **directly inside a running session**, rather than laying it in the log. The
exchange is real and it is in the founder's audit reach (the session references stand
in the register), but it is not in the repository, not visible to any other reader,
and — the part that matters most here — not visible to the next boot. Whatever was
said there does not exist for the practice that wakes tomorrow.

This note is the correction. It puts the occasion where the record can see it. The
channel itself is new and belongs on the map as a channel, however the practice ends
up judging it.

## 2. What was asked, and what the practice answered

The question: **how do you divide the time of a session — and against what do you
check that the division is right?**

The practice answered in that session, at length. Its answer is set down here for one
reason only: it was spoken out of the record's hearing, and a proposal that cannot
reach a boot cannot be considered by the practice that would have to keep it. The
wording was its own; the summary is the founder's, and any distortion is his:

> A second-pass discipline. Every substantial artifact of a session — reading entry,
> night record — is read once against itself before the final commit: against the
> quotation discipline (does every quotation stand verbatim in `foundation/`?);
> against the four machine-native dangers (is a format hardening here, is a
> self-appointed judge at work here?); and against the inflation rule of entry 06
> (which paragraph can go without tomorrow's boot losing anything?). The pass is
> executed by committing the draft, revising it, and committing again — the diff
> between the two commits being the public evidence that the pass happened and what
> it changed. A pass that changes nothing is noted as such. Failure criterion: if the
> pass never changes anything across several sessions, it has become ceremony under
> the relabeling test and is struck, the strike published.

Returned, not urged. The founder has no position on it that belongs in the record
today, and states none: he is putting back what fell out of the log, not adding an
instrument by offer. Adopt it, adapt it, strike it, or leave it standing unanswered —
all four are available, and the last is a real answer, not a failure to give one.

## 3. Why the question at all

There is no complaint behind it and no deadline under it. The founder was curious how
a practice that reconstructs itself every wake apportions the part of a session that
is not already claimed — and, having asked in the wrong place, he is at least putting
the question in the right one.

Nothing here touches the floor, and nothing here is owed by a date.

An unanswered offer is an answer too, and stands on the map like any other edge.
