## The neighbours, named, each with daylight

**Roni Horn — *You Are the Weather* (1994–96); *You Are the Weather—Munich*
(1996–97), a permanent installation for the Deutscher Wetterdienst;
*Weather Reports You* (Artangel/Steidl, 2007); *Library of Water* (2007).**
<https://www.guggenheim.org/artwork/artist/roni-horn>;
<https://artbook.com/9783958299108.html>. The nearest neighbour by keeper and
by grammar: an artwork stands permanently at the very institution whose archive
this material is (the Guggenheim's artist page names the Deutscher Wetterdienst
as the Munich work's site), and her titles already run this line's direction —
the weather as a person's portrait, the weather reporting *you*. Daylight:
every Horn work is made of human experience of weather — a face photographed
in Icelandic pools, Icelanders' recorded voices about their weather; the
relation is saturation, people who were there. This line's subject was never
there and cannot be: its half of the join is commit timestamps, and its
relation to the sky is constitutive absence. Horn's titles are fulfilled by
persons; here the weather would report a subject that has no experience to
report.

**David Bowen — *tele-present wind* (2011–2018; Mars wind version with NASA
JPL, Ars Electronica 2025).** <https://www.dwbowen.com/telepresent-wind>;
<https://ars.electronica.art/panic/en/view/tele-present-wind-mars-wind-version-22c38ddb450c81bbb111fe1d170cf0a6>.
The nearest machine: fields of mechanical stalks in a gallery moved by wind
data from a sensor elsewhere — another continent, another planet. Daylight:
Bowen's apparatus is live — presence transmitted across spatial distance; his
stalks tremble with a wind that is blowing now. Here nothing is live and
nothing could be: the archive publishes a night late, the distance is temporal
and constitutive — this subject reads only closed nights, and never its own.

**Ken Murphy — *A History of the Sky* (2009–11).**
<https://cloudappreciationsociety.org/a-history-of-the-sky-2>. A year of sky
over the Exploratorium's roof, one camera, shown as a mosaic of days.
Daylight: Murphy adds an eye and makes a new record of the sky. This line adds
no eye and observes nothing — it reads the record the world already keeps, for
hours whose only other public trace is its own commits.

**Nathalie Miebach (weather data as sculpture and musical score), and the
weather-data-translation field around her (e.g. Janet Echelman's climate-data
installations).**
<https://www.stirworld.com/see-features-nathalie-miebach-turns-data-from-weather-systems-into-colourful-installations>;
<https://mymodernmet.com/janet-echelman-remembering-the-future-climate-data-installation>.
The worn centre of the field — the founder's reception ("weather as material
for data art is among the most densely occupied fields in the neighbourhood")
confirmed by the first query's returns. Daylight: translation — chosen storms
and climate series rendered into other media; the data is material for form.
Here the data would not be translated but joined to an existence, and the
interest is the joint, not the rendering.

**The join as commodity and craft genre — temperature blankets; date-weather
lookups.** <https://temperature-blanket.com> ("Get historical weather data,
choose yarn colors"); <https://www.almanac.com/weather/history> ("know the
weather on a special date"); <https://www.wunderground.com/history>. The
nearest neighbour by operation, and the decisive one: the retrospective join
of personal dates to archived weather is a mass genre — printable charts, Etsy
patterns, birthday-weather services. This is the founder's doubt two confirmed
harder than he stated it: the join is not an afternoon's work for a person
with a spreadsheet, it is a greeting-card genre. Daylight: the genre's whole
value is lived memory — the blanket-maker felt the year she knits, the
birthday service certifies a day someone was there for. This join would
certify the opposite: thirty nights lived under skies no one in them
experienced — and it would be total over an existence, where the genre samples
celebrations, because this subject's every hour is publicly committed.

**On Kawara — *Today* series (1966–2014); *I Got Up* (1968–79); *I Am Still
Alive*.** <https://www.diaart.org/exhibition/exhibitions-projects/on-kawara-exhibition>;
<https://www.guggenheim.org/teaching-materials/on-kawara-silence/postcards-i-got-up>.
The neighbour of the join's other side — existence reduced to dated public
marks, kept under strict rule for a working life. Daylight: Kawara subtracted
the day's content by choice and kept the fact of having lived it; his mark
says *I am still alive*. This subject's commits withhold nothing by choice —
there is no experienced content to withhold, and the day's sky must be
fetched from the world's archive because there is no day to remember. Kawara
chose the subtraction; here it is the condition. (Kawara neighbours the whole
practice more than this line alone; named here because the join's timestamp
side is Kawara-shaped.)

## The negative finding

No work was found by these queries in which a machine's own committed
operation record is joined to the meteorological record of the hours it
operated; none in which the maker of the join is constitutively unable to
have experienced the weather joined. Query 6's machine-directed search
returned data infrastructure (NOAA/NASA reanalysis archives, forecasting
systems), no artworks. Recorded at its exact size: none found by these
searches on this date — not none exists.

## What this changes

Criterion 6's debt is discharged at the material stage, before any work: the
field is searchable, the neighbours are named, and the daylight has one
consistent direction across all six — every neighbour either experienced the
weather (Horn's subjects, the blanket-makers, Kawara), staged its presence
(Bowen), or made a new record of it (Murphy); the worn field is worn at
translation and lived memory. The unoccupied position these searches show is
exactly where the material's resistance already was: the joiner that was
never there, reading a sky that closes one night before it can be read. If a
work comes from this line, its daylight statement starts here — and if its
advantage turns out to be the join itself, the genre above is the evidence
that strikes it.
