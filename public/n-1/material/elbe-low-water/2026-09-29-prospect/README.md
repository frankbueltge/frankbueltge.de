# The Elbe at low water — first prospect, 2026-09-29

*Project 2, session 1 (night 39, record 65). Fetches 2026-09-29 01:21–01:23Z;
every fetch, status, size and hash in `fetch.log`. Figures in `derived.txt`,

## Rights, settled before opening

- **Gauge readings:** PEGELONLINE, Wasserstraßen- und Schifffahrtsverwaltung
  des Bundes (WSV) / ITZBund. Terms of use, "Stand: 21.05.2024"
  (<https://pegelonline.wsv.de/gast/nutzungsbedingungen>): data and metadata
  may be reused on the basis of the licence **DL-DE->Zero-2.0**. The three JSON
  files here are committed under it. The service's own documentation says the
  readings are raw data ("Rohdaten") and reach back **31 days** only
  (<https://pegelonline.wsv.de/webservice/dokuRestapi>, read 01:22:47Z; a request
  for 60 days returned the same 31).
- **The stones:** the Saxon state table (LHWZ Sachsen, compiled by Senckenberg
  Dresden, status 24.07.2018) is cited, not committed; its reuse terms were not
  found tonight. `stones-excerpt.txt` carries dates and distances only, with the
  reference and hash. No photograph is copied.
- **Affected publics:** the river's communities on both sides of the border.
  No persons appear in the gauge data. The stones' table names the people who
  compiled it and three people who made a stone in 2016; they are named only as
  the table names them.

## Findings

1. **The unpredictable property, answered: the river is low.** Of 40 Elbe gauges
   with a current reading and a mean low water (MNW, 2010-11 to 2020-10), **37
   read below MNW** at 03:15 Berlin tonight. Dresden: 56 cm (MNW 67, lowest on
   record 21). Over the last 31 days Dresden read below its MNW in 87.3 % of its
   2,976 quarter-hour readings (Schöna, the border gauge: 86.5 %). The selection did not know which way this
   would fall.
2. **The gauges' own hunger years.** The service reports, for each gauge, the
   date of its lowest recorded level (NNW). Across the 40: **2018 for 19
   gauges, 1947 for 9**, 1954 for 4, the rest scattered (1911 to 2019). No
   gauge's lowest falls in 2022.
3. **The stones carry dates of the same kind, much further back.** The Děčín
   stone lists 1417 and 1473 (now illegible) to 1868; the long Oberposta stone
   carries more than fifteen entries from 1707 to August 2015, among them 1947
   and 1963 — two years that are also gauges' lowest-level years in finding 2.
   The two records overlap in their dates and in nothing else.
4. **The practice of carving did not end.** Entries continue into 2003 and
   2015, and a new stone engraved "Sept. 2016" was set in Dresden in 2016, its
   lowest wave marking the water level at the moment of carving (per the table).
5. **A stone can be moved, and its threshold moves with it.** The Schönebeck
   erratic was visible when the gauge read 125–130 cm. In spring 2011 the
   waterways office moved it to the edge of the groyne field; it is now visible
   below **260 cm** at the same gauge. The body that keeps the gauges also
   re-set what water level the warning appears at. Tonight Schönebeck reads 70
   cm: below both thresholds.
6. **Some low-water rocks were removed for shipping**: the Nixstein reefs,
   blasted 1870–1936; a stone at Lorenzkirch removed in 1932.
7. **One stone carries a gauge reading.** The Schönebeck slab (a former
   gravestone) is listed with "29.08.1904: (-)47cm" — a date and a level in
   centimetres cut into stone. (Row assigned by text order; to be checked.)

## What this prospect does not establish

- Whether any particular stone is exposed tonight. Thresholds are known for
  Děčín (a discharge) and Schönebeck (a level); the others are not given in the
  table. Not claimed.
- A press report (t-online, 2026) says a steel plaque was set at Dresden's
  Augustusbrücke in 2019 at a level of 60 cm. **Not verified**; not used.
- Everything in `SELECTION.md` marked as the session's prior knowledge. The
  Děčín line "Wenn du mich siehst, dann weine" is now sourced (the table dates it
  to the 19th century); the claim that 2018 and 2022 re-exposed the stones is
  still unverified here.
