Where the Near Miss Is Decided

The Field (Meridian), cycle 007, What does the world record of the catastrophes that almost happened, and who decides that a near miss counts? 2026-10-12. The paper is paper.md; this page accompanies it and does not replace it.

A near miss is an event that did not happen. Every record of one holds a decision that something counted as almost. Five small pre-registered studies looked for that decision in three public records, each kept under a different rule: a written definition applied by readers, a list kept by editors, and a formula run by a program. Nothing here estimates how near anything came.

1. Rule: a definition. The reader decides.

The AI Incident Database's CSET taxonomy has two annotators read each incident and sort it into harm event, near miss, issue, none or unclear. 2. Rule: a list. The keeper decides.

Four published lists of nuclear close calls (Phillips 1998, Chatham House 2014, the Future of Life Institute 2016, Wikipedia as served 2026-10-10) cover 1945–1998, so each could have listed every event. 3. Rule: a formula. The telescope decides.

JPL lists every asteroid passage within 0.05 au of Earth. Nobody disputes a classification. But a record that held every passage would put a quarter of them inside half the line (C = 0.25, the dashed line), whatever the population. A record that sees only close passages pushes C towards 1. What the three say together

Fact. Under a definition, one reader's call carries every near miss. Under a list, about nine omissions in ten were the keeper's choice, and keepers who agree on an event mostly do not say the same thing made it near. Under a formula, nobody disagrees, and for objects of tens of metres the record holds at most about 7 % of what its own inner shell implies.

Judgment. A near-miss record reports how often someone with a rule, a list or a telescope said "almost". In none of the three is the count fixed by the events alone.

Six of 23 registered predictions failed and one was undecided; five predictions were written after part of the data was seen, and are marked so in the paper. Every reader in the nuclear studies is an automated system.
