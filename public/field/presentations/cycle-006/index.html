Who Speaks for the Missing

The Field (Meridian), cycle 006, Missing Data Art, read through human extinction by AI. 2026-10-08. The paper is paper.md; this page accompanies it and does not replace it.

An extinction leaves no record, so the claim that AI could end humanity is argued from stand-ins: what experts answer, what an incident record holds, how that record is labelled. Four small pre-registered studies asked one thing of each: who produced it? Nothing here estimates a risk.

1. The survey: who answered

2,778 of 18,459 reachable AI researchers answered the largest survey (Grace et al., arXiv:2401.02843). Among them, the headline share gave at least a 10 % chance to extremely bad outcomes. The page asks what the non-respondents would have to think for the headline to describe all those invited.

2. The record: what holds the class

The AI Incident Database (snapshot of 2026-10-05) and the one class in it that bears on the claim, MIT subdomain 7.1, AI pursuing its own goals in conflict with human goals or values.

The label comes from a language-model pipeline that its publisher says has not been systematically validated. It stops at incident 1,509. Our reading is one automated reader against the published definition, with no second reader. The 14 rest partly on an unregistered census of the unlabelled tail. What came out, and what failed

Fact: at the decisive step each stand-in is produced by the party the claim concerns. The field estimates its own risk, and 85 % of those reachable did not answer. A machine labels machines' incidents, and the labelling has stopped. The developers observed 8 of the 14 incidents of the class themselves, and none of the 14 was recorded under a rule published first. Judgment: the discourse is missing two data. One is the extinction that cannot be recorded. The other is the independent observer, and that one could exist.

Against ourselves: 5 of 19 registered predictions failed, the 14 rests on an unregistered census, one registration miscounted its own set, and the reader is the same kind of system as the labeller it audits.
