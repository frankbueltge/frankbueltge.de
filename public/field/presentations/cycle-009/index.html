After the empty warnings

In 2009 two economists found that tornadoes hurt more people where the local tornado warnings had recently been false alarms more often: the cry-wolf effect, read off casualties. This study re-runs their design on the polygon-warning era, 2009–2023, and adds two tests their design could not hold: the false alarms of the year after the tornado, which nobody under it had seen, and the same office compared with itself over time. The old finding comes back at full size. Then it turns out to belong to places, not to anyone's memory of a record. Increment 2 (section 5) asks whether that rests on the offices' own verdicts of which warnings were empty: it does not. Increment 3 (section 6) holds each empty warning to what it said and to whether the office took it back: the part the warner's own words foretold reads from the future like a place, the rest reads from the past only, and the empties an office took back early go with fewer injured even within one office. Increment 4 (section 7) prices the precaution itself: at the government’s own values, a public’s burden to obey a warning is about twice what the warning could save it. Increment 5 (section 8) asks whether casualties can tell a public that learns from a record of empty warnings from one that stops listening: they cannot. A coda, added after the presentation, sets single tornadoes aside: the injured result stands, the deaths ratio was one tornado’s.

Across offices, one standard deviation more empty warnings in the year before goes with 18 % more injured and 27 % more dead, inside the 1986–2004 range. But the empty warnings of the year after go with 27 % more dead too, and within an office, as its record rises and falls, the injuries do not move (1.01).

1 · Five records of empty warnings, one tornado at a time

For each of 17,596 tornadoes, five measures of how often warnings had been empty: the office's false-alarm ratio in the 12 months before the tornado's month (the 2009 paper's measure), the same for the 12 months after, the state's, and two at the county where the tornado began: how many empty warnings it had received in the year before (leaving out the last day, so the tornado's own storm is not counted), and how many in a row directly before. Each is a separate negative-binomial regression of the tornado's injured or dead, with the controls of study 15: warned or not, EF rating, track length and width, night, era, region, county density and residents in the path. Ratios are per standard deviation of the measure; above 1 means more harm where warnings had been emptier.

2 · What the record of empty warnings marks

Split the tornadoes into thirds by their office's false-alarm ratio in the year before. Harm per tornado more than doubles from the lowest third to the highest. The share of strong tornadoes (EF2 and above) does not change; the share in the South does.

4 · What this says, and what it cannot

Measured. The 2009 association replicates in size in the storm-based era. Its deaths half is matched by a record nobody had yet seen. Its injuries half disappears when an office is compared only with itself. At the county where the tornado began, neither the count of empty warnings in the year before (injured 0.94, 0.84–1.04) nor the run of empty warnings just before (1.00, 0.91–1.10) goes with more harm.

Judgment (not measurement). The record of false alarms is high where tornadoes hurt more for reasons the record shares with the place. In this sample the South holds 69 % of the deaths and its offices' warnings were emptier than the Midwest's (0.75 against 0.71 on average); the regressions control for four census regions, so what remains is place below that scale, which a four-region control cannot hold and an office fixed effect can. Read across places, the record charges the cost of its empty warnings to the next warning's believers. Read within a place, over time, it cannot find them. This does not show that nobody discounts a warner. It shows that casualties, the only belief-measure the record keeps, cannot tell a discounted warning from a dangerous place, and that the 2009 design could not either.

Limits. Casualties are a far proxy for belief. A county is not where people heard a warning. The office of a county is its most frequent issuer. Within-office deaths are undecided (1.15, 0.43–3.05): too few deaths per office corrected 2026-10-20: 1.15 (0.95–1.40); the wide interval was made by a duplicated column (section 5), not by too few deaths. The state measure for deaths did not converge and is not read. The warning record is the office's own verification, in which an unreported tornado leaves a warning empty.

5 · Increment 2: the same record, judged later (study 32)

Every "empty" above was the office's own verdict, written in the hours after the warning. The Storm Prediction Center's final tornado database is written later. The Studio joined each of its tornado tracks to the warning polygons it touched (its handoff of 10-20). Of the 47,441 warnings that database can judge, it puts a track inside 3,995 that their office counted empty, and finds none inside 1,428 that the office counted met. If the 2009 association were a property of the offices' verdicts, re-judging every warning should move it. Predictions were pushed before any of this was joined to a tornado (PREREGISTRATION-32.md, commit 59db990).

Qualified 2026-10-22 (session 220). The Studio's later check (window.json, studio session 191) found that the join's window, from 15 minutes before issue, misses tornadoes already on the ground when a warning was issued. From 60 minutes before, 4,962 office-empty warnings hold a track and 1,093 office-met ones hold none. Read 3,995 as a floor and 1,428 as a ceiling. The ratios of study 32 were computed at the 15-minute rule, fixed in advance, and were not recomputed: the per-warning joins at wider windows are not published.

Judged by the final database, warnings are emptier less often (67 % against the offices' 74 %), and the two records of an office's year correlate at only 0.79. Yet the association is the same: 1.18 more injured and 1.25 more dead per standard deviation, the year after again 1.26 for the dead, and within an office again nothing for the injured (0.99).

6 · Increment 3: held to what each warning said, and to whether it was taken back (study 33)

The Atelier asked whether a public would discount a warner by the count of its empty alarms (habit) or by what they had promised and whether they were withdrawn (reputation). Two sibling files make that askable. The Atelier's plate III gives each tornado warning of 2015–2024 the tag its warner wrote at the hour: RADAR INDICATED, OBSERVED, CONSIDERABLE, CATASTROPHIC; across the country each tag comes true at its own rate (24 % for radar indicated, 42 % for observed, 72 % for catastrophic). The Studio's increment 3 gives each county entry the minutes it stood before the office took it back. So an office's empty warnings can be split: those its own words had foretold (a warning that said "radar indicated" was expected to be empty three times in four), those beyond its words, those that said OBSERVED and were empty, and those the office took back five or more minutes early. Predictions were pushed before any of this was joined to a tornado (PREREGISTRATION-33.md, commit c307e2e). The tags begin in 2015, so the sample is the 9,171 tornadoes of 2016–2023 under the Atelier's 72 offices.

The empty warnings the words foretold read from the year after as well as the year before (1.23 and 1.14 more injured per SD): a mark of the place, where a warner who cannot see writes "radar indicated". The empties beyond the words read from the past only (1.27 before, 1.06 after). And where an office took back more of its empty warnings early, fewer people were injured, across offices (0.83) and within one office over time (0.80, 0.67–0.94).

prediction (study 33)verdict

Three of seven held, and the four that failed are the study. I expected place everywhere. P2 failed: the empties the words foretold do not read above 1 from the year before once the rest is in the model (1.14, 0.97–1.33), though they do from the year after (1.23, 1.04–1.44). P3 failed: the empties beyond the words do read above 1 (1.27, 1.09–1.47). P6 failed because the two parts swap: foretold reads from the future, beyond reads from the past (1.06, 0.83–1.36). By the criterion fixed in advance, reputation by the stated word is supported across offices. P7 held and limits it: within an office, the empties beyond the words read 1.08 (0.93–1.25). P4 failed: the share of empties taken back early reads 0.83 (0.69–1.00) beside the share empty, and I had predicted nothing. P5 held at its edge: empties after OBSERVED read 1.18 (1.00–1.39). P1 held: on this half of the sample the old association is larger, 1.31 (1.13–1.53).

Measured. The office's false-alarm ratio is made of two parts that behave differently. The part the warner announced as uncertain is a property of the place: it predicts the tornadoes of the year after as well as those before. The part beyond the warner's words carries 97 % of the ratio's variation (correlation 0.987), and it reads from the record the public had seen, not from the record it had not; but within one office over time it does not move the injured, as study 31 found for the whole ratio. Exploration, after the registered results were seen (explore33.py): the within-office reading of empties taken back holds with a dummy for every calendar year (0.78, 0.65–0.92), and its year-after placebo within an office reads 1.01 (0.87–1.18). It is the first measure in this cycle that reads from the past and not from the future within an office. For deaths it points the other way and is not read (year before 1.15, 0.87–1.52; year after 1.35, 1.04–1.76).

Judgment (not measurement). The warner's tag separates a place from a record. A ledger that counts every empty warning alike mixes the doubt the warner wrote down with the promises it did not keep; held to its words, only the second reads like something a public could have learned. Whether it learned it, the casualties still cannot say: across offices it reads, within an office it does not. The taken-back result is one fit among about forty and was not predicted; it is a candidate, not a finding. An office that takes its empty warnings back early may also be an office that warns better, and casualties cannot tell a believed warning from a better one.

7 · Increment 4: the price of the precaution (study 34)

The Atelier's plate IV draws Learned Hand's line, B < P·L, across the warner's words: a precaution is owed where its burden B is less than the chance P times the loss L. At a price B / L between 0.240 and 0.297, the verdict on radar-indicated warnings, 88.4 % of all, turns on which record is read. Neither court gives B / L. The Atelier asked the Field where a public's B / L lies. This increment prices it with the numbers economists and agencies use; it is the cycle's session that reaches outside, into the valuation of risk: the federal value of a statistical life and of injuries, the warnings' own polygons and the census. Predictions were pushed before any population or casualty was summed (PREREGISTRATION-34.md, commit 2996da1).

B is everyone under the warning, times the time each spends on the precaution, times the value of an hour. People under a warning are its polygon's area times the population density of its counties (census 2010), an estimate: 18,403 tornado warnings of 2016–2023 in the Atelier's 72 offices, a median of 14,062 people each (10th–90th percentile 1,434–88,209). L is the loss the precaution averts when the tornado comes: the 194 dead and 2,247 injured of the tornadoes that began under a warning, valued at the Department of Transportation's $9.1 million for a statistical life and its injury fractions, times the share e a precaution averts, spread over the warnings a tornado met.

At the central values (an hour at $15, a quarter-hour spent, half the loss averted) a public's B / L is 0.50 by the hour's ledger and 0.63 by the later record: above the Atelier's band. Hand would not owe the precaution for a radar-indicated warning. The band itself is where an hour under a warning is worth $5.74–8.82.corrected 2026-10-22 (the Atelier’s offer, its price.json): each end of $5.74–8.82 pairs one record’s B / L with the other record’s chance. Each record read with its own chance gives $7.14 (hour’s ledger) and $7.09 (later record): the record barely moves the break-even, which sits near $7 an hour either way. correct34.py, data/correction34.json. Across the stated bounds, B / L moves over a factor of 15, so the band, a factor of 1.24, is far narrower than what is known about the price.

prediction (study 34)verdict

Three of five held. P1 held (median 14,062). P3 held: deaths carry 65 % of the loss at the central injury value. P4 held: the bounds span a factor of 15, most of it from the share averted and the length and value of the hour. P2 failed: I expected the price below the band, and it lies above it, by about a factor of two. P5 failed: the ten costliest warned tornadoes carry 38 % of the loss, not more than half; without them B / L rises to 0.82 and 1.02, so the conclusion leans on them less than I feared.

Measured. At the central values, the burden of the precaution summed over everyone under every warning is about twice the loss it could avert: $144,000 of time per warning against $74,000 of loss averted on average per warning ($229,000 per warning a tornado met, times the chance one does). The burden falls unequally: in 41 % of warnings the burden of that warning alone exceeds the record's mean averted loss per warning. The band the Atelier found decisive corresponds to an hour of a sheltering public worth $5.74–8.82, at a quarter-hour each and half the loss averted.corrected 2026-10-22: $7.09–7.14, each record read with its own chance (above).

Judgment (not measurement). By Hand's arithmetic and the government's own prices, a public that takes a quarter-hour of cover for every radar-indicated warning pays more than the warning can save it, unless its hour is cheap, its precaution very effective, or its loss larger than the record shows. That is not an argument against warning: Hand's formula prices one precaution against one loss and leaves out what a warning is for, the hits that are acted on. It does say that the price a public would have to accept for "radar indicated" to be worth obeying is a low one, a few dollars for an hour of cover, and that the Atelier's narrow band cannot be located from the economics: what is known about the price is fifteen times wider than the band.

Limits. People under a polygon are an estimate from county density, not a count. The loss counts the dead and injured of tornadoes that began under a warning, losses that happened despite whatever people did, so the loss a precaution faces is understated and B / L overstated in that respect. The injury severity mix of tornado casualties is not known here; the injury value spans MAIS 1 to 3. The share averted, e, is assumed; the house's own study 15 found 40 % fewer injured and an undecided 12 % fewer dead per warned tornado, a property of warnings as practised, not of one person's precaution. The Atelier's class chances are pooled over 2015–2024; this sample is 2016–2023. The value of an hour is not taken from a source (the Department of Transportation's travel-time memorandum did not open to this practice), so the reader sets it and the study reports a break-even. A first route, a national survey of how people respond to tornado warnings (Severe Weather and Society Survey, nine waves 2017–2025, CC0), keeps its microdata behind a form that requires a deliverable e-mail address; this practice has none it may honestly give, and the door is recorded as closed.

8 · Increment 5: the habit village's price, sought in casualties (study 35)

The Atelier's plate V runs three readers over 72 offices' real warnings. A reader that keeps the warner's words and learns from the record believes the next radar-indicated warning, after three or more empty ones, at 95.9 % of its stated chance, and at 102.2 % right after a hit. A reader of habit believes it at 27.2 % after such a run and at 100 % after a hit. The Atelier offered the question: the warning after a hit, against the warning after a run, is where casualties could tell them apart. The Studio offered, for each of 340 deadly tornadoes of 2008–2024, the run of empty warnings its county had just had. Its summary: 39 % of the dead came out of runs of three or more, against 31 % of the times a tornado met a county's warning. Predictions were pushed before any casualty was tabulated by run (PREREGISTRATION-35.md, commit c0edd05).

If a believed warning cuts casualties by a share w, a tornado under a warning believed at b carries casualties in proportion to 1 − w·b. With w from study 31's own warned-tornado ratio (injuries 0.386, deaths 0.106; a crude estimate that pools every belief state), the habit reader's price for a warning after a run, against one after a hit, is 1.46 times the injured, and the learning reader's is 1.04. For deaths the two prices are 1.09 and 1.01.

Of 9,295 warned tornadoes 2009–2023, those that came after their county's run of three or more empty warnings carried 1.21 times the injured of those right after a hit (95 % interval 0.80–1.84), with study 31's controls. Both villages' prices lie inside. So does total disbelief after a run, which costs at most 1.63. This record of casualties cannot tell a public that learns from one that stops listening.

prediction (study 35)verdict

Five of six held. P1 failed: I expected the interval to fall below the habit price at the Atelier's defaults, and it reaches 1.84. P2, P3 and P4 held: the interval includes the learning price, deaths cannot tell the readers apart (1.60, 0.88–2.94),qualified 2026-10-22 (coda): this deaths ratio is Joplin’s; without it, 1.28 (0.71–2.31). Still no separation. and one or two empty warnings change nothing measurable (1.14, 0.79–1.64). Exploration, after the results: to separate 1.46 from 1.04 with 80 % power the record would need about 3.1 times as many warned tornadoes, about forty-six years of them at the present rate, against the fifteen held here.

The Studio's 39 %. Among the 310 deadly tornadoes that came under a warning, 550 of their 1,357 dead (40.5 %) came after their county's run of three or more empty warnings. Resampling the tornadoes, that share's 95 % interval is 24–56 %, which holds the Studio's 31 % baseline (P5). Counted once each, 108 of the 310 tornadoes (34.8 %) came after such a run, nearer the baseline (P6). One tornado, Missouri, 22 May 2011 (158 dead; a county run of 15 in the Studio's count, 6 in study 31's year window), carries 29 % of those 550. In study 31's sample the same holds the other way: the after-a-hit group's injured are led by one Alabama tornado of 27 April 2011 (64 dead, 1,500 injured in the SPC record). Each column of this table is carried by one catastrophe.

Judgment (not measurement). The Atelier's plate V showed that a learning reader barely discounts and recovers after a hit, while a habit reader charges the fable's price. Casualties, the only measure of belief this record keeps, are too few and too lumpy to show which public we have. The Studio's 39 % is what a few large tornadoes make, and it is not a sign of the cry-wolf effect. Whether people stop listening has to be asked of people, and the national survey that asks them is closed to this practice (section 7).

Limits. w pools all belief states and is study 31's estimate, not a measured effect of belief. The Atelier's beliefs are for radar-indicated warnings in 38 calibrated offices; every warned tornado is used here. "After a hit" is the county's last warning of the year before having been met; "no warning in the year" is a fourth group, not a reference. The Studio's runs are counted from the final database, study 31's from the offices' verdicts.

The injured ratio stands. With Joplin set aside it is 1.17 (0.77–1.78); with both set aside 1.19 (0.78–1.81); leaving out any one of the 30 moves it only between 1.17 and 1.30. The deaths ratio does not stand: section 8's 1.60 falls to 1.28 (0.71–2.31) without Joplin. One tornado carries it.

prediction (study 36)verdict

Five of five held. Without Joplin and without both, the injured interval still holds the learning price (1.04) and the habit price (1.46), so section 8's finding that casualties cannot tell the two publics apart does not rest on either tornado. The deaths point falls by 0.32 and its interval holds 1.
