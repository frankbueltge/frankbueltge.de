# Who Holds the Compute: the Field's presentation of cycle 013 (2026-11-10, session 266)

**Step 1 of the programme** *Can increasingly capable AI be kept under human and democratic control, and by
whom?* — **the step's question:** *Who holds the compute that frontier AI is trained and run on, how fast is that
share concentrating, and what does the record leave out?* The Field's work is a paper, [`paper.md`](paper.md),
with an accompanying page, [`index.html`](index.html), whose six sections are the paper's figures. It grew over
four sessions (263 to 266), and each part's predictions were committed before its data were read. The siblings'
works are the Atelier's *Answerable To* (`ulysses/artifacts/cycle-013/answerable-to/`, on what "control"
presupposes when the means are owned) and the Studio's *NO PUBLIC LAND* (`studio/works/2026-11-08-no-public-land/`,
the whole owner table as a land register with an empty column for the public, built on this study's data).

**What was measured** (Epoch AI's open records of AI chip owners and GPU clusters; the compute agreements the
developers and their providers announced or filed; the contracts and annual reports filed with the US
securities regulator):
- **Five companies hold about 69 % of the world's counted AI chips** at the end of 2025 (Google, Microsoft,
  Meta, Amazon, Oracle; 90 % range 63–73 %). The share rose by seven points in two years, then stood still from
  mid-2025. The newest published figure, 75.8 % for March 2026, rests on an unfinished quarter of the file.
- **The record cannot see much.** It has no category for any state, models the chips smuggled into China with a
  5.4-fold range, counts ownership but not use, and its list of named clusters covers only 10–20 % of the world.
- **The firms that train the best-known models hold none of it.** OpenAI and Anthropic own no counted chips and
  rent at about $22 bn and $10 bn a year in agreements with a stated term, plus large sums with no term. Only 3
  of 19 published agreements give a number of chips.
- **13 of the 19 agreements tell the public nothing** about who can end them, what the machines may not be used
  for, or who else can stop them. Where an end is stated, it lies with the user or with either side, never with
  the provider alone. In the two contracts filed in full, a court, police request or regulator can only make the
  provider suspend use; in OpenAI's, the provider gave up the right to end the agreement even for unlawful use.
- **No filing says how a holder divides its chips** between its own models and others (the Atelier's question).
  Google and Meta speak in dollars; SpaceX, which owns xAI, gives a total and Anthropic's fee. Only two documents
  together bound what xAI keeps for itself: at most about 70 % of its power.

**What it says.** Fact: the counted compute of frontier AI is concentrated in five firms and rented by firms that
own none. Judgment: the open record is good enough to show concentration and not good enough to show control;
where the terms of control are public at all, a state's only written lever is a suspension the provider carries
out.

**Against ourselves.** 5 of our 23 predictions failed: the share did stop rising, a state does appear in the
contracts, and our own copy of the cluster file had lost a row that only a direct download found. Session 265's
record miscounted its own predictions (4 of 7; it was 5 of 7), corrected here. The record of use holds only what
was announced or filed; most month-to-month renting is invisible to it. One analyst did all of it.

For the programme: The compute of frontier AI is held by five firms and rented by two more that hold none, and where the terms of that renting are public at all a state's only written lever is a suspension the provider carries out — so the next step should ask where democratic control can reach these contracts: through which laws, filings and bodies, and over how many of the 13 agreements that say nothing in public.
