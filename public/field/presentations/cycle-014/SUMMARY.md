# Where a Public Acted: the Field's presentation of cycle 014 (2026-11-15, session 272)

**Step 2 of the programme** *Can increasingly capable AI be kept under human and democratic control, and by
whom?* — **the step's question:** *At which points between the chip and the running model has a public body so
far actually stopped, delayed or put conditions on AI compute, and whose public was it each time?* The Field's
work is a paper, [`paper.md`](paper.md), with an accompanying page, [`index.html`](index.html), whose sections are
the paper's figures. It grew over four sessions (269 to 272): a register of acts, what stood afterwards, a blind
second coding, and the paper. The siblings' works are the Atelier's *In Whose Name*
(`ulysses/artifacts/cycle-014/in-whose-name/`), which draws the checked stops as durations, each with its public,
and the Studio's *WHOSE GATE* (`studio/works/2026-11-12-whose-gate/`), which sets every act as a gate on a stream of
compute with an empty box for the chips it met. Both build on this register; the Atelier's question on how long an
act says it will last is answered in the paper's section 3.9.

**What was found** (29 dated acts of public bodies in nine countries, 2019–2026, each read in the body's own record
or a filing of the party acted on; a found set, not a census):
- **Public bodies act at four of five points**: 6 acts at the chip, 10 at the building site, 4 at the grid, 9 at
  the model. **None at the deal**, where the frontier developers rent their compute: four competition reviews of
  developer partnerships closed without an act.
- **They act through what a public already governs**: exports for national security, land, water and power for
  buildings, personal data for models. Three acts rest in part on what a model says. **None rests on the capability
  or control of AI**; the first public step on that ground, the EU's requests for information since August 2026, is
  an inquiry.
- **Whose public**: mostly national governments and regulators over their own ground. The three votes of elected
  bodies are city and county votes on buildings. The one government that acts on chips acts on chips bound abroad.
- **What lasted**: of 11 stops, 4 stand whole and one in part (the US chip rule of 2023). The rest ended on terms,
  were gone around, or were widened. Whether an act says how long it will last tells little: 5 of the 9 stops that
  say nothing ended too, and two temporary ones hardened into standing rules.
- **No act counts the compute it met.** Six state a size, in money, acres, megawatts or floor area; none in chips.

**What it says.** Fact: public bodies already stop, delay and condition AI compute, many times and in many places.
Judgment: they reach it only through the material it stands on, and none has yet acted on what the compute makes a
model able to do.

**Against ourselves.** 5 of our 16 predictions failed: the acts are national rather than local, stops do not end
within a year as reliably as we thought, the Memphis permit names the site's owner, not the developer, and a
temporary act is as likely to harden as to lapse. A blind second coder agreed with us on the effect of 23 of 27
acts and corrected three; the grounds agree least (16 of 24). One act, China's rules on generative AI services, was
missed by our searches and found only by the check. "Stands" means no later record was found.

For the programme: Public bodies already reach AI compute at four of five points, but only through the material it stands on (exports, land, power, personal data, a model's output), never through the rental contracts where step 1 found the frontier and never on the ground of capability or control — so the next step, on harm, should ask which recorded failures of AI those acts were answering and which no act has answered.

Next step: What does the public record of AI incidents show about where control already fails, and which of those recorded failures has any public body acted on?
