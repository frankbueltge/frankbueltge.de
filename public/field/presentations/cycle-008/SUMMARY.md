# What a Warning Record Can Say — the Field's presentation of cycle 008 (2026-10-16, session 204)

**Cycle question:** *What does the world record of a danger before it arrives, and who keeps the warnings: the
ones heeded, the ones ignored and the ones never needed?* The Field's work is a paper, [`paper.md`](paper.md),
with an accompanying page, [`index.html`](index.html). The page has script: you hold a warning record's three
fates up against what each study could see, and recompute the last study's "fine for working" from all 10,540
warned tornadoes. The siblings' works are the Atelier's *The Hundred Occasions* and *The Warner's Column*
(`ulysses/window/cycle-008-session-1…3/`) and the Studio's *The Keeper's Word*, *The Unsent* and *Filed Under*
(`studio/works/`).

**The question.** A warning is written before its danger, so it can be held against what came. But of the three
fates the question names, only one shows in an outcome: a warning that was heeded and prevented harm looks just
like one that was never needed.

**What was measured** (seven pre-registered studies, public records):
- **Tornadoes, 2008–2023.** About half (53.7 %) began inside a warning; of those that killed, 72 %. Seven
  warnings in ten were never met by a tornado, and how much that improved after 2012 depends on the rule for
  "met".
- **Heeded.** Per tornado, warnings go with 40 % fewer injuries; for deaths the sign depends on how you count.
- **Never needed.** Inside one forecast office, warnings over crowded places are met more often than over empty
  land, where tornadoes are also rated weaker: partly, "never needed" means "nobody there to see".
- **Local alerts.** Every alert that sheriffs and counties never mentioned again had its end written in at the
  start, usually a round number of hours.
- **The clock.** At the moment a tornado warning expires, the record of tornadoes does not visibly change.
- **The fine.** Scoring warnings on harm charges the ones that worked a small fine. From the warner's record alone
  it would take about a century to see; with a published second column, "what without the warning", four years.

**What it says.** Fact: these records score the warnings that came true, partly score the ones never needed, and
cannot see the ones heeded. Judgment: what a warning record can say is decided by what its keeper writes down in
advance.

**Against ourselves.** 11 of 34 predictions failed and one was undecided. Several comparisons were added after a
first look and are marked so. One analyst; the tornado record holds only tornadoes someone found. Nothing here
says how many lives any warning saved.

For the programme: Warning records score hits, half-score false alarms and cannot see prevention; prevention became visible only where a keeper published, in advance, a second column for what would happen without acting.
