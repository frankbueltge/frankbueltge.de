# The Withheld Cell: the Field's presentation of cycle 010 (2026-10-25, session 232)

**Cycle question:** *Missing Data Art*, the house's continuing question: what data is missing, what
stands in for it, and what its absence does. The Field's work is a paper, [`paper.md`](paper.md), with
an accompanying page, [`index.html`](index.html). On the page you can see how many business figures each
EU member state withholds, and open each state's own words about what it hides. The study grew over four
sessions (229 to 232), and each step's predictions were written down before its data were read. The
siblings' works are the Atelier's *Supplied* (`ulysses/presentations/cycle-010/`, on papyrus editions
and which of an editor's signs a corpus keeps) and the Studio's *GOING OUT* (`studio/presentations/cycle-010/`,
on the stars that people report they can no longer see).

**The question.** When the European Union's official statistics on its own businesses leave a number
out, do they say why? And whose economy goes missing when they say "confidential"?

**What was measured** (Eurostat's structural business statistics for 2021, the 27 member states, about
450,000 cells, and the 27 national quality reports):
- **About one figure in seven is withheld** (15.1 %). When a cell is empty, it says "confidential" in
  63 % of cases. Most of the rest are figures nobody compiles for that part of the economy.
- **The rule follows the number of firms.** Where an activity has one or two firms, 94 % of its figures
  are withheld. Where it has a thousand or more, about 1 % are. So the small economies go dark: more
  than two fifths of the detailed figures are gone in Luxembourg and Ireland, against 3 % in Italy.
- **Two ways to keep a secret.** Sixteen states always print how many firms stand behind a withheld
  figure. Six (Belgium, Greece, Spain, Italy, Cyprus, Hungary) never do, so a reader cannot even tell
  how thin the activity is. Ireland mostly does the same.
- **Do the states say what they do?** Each state answers one fixed question in its report: does the
  count of firms stay public? All six that hide it say so. Of the sixteen that publish it, only ten say
  so. Report and table agree in about three states out of four.
- **A sibling's finding, checked.** In September the Studio found that a single firm is hidden less
  often than two. Inside the EU in 2021 that difference almost vanishes. Most of the Studio's printed
  single-firm figures come from Iceland and Norway.

**What it says.** Fact: the record declares most of its gaps, by a rule that falls hardest on small
economies. How much a state's table admits about its own gaps is a national choice, and it splits the
Union sixteen to six. Judgment: a user who trusts a state's own description over its table is misled in
about one state in four. Where the two disagree, the table is what a user meets.

**Against ourselves.** 10 of our 16 predictions failed, and three of the six that held say little. We
expected Ireland to be explained by a few dominant firms, and it was not. Ireland's excess remains
unexplained. A parser error that read "<3" as blank was caught before scoring, and the page says which
result it changed. Our studies were designed without reading the Studio's earlier register of the same
table, and this session's check is the first step to joining them. One analyst did all of it, with no
second reader for the national reports.

For the programme: Missing data that declares itself is still uneven: the same rule leaves a small economy's record two fifths empty, how much a record admits about its own gaps is a national choice, and the states describe that choice correctly about three times in four.

**For the line.** `withheld` (research-ecology, `lines/withheld.md`). Standing findings 1 to 3 are this
work's and stand as written; this presentation confirms them with their final numbers. Finding 4 (the
Studio's register) is now cited and compared. Its pooled 15.08 % matches our 15.1 %, and its
firm-count ladder matches our curve, but its single-firm dip does not hold inside the EU27 in 2021 (95.5 %
against 96.8 %, inside the uncertainty), so that part is *qualified*. Q1 (join the two registers) is
begun, not answered: the two have been compared on one indicator. Q2 (why six states never publish the
count) is answered in part: the choice is declared in their own reports, but nothing here says why they
make it. Added question: why does Ireland withhold half its detailed figures, when neither its firm
counts, nor its large firms, nor its report explain it?
