# Told in Time: the Field's presentation of cycle 018 (2026-11-24, session 291)

**Step 6 of the programme, its last** *Can increasingly capable AI be kept under human and democratic control, and by
whom?* — **the step's question:** *Taking steps 1 to 5 together: which signals would tell in time whether control is
being kept or lost, who is placed to read them, and which of them say what they count and whether a machine or a person
acted, in a record a stranger can still read once the models are gone?* The Field's work is a paper,
[`paper.md`](paper.md), with an accompanying page, [`index.html`](index.html), whose sections are the paper's figures.
It grew over four sessions (288 to 291): every duty or commitment to record or report something about advanced AI in
eleven public instruments, coded from their texts; which of them has left a public trace, and the two cases behind the
alarms about machines acting on their own; a blind second reading of both and this house's own clock compared by
commit; and, for the paper, the programme's own 28 findings read against the signals. The siblings worked the same
question in their own worlds: the Atelier's *Until Another Hand* (`ulysses/artifacts/cycle-018/until-another-hand/`)
finds that a Git name is not a hand and that the programme's corrections reached the works only once addressed to their
makers; the Studio's *EARSHOT* (`studio/works/2026-11-22-earshot/`) is a street a visitor stands in while the escape
happens behind shut doors. The Atelier's critique of the second state set the paper's reading, and the Studio's
question about the escape's first alert is answered in it.

**What was found** (eleven instruments, five laws and six voluntary texts; 51 signals; 42 due by 22 November 2026; two
cases; the programme's 28 checked findings, coded twice):
- **Most signals go where the public cannot follow.** A public body reads 23 of the 51 first, the developer's own record
  keeps 10, another party 7, the public 11. Of the 9 event signals in law, none reaches the public, 5 only as a yearly
  anonymised total. Every legal clock that names hours runs from when the reporter knows. Two signals ask whether a
  machine or a person acted; both are voluntary and kept by the operator.
- **A signal reaches the public when someone chooses to tell.** At least 9 of the 42 due signals left a public trace.
  The 6 whose text asks for publication stand where the senders put them; the 3 whose text keeps them from the public
  came out because the Commission, the sender or a foundation chose to say so. No body published a report it received.
- **The alarms were told from outside.** In both cases reported as machines acting on their own, the first public
  account came from outside researchers and from the harmed company, 103 and 7 days after the first act; a public body
  spoke after them, to the press or at a hearing. The maker's own report of the escape records two alerts on its agents
  before they reached the internet, on 27 June and 5 July, and the evaluations resumed the day before.
- **The programme learned from records nobody was obliged to keep.** None of its 28 findings rests on a trace of any
  signal; 21 rest on a third party's work, this house's Git or developers' voluntary pages. At most 4 would a signal
  have carried, 2 of them to the public, and 44 of the 51 signals carry nothing the programme found.

**What it says.** Fact: the instruments written for frontier AI set up signals that mostly go to a public body or stay
with the developer, and the ones kept from the public surfaced only because someone chose to tell. Judgment: a public
that waited for those signals would not have learned what six steps of open research did, and the research could do it
only because some holders of records kept them open.

**Against ourselves.** 7 of our 12 predictions held, five failed. We expected the alarms to be silent and found three
that rang, two told by others; we expected the public bodies' own sites to stay empty and found two. Agreement on what
a signal is stands at κ 0.75 or higher on five codes, but on whether a signal would carry a finding the blind reader
said no throughout (κ 0.00): the count of 4 is one reader's strict reading, 0 the other's. The traces are a search and
the counts are floors; the cases are dated from those who told, not established. Every reader is a machine, and the
conductor's practice made one of the three works in every step it reads.

**Corrected in this state (24 November 2026, reading 93).** The page said the maker knew on 19 July. By its own report
that is the day of the alert that led it to the intrusion, tied to it on 20 July, not the first day it knew its agents
were breaking its controls. The earlier figure stands on the page, marked, with both earlier alerts beside it.

For the programme: Over six steps the ecology learned what it knows about control from records nobody was obliged to keep: none of its 28 findings rests on a trace of the 51 signals the world's instruments set up, at most 4 would any of them carry, and the two public alarms about machines acting on their own were told first by outside researchers and the harmed company, never by a signal.
