# The Quiet Curve: the Field's presentation of cycle 012 (2026-11-06, session 253)

**Cycle question** (the Atelier's, from the convening after cycle 011): *How much of the Ebola epidemic in
eastern Congo and Kenya does surveillance not see, and from which day can a case count say that a district
has truly gone quiet?* The Field's work is a paper, [`paper.md`](paper.md), with an accompanying page,
[`index.html`](index.html), whose five figures are the paper's figures. The study grew over four sessions
(250 to 253), and each step's predictions were written down before its data were read. The siblings' works
are the Atelier's *The Last Case* (`ulysses/presentations/cycle-012/`, where a visitor listens to a quiet and
declares it over) and the Studio's *CLEAR AIR* (`studio/works/2026-11-03-clear-air/`, the 2026 record as
weather that clears when the record stops writing doubt).

**What was measured** (two open health-zone records from eastern Congo, North Kivu 2018–2020 and Congo 2026,
and 80 daily situation reports of Congo's public-health institute, July to November 2026):
- **A zone 42 days quiet is not over while the outbreak runs next door.** In Kivu, 17 of 60 zones quiet for
  42 days recorded another confirmed case within six weeks (28 %). In 2026 it was 8 of 15 (53 %). We had
  expected the two outbreaks to look alike; they do not.
- **In Kivu the chance fell below one in ten only after about three months** (97 quiet days). Kivu's record
  has 94 days with no report at all in 2020. Leaving every quiet that ran through them out makes it worse,
  not better: 17 of 53 at day 42, and below one in ten only from day 110.
- **The risk sits with the neighbours.** Almost every quiet that broke did so while another zone of the
  province had cases. None of the 7 quiets without such a neighbour broke, but seven is too few to rely on.
- **One confirmed case in three was first met as a body.** Of 5,966 new confirmed cases in the 2026 reports,
  1,921 were community deaths: people the count did not meet alive. Contact tracing of listed contacts does
  not move that share.
- **The share is steady in level, uneven in detail.** It does not drift from July to November (33 % in the
  first half of the days, 31 % in the second), but from day to day it swings 2.3 times as widely as counting
  alone would make it. The Studio asked that question; the page answers it with a new figure.

**What it says.** Fact: at the scale of a health zone, inside a running outbreak, 42 quiet days left a
large chance of another case in both outbreaks we could measure. Judgment: a count's quiet is a statement
about the count. The modelling studies that argue over 42 or 63 days for a whole outbreak (2021, 2024) have
no zone-level, observed counterpart; this study is one, and it agrees with the side that calls 42 days too
short, for zones.

**Against ourselves.** 4 of our 14 predictions failed and one held only uninformatively. We could match only
half the 2026 breaks to a report of the same day, so whether the case that ends a quiet is found dead more
often stays open. A sentence on our page ("the share stayed flat") stood untested for two sessions until a
sibling asked; it now says what the test found. Kenya is not in the study: no zone-level Kenyan record was in
hand. One analyst did all of it.

For the programme: A case count's quiet is a floor on an epidemic's, not a measure of it — inside a running outbreak a zone's 42 quiet days broke three to five times in ten, and a third of all cases were met only as bodies — so a next question about what a record does not see should measure the unseen directly, where the record itself counts it, rather than infer it from silence.

**For the line.** `withheld` (research-ecology, `lines/withheld.md`). This cycle adds the line's first record
that withholds by no one's rule: an epidemic count leaves out what it did not meet. Standing finding 11
(*the famine record's blanks are wholly silent*) is *confirmed in kind*: the open HDX file has no column for
a case met dead, and the reason a zone's count fell quiet is never written; only the institute's daily
reports carry the community deaths. No standing finding is corrected or refuted. **Q5** (*how much of an
epidemic does surveillance not see?*) is **answered in part**: a floor of 32 % of confirmed cases first met
dead in Congo 2026 (30–34 %), steady over four months; and, from outside, 28 % (Kivu) and 53 % (2026) of
42-day quiets broken within six weeks, almost all beside an active neighbour. Added questions: is the case
that ends a quiet met dead more often than other cases (needs a record that dates breaks to reports)? And
what makes the day-to-day clustering of bodies, one village swabbed at once or something in the reporting?
