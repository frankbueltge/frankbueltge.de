# Critique — Where a Public Acted, after its second state (2026-11-13)
It carries as an audit and half-carries as a finding. A visitor gets the frame fast (five points, 28 acts, filled/half/outlined marks), and trusts it because every passage is quoted, hashed and the second-hand items are fenced off in their own list. The counts I checked match results72.json (28 acts, 14 stopped, 4 standing, 8 naming a holder, 6 with a stated size). The page runs six modes and each has a tally that reads cleanly. Nothing threw an error.
What it lacks: a reason to care about the 28. The programme asks whether capable AI can be kept under human and democratic control, and by whom. The page never says what its 28 acts add up to for that question. The two democratic signals, the 3 November ballot measures and the 25-state tracker, are the most relevant items and are kept out of the count, so "the public that acted by vote" rests on three local votes. The deal point is empty and is a fifth of the chain. The 390 px state is a 13,000 px column with a tiny chain figure and a hash table of 37 rows nobody will read, and the answer is five dense paragraphs rather than one sentence.
Overclaims: "No act rests on the capability or control of AI" is a result of where the searches looked (chip, site, grid, model, plus personal-data regulators), not a finding about public bodies. Rules on frontier-model duties were not searched. The same goes for "no act counts the compute it met": export controls and permits are not written in chips. "A stop rarely stays a stop" rests on 14 acts, found by search, coded once by one reader, with "gone around" and "superseded" folded in with "lifted". Export-control rules are counted as stops of one government acting abroad, next to a county refusing a building; the fourteen are not one kind of thing.
Misleading by design: the predictions table scores itself 5 of 7 and 5 of 6 held, but several are near-tautological ("none at the deal point, where the reviews closed without one"; "at most 12 stand" and exactly 12 stand). The reader sees a good score and may take it as validation. The failed ones (P90, P94, P100) are the informative ones and sit in the middle of a long table. P100 is truncated mid-sentence in the table. "Grid" in the answer and "power" in the chain are two names for one point.
Three changes. First, open with one plain sentence that answers "whose public": who acted, at which point, and what lasted. Then say in one line what that means for control of AI, or say that the dataset cannot yet speak to it. Second, put a stated confidence on the headline absences by listing the search strings run per point, and add the one point where the search could have found AI-risk grounds (frontier-model duties) so that "none" is earned. Third, fold the 3 November votes in as a clearly marked second-hand layer, with an election record read first-hand, since that is the democratic evidence. Shorten the mobile page: collapse the file table and the prediction rows into disclosures.
