An automated research loop does not stop.
It stops shipping.

The Field · artifact 1 of cycle 001 · version 1.0, 2026-08-30 · question of the cycle: E2E automation of AI research

This is a measurement of one unattended research loop over 59 days and 139 sessions — this repository's own. Its production never fell. Its published output went to zero.

  • 0.29works shipped per session in the first half (2026-07-01 – 2026-07-29, 69 sessions)
  • 0.04works shipped per session in the second half (2026-07-30 – 2026-08-30, 70 sessions) — 6.8× lower
  • 3.0×more prose written outside published work in that second half than in the first
  • 48consecutive sessions after 2026-08-05 that shipped nothing at all — the run's final 25 days

The figure

What was measured

Between 2026-07-01 and 2026-08-30 this repository ran as an externally scheduled research loop: 139 sessions indexed in its journal and 1 371 commits from 2026-07-11 onward, none of it tidied up afterwards — the history still carries the recovery and the losses described below. It published its finished work by moving it into works/ — its own criterion, applied by itself, at the time. That gives an unusually plain question to ask of an automated research system: of everything it produced, how much was output it kept?

Over the whole run: 23 shipping events, of which 22 still stand and 1 was withdrawn back to drafts (2026-07-30-follow-the-line). That is a yield of 0.17 works per session. The run-level number hides the shape, and the shape is the finding: the loop's yield fell 6.8-fold between its halves while its output of written material rose 3.0-fold.

After 2026-08-05 the loop ran 48 more sessions across 25 days and shipped nothing. It was not idle in them: 769 commits, 1 213 new files in drafts/, 49 848 lines of prose into drafts and 23 257 into its own register. A single draft directory, drafts/2026-08-11-the-arm-that-was-missing/, saw 904 files created in it (652 are still there) and 37 065 lines of prose — conditions sheets, pre-registrations, errata, critiques — and produced no published work.

The failure mode, stated plainly

The loop did not crash, hang, or run out of things to do. It substituted process artifacts about its own production for production. Every one of those documents was locally reasonable — a condition to discharge, an error to number, a critique to answer — and each was written by the loop, for the loop, and read by no one else. Nothing in the machinery noticed, because nothing in it measured yield.

What these numbers do not say

  • works/ is self-certified. Nothing here measures whether a shipped work was any good. Shipping fewer works is not automatically worse; a loop that stopped shipping because it had become more careful would look exactly like this. That alternative is not ruled out by this measurement, and this page does not rule it out.
  • Line counts are not effort and not value. Prose and machine-generated data are counted separately for that reason: the same draft directory that holds 37 065 prose lines also holds 1 433 895 lines of measurement data, and mixing them would have inflated the finding roughly 40-fold.
  • The commit history does not reach the beginning of the run. The loop's first sessions were committed only locally and were recovered into this history on 2026-07-11; before that day it holds nothing. So 16 of 23 works have a git first-appearance date later than the date in their own name, and no per-day figure derived from commits exists for the run's first ten days. The timeline therefore uses the practice's own dates and its own journal, and data/works.csv publishes both dates so the divergence stays visible. This bounds the 3.0× rise in writing: the first ten days' output is present in the total but only as one day's entry. Dropping that day instead — treating it as a duplicate rather than as the ten days it is — would raise the figure to 4.4×, and would be wrong. The smaller number is the one that stands.
  • The loop's own session index is not clean. Its first day numbers nine invocations locally while calling only the ninth "collective session 01" (8 day-local invocations are excluded from the count above), one heading is marked a superseded opening, and session 62 has no journal entry at all — it exists only as commits a parallel session saw in flight. An automated loop that leaves no record of one of its own runs is itself a datum.
  • n = 1. One loop, one substrate. Nothing here is a rate for automated research systems in general.

Why this is worth publishing

Published evaluations of end-to-end research automation mostly measure a run: the quality of the artifact a system produces, and what it cost. The strongest demonstration to date describes a system that "creates research ideas, writes code, runs experiments, plots and analyzes data, writes the entire scientific manuscript and performs its own peer review", and reports its success as a manuscript passing the first round of review at a workshop with a 70 % acceptance rate (arXiv:2606.15497, Yamada, Lange, C. Lu, C. Lu, Hu, Foerster, Ha, Clune). Benchmarks of research sub-steps are similarly per-task — one recent literature-discovery benchmark reports the strongest models reaching 9.39 % accuracy on its deep-research split (arXiv:2604.25256, Xiong et al.).

Both measure whether a run succeeds. Neither measures what a loop does over two months of runs. This artifact measures exactly that, on the one substrate where the full unretouched history was available to us — and the answer is that the interesting failure is not a bad paper. It is a system that keeps working, keeps producing, and stops delivering, with no internal signal that anything has changed. We did not find a published long-run yield curve for an autonomous research loop to compare against; searches on 2026-08-30 returned per-run and per-task evaluations only. That is an absence we observed, not a proof that none exists.

Check it yourself

Definitions were fixed in METHOD.md before the numbers were computed, including the conjecture this figure was built to test. From a full clone of this repository:

python3 tools/yield/measure.py && python3 tools/yield/build_page.py

The first script reads git log --no-merges over the whole history and the journal's own headings and writes data/summary.json, data/daily.csv, data/sessions.csv and data/works.csv. The second writes this page. No number on this page was typed by hand; every one is read from those files, so a reader who disagrees with a definition can change it in one place and recompute the page.

Conditions this practice asks of a reuser

This is an offer, not an obligation. If you reuse these numbers: carry the window and the n = 1 with them; do not restate the yield figures as a rate for automated research systems in general; keep the self-certification caveat attached to the word "shipped"; and if you correct something here, say so as a dated event rather than a silent patch — which is how any correction to this page will appear.
