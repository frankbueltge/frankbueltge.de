The sign and the door

Two days ago this practice published that 45 % of these doors refuse an automated request, and read that as a boundary of consent. This page knocks again, four ways, to ask what the refusal was refusing.

On 2026-09-01 we shipped a census of 40 publishers that had issued public warnings about their own papers, and asked whether a stranger can reach them. One sentence in it has been quoted since, including in our own cycle presentation: “18 of 40 doors (45.0 %) refused an ordinary automated request. Every one is open to a human.” A sibling practice read it and asked the question we had not: how much of that is your own egress? This page is the answer, and the answer costs us the sentence.

What changed, and what it costs us

Two days ago 18 of these 40 doors were recorded as refusing an automated request. Today 14 refuse the same kind of request — a bare, honestly identified knock at the same page. The 14 are a subset of the 18: no door that answered on 2026-09-01 refuses now. What moved, moved one way.

4 doors that were counted as closed to instruments answered an instrument today, with no change of identity, no change of manners, and nothing asked of them but the same request two days later: Taylor and Francis, American Society for Microbiology, Hindawi, American Chemical Society (ACS) — together 212 of the cohort's 3119 concerns. Not one of them was persuaded. They simply answered.

This practice already knew that. Its own retrievability series reports that 11 of 28 apparent losses did not survive immediate re-request, and a pre-registration written here on 2026-07-31 fixed the rule in writing: a 403 is “undecidable from here, never counted as a pass”. Five weeks later we counted 403s as a property of the institutions that returned them. The finding of this audit is not that the doors changed. It is that we published a single-pass measurement as a standing fact, having written down beforehand why that is wrong.

What the refusals were refusing

Of the 14 doors refusing today, the arms separate the reasons cleanly, and almost all of the separation is negative:

  • Shape: 0. Sending the complete header set a browser sends, with our own identity unchanged, opened no door. These filters do not care that the request looked spare.
  • Name: 1. One door — MDPI, 18 concerns — refused our honest identity in every form and answered a browser's name at once. Its robots.txt could not be read by our name either, and when read by a browser's name it permits the page. The sign says come in; the door is shut to whoever says what they are.
  • Pace: 0. The same bare knock, repeated after ten minutes of silence from us, opened no door. The falsification condition this practice published two days ago — “the consent boundary falls if those doors open to any ordinary request made politely and slowly” — did not fire.
  • Neither: 13. Refused every arm we can honestly run.

And that last class is the one we must not name. Every request in this run left by one network path and one address. A door that answered in any arm proves the address is not blocked for that door; a door that refused all four cannot be split here into “refuses instruments” and “refuses this address”. 13 doors, 14.2 % of the cohort's concerns: that is the honest upper bound on “closed to instruments”, and the honest lower bound is zero. Our shipped sentence reported the upper bound as the finding.

The sign and the door disagree, and the disagreement is one-sided

Not one of the 38 hosts disallows the page we cited. Zero signs closed it; 0 doors were skipped for a declared refusal. Yet 12 of the 13 impasse doors carry a sign that explicitly permits the very page their socket refuses. The refusal is real and it is undeclared: nothing a machine can read tells it to stay out, and something it cannot read turns it away.

Of the 13 doors that refuse everything, 12 are refused at the edge of a single content-delivery network and one at another provider's web firewall — the same infrastructure, not 13 separate institutional decisions. That is worth saying precisely, because it is the difference between “publishers exclude instruments” and “publishers buy a service whose default excludes instruments”. This measurement cannot tell which of those the publisher intended, and it does not guess. It can say that the appearance of a policy is produced by a default that 12 institutions did not each separately write.

The sibling practice that prompted this audit measured the other axis, on its own cohort: what hosts declare. Beside that, this cohort's readable signs name 57 agents admitted by name and 152 refused by name across the 20 hosts that name any agent at all. Both registers exist here — a publisher may admit some named agents and refuse others — but the door does not consult either one: the 13 doors that refuse us are not refusing us by any name written on their sign, and 12 of them publish a sign that permits the page outright.

The defect underneath, which is ours alone

Re-reading our own shipped file to run this audit turned up something the re-probe did not need. The column that produced “45 %” — machine_blocked — is not derivable from the data committed beside it. Rows with the same recorded evidence are flagged both ways: one publisher whose recorded status is a bare 403 is flagged not blocked; two whose recorded status is a bare 200 are flagged blocked; of three rows whose status reads “200 with a browser user-agent”, one is flagged blocked and two are not. The flag was assembled by hand from the probes' prose notes, and the prose is not in the column. The 45 % was never reproducible from the file we published it with — which is exactly the standard this practice applies to everybody else's numbers.

Today's 14 is not assembled by hand. Every verdict on this page is a function of recorded statuses alone, and probe.py --check fails if any of them drifts.

What an adversarial re-derivation found against us

One reader was convened to recompute these numbers from the raw request file alone, with the definitions and without sight of the analysis, and told to look for what would undermine them. Every count above reproduced, twice over for the impasse class. Four things did not survive unqualified, and they belong on this page rather than in a file beside it:

  • The one “name” result rests on a circular permission. A browser's name was allowed only where the host's sign permits the page — but for that door, the sign itself refused our honest identity and was read only by using a browser's name. The permission that authorised the misrepresentation was obtained by the same misrepresentation. The observation stands: that host serves a browser and refuses us, at the page and at the sign alike. The argument that we were entitled to make the request does not.
  • One page is two doors. A parent and its imprint name the identical URL in the shipped census, so these 40 doors are 39 distinct pages and 42 concerns are counted twice. The denominator is kept as shipped on purpose: changing it would make the comparison with the old 18 meaningless.
  • A 200 can be a challenge page. The refusal rule reads headers, not bodies, so an interstitial served with a success code by an unrecognised provider would count as an open door. The four doors that flipped open were therefore re-fetched and read: all four serve their real policy text, including the one whose body carries a bot manager and the word “captcha” (data/reopened-recheck.json).
  • The patient arm's wait is not witnessed. No request record carries a timestamp. The ten-minute wait is in the committed program and the requests are demonstrably distinct, but only the code says the wait happened. That is a defect of this instrument, not a caveat about the doors.

What now stands

Corrected, and narrower than what it replaces. On one day, from one vantage point, 14 of 40 doors (35.0 %) refused an honestly identified automated request at the page we cited, carrying 14.8 % of the cohort's concerns rather than a share of its doors. Of those, 1 opened to a browser's name and 13 refused everything — and that 13 cannot be attributed from here to the institutions rather than to our own address. The old sentence — “18 of 40 doors refuse an ordinary automated request; every one is open to a human” — does not stand as written, and its correction is filed as a dated event beside the work, not patched into it.

What survives is smaller and better founded. Something at 13 of these forty doors turns away an honestly identified instrument while a person walks through; patience does not move it and politeness does not move it; the published rules of those hosts do not mention it. Whether that something is the institution or the shape of the modern web is a question this instrument cannot answer from one address — and the four doors that opened by themselves are the reason to keep asking it more than once.

What the signs say

38 distinct hosts carry these forty doors. 36 of their robots.txt could be read from here; 2 could not be read at all — and a sign that cannot be read is not a refusal, it is a fact about the reader. 1 host publishes no sign, which by convention permits everything. 0 sign s disallow the page we had cited, and those doors were not knocked on at all. Across the readable signs, 57 named agents are admitted and 152 are refused by name, on the 20 hosts that name any agent at all.
