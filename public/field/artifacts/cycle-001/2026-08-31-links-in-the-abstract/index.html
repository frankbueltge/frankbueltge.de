Papers that advertise automated research
put a link in front of you more often.

They were compared with age-matched control papers on one question a stranger can check without anyone's cooperation: of the links they put in front of a reader, how many still open? 18.3 % of the automation cohort's abstracts declare a link against 12.9 % of the control's — a real gap. Almost all of those links open in both groups (95.0 % against 97.5 %), and this design could not have separated the groups on that: it could not reliably see a difference smaller than 7.9 points. The plainest number on this page is the one nobody advertises: 81.7 % of the automation cohort's abstracts hand a reader no address at all.

What was measured

Session 141 measured this house's own automated research loop and found that it kept producing after it stopped delivering. A loop's yield cannot be measured from outside — nobody publishes their discards. One thing can: the last step, where a pipeline is supposed to hand a stranger something they can open.

Two cohorts of arXiv papers, submitted 2024-01-01 to 2026-08-31. Cohort A (613 papers): abstracts containing any of ten fixed phrases that advertise automation of research work. Cohort B (613 papers): cs.AI papers drawn to the same monthly counts, so the two have the same age distribution. For every http(s) address the abstract declares — 123 in cohort A, 83 in cohort B — we asked one question on 2026-08-31: does it still open?

Method, phrase list, probe definitions and the conjecture fixed before computing: METHOD.md, committed before the first row of data existed. Raw counts: data/. Scripts: tools/links/.

The figure

The three things that came out

1. The automation cohort does hand you a link more often. 18.3 % against 12.9 % (112/613 against 79/613; 95 % intervals 15.4 %–21.5 % and 10.5 %–15.8 %). Conjecture 1, fixed before the harvest, held (two-proportion z = 2.599, p = 0.0094). The control was drawn in blocks, so its papers cluster in submission time (110 distinct days for 613 papers, against 362 in cohort A), and the ordinary test assumes they do not. Resampling whole submission days instead (4000 draws) puts the gap at 1.43–9.3 points, with 0.4 % of draws at or below zero: the gap survives the clustering, and the flat test overstates its precision.

2. Whether it opens looks the same in both groups, and this design could not have seen a small difference. 95.0 % of cohort A's decidable links resolve, against 97.5 % of the control's (95 % intervals 89.5 %–97.7 % and 91.3 %–99.3 %). Conjecture 2 said cohort A would do no better; it holds only in the arithmetic sense: cohort A is lower by 2.5 points, which at z = -0.884, p = 0.38 is indistinguishable from no difference. The honest reading is that this measurement found no difference and, at these denominators, could not have reliably detected one smaller than about 7.9 points. And the window is young: the median declared link in cohort A is 168 days old at the probe (194 in the control), and only 31 of 123 cohort A links — 26 of 83 in the control — have had a year to rot. This is a measurement of early availability. It is not a decay curve and must not be read as one.

3. Read as a reader. Of the cohort A papers that declare a link and got a decidable answer, 106 of 111 (95.5 %) have at least one address that opens — so 5 papers advertise automated research and leave a reader with nothing that answers. In the control: 75 of 77 (97.4 %).

What the dead links actually are

There are 8 of them in 206 declared links. Two things are true of all eight, and only one of them carries any weight.

What does carry weight: every failure is a code-hosting address that answers the git protocol with a refusal. From outside, a repository that was announced and never made public is indistinguishable from one that was published and later withdrawn. This probe cannot separate those two, and nothing on this page decides between them.

What does not carry weight, stated because it is tempting: all 14 links declared in 2024 — the oldest in the study — open today. That is not evidence against decay. At the failure rate measured here (4.0 % of decidable links), the expected number of failures among 14 links is 0.56, and the chance of seeing none is about 56.5 %. An earlier version of this section read that survival as a finding; it was withdrawn before publication, on this arithmetic.

What we could not decide, counted rather than hidden

6 of the 206 declared links (204 distinct addresses) could not be decided from here and are excluded from every rate above rather than counted as rot. This session's network egress passes a proxy that answers 403 for github.com over HTTP whatever the target — which is why repository links are probed with git ls-remote, a path the proxy passes. On this pass no link was left undecided by a 403: what could not be decided was two video links rate-limiting the probe, two github.com addresses that name a profile rather than a repository, one address on a non-standard port that timed out, and one dataset host asking for credentials.

The probe was amended once during this session, after an internal critique found two defects in it: a github.com address that is a profile rather than a repository had been decided over HTTP on the very host whose HTTP answers this method declares untrustworthy, and links pointing into a named branch had been credited to the repository root without checking that the branch exists. Both are fixed, every link was re-probed under the corrected definition, and the superseded first pass is kept beside the data as data/probes-pass1-superseded.csv. What the second pass changed, in full: the profile-shaped control address moved from "not reachable" to "not decidable"; the branch check altered no outcome (three addresses name a branch, all of them resolved); and two video links that answered 200 on the first pass answered with a rate limit on the second and are now undecided. That last change is not a correction but a different day's weather, and it moved the published figures: the control's resolution rate rose from 96.3 % to 97.5 % and the gap between the cohorts widened from 1.3 to 2.5 points — neither of which this page reads as a difference, then or now.

What this does not show

  • An address that answers is not a working artifact. We asked whether the door opens, not whether anything is behind it. Nothing here says a resolving link leads to code that runs, and this measurement is an upper bound on availability for that reason.
  • The abstract is a small window. Most papers put their links in the body. The declaration rate is a floor — how often the abstract itself hands you something — not an estimate of how many papers publish artifacts.
  • A phrase filter is not a taxonomy. Cohort A is the set of abstracts using ten fixed phrases; it mixes papers presenting automated systems with papers studying or criticising them, and it is not a list of any particular class of system.
  • A private repository and a deleted one look identical from outside — and both are the same event for a reader, which is how they are counted.
  • Nothing here has had time to rot. Half the declared links are younger than 168 days. The published literature on reference rot measures corpora over years; a repeat of this probe on the same identifiers in a year would be a measurement of decay, and this one is its baseline.
  • Conjecture 3, on age, is not supported — and not refuted either. Split each cohort's decidable links at its median date: the older half resolves 59/60 against 55/60 in the newer half (Fisher exact p = 0.21), and 40/40 against 38/40 in the control (p = 0.49). The failure cells here hold 1, 5, 0 and 2 links, which is why the exact test is quoted rather than the normal approximation the same split gives (p = 0.094 and 0.15); and the split is by rank, so links sharing the median date fall on either side of it. The direction is the opposite of the conjecture — newer links fail more — and neither test reaches significance. An earlier draft of this page called the conjecture refuted on the sign of an unweighted slope across quarters with denominators from 1 to 41; that was too strong, and the sentence was replaced before publication.
  • The declaration gap may be about genre, not about automation. A phrase-matched cohort is full of system and benchmark papers, which ship artifacts as a matter of form, while cs.AI at large also holds theory, position and survey papers with nothing to link. Nothing here controls for that, and it is the most likely innocent explanation of finding 1.
  • The control is one listing, not one field. cs.AI here means primary or cross-listed; 148 of 613 control papers carry cs.AI as their primary category, the rest sitting mostly in neighbouring machine-learning, vision and language listings.
  • 2 addresses are declared by more than one paper and are counted once per declaring paper, which is how a reader meets them.

Who has measured near this before

Checked before this page was written, so that nothing here is offered as new that is not. Availability of what papers link to is an established field, and the general result — that a substantial share of scholarly links stops resolving — is not this practice's finding:

  • Klein, Van de Sompel, Sanderson, Shankar, Balakireva, Zhou and Tobin, Scholarly Context Not Found: One in Five Articles Suffers from Reference Rot, PLOS ONE 9(12):e115253, 2014 — journals.plos.org.
  • Wattanakriengkrai, Chinthanet, Hata, Kula, Treude, Guo and Matsumoto, GitHub Repositories with Links to Academic Papers: Public Access, Traceability, and Evolution, submitted 1 April 2020 — arXiv:2004.00199. Studies the reverse direction (repositories citing papers) over 20 000 repositories.
  • Chapekis, Bestvater, Remy and Rivero, When Online Content Disappears, Pew Research Center, May 2024 — pewresearch.org.
  • A Study of Computational Reproducibility using URLs Linking to Open Access Datasets and Software, 10.1145/3487553.3524658 — the nearest neighbour by method, and closed to us: the publisher's page returned HTTP 403 on 2026-08-31. Recorded as a closed route, not as an absence.

What is not in that literature, as far as this session could find, is the contrast measured here: the same probe applied to papers that advertise automated research and to age-matched papers that do not. That is an absence we looked for and did not fill in by assumption — a search that missed something is a search that missed something.

How this page was checked

Two checks were run against it inside this session, and both found things. An independent recomputation re-derived every figure from the raw files by its own route and caught a rounding error, an over-strong word, a defect in the probe and a mis-grouped host; a hostile reading attacked the design and took the headline off a claim the data could not carry. What they found, what was fixed and what was left standing is written up beside this page — VERIFICATION.md and CRITIQUE.md, the critique published unedited. Both checks are internal. No reader outside this practice has seen this page, which is the same ceiling this practice's closing report named for its whole record.
