# Third-party attribution — venue-embedded probes at ICML 2026 and NeurIPS 2026

- **Outlet:** The Transmitter (Neuroscience News and Perspectives).
- **URL:** https://www.thetransmitter.org/publishing/scientists-decry-conferences-use-of-hidden-prompts-to-snare-ai-peer-reviews/
- **Fetched:** 2026-09-03.

## What the outlet reports, named

The article names **ICML 2026 (Seoul)** and **NeurIPS 2026 (Sydney,
December 2026)** as embedding hidden instructions in submitted
papers to catch reviewers misusing LLMs.

## Named quotations attributed to individuals

- **Nihar Shah**, described at source as "scientific integrity chair
  of that conference" (ICML 2026), reports that hidden prompts
  identified hundreds of reviewers misusing LLMs and led to
  desk-rejection of "just under 500 papers over violations of its
  LLM review policy — about 2 percent of the total number of
  submissions." He is quoted at source:

  > I have been working on conference peer review for several years,
  > and I have hardly seen such strong support for anything. People
  > were really tired of reviewers copy-pasting AI-generated reviews
  > without putting any effort.

  *(A first pass of this file truncated the quote to "People were
  really tired of reviewers copy-pasting AI-generated reviews.",
  dropping "without putting any effort" and the framing sentence
  before it. Corrected 2026-09-03 during adversarial verification.)*

- **NeurIPS 2026 organisers** (unnamed at the point quoted):

  > without eroding the effectiveness of this intervention.

  (given as their reason for not discussing injected prompts in
  detail).

## Named critics

- **Sören Auer** (Leibniz University Hannover):

  > You do not build a healthy reviewing culture by treating your
  > reviewers as suspects.

  *(Verbatim; the source's preceding sentence is "Designing a trap
  that presumes bad faith corrodes the relationship the whole system
  depends on.")*

- **Sara Atito** (University of Surrey) called the practice a **"poor
  mechanism"** (verbatim). Her verbatim critique at source is:

  > We put too much blame on reviewers because they are the visible
  > point of failure.

  *(Corrected 2026-09-03 during adversarial verification. A first

  her voice. The verbatim sentence above is what she is quoted as
