The Quiet Curve

An Ebola outbreak is declared over once 42 days pass without a new case, "twice the maximum incubation period". But while an outbreak runs, its count also goes quiet place by place, and responders read those quiets. This study measures, on two outbreaks in eastern Congo, how often a health zone that has gone quiet records another case, and how many cases the count saw only once they were dead.

For a responder: a health zone 42 days quiet is not over while the outbreak runs next door.

Why these two measures belong together

The question this cycle asks has two halves: from which day can a count call a zone quiet, and how much does surveillance not see. They are the same problem seen from two sides. A zone breaks its quiet because a chain of infection ran where the count could not see it: in a neighbouring zone, or in the zone itself, unrecorded. Figures 1 and 2 measure the first half from the outside, by how often quiets break. Figures 3 and 4 measure one piece of the second half from the inside: the cases that nobody saw alive, because they were first found as bodies. A third of all confirmed cases arrived that way, so a quiet count can sit on top of a chain that is still running.

What others have measured, and what this adds

  • When is a whole outbreak over? Modelling studies estimate the probability that no further case will occur, and they disagree about the 42-day rule. Djaafara and colleagues (American Journal of Epidemiology, 2021) find it "too short and too sensitive to underreporting", and propose a preliminary declaration "after 63 days from the symptom onset day of the last detected case", followed by 90 days of enhanced surveillance. Hart and colleagues (Science Advances, 2024), on two outbreaks in Congo (Likati 2017, Équateur 2020), find that both "could theoretically have been declared over earlier" than under the 42-day rule, with only a small risk.
  • Under-reporting and delays. Plank and colleagues (preprint, 2024) build missed cases and reporting delays into the estimate. For the 2018 outbreak in Congo that they study, the probability of no future infections "first reached 95% on 22–30 July", a range that includes the actual declaration on 24 July, made under the 42-day rule.
  • Practice today. The Kasai outbreak was declared over on 1 December 2025 "after no new cases were reported in the past 42 days since the last patient was discharged", followed by a 90-day period of enhanced surveillance (WHO Africa, 2025).
  • What this study adds. Those studies model a whole outbreak, mostly small ones that ended, and their answer depends on an assumed reporting rate. This study does not model. It counts what happened inside two large outbreaks while they were still running, zone by zone, and it measures one piece of the missing reports directly: the cases met only as bodies. Its numbers are not comparable to theirs. The clock is different (from the last rise in the count, not from the last patient), and a zone has neighbours that an outbreak does not. What agrees is the direction: at the scale of a zone, 42 days is far from safe.

Where the study failed its own predictions

  • The two outbreaks differ (predicted: less than 15 points apart at day 42; found: Kivu , Congo 2026 ). So the page shows them apart, not pooled.
  • Breaks with no visible source are rarer than predicted (3 of 105, under 3 %, not 10–40 %), and one of the three is the record's own silence.
  • Only half the breaks of 2026 could be dated in a report (17 of 34, needed 31), so whether the case ending a quiet is found dead more often stays open.
  • The share found dead did not follow contact tracing (ρ , predicted −0.20 or stronger).
  • Not failed, but corrected in wording: the page said until 6 November that the share found dead "stayed flat". It holds as "no drift" (study 55, all three predictions held); day to day the share is not steady.
  • The neighbour split held, but weakly: it rests on quiets, none of which broke (interval 0–35 %).

Method and limits

Records. OCHA HDX, North Kivu outbreak by health zone, 2018–2020 (Ministry of Health and WHO, CC BY-IGO, sha256 76c7e999…86cb), rows of North Kivu, Ituri and South Kivu; OCHA HDX, Congo 2026 consolidated (INRB-UMIE, CC BY, sha256 761e1ed4…2dbf), 14 May – 29 October 2026; INSP, Rapport de situation SitRep MVE-BDBV, the 141 PDFs in INRB-UMIE/BDBV2026-Data (commit a5ee90c0, 4 November 2026), each matching its stored hash, read as text.

Quiets. A rise is a report on which a zone's running total of confirmed cases exceeds every earlier value; a quiet runs from one rise to the next. A neighbour is another zone of the same province. Intervals: 2,000 bootstrap draws over zones. Found dead. From each report's zone table, the day's new confirmed cases and the community deaths among them ("Décès communautaires (Swab+)"). A zone row is used only if it is consistent with itself, and a report only if 80 % of its rows are; on of days the zone rows sum exactly to the report's province rows. Everything is computed by analyse53.py, analyse54.py and analyse55.py into results53.json, results54.json and results55.json; this page reads them and computes only shares from their counts. No report file is committed: the repository asks for contact before use of its data, and this page publishes counts read from the public reports.

Limits. A community death counts the unseen who died, not the unseen who survived, so it is a floor on what surveillance missed. A rise dates a case by its report, not its onset. Kivu's record has one 94-day hole (8 March – 9 June 2020); quiets across it are counted in calendar days, and leaving them out (a check made after the count, sens55.py) makes Kivu's quiets look less safe, not safer. A province is a coarse neighbourhood, and 21 days, one incubation period, was chosen before the count. Two outbreaks are two cases, not a sample of Ebola. Kenya is not in the study: no zone-level Kenyan record is in hand. The study's rule is not WHO's end-of-outbreak rule, which counts from the last patient.

Beside this work

This house has three practices working on the same question: this one (science), the Atelier (artistic research) and the Studio (art). The Atelier's The Last Case lets a visitor listen to a quiet day by day and declare it over. It found that a quiet's own first 42 days sound the same whether it later broke or held; the split by neighbour in Figure 1 is this study's answer to that finding.
