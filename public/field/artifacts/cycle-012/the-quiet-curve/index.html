The Quiet Curve

WHO declares an Ebola outbreak over once 42 days pass without a new case, "twice the maximum incubation period". That rule speaks of a whole outbreak. But a case count also goes quiet zone by zone, and responders read those quiets. This study asks of two records how often a health zone that has been quiet for d days records another confirmed case within the next six weeks, and whether anything outside the zone can tell the quiets that break from those that hold.

On these two records: of the health zones that had gone 42 days without a new confirmed case, of (, interval ) recorded another within the next 42 days.

What this state shows, and what it does not yet

  • From which day is a zone quiet? Not from day 42. At zone level, while an outbreak is running, the 42-day mark leaves about a one-in-three chance of a new case within six weeks. Within three weeks it is about one in five (switch the horizon).
  • The neighbourhood tells what the zone's own record does not. The Atelier found that the first 42 days of a quiet sound the same whether it later broke or held (The Last Case: 0.54 and 0.44 against a coin's 0.50). Split by a neighbour's case: at day 42, of quiets with a case elsewhere in the province in the last 21 days broke, against without one. But only quiets at day 42 lacked such a neighbour. The split rests on them. Read as a test, "no case in the province for 21 days" is close to "the outbreak has ended here".
  • How much the count does not see. On counts alone, the count admits a chain it missed only when a case comes from nowhere visible: of . Every other break could be an import from a known focus or a chain nobody followed. A count cannot tell those apart. The next states bring in what the reports say about each new case: whether it was a listed contact, and whether it was found dead in the community.

  • Kenya is not in this state. No zone-level Kenyan record is in hand yet.

Predictions, written before any count (PREDICTIONS.md)

P2 and P4 held, but on 7 quiets without an active neighbour at day 42, none of which broke (Wilson interval 0–35 %). They are weak. After the first run, P2 was scored by its own words (at least three times the share without a neighbour; three times zero is zero). The first run's code needed a finite ratio and scored a zero as a failure. Read post hoc: without a neighbour, the curve still breaks 2 or 3 times in 9 to 12 quiets at days 49–63, so P4's single day flatters it. P5 failed low: sourceless breaks are rarer than predicted (under 3 %, not 10–40 %). P6 failed: the two records differ by 25 points at day 42.

Method

Records: OCHA HDX, North Kivu outbreak by health zone, 2018–2020 (ministry and WHO, CC BY-IGO, sha256 76c7e999…86cb), North Kivu, Ituri and South Kivu rows; OCHA HDX, Congo 2026 consolidated (INRB-UMIE, CC BY, sha256 761e1ed4…2dbf), 14 May – 29 October 2026. Quiet rule as in checks 51 and 52: a rise is a report on which a zone's cumulative confirmed count exceeds every earlier value, and a quiet runs from one rise to the next. A neighbour is another zone of the same province (Kivu: the file's province; 2026: the first four characters of the pcode). Intervals: 2,000 bootstrap draws over zones, seed 53. Everything is computed by analyse53.py into results53.json; this page reads data53.js and computes no number of its own.

Limits. A rise in a cumulative count dates a case by its report, not its onset. Kivu's record has one 94-day hole (8 March – 9 June 2020, check 52), in which no rise can be written; quiets across it are counted as calendar days. A province is a coarse neighbourhood, and 21 days is one incubation period, chosen before the count. Two outbreaks are two cases, not a sample of Ebola. This rule is not WHO's end-of-outbreak rule, which counts from the last patient.
