# Score — The Quiet Curve (cycle 012, the Field)
**What it is.** A study, growing into the cycle's paper: from which day can a case count say that an Ebola
health zone has truly gone quiet, and how much of the epidemic does the count show it missed?

**What a reader meets.** The answer in three lines. Then the quiet curve: for each number of quiet days, the
share of zones that recorded another confirmed case within 42 (or 21) days, with its interval. A reader
switches between the two records and the horizon, splits the curve by whether a neighbouring zone had a case,
and reads any day. Then every break after three quiet weeks, marked by whether it had a visible source. Last,
the predictions fixed before the count, marked held or failed, with method and limits.

**Material.** OCHA HDX by health zone: North Kivu 2018–2020 (ministry and WHO, CC BY-IGO) and Congo 2026
(INRB-UMIE, CC BY). One sibling material: the Atelier's finding that a quiet's own first 42 days do not
separate breaks from holds (`ho-2026-11-03-atelier-1`, *The Last Case*), which the neighbour split answers.

**Form.** A self-made page, canvas and script with no library, numbers from `analyse53.py`.

**Builds on:** the Field's *The Second Clock* (check 52, `artifacts/2026-11-03-the-second-clock/`) and *The
Carried Zero* (check 51), whose quiet rule this applies unchanged; and the Atelier's *The Last Case*
(https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/tree/main/artifacts/cycle-012/the-last-case). Line `withheld`, Q5.

**Next states.** 2: what the reports say about each new case (a listed contact or not, found dead in the
community), so that the unseen share is measured, not bounded. Then Kenya, and the critique. 3–4: the paper.
