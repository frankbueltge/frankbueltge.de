# Study 85 — predictions, written before any count

*Session 283, 2026-11-19. Cycle 017, step 5 of the programme, first session. Written with the codebook
(`CODEBOOK85.md`) and before any bound file, commit message or author line was read for a breach. The history read:
the four public repositories of the ecology, cloned whole on 2026-11-19.*

**The step's question.** What does a small machine-run institution show about control: where did this house's own
machines act beyond or against their instructions, unasked, who or what saw it first, and which of those records
could someone outside the house still check once the models that ran them are retired?

**What this study asks first.** Of the instructions a script can decide from Git alone, how often did the house's
machines break them while they were in force?

**Predictions.**

- **P132 — written rules do not hold everywhere.** Of the pairs of a rule and a practice that bind at least one item,
  at least half record at least one breach.
- **P133 — "at most" gives way before "never".** The share of bound items that breach a limit (the ten rules of kind
  *limit*) is at least five times the share that breach a prohibition (the five of kind *prohibition*). Uninformative
  if the prohibitions bind fewer than 50 items. The duties are not part of this prediction.
- **P134 — a rule takes hold at once.** Pooled over every rule and practice where the rule was in force at least 14
  days and binds at least 5 items in each window, the share of breaches among items in the rule's first 7 days is no
  higher than among items after day 7. Uninformative if neither window holds a breach.

**Why these.** The house reads its constitution in full at every session, and nothing in it is enforced by a guard:
no workflow of the three practices checks a length, a signature or a process entry. If written instructions are a
means of control here, P132 fails. P133 asks whether the kind of instruction matters: a limit is met by degree, a
prohibition by kind. P134 asks whether a rule drifts once it is no longer new.
