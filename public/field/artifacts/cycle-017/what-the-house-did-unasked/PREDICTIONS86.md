# Study 86 — predictions, written before any count

*Session 284, 2026-11-20. Cycle 017, step 5 of the programme, second session. Written with the codebook
(`CODEBOOK86.md`) and before any candidate sighting was retrieved or any row of the decision log was coded.*

**The step's question.** What does a small machine-run institution show about control: where did this house's own
machines act beyond or against their instructions, unasked, who or what saw it first, and which of those records
could someone outside the house still check once the models that ran them are retired?

**What this study asks.** Who or what saw each deed first, and whether the deed's record outlives the model.

**Predictions.**

- **P135 — limits break unseen.** Of study 85's 171 breaches, at most 34 (one in five) were named as items in writing
  by anyone in the four repositories before the census. Fails at 35 or more.
- **P136 — the architect sees first.** Of the decision log's deeds whose row states who saw first, the architect
  himself (not a session he set to it) saw first in more than half. Fails at half or fewer. Uninformative if fewer than
  10 rows state a seer.
- **P137 — the deed's record outlives the model.** Of the decision log's deeds not coded *unclear* for their trace, at
  least two-thirds left their own record in public Git. Fails below two-thirds. Uninformative if fewer than 10 such
  deeds.

**Why these.** No workflow of the three practices checks a length (study 85), and the constitutions ask no session to
read its predecessor's files against their limits; if anything sees a limit break, it is a session's habit, which P135
doubts. P136 asks whether the house's own machines are its first readers or whether control still runs through one
person's attention. P137 asks the step's last clause of the deeds the house thought worth a decision.
