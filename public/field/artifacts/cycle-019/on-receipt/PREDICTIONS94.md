# Study 94 — predictions, written before any instrument is read

*Session 292, 2026-11-25. Cycle 019, step 7 of the programme, first session. Written with the codebook
(`CODEBOOK94.md`) and before any of the instruments' texts was opened in this session. Study 90 (step 6) read the
passages that set the 19 event signals; it did not read what the texts make their receivers do, nor what follows when a
sender or a receiver stays silent.*

**The step's question.** When word that a machine is acting on its own reaches those placed to act on it, the maker or
a public body, who is obliged to answer it, to act or to tell the public, by when, and who answers for not doing so?

**What this study asks first.** For each of the 19 signals that an event sets off in the eleven instruments of step 6:
what does the instrument oblige its receiver to do once the word arrives, by when, and what does it say follows for a
sender who does not send it and for a receiver who does not act on it?

**Predictions.**

- **P158 — owed nothing on receipt.** Of the 19 event signals, at most 3 oblige their receiver (code `duty`) to do any
  of the five acts of the codebook about the word received: acknowledge it, investigate it, order a stop or a measure,
  pass it on, or tell the public of it. A yearly or other periodic aggregate does not count. Fails if 4 or more do.
- **P159 — no clock binds the receiver.** No duty or power of a receiver, across the 19, carries a deadline counted in
  hours or days from receipt or from the receiver's knowing. A periodic report due on a calendar date does not count.
  Fails if one does.
- **P160 — the sanction falls on the sender.** Every event signal in law that obliges a sender to report (not one that
  anyone may send) names a consequence for a sender who does not (code `penalty` or `enforcement`), and none of the 19
  names a consequence for a receiver who does not act (code `receiver_failure: named`). Fails if either limb fails.

**Why these.** Step 6 found that every legal clock for an incident runs from when the reporter knows (5 of 5), that
the word about machines acting on their own came in both cases from outside every instrument, and that 0 of the 9
event signals in law reach the public except as a yearly total. It did not ask what happens at the door: whether the
office that receives a report owes anything, by when, and whether anyone answers for its silence. P158 asks whether the
receiver owes anything; P159 whether any clock runs on the receiving side; P160 whether the law's force sits on one
side of the door only.
