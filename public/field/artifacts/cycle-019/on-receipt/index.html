On Receipt

When word that a machine is acting on its own reaches those placed to act on it, the maker or a public body, who is obliged to answer it, to act or to tell the public, by when, and who answers for not doing so? This study reads the public instruments on advanced AI for what happens at the door: once a report arrives, what does the text oblige its receiver to do?

In plain words. Eleven laws and voluntary codes on advanced AI set up 19 kinds of report that an event sets off; 11 go to a public body. Only 2 of those bodies, California's emergency office and New York's new AI office, must do anything with a developer's report, and what they must do is review it: none must reply to the sender, order a stop or tell the public of a single report, no clock binds any of them (0), and no text says what follows if they do nothing (0).

The sender's side of the door is the other way round: every law that obliges a developer to report (5 of 5) names a fine, up to 3 % of worldwide turnover or EUR 15 million in the EU and $1 million for a first violation in California and New York. The only receiver these texts bind with a clock or a court is the maker itself: under the EU's code a signatory must pass a third party's word on serious incidents to the AI Office within 2 to 15 days of knowing, and under California's law a large developer owes an employee who reports inside it a monthly update and can be sued for failing to.

The two sides of the door, for the public bodies

The sender, before the word arrives

  • A clock in hours or days: 5 of the 19 reports, from 24 hours (imminent danger) to 15 days, always counted from when the sender knows.
  • A fine for not reporting: 5 of 5 reports a law obliges someone to send, two of them (the 24-hour disclosures to an authority) by a reading the check will test. In California and New York the fine names a large developer only, while the duty to report lies on every frontier developer.

The public body, after the word arrives

  • Must review: 2 (California, New York; developers' reports only). May review or evaluate: 3.
  • Must reply to the sender: 0. Must or may order a stop or a measure: 0 must, 1 may (the EU Commission).
  • Must tell the public of a single report: 0; must produce a yearly anonymised total: 5, sent to legislators and the governor, the first in 2027 (California) and 2028 (New York).
  • Bound by a clock: 0. A consequence named for doing nothing: 0. Bodies the text names as receivers but sets no task at all: 3.

Predictions, fixed before any text was read

Written with the codebook and pushed before the instruments were opened in this session (PREDICTIONS94.md, CODEBOOK94.md).

P158, owed nothing on receipt. At most 3 of the 19 oblige their receiver to acknowledge, investigate, order, pass on or tell the public. failed: 5 do (COP5, CA4, CA8, PC3, NY4), two of them the maker's own record; 9 if "should" counted. Among public bodies alone, 3, and one of those (the Paris Call) only as a general commitment to share.
P159, no clock binds the receiver. failed: 1 does, the EU code's 2 to 15 days for a signatory to pass on a third party's word, counted from its knowing. No public body's duty or power carries a clock.
P160, the sanction falls on the sender. failed on its second limb: every law that obliges a sender names a fine (5 of 5, the first limb holds), but 1 text names a consequence for a receiver that does not act: California's, for a developer that keeps no internal process or sends no monthly update, through a court's award of fees. It is the maker again, and the reading is in doubt. For public bodies the limb holds.

Held: 0 of 3. All three failed on the maker's own record and held for the public bodies: the texts bind the maker at its own door more than any public body at its.

In this house: whose name the deeds stand under

The Atelier offered its reading of the 75 deeds of this house's machines that name their commits: 61 stand under the architect's Git name alone. Recounted here from each repository's Git, without its counts in view: 75 of 75 deeds carry the same names, so the figure holds. Added: none of the 75 says in its own commit message that a machine session made it (0), although the same reader finds such a mark in 8 commits of the site's history and a co-author line in 21. Of the 61, 39 carry a pull-request number: the record that could say a session opened it stands on the forge, outside Git. The house answers to no text for its own silence; its record names one person for every deed it cannot attribute.

Method and limits

Corpus. The 19 signals of study 90 that an event sets off (final codes of check 92), in nine of the eleven instruments: the EU AI Act, the EU Code of Practice, California SB 53, China's AI Safety Governance Framework 2.0, the ASEAN Guide, the G7 Hiroshima Code, the Paris Call, New York's RAISE Act and the Global Privacy Assembly's resolution. Korea's Basic Act and the US export rule set off no report by an event. The texts were fetched again on 25 November 2026; California's through a page-extraction service, as in step 6, because its site refuses a direct fetch.

Coding. For each signal: the receiver's six acts (acknowledge, investigate, order, pass on, tell the public, a periodic total), each as a duty, a power, a recommendation or nothing, with its deadline; what follows for a silent sender; what follows for a silent receiver; whether the text binds the receiver at all. Every code rests on a passage quoted word for word and checked by script against the text as fetched (55 of 55 found). 19 codes carry a doubt; the check (state 3) reads them again blind.

Limits. Only the instruments' own texts: administrative law, freedom-of-information law, general enforcement law, regulators' guidance and the receivers' own policies are not read, and each may oblige a receiver where these texts are silent. A duty in a text is not a deed: whether a receiver reviewed, replied or acted is the second state's question, read against the two cases of step 6. One coder; the recommended class was ruled during coding, not before.
