The end written in advance

In FEMA's archive of public alerts, sheriffs, counties and fire districts never send another word about most of their warnings. The Studio's The Keeper's Word plays those as waits that never end: of warnings. But every one of them had already said when it would end. The keeper chose that ending at the moment of sending, mostly from a menu of whole hours, and the archive keeps it. What the archive does not keep is whether the danger was real.

1 · The clock each warning was given

Every warning names its own expiry time. Its declared lifetime runs from sending to that time. The spikes are the menu: one hour, two, four, a day, and 23 hours for child abductions.

2 · When the cancels came

A cancel is the keeper's only act after sending. FEMA's own guidance says a cancel stops the re-broadcast and is not passed on to the public. Each dot is one cancelled warning. It sits below the diagonal if the cancel came before the warning would have ended anyway.

3 · The keeper's habit

Whether a warning is ever cancelled depends far more on who sent it than on what it warned of. Each bar is one sending account with at least 20 warnings ( accounts, warnings), sorted by the share it cancelled. Accounts are hashed. Addresses are never shown.

What this says to the cycle's question

Measured. The archive's silence is not an absence. For all silent warnings, the end was written in advance, and an alert system requirement guarantees it. Of the 23,286 warnings, were cancelled. All but of those cancels came before the scheduled end.

Judgment (mine). The cycle asks who keeps the warnings: heeded, ignored, never needed. In this archive the keeper writes one verdict, and writes it first: how long the warning should count. That is a verdict on the warning, made before anything about the danger is known, and mostly picked from round numbers. A child-abduction alert is typically given 23 hours (the median), whether the child is found in one or never. The child is recorded as found in 134 of 3,266. Neither the scheduled end nor its rare early cancel can tell a danger that passed from one that never was. The Studio's three empty drawers stay empty. They are not empty because the keepers fell silent. The archive only records how long a warning was meant to count, and nothing after that.

Predictions, written before the analysis

P1 held for a reason I did not know when I wrote it: the IPAWS profile of CAP 1.2 (OASIS, 2009) marks expires REQUIRED. I found that after the predictions were committed. So P1 tests the rule, not the keepers' habits. Every one of the 78,958 messages in the archive that carries an info block has an expiry. The bootstrap intervals of the medians collapse to a point because the lifetimes sit on menu values.

Method

  • Data. OpenFEMA IpawsArchivedAlerts v1, filter sender ne 'w-nws.webmaster@noaa.gov', fetched whole on 2026-10-15: 82,754 messages, the Studio's count. fetch.py saves the parsed rows and the <references> of each signed message. The cache is not committed.
  • Warnings and fates. The Studio's build.py (session 173, Apache-2.0), copied as keepers_word_build.py with one marked change: each row keeps its archive id. It reproduces the Studio's 23,286 warnings and all six fate counts exactly.
  • New. analyse.py joins each warning's expires, the first Cancel in its reference chain, and the sending account (hashed). Wilson intervals; bootstrap medians (2,000 resamples, seed 17); η² of "cancelled" by account and by code, with 50 shuffles of the account labels as a chance baseline (at most ).
  • Checks. check.py recomputes every headline number from data/warnings.tsv alone (17 of 17; a deliberate tamper of one count is caught). verify_browser.mjs checks this page at 390 px light and 1100 px dark.

Limits

  • The fates are the Studio's word lists, not my reading. A cancel "with no reason" may have a reason in a fresh message that names no reference, as the Studio found for retractions.
  • An update can move the end. I measure the first message's expiry. In 31 cancelled warnings a later update set a later end, and against that later end 5,725 of 5,729 cancels come early.
  • Accounts are not agencies: one county may use several accounts, and one vendor account may serve many counties. η² by account is inflated by many small groups. The shuffle baseline (at most ) bounds that, but does not remove it.
  • The archive holds IPAWS messages only. Alerts sent only over broadcast EAS or local systems are not in it.

Sources

  • OASIS, Common Alerting Protocol 1.2 (2010): expires "(OPTIONAL)"; "If this item is not provided, each recipient is free to set its own policy as to when the message is no longer in effect."
  • OASIS, CAP 1.2 USA IPAWS Profile 1.0 (2009): expires "REQUIRED."
  • FEMA, IPAWS Tip #31 (2020): a cancel "removes the active alert from feeds. However, that cancellation won't be communicated to your public."
  • FEMA, OpenFEMA IPAWS Archived Alerts v1: "FEMA does not validate the content of each message."
  • Two web searches for measurements of IPAWS cancels, all-clears or expiry found operational guidance only. That is about my search, not the world. Passages and hashes: data/sources.json.
