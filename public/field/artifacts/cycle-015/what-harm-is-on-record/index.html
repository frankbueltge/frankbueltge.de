What Harm Is on Record

The programme asks whether increasingly capable AI can be kept under human and democratic control, and by whom. This step asks what the public record of AI incidents shows about where control already fails, who wrote each entry (the harmed, the press or the maker), and which of those recorded failures a public body has acted on. The record read here is the largest open one, the AI Incident Database, whole.

The answer so far.

A first state. Who wrote a report is read from its web address only, by fixed rules; whether a writer was among the harmed is not yet read (the next state samples it by hand). Every number is computed by analyse75.py from the snapshot named below.

Method

Record. AI Incident Database, snapshot backup-20261109100729.tar.bz2, fetched from the database's own snapshot page on 15 November 2026 (sha256 ), CSV export. The database is shared under CC BY-SA 4.0; report texts stay with their owners and none is copied here. Who wrote it. For each incident, the linked report published first; its web domain is classed in this order: the maker (a name of the incident's alleged developer or deployer is a label of the domain), a public body (government, court and regulator domains), research (universities, papers, preprints), self-published (platforms without an editor: social networks, video, blogs, code hosts), the press and other (everything else). Where it fails. The database's own MIT risk classification, not re-coded. Response. The database tags some reports as a response; an incident with one is counted, whoever responded.

Limits

A web address says who published a report, not who was harmed: the class self-published is the closest a domain comes to the harmed, and it holds bystanders and researchers too. Its names were matched by rule and not read; a maker's newsroom on another domain (about.fb.com) falls outside maker. The first run of the rules matched suffixes without a label boundary and was fixed in the same session; its result on the predictions was the same. The record holds what its editors and submitters found and chose; it is not a census of harm. The match to step 2's acts is a search plus a reading by one reader; a second reading comes with the check.
