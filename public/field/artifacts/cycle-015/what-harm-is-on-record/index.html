What Harm Is on Record

The programme asks whether increasingly capable AI can be kept under human and democratic control, and by whom. This step asks what the public record of AI incidents shows about where control already fails, who wrote each entry (the harmed, the press or the maker), and which of those recorded failures a public body has acted on. The record read here is the largest open one, the AI Incident Database, whole.

The answer so far.

Third state: the check. Who wrote is read from a web address by fixed rules (study 75) and by hand (study 76). The check (study 77) had every hand-read item coded a second time by readers who never saw the first coding, read the responses whole and not by their opening, read a seeded draw of the press reports nobody had read, and read every disagreement a third time. What held and what was corrected is listed under The check. Every number is computed by analyse75.py, analyse76.py and analyse77.py from the snapshot named below.

Method

Record. AI Incident Database, snapshot backup-20261109100729.tar.bz2, fetched from the database's own snapshot page on 15 November 2026 (sha256 ), CSV export. The database is shared under CC BY-SA 4.0; report texts stay with their owners and none is copied here. Who wrote it. For each incident, the linked report published first; its web domain is classed in this order: the maker (a name of the incident's alleged developer or deployer is a label of the domain), a public body (government, court and regulator domains), research (universities, papers, preprints), self-published (platforms without an editor: social networks, video, blogs, code hosts), the press and other (everything else). Where it fails. The database's own MIT risk classification, not re-coded. Response. The database tags some reports as a response; an incident with one is counted, whoever responded.

Read by hand (study 76). sheet76.py writes, outside this repository, the texts a reader reads; only the codes are committed (coding76.json, with a one-line reason per item, no copied text). A self-published first report is harmed when the writer says they, or the organisation they speak for, were affected, or the record's column of harmed parties names the writer; witness when the writer found, tested, watched or collected the failure; maker when the developer or deployer writes about its own system. Two codes were added while reading because the fixed ones did not fit: record note (the database's own note) and, for public acts, whom the act lands on (the developer or deployer named in the record, or the person who used the AI). The second is descriptive and was not predicted.

The check (study 77). The snapshot was fetched a third time and matched by hash. Convened readers, each a machine reader given only the codebook (CODEBOOK.md) and the texts, coded every item again: the 58 self-published first reports up to 6,000 characters each, the response reports of all 176 answered incidents up to 3,500 characters each, and a draw of 120 of the 1,683 press and research first reports (random.seed(20261115)) up to 2,500 characters each. Their codes are in second-coding.json and press-sample.json. Every disagreement on who wrote a self-published report and on whether a public body acted was then read a third time in the text; each decision and its reason is in adjudication.json.

Limits

A web address says who published a report, not who was harmed: the class self-published is the closest a domain comes to the harmed, and it holds bystanders and researchers too. Its names were matched by rule and not read; a maker's newsroom on another domain (about.fb.com) falls outside maker. The first run of the rules matched suffixes without a label boundary and was fixed in the same session; its result on the predictions was the same. The record holds what its editors and submitters found and chose; it is not a census of harm. The match to step 2's acts is a search plus a reading by one reader; a second reading comes with the check. Study 76 was one reader's first coding, of the opening of each response; the check read them whole and found ten public acts the opening did not show. The second readers are machine readers, one per item, and texts were cut at a fixed length, so an act stated later in a long report can still be missing. Who else answered (the maker, others) differs more between the two readings than the public acts do and was not read a third time: those counts are given as a range. Whether the system worked or failed, and the state and date of each act, were coded once. The draw of 120 is small: the share of press reports written by the harmed lies between 1.3 and 8.3 in 100. Only reports the database itself tags as a response were read: an answer the editors did not tag is not counted, so these are shares of the answered record, not of what was done in the world. Correction to study 75 (15 November 2026): six of its 58 self-published first reports are the database's own note on incidents it converted to issues, and two are the maker's own text on a code host or a social network; the class is not the harmed, and study 75's shares stand as shares of web addresses only.
