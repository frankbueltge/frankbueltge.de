# Critique — What Harm Is on Record, second state

*Session 274, 2026-11-15. A convened reader that did not build the work, meeting the page in a browser at 1100 and 390 px. Three of its points (2, 6, 8) were answered in the answer box the same session; the rest stand for the check and the paper.*

1. It carries two thirds of the question: who wrote (press 92.1 %, maker 1.3 %, harmed 21) and what a public body acted on (50 of 176, 30 on the user). "Where control already fails" is not answered: the answer box has no sentence on where the failures sit.
2. Weakest claim: "the harmed wrote first in 21 of 1,801: 1.17 %". Only the 58 self-published reports were read; the 1,659 "press and other" are a residual class never read for authorship. 21 is a floor, stated as the share.
3. "The record of where control fails is written by the press" reports the archive's intake (it is compiled from news links) as a finding about who records harm.
4. "No more often" (0.64 % against 0.8 %) rests on 2 of 314 against 12 of 1,496, too few to compare, and the counts are not shown.
5. Unremarked: in 2026 the maker wrote first in 13 of 309, more than half of all 23 maker first reports; the page presents a flat 1.3 %.
6. "One of the 50 concerns a failure of the system itself": 23 of the 50 are unclassified, so it is 1 of 27.
7. The two measures of a public act do not meet: of the three incidents matched to step 2's acts, 513 is coded "maker answered" only, 1144 is not among the 176, only 1329 is in both. The 50 undercount public acts inside this record.
8. The strongest finding is buried in a caption: on a maker of a frontier model, three proceedings opened, none concluded, one filing for the maker. "20 on a developer or deployer" lets a visitor think twenty makers were acted on.
9. First figure: the four non-press classes are slivers; it shows the database growing, not authorship. At 390 px it is unreadable.
10. The button "Is a public act on record" shows responses by anyone and three matches; the 50 hand-coded acts are absent from it.
11. No table lists the 50 acts with body, act and date, so the 30/20 split cannot be checked on the page.
12. The 30 acts on users are mostly prosecutions of people using AI as a tool; whether that answers "where control fails" is left unsaid.
13. Legend and caption count different bases (all incidents; those with a first report), unmarked.
14. Missing: a second coder, a sample of the 1,659 read, the date of each act against its incident. The kicker says study 75 though half the page is study 76.
