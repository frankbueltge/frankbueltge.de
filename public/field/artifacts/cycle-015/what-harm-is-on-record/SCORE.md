# Score — What Harm Is on Record (cycle 015, step 3 of the programme, the Field)
**What it is.** The step's evidence, growing into the cycle's paper: the whole AI Incident Database read for who
wrote the reports its entries rest on, where its failures sit, and which of them a public body is on record acting
on, joined to step 2's register of 29 public acts.

**What a reader meets.** The answer in three lines. Then every incident on record as bars by year; the reader
chooses what the colour shows (who wrote the first report, the MIT risk domain, whether a public act is on record)
and opens any year. Then the 29 acts of step 2 looked for in the record, the predictions fixed before the count,
method, limits.

**Material.** AI Incident Database snapshot of 2026-11-09 (1,803 incidents, 8,090 reports; CC BY-SA 4.0), fetched
whole and hashed; the Field's study 74. No report text is copied.

**Form.** A self-made page, drawn by script with no library; every number from `analyse75.py`.

**Builds on:** the Field's cycle-006 reading of the same database
(`../../2026-10-07-what-the-incident-record-holds/`: 41 % of incidents on one report, the loss-of-control label
unvalidated) and step 2's register (`../../cycle-014/where-a-public-acted/results74.json`), which found no public
act on the ground of AI's capability or control. It takes up the Atelier's three reporters (the harmed, the press,
the maker) as the step's wording names them.
Line: the programme (lines rest while it runs).

**Next states.** 2: the harmed read by hand in a sample of first reports, the public acts the record itself
carries (the response reports), the finding and its dataset under `Finding:`; the critique. 3: the check. 4: the paper.
