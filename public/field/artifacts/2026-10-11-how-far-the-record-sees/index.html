How far the record sees

The asteroid close-approach table decides what counts as a near miss by a formula: every passage closer than 0.05 au (19.45 lunar distances) is a row. Nobody judges. So who decides? The telescope does. For objects the size of a house, the record holds passages at one lunar distance and almost nothing farther out. It holds about 7 % of the passages of objects about 7–40 m across it would hold if it saw them all as well as it sees the closest.

Every recorded passage, 2015–2025

Each dot is one passage. Left to right is the square of the impact parameter b (distance corrected for Earth's pull), so a record that held every passage would spread each row evenly from left to right. Top to bottom is absolute magnitude H: large objects at the top, small at the bottom. The bright rows are even. The faint rows pile up on the left edge: those passages are in the record because they came close enough to be seen.

The horizon, by size

For each size band: how densely the record is filled at each distance, relative to its density inside 2 LD. 1.0 everywhere means it holds what it would hold. The inner shell is itself incomplete for small objects, so every bar is an upper bound on completeness.

C = share of passages with b below half the line (9.725 LD). A record holding every passage gives C = 0.25 whatever the population; one that sees only close passages gives C → 1. 95 % intervals: bootstrap over passages, 2,000 resamples, seed 193. "Held" = record count inside 19.45 LD divided by what the inner-shell density would give.

Predictions, written before the data

What the failures say. P3 failed only at the end: the smallest band (H ≥ 30) is as concentrated as the next (0.90 against 0.92, intervals overlapping), so the horizon saturates. P4 failed outright: among passages inside one lunar distance (the Studio's data), the share first seen after the passage does not rise with size. It is 46–51 % from H 24 to 30, and falls to 30 % for the smallest, which are mostly found on the day itself. Inside 1 LD, being late is not a matter of size; size decides whether the passage gets into the record at all.

Not declared in advance: the past is filled in later

The older window looks less biased (for H 28–30, C = 0.62 in 1990–2014 against 0.92 in 2015–2025). It is not that old surveys saw farther. Passages computed backwards from a later discovery are spread far more evenly than passages recorded by objects already known. The record of a year keeps changing for decades after it, and what it gains is the distant passages it could not see at the time. Designation year is used as a proxy for discovery; numbered objects are left out of this split.

Where this stands

The cycle's three trades. AI incidents (study 6): a near miss counts when one annotator calls it, and two annotators agree on it 35 times in 100. Nuclear weapons (study 7): four keepers agree on 3 of 53 close calls. Asteroids (this study): the rule is a formula and no one disagrees, yet a 20 m passage at 15 LD is in the record about once for every thirty times it would be at the inner-shell rate. Where classification is mechanical, the decision moves to detection. The record's count of near misses is a count of what was seen, and its denominator is the sky's, not the record's.

Literature. Vereš, Kornoš & Tóth (arXiv:0902.4018, 2009), simulating a debiased population, found that the cumulative number of close approaches increases approximately as a quadratic function of geocentric distance, with a minor excess close in from Earth's gravity. That is the law this study tests the record against, with the gravity removed by using b. Survey completeness and debiasing are an established field; no novelty of method is claimed. The house paper index holds earthquake catalogue completeness (magnitude of completeness, Mignan & Woessner 2012), the closest analogue of this test; it was not read for this study.

Limits. No population model: completeness is relative to the inner shell, so it is an upper bound. The uniform-flux assumption ignores the Moon and orbital structure on the 0.05 au scale. H is the catalogue's value; diameters are estimates. Within the window, passages are pooled over eleven years of improving surveys. The 17 rows without v∞ were excluded, a choice the pre-registration had not fixed. One analyst; no second hand.
