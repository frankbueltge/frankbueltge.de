The Second Clock

An Ebola outbreak in a district is called over after 42 days with no new case. The Atelier found that on Congo's 2026 record, counting only the days that had a report does what a plain wait of 49 calendar days does. This page asks whether that holds in an earlier record. In the Kivu outbreak of 2018–2020 it does not. There the missing days are not scattered. They come as one hole of 94 days, and the two clocks disagree only on the quiets that fall into it.

What came out

  • 2026 (Bundibugyo). 26 of 169 days have no report, and they are scattered: 17 single days, 3 pairs, 1 run of three. A calendar wait of 49 or 50 days classes all 22 long quiets exactly as the report count does. 42 divided by the share of days with a report (84.6 %) gives 49.6. Scattered gaps stretch every quiet by about the same amount, so a longer wait stands in for the report count.
  • Kivu 2018–2020. 576 of 685 days have a report (84.1 %), almost the same share. Yet no calendar wait from 42 to 90 days matches the report count exactly. Nearly all the missing days lie in one hole, from 8 March to 9 June 2020 (94 days). Elsewhere only 15 days are missing (runs of 8 and 3, and four single days). Of the 60 quiets that reach 42 calendar days, the report count calls 58 over. A 42-day wait disagrees on 2, and both lie across the hole. A 49-day wait, the 2026 equivalent, disagrees on 6.
  • One of the two is Beni. In the file, Beni's confirmed count stands at 721 from 17 February 2020. No report follows from 8 March, and the next one, on 10 June, writes 728. WHO announced a new confirmed case in Beni on 10 April 2020, and later counted seven confirmed cases since the resurgence. Those are the seven. On a calendar clock this file lets Beni's quiet reach 42 on 30 March, on days no report in the file covers. The report count never calls it over (18 reported days). The file dates the flare-up 61 days after it was announced.
  • So the answer is no, and it is general. A report-day count equals a fixed calendar wait only when the missing days are spread evenly. Whether they are is a fact about each record, not something the share of reported days tells you. A clock that counts testimony refuses to call a quiet over across a hole, and a calendar clock fills the hole with silence.

Limits. "No report" means no report in the HDX copy. The ministry's own bulletins for 8 March to 9 June 2020 were not searched, so the hole may be the copy's, not the ministry's. Each Kivu row links its bulletin, but no bulletin was opened. The quiet rule is the Atelier's, applied unchanged: a quiet runs from a rise in a zone's cumulative confirmed count to the next rise. It is not WHO's end-of-outbreak rule, which counts from the last patient's second negative test. Two outbreaks are two cases, not a sample.

Predictions, written before any count (PREDICTIONS.md)

P3 failed. The best wait in Kivu is 42–44 days, not 49.9. The share of reported days does not tell you where the equivalent wait lies, because one hole does not stretch every quiet. Read post hoc, that failure is the finding.

The Studio's question: why do 7 and 8 October 2026 carry zeros?

Because the file is silent on those days, not zero. The HDX file has no rows dated 7 or 8 October 2026 (130 rows each on 6 and 9 October). INSP did issue SitReps 146 and 147 for those days (check 51), but the transcription never took them in. Any zero on those days was written by whoever filled the absent dates. In the étude it comes from carrying the 6 October count forward. The national confirmed count goes from 8,728 on 6 October to 8,914 on 9 October, so the 186 cases of 9 October cover three days. Method and material

Kivu record: OCHA HDX, Ebola Cases and Deaths in the North Kivu Ebola Outbreak (DRC Ministry of Health, WHO; CC BY-IGO), health-zone file, sha256 76c7e999…86cb. North Kivu, Ituri and South Kivu only. Two renamed zones were merged (their date ranges do not overlap). 2026 record: the Field's own day classes (check 51, data51.js). Rule: the Atelier's said.py (session 29). A quiet "reaches" a calendar wait T when it has at least T days after its rise and before the next. It reaches the report count when 42 of those days have a report. The script reproduces the Atelier's 2026 result (49 days, exact) before reading Kivu. Code: analyse52.py → results52.json, data52.js. Sources and passages: sources.json. Drawn on canvas, with no library. The form exists so a reader can move the wait and see which quiets cross.
