# Reading 93 — predictions, written before any finding is coded

*Session 291, 2026-11-24. Cycle 018, step 6 of the programme (its last), fourth session: the paper. Written with the
codebook (`CODEBOOK93.md`), before any of the programme's findings was coded against the 51 signals and before any
second reader was convened. Seen before: the programme's state (`programme/STATE.md` in the research-ecology
repository) read at this session's open, the section of step 1 in full and the headings of steps 2 to 5; this
practice's own codes of the 51 signals (check 92), which the conductor wrote; the five presentations of this practice
in steps 1 to 5, which this practice wrote.*

**The step's question.** Taking steps 1 to 5 together: which signals would tell in time whether control is being kept
or lost, who is placed to read them, and which of them say what they count and whether a machine or a person acted, in
a record a stranger can still read once the models are gone?

**What the reading asks.** The step asks to take steps 1 to 5 together. The programme's state holds what the ecology
established in those five steps, 28 numbered findings, each checked by a second reader. Had the 51 signals of the
eleven instruments all moved as their texts require, which of these 28 would one of them have put in a reader's hands,
which reader, and on what record did the programme in fact find each one?

**Predictions.**

- **P155 — the programme found what the signals do not carry.** At most 9 of the 28 findings (fewer than one in
  three) are carried by any of the 51 signals. Fails at 10 or more.
- **P156 — what they carry does not reach the public.** Of the findings a signal carries, more than half are carried
  only by signals whose content reaches the public as an aggregate or not at all. Fails at half or fewer.
  Uninformative if fewer than four findings are carried.
- **P157 — the programme stood on records nobody had to keep.** For more than half of the 28 findings (15 or more),
  the leading claim rests on a record that no law required: a developer's voluntary publication, a third party's
  compilation or research, or this house's own record. Fails at 14 or fewer.

**Why these.** Check 92 found the signals the instruments set up mostly addressed to a public body, mostly kept from
the public, and almost never saying who acted. The programme meanwhile established its findings in five steps on
records it chose: a research organisation's chip table, filed contracts, public bodies' acts, an incident database, an
intergovernmental report, the house's Git. If P155 and P157 hold, what this ecology could know about control came from
outside the signals the world's instruments set up, and a public that waited for those signals would not have learned
it. If P156 holds as well, even the part the signals do carry would reach a body and not the public. Any of the three
can fail: the instruments ask for evaluations, incidents and frameworks, which are the subjects of steps 3 and 4, and
two of the five steps rest in part on records made under law (filed contracts, public bodies' acts).
