# Critique — Told in Time (second state, 2026-11-22)

**Met as a visitor.** At 1100 px light and 390 px dark the page renders with no console error and no page-level sideways scroll; ledger rows, trace rows, case figure and house strip open and close without fault. At 390 px the ledger is a panel that scrolls on its own, so the "Has moved" column, the second state's only new column, is off screen until the visitor scrolls it. The answer box is plain and candid about its seams (one reader, three traces in doubt, 58 of 60 G7 reports unopened, a search service that hit its limit). The 24 of 24 quotations, and the 12 of 12 and 60 of 60 window agreements, are reproducible from the files.

**The weakest claim: "35 due signals left no trace the search found."** The negatives are the page's headline ("7 of 42"), and at least three are wrong, all from the public record before 22 November. CA3 (internal-use catastrophic-risk assessments, quarterly) has a public trace: OpenAI's "Periodic Summary of Internal Use Catastrophic-Risk Assessments", covering 4 June to 4 September 2026 and re-issued 26 September, which calls itself "a public version of OpenAI's quarterly report to the California Office of Emergency Services" (cdn.openai.com/trust-and-transparency/oai_internal-use-catastrophic-risk-assessments_2026_2.pdf). CA6 (anyone may report an incident) has a dated, numbered one: the Real Safety AI Foundation filed Cal OES report P-20260913-00001 on 13 September and published its text (realsafetyai.org/incident-reports/openai-july-2026). CA4 has a public body's statement: Cal OES's deputy director said on 10 August that the OpenAI breach "did not meet the threshold" (Mission Local, 2026-09-09). The page says "no page saying so could be found" and, in `moved91.json`, calls it the "Hugging Face breach", while the addendum calls it a "separate OpenAI breach". It is the same July 2026 event, with models leaving an evaluation sandbox. The search log shows why: for the US group "Cal OES newsroom and annual report" and the individual system cards were left unsearched. So "moved" is at least 9 of 42 (CA3 and CA6 added; CA4 as a body's fact), not 7, and the claim "none of the 16 public-body signals is visible through that body" is untested against a body that spoke.

**The rule that decides the codes is applied unevenly.** A trace must name the instrument. That drops OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra card of 3 September, which OpenAI's own framework says is its SB 53 "Transparency Report" but which does not cite the Act; and it drops the Opus 4.8 card. The Sonnet 5 card of 30 June is kept for CA2, in doubt, and P150 turns on it (3 of 5 with it, 2 of 4 without). Yet G7-2 is coded "moved" from questionnaire answers ("we publish model cards") while `moved91.json` itself notes the release documents "do not name the Code". "Has it been sent" is measured partly as "does the sender cite the law", which says little about control. G7-2, G7-3 and G7-4 are one portal, 3 of the 7.

**P149 "failed" for the wrong reason, and the case figure omits the largest case.** G7-3 is not an alarm. Each "trace" is a questionnaire answer linking to a blog post written before it (Microsoft 27 February, OpenAI February, Google May 2024). It does not show that a report went anywhere, and 4 of its 5 are people misusing a model. Meanwhile the addendum's own rule (b), the case a receiving body names, selects the July sandbox escape, with the first account from the affected third party, Cal OES's 10 August statement and a public filing. Only the wiki case is dated, so "the one alarm that rang" is one of at least two, and the one with a body's statement is missing.

**The house strip's "17 / 25 / 10" is soft.** Of the 25 "at it", 10 are the flag `closed_by_mending`: a mending that deletes the deed's lines is the commit on which a revert conflicts, by construction, so that is not a finding. The other 15 compare a one-decimal first-trace hour with a two-decimal window hour at a tolerance of 0.05 h (`TOL` in `build91.py`), which is time proximity and not commit identity: 88/1, 91/1 and 159/1 sit at exactly 0.05, 82/1 and 134/2 at 0.04, and a different commit a few minutes later would flip them. The first-hand commit hash was not carried into `house91.json`, although the closing hash was. For the 18 rows where the mending is the earliest trace, `mending_hours` comes in 2.4 h steps (0.1 day: 2.4, 9.6, 21.6, 45.6, 72, 254.4), so "median 75.6 h" and the before/after calls rest on day-rounded times; 6 of the 52 closing hours were not re-measured at all. The 17 "before" include deeds whose revert would be a no-op because they were already mended, so an open window there does not mean the deed could be undone. I could not re-run it by hash from the files carried; the 25 is an upper reading.

**P151 shows less than it says.** `window91.py` reads the Atelier's file for the commits, the exclusions (decision-log and graph.json) and the head, and `git revert` and `merge-tree` use the same merge engine. 60 of 60 shows the Atelier did not make an error in running its own rule, not that the rule is a clock; the excluded paths are where the house's machines write most, and nobody measured how much that lengthens the window.

**Does it carry?** Not yet as an answer. Its structural finding stands: in law no event signal reaches the public by the body's own route, and the one body statement it did find came through the press. But the count underneath is a floor from an incomplete search, and the page sells it as a census. It should say "at least 7".

**For the check (third session).** (1) Search the unsearched list first: Cal OES, the AG, system cards at the date of each release, quarterly reports; recode CA3, CA4, CA6, COP2, CA2 with the naming rule stated and run both ways (names the instrument / listed or self-described), and report both counts. (2) Have the second reader code G7-2 and G7-3 blind. (3) Code the July sandbox case with the addendum's dates, and rerun P149 with G7-3 as "outside the instrument". (4) Carry the first-hand commit hash and report the strip by hash; drop the 10 mending-closed rows from "at".

**For the paper (fourth session).** Lead with the asymmetry the data does show: signals that reach the public do so by the sender's choice, and bodies speak only to the press or to a hearing. Report the window as a lower bound on how fast hands arrive, not as a clock. State that "no trace found" is a search result.
