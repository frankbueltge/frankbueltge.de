# Study 90 — predictions, written before any instrument is read

*Session 288, 2026-11-22. Cycle 018, step 6 of the programme (its last), first session. Written with the codebook
(`CODEBOOK90.md`) and before any of the eleven instruments of its corpus was opened in this session. Study 83 (step 4)
read seven of them for evaluation and pass marks, not for what they make anyone report; the other four this practice
has not read.*

**The step's question.** Taking steps 1 to 5 together: which signals would tell in time whether control is being kept
or lost, who is placed to read them, and which of them say what they count and whether a machine or a person acted, in
a record a stranger can still read once the models are gone?

**What this study asks first.** Of the signals the world's public instruments on advanced AI require or commit someone
to send, who reads each first, how early can it move, does its record say whether a machine or a person acted, and
could a stranger read it later?

**Predictions.**

- **P146 — the office reads first, the public seldom.** Of the signals in the instruments coded `law`, fewer than half
  are coded `public` (the instrument's own rule publishes the content of the signal, in full or as a summary of each
  report). Aggregate statistics a body publishes across reports do not count as public.
- **P147 — no record says who acted.** At most one signal in the whole corpus is coded `required` on machine or person
  (the instrument requires its record to say whether an act was the AI system's own or a person's).
- **P148 — the clock starts when the reporter knows.** Of the signals fired by an event (codes `event short of harm`
  and `harm`) that state a deadline, more than half run it from the reporter's awareness (discovery, determination,
  becoming aware, establishing a link) rather than from the event itself. Uninformative if fewer than four such signals
  state a deadline.

**Why these.** Steps 2 to 5 found that a public appears as law, not as holder (step 1), that the record of harm exists
mostly because the press or two makers wrote it (step 3), that results an outsider can check expire with the models
(step 4), and that this house's own record shows a machine as the doer for 12 of 80 deeds (step 5). P146 asks who is
placed to read a signal; P147 asks the step's own clause of the instruments; P148 asks what "in time" can mean when the
clock is the reporter's: a signal timed from awareness is never earlier than the reporter's own sight, and in step 5
the first to see a machine's deed was never anyone outside the house.
