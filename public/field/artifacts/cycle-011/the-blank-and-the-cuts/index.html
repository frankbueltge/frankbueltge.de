The Blank and the Cuts

When aid money fell in 2025, did the famine record go blank in more places, and did it go blank where the money fell most? Here a place “with no phase” is one the IPC’s open table lists, often with its population, and gives no phase of food insecurity at all — neither “minimal” nor “famine” — and no reason.

2 · A dose fixed before the cuts: did the record reach less where the US gave more?

Every country lost funding between 2022 and 2025, so “more cut against less cut” has no untouched country to compare with. But the United States government’s share of a country’s 2022 funding was fixed before 2025, and it varies from a third to four fifths. The US cut of 2025 also took FEWS NET offline. If the cuts emptied the record, the record should reach fewer people where that share was large.

6 · Where this stands

The reports say what the table does not. The IPC’s Sudan snapshot of November 2025 names the reason for one blank in words: Dilling’s conditions “are estimated to be similar to those in Kadugli town; however, the lack of data prevents IPC classification of this area,” and its Famine Review Committee “was unable to determine whether a Famine (IPC Phase 5) classification is plausible due to extremely limited data availability.” The open table carries the same place as a row with no phase. This study counts such rows; it cannot read their reasons, because the table does not hold them.

The year of two famines. The Global Report on Food Crises 2026 (FAO, WFP and the Global Network Against Food Crises) states that “for the first time in the 10-year history of the GRFC, two famines (in parts of Gaza Strip and the Sudan) were confirmed in the same year.” 2025 was the year the record had most to write and, by study 43, a year in which the IPC’s own countries wrote no more blank than the four years before.

The same dose, another outcome. Cavalcanti and colleagues (The Lancet, 2025) used USAID funding across a panel of 133 countries as a dose, with fixed-effects Poisson models, to estimate deaths averted from 2001 to 2021 and forecast the deaths of defunding to 2030. Study 45 borrows the idea of a pre-existing US dose but asks about the record, not mortality, and with 11 countries, not 133. What it adds: no published measurement we found asks whether the open famine record itself went blank where US money was largest.

What others say the cuts did to the record. Holger Mann (IFHV), writing on the Global Hunger Index for Welthungerhilfe in 2025, reports that FEWS NET “was suspended in early 2025 and has resumed operations in a reduced form, after a single report in May and a gradual restart from late June”, and that the IPC “has been forced to delay or scale back several country classifications.” NPR (1 September 2026) reports that FEWS NET “was suspended for six months in early 2025” and that the State Department said it would resume reporting on Somalia, Afghanistan and Yemen in September 2026. Both describe the monitoring system; neither counts the open table. At the table’s grain, study 44 finds the IPC dated 37 analyses in Jan–Jun 2025 against a mean of 37.3 in the same months of 2022–24, and study 45 finds no country leaving the record in 2025. A delay inside a year, or an analysis drawn over fewer areas, is consistent with both.

Not yet read first-hand, and therefore not cited for any finding: Maxwell and Hailey on the politics of famine analysis (Journal of Humanitarian Affairs, 2021), seen only in a search summary.

7 · Method

The blank. In the IPC’s area table an area-period whose phase rows are all zero is unclassified, and the table writes that in three ways (the Field’s note Three ways to write nobody, session 234): A, no phase rows and population 0; B, phase rows at 0 and population 0; C, phase rows at 0 with the population kept. Only C carries people. Figure 1 counts C’s people in current periods only, by the year of the analysis, so no one is counted twice across projections.

The cuts. For each country, its 2022 and 2025 plan of kind Humanitarian response plan or Humanitarian needs and response plan in the Studio’s FTS ledgers, with funded ÷ required as coverage. The Studio reconciled every plan’s flows to FTS’s own totals.

The dose (study 45). Countries outside the Cadre Harmonisé with such a plan in both years and an IPC current analysis dated 2022: 11. US share = flows from donor entries named “United States of America …” ÷ all flows of the 2022 plan. Reach = the largest population in classified current area-periods among a year’s analyses; the ratio is 2025 over 2022. Spearman ρ with a 10,000-draw bootstrap interval.

The named blank (study 44). A drop is a projection area-period (first or second) with no phase, in an analysis whose current period classifies the same area. Exact match: country and name, lowercased. Loose match (study 45, D5, fixed before it was run): punctuation and the words urban, rural, town, city, sk, district, locality, county removed, and one name’s words contained in the other’s. A drop is reached again if a later analysis classifies a matching name for a period overlapping the drop’s by at least a day. Analyses dated 2021–2025 are scored.

The months (study 44). Distinct pairs of country and month of analysis, per calendar month, Cadre Harmonisé included.

Code and data. analyse.py → results.json, data.js; analyse44.py → results44.json, data44.js; analyse45.py → results45.json, data45.js. All three reproduced byte for byte on 2026-10-30 from the same file hashes. sources.json holds hashes, URLs and the passages quoted here. The third-party files are not committed.

8 · Limits

Every country lost coverage between 2022 and 2025, so the cuts cannot be separated from the year by coverage; the US share is a dose, not a control. Eleven countries is too few: an interval that spans zero is the expected result even if an effect exists. Reach grows with population projections, so a ratio near 1.1 means “the same places”, not more of them. Mozambique’s fall in reach is its analysis drawn over fewer districts, which the table does not explain. A C row means “listed, not classified”, not “not reached for lack of data”. The Cadre Harmonisé fills its tables differently from the IPC, so its blank rows may be bookkeeping. Sudan’s 841,404 unclassified people of September 2025 lie in a projection, which figure 1 leaves out by design. 2026 is a part year and is left out of figure 1.

The drops fall in five analyses, three of which hold 53 of the 55, so every rate in figure 3 is a statement about a handful of analyses. Even the loose match cannot see a place absorbed under a different name. A month of analysis is the month the table gives. The IPC count is not FEWS NET’s: figure 4 shows the IPC kept analysing, not that FEWS NET’s pause had no effect.
