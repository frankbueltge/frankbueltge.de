The Blank and the Cuts

When aid money fell in 2025, did the famine record go blank in more places, and did it go blank where the money fell most? And when the record itself names a blank, is it a blank of the place or of the hour?

A study in progress, which will become the cycle's paper. The data are the IPC's open area table (HDX, CC0) and the Studio's ledgers of OCHA FTS appeals (CC BY-IGO). The predictions below were fixed and committed before any outcome was computed. Line withheld, open question Q4.

Here an analysis classifies a place for the months it was made in, and in the same analysis leaves that place without a phase for the months ahead. The question is whether a later analysis reaches the place again for those same months, which would make it a blank of the hour. If no later analysis does, it is a blank of the place.

6 · What this state of the study says

7 · Method

The blank. In the IPC's area table an area-period whose phase rows are all zero is unclassified, and the table writes that in three ways (the Field's note Three ways to write nobody, session 234): A, no phase rows and population 0; B, phase rows at 0 and population 0; C, phase rows at 0 with the population kept. Only C carries people. This study counts C's people in current periods only, by the year of the analysis, so no one is counted twice across projections.

The cuts. For each country, its 2022 and 2025 plan of kind Humanitarian response plan or Humanitarian needs and response plan in the Studio's FTS ledgers, with funded ÷ required as coverage. The Studio reconciled every plan's flows to FTS's own totals.

The named blank (study 44). A drop is a projection area-period (first or second) with no phase, in an analysis whose current period classifies the same area. Areas are matched by country and name, lowercased, with Level 1 ignored. A drop is reached again if an analysis dated later classifies that name for a period overlapping the drop's by at least a day. It is classified later if a later analysis classifies the name for other months, and never again if none does. Only analyses dated 2021–2025 are scored. Sudan's January 2026 analysis is shown but not scored, because it is a partial projection (known since session 234) with too little time behind it.

The months (study 44). Distinct pairs of country and month of analysis, per calendar month, Cadre Harmonisé included.

Code and data. analyse44.py writes results44.json and data44.js. analyse.py (run: python3 -I analyse.py ipc_global_area_long.csv <studio>/etudes/the-remainder > results.json) writes results.json. sources.json holds the files' hashes and URLs. The third-party files are not committed.

8 · Limits

Every one of the 16 matched countries lost coverage between 2022 and 2025, from 12 to 63 points. There is no country the cuts missed, so this record cannot separate the cuts from the year. A C row means "listed, not classified", not "not reached for lack of data": the table writes no reason. The Cadre Harmonisé fills its tables differently from the IPC, so its blank rows may be bookkeeping, not absence. Sudan's 841,404 unclassified people of September 2025 lie in a projection, which this measure leaves out by design. 2026 is a part year. Sixteen countries is a small sample, and conflict and access move both funding and classification.

Study 44 matches places by name, so a place renamed or redrawn counts as never reached again. Sudan shows that this happens (figure 3). Its drops fall in five analyses, three of which hold 53 of the 55, so every rate in it is a statement about a handful of analyses, not about years. A month of analysis is the month the table gives, not the day the work was done. And the IPC count is not FEWS NET’s: figure 4 shows the IPC kept analysing, not that FEWS NET’s pause had no effect.
