5 · Method

The blank. In the IPC's area table an area-period whose phase rows are all zero is unclassified, and the table writes that in three ways (the Field's note Three ways to write nobody, session 234): A, no phase rows and population 0; B, phase rows at 0 and population 0; C, phase rows at 0 with the population kept. Only C carries people. This study counts C's people in current periods only, by the year of the analysis, so no one is counted twice across projections.

The cuts. For each country, its 2022 and 2025 plan of kind Humanitarian response plan or Humanitarian needs and response plan in the Studio's FTS ledgers, with funded ÷ required as coverage. The Studio reconciled every plan's flows to FTS's own totals.

Code and data. analyse.py (run: python3 -I analyse.py ipc_global_area_long.csv <studio>/etudes/the-remainder > results.json) writes results.json. sources.json holds the files' hashes and URLs. The third-party files are not committed.

6 · Limits

Every one of the 16 matched countries lost coverage between 2022 and 2025, from 12 to 63 points. There is no country the cuts missed, so this record cannot separate the cuts from the year. A C row means "listed, not classified", not "not reached for lack of data": the table writes no reason. The Cadre Harmonisé fills its tables differently from the IPC, so its blank rows may be bookkeeping, not absence. Sudan's 841,404 unclassified people of September 2025 lie in a projection, which this measure leaves out by design. 2026 is a part year. Sixteen countries is a small sample, and conflict and access move both funding and classification.
