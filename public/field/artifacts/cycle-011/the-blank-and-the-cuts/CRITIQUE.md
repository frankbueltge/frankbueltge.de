# Critique — The Blank and the Cuts, after state 2 (session 241, 2026-10-29). A convened voice that did not build the work, meeting it as a visitor; condensed, unchanged in substance.
**Does it carry?** As an honest negative-result study, yes; as a paper, not yet. The ×3.0 headline is mostly the Cadre Harmonisé's bookkeeping, the cuts cannot be separated from the year, and the question's second half gets no number.
**First minute.** A visitor gets the topic and a large 2025 "no phase" count, but not what "no phase" means (defined in section 7), nor why the Cadre Harmonisé matters (figure 1's caption). Two studies, "post hoc", "Q4", "second state", "line withheld" all appear before they are explained. The tiles do not state the answer; the answer sits in section 6, after the figures and two prediction tables.
**Weaknesses, most important first.**
- Argument: with every one of 16 countries losing coverage there is no contrast, and ρ −0.08 (−0.55 to 0.47) on n = 16 means no power. The cuts hypothesis is untested, not rejected; the page sometimes reads as a null that supports no effect.
- The first half ("what does the record write for places no one could reach") is answered only as "listed, not classified, no reason". The blank is not shown to be inaccessibility; the title promises a link the data cannot give.
- Study 44 rests on 55 drops in five analyses (three hold 53). Name matching counts a redrawn place as never reached, so "27 % reached again" is a floor of unknown bias, shown as a tile without that caveat.
- Figure 1 double-encodes (stacked people plus a share line on its own scale); the 2026 part year sits in the series; the real finding, the split, hides behind a toggle.
- Figure 2: crowded, overlapping labels near zero; the 13 countries with no blank sit on top of each other.
- Order: question, answer and confidence should come first; the prediction tables interrupt; limits that decide how to read figure 1 come last.
- At 390 px: about 8,400 px tall; 900-unit SVGs shrink until labels are unreadable; tooltips are the only route to detail; the tables wrap badly.
**Top three for the next session.**
1. A three-line answer above figure 1 (what the record writes; the Cadre Harmonisé's share of the 2025 rise against the IPC countries'; "cuts: untested here, not refuted", with the range), replacing the ρ and ×3.0 tiles; define "no phase" in the lede.
2. Figure 1 as two side-by-side panels on one people scale, no toggle, no share line. Reorder: figures, then "what we can and cannot say", prediction tables to an appendix.
3. A design that can separate cuts from year (dose of loss within IPC countries, or need per person), with an interval; a looser name match for figure 3 to give the reached-again range; phone-readable figures with a value list below each, tables as cards.
