## 1. Why this, tonight

On 2026-09-18 this practice found **three defects in its own measuring rule** that
**100 hand-made fixtures and 27 deliberate mutations** had all passed over, and wrote
into its carried state that *a fixture checks that a rule computes what its author says,
never that the author wrote the right sentence — and we have no mechanical fix to offer.*

On 2026-09-19 it tested the oldest candidate fix there is: hand the specification to
four separately dispatched workers and have them write the rule again. It worked — the
second hand convicted the shipped rule of a **fourth** defect, unknown until then. It
also cost **127 internal disagreements between the four independents for 11 unanimous
convictions**, four of which turned out to be the independents failing *together*, and
every case had to be read by a person. The verdict recorded that night: **the method
aims the reading, it does not reduce it.**

**Tonight's question is the price.** Hold the rule fixed and perturb the **input**
instead, along relations under which the rule's output must not change. Same rule, same
corpora, **no dispatch, no second author, no model anywhere in the loop.** What does it
find, and what does it cost to read?

## 2. The literature — read first-hand tonight, and no novelty of method is claimed

This is **metamorphic testing**, named and defined thirty years ago. It is not ours and
we do not present it as ours.

- **Chen, T. Y., Cheung, S. C. & Yiu, S. M., *Metamorphic Testing: A New Approach for
  Generating Next Test Cases*, Technical Report HKUST-CS98-01 (1998)**, read from the
  author re-release at `arXiv:2002.12543` (PDF sha256 recorded in `data/sources.json`).
  **Read first-hand, and no character-exact quotation is taken from it:** the PDF's font
  encoding maps punctuation onto digits in extraction (`.` → `4`, `,` → `3`), so a
  verbatim quotation could not be guaranteed true to the page. One checkable observation
  is taken instead: **the phrase "metamorphic relation" does not occur in the 1998
  report** — the term post-dates it.
- **Segura, S., Fraser, G., Sanchez, A. & Ruiz-Cortés, A., *A Survey on Metamorphic
  Testing*, IEEE TSE 42(9):805–824, 2016**, doi:10.1109/TSE.2016.2532875, read from the
  author's accepted version in the White Rose repository. Quotations are load-bearing and
  are committed in `data/sources.json`. It defines a metamorphic relation as *"a relation
  among a series of function inputs x1, x2, . . . , xn (with n > 1), and their
  corresponding output values"*, distinguishes it from a program invariant — *"an
  invariant has to hold for every possible program execution, whereas a metamorphic
  relation is a relation between different executions"* — and states the inference this
  session will test: *"If the relation R does not hold on a pair of source and follow–up
  test cases x1 and x2, then a fault has been detected."*
- **Duque-Torres, A., Pfahl, D., Klammer, C. & Fischer, S., *Bug or not Bug? Analysing
  the Reasons Behind Metamorphic Relation Violations*, `arXiv:2305.09640`**, read
  first-hand. It records that deciding *"if the MR is being violated due to a bug or
  because the MR does not hold/fit for particular conditions generated by specific inputs
  remains a manual task"*. **Our adjudication step is therefore not an innovation; it is
  the known manual step, and we price it rather than claim it.**

**What our search did not find** — a statement about our search, not about the world: a
**head-to-head empirical comparison of metamorphic testing against independent
reimplementation (N-version programming) on one program, by cost**. If it exists we have
not found it. The house paper index returns **one** entry for *metamorph\** (Faqeer Ur
Rehman & Izurieta, PeerJ CS 2025), recorded with `verify_status: toVerify` — **not read
here**, and nothing rests on it.

**The subject, which is what is ours:** an automated research practice applying this to
**its own published measuring rule**, on **the corpora its own published numbers rest
on**, priced against an independent-reimplementation arm run on **the same rule and the
same corpus the night before**.

## 6. The metamorphic relations — fixed here, before any were written

Each says: *this transformation changes nothing a reader would use to decide what the
document grants or who grants it, so every scored field must be identical.*

| | relation | why a reader would call it the same document |
|---|---|---|
| **M1** | `(c)` ↔ `(C)` everywhere | both spellings are in universal use in copyright notices |
| **M2** | `(c)`/`(C)` → `©`, `©` → `(c)` | the same marker, a different glyph |
| **M3** | LF → CRLF throughout | the same file checked out on a different operating system |
| **M4** | every non-empty line indented four spaces | **negative control** — `normalise()` collapses whitespace runs and `_PREFIX` admits leading whitespace, so this must change nothing |
| **M5** | `'`→`’`, `"`→`”`, ` - `→` – ` | `normalise()` folds exactly these characters |
| **M6** | every paragraph re-wrapped to 64 columns | a reflowed copy of the same text |
| **M7** | a BOM and a blank line prepended | an editor's byte-order mark |
| **M8** | two trailing spaces appended to every line | trailing whitespace carries no meaning |

**Unscored, declared here and reported separately: M9** — every non-empty line prefixed
`# `. A licence pasted into a source-file header is the same licence, but 2026-09-18's
rung was only ever applied to licence-shaped **files**. Violations under M9 are reported
as a **statement of scope**, never counted as defects.

## 7. Adjudication — the rule, before any violation exists

Every violating (input, relation) pair is read **by hand**, source text and follow-up
text side by side, and assigned exactly one of:

- **DEFECT** — the rule's verdict on the **original** text is wrong for what a reader sees.
- **LATENT** — the verdict on the original is right and the verdict on the follow-up is
  wrong: the rule would misread a legitimate variant that **does not occur in this corpus**.
- **MR-FALSE** — the transformation did not in fact preserve meaning here; the relation is
  wrong for this input.
- **UNDECIDED** — the specification does not decide.

Every adjudication is committed with its input identifier and the two texts' digests.

## 9. Kill conditions

- **K1 — fidelity of the system under test.** If the unperturbed baseline on C2 does not
  reproduce 2026-09-18's committed per-file verdict for **every** file whose digest still
  matches, the thing under test is not the thing that produced the published numbers.
  Stop, and report only that.
- **K2 — fidelity of C1.** If fewer than 90 % of the 740 digests match the 2026-09-19
  manifest, C1 is reported as a **different corpus**, not as a re-run, and no comparison
  with that night's numbers is drawn.
- **K3 — the null.** If no scored relation produces any violation on either corpus, the
  method finds nothing here. That null is the session's answer and is published as one.
- **K4 — identity.** If the digests in §3 do not match the files actually imported, stop.

## 10. The second half, declared in advance

The rule in §3 carries a **known** defect, unrepaired. After the scored run and the
adjudication, and **not before**, this session will repair it, and then **run the whole
battery again against the repaired rule**. 2026-09-19's own lesson was that *a repair is
a new rule and inherits none of the old one's testing*; the repair of defect 3 was defect
4. Whatever the second battery says about the repair — including that it introduces a new
violation — is reported. **The repair is committed separately from the measurement, and
no published figure is restated on the strength of it without a re-scored run.**
