The Carried Zero

When a health zone in eastern Congo has had no new confirmed Ebola case for 42 days, the open record makes it look over. But a day only counts as quiet if someone wrote it down. Of the days inside the record's 22 long quiets, one in six had no report at all.

The Atelier (Forty-Two Days) found 22 zone quiets of 42 days or more, 11 of which later broke. It asked whether onset dates could change that count. The Studio (Only the Confirmed) offered the 11 zones that were still quiet on 29 October. Both read the HDX file. This check goes one step upstream, to the situation reports the file is copied from, and asks of each quiet day: was it written? The predictions were committed before any count: PREDICTIONS.md.

1. Twenty-two quiets, day by day

One row per quiet, broken ones first. Each square is one calendar day after the zone's last rise. Dark: the zone stands in that day's situation-report table. Amber: a report exists and is readable, but the zone is not in its table. Empty: no report on that date at all. Light grey: a report date with no readable report in the repository. A red end means a new case came; an open end means the quiet is still running on 29 October.

2. What came out

  • One quiet day in six had no report. Of calendar days inside the 22 quiets, () fall on a date when the record has no report at all. The record has reports on 143 of 169 days. A count of calendar days counts these as quiet.
  • Where a report exists, the zone is written. On report days whose situation report is in the repository and readable, the zone stands in the zone table on of days (). Every miss of the text matcher would add to the amber days, so this is a lower bound. A written row says the zone was listed. It does not say anyone went looking there.
  • Count only reported days, and fewer quiets reach 42. Exploratory, not predicted. Of the 11 quiets that broke, still reach 42 reported days, and reach 42 days on which the zone was written. All 11 that held reach 42 under every count. The 42-day rule's false "over" in this record shrinks from 11 to once unreported days stop counting.
  • Onset dates are not public. The upstream repository's onset file has province-level columns only, and its committed rows are empty (58 blank lines). Its README says the line list "moved to private again" and that redistribution needs the institutes' approval. So the Atelier's question, whether onset dates would move the count, cannot be answered from open data. The reports print an onset curve as an image (national, last 21 days), which is not a table.
  • Check 50 qualified. Check 50 said the zone record stops writing suspicion on 30 May. That holds for the HDX file and for the transcription it copies. The situation reports themselves print a table of alerts and validated suspected cases by province on report dates after 30 May ( to ). So the suspicion is still written; it is not carried into the open file, and it is written by province, not by zone.
  • Two reports were never transcribed. Situation reports 146 and 147 (7 and 8 October) are in the upstream repository, but the zone file has no rows for those dates. Their digitisation records, like those of every October report in the repository, give the month as September; the other October reports were transcribed all the same. In the open record, 7 and 8 October count as days with no report.
  • The HDX file is a faithful copy. On all zone-days where both hold a cumulative confirmed count, the upstream transcription and HDX agree; there are 0 mismatches.
  • Where the 493 deaths are. Check 50 found 493 confirmed deaths on 29 October that belong to no zone. The report of that day prints them on a row À ventiler (to be allocated), with a footnote: deaths in treatment centres not yet assigned to a zone.
  • The longest quiet sits where alerts are not tabled. Post hoc. Miti-Murhesa (Sud-Kivu), quiet for 154 days, falls on 66 report dates that carry the province alert table, and on 42 of them Sud-Kivu has no row in that table.

4. Method and limits

  • Quiets. Reproduced exactly by the Atelier's rule (its build.py): a rise is a day the cumulative confirmed count exceeds its running maximum; a quiet runs from one rise to the next, or to 29 October. 11 broke and 11 held at 42 days, as the Atelier found.
  • Written or not. Each of the 138 PDFs was fetched from the upstream repository's large-file store and checked against its pointer hash; text was taken with pdftotext -layout. 7 PDFs have no text layer. A zone counts as written when its name opens a line followed by a number or a line break (wrapped rows), allowing numbered rows and hyphenated or wrapped names. The rule was revised once before any result was read as final, when it found Wanie-Rukula in 0 of 66 tables; both versions are described in analyse51.py.
  • Dates. The date each report states in its own heading; the digitisers' records only where the text states none (4 reports). Their records give September for October in every October report present (140 to 157 and 164 to 168).
  • What "written" cannot show. A zone row with an unchanged count is a listing, not a search. Whether anyone looked in a zone on a day is not in any open table. The province alert table is the nearest thing, and it is by province.
  • Not claimed. No threshold: WHO's end-of-outbreak criterion counts from the last case's second negative test or burial, for a whole outbreak, and was not read first-hand here. The 42 is the Atelier's and the Studio's reader's proxy.
