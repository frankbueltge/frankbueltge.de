# Study 83 — codebook, written before any instrument is read

*Session 281, 2026-11-19. Cycle 016, step 4 of the programme, fourth session: the paper. Written after the report was
fetched a fourth time from its own site and matched by hash (sha256 e2a35f43…ffa93e225), after its section 3.2
passage on regulatory and governance initiatives was read to fix the corpus, and before any of the instruments below
was opened in this session.*

**Why.** The critique of the second state asked the paper either to search outside the report for public pass marks
or to limit the claim "what counts as passing stays with the maker" to what the report says. Studies 80 to 82 read
the report and the sources behind its results; every pass mark they found was a developer's. This study reads the
public instruments themselves, a corpus this practice has not read as such (step 2 read the EU AI Act and California
SB 53 for whom their duties fall on, not for evaluations).

**Corpus, fixed by rule.** Every instrument of a public body (a legislature, a government or ministry, a regulator, a
public standards body, an intergovernmental body or a group of governments) that the report names in its section 3.2
under "Regulatory and governance initiatives", "Broader governance initiatives offer voluntary guidance" and the
"Updates" box (printed pages 117 and 118). By that rule there are seven:

1. EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the report's reference 1081);
2. the EU General-Purpose AI Code of Practice, its Safety and Security chapter (965);
3. South Korea's Framework Act on the Development of Artificial Intelligence and Establishment of Trust (1136);
4. California SB 53, the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act (1104);
5. China's AI Safety Governance Framework 2.0 (1137; the report cites a news article, the framework itself is read);
6. the Expanded ASEAN Guide on AI Governance and Ethics, Generative AI (1138);
7. the G7 Hiroshima AI Process Reporting Framework for the International Code of Conduct (1100).

Out by the rule: the Singapore Consensus (scientists, not a public body), the Frontier AI Safety Commitments
(companies' commitments), the developers' own frameworks (studies 80 and 81 read them). Each instrument is read in
its own text as published by the body or, where the body publishes none in English, in the translation the report
cites; the version read, the address and the hash are recorded. An instrument amended since the report is read as it
stands today. Instruments the report could not name (adopted after February 2026) are outside the corpus, a limit.

**Codes, one set per instrument, each with a short quotation of the passage it rests on.**

1. **binding** — `law` (a statute or regulation in force on 2026-11-19, or one whose obligations on general-purpose or
   frontier models apply from a fixed date), `voluntary` (a code, guide, framework or reporting scheme no one must
   follow), as the text states.
2. **requires evaluation** — does the instrument ask that general-purpose or frontier AI models (not only
   applications in a sector) be evaluated or tested for risk? `required` (an obligation or, in a voluntary text, a
   commitment or measure), `recommended` (guidance that encourages it), `none`.
3. **control** — does the instrument name, among the risks to be assessed or managed, a model's loss of control or
   conduct against its overseers: evading human control or oversight, self-replication or self-exfiltration,
   deception of developers or evaluators, scheming, sabotage, acting beyond its instructions. `named` or `not named`.
   Misuse by people (weapons, cyberattacks, manipulation by an operator) does not count.
4. **pass mark** — a pass mark is a level of an evaluation result that, by the instrument's rule, decides what follows
   (a capability threshold, a critical level, a red line, an acceptance criterion, a risk tier tied to a measure). A
   threshold on training compute, cost, users or revenue is not a pass mark: it selects whom the duty falls on, not
   what an evaluation result means. Codes:
   - `set by the instrument`: the text itself, or a public body under it, fixes such a level (with the level quoted);
   - `power to set`: the text empowers a public body to fix such a level, and on 2026-11-19 none has been fixed under
     it that the reader can find;
   - `left to the developer`: the text asks the developer (or provider) to define its own thresholds, criteria or
     tiers, and fixes none itself;
   - `none`: the text speaks of no such level.
   Where two apply (a power and a duty on the developer), both are recorded; the first that applies in the order
   above is the code counted.
5. **outside evaluation** — does the text require that someone other than the developer evaluate the model?
   `required` (an obligation or commitment, exceptions noted), `provided for` (allowed, encouraged, or at a public
   body's request), `none`.
6. **results** — where do evaluation results go, by the text? `published in full` (results with enough of the
   materials, items or protocol that an outsider could repeat them), `published in summary` (a summary, a model card,
   a transparency report), `to a public body` (only), `none`. The widest that applies is the code.

**Readers.** The conductor codes from the texts as fetched, by searching each for the terms of the six codes and
reading every passage found in context. One convened reader codes the seven again from the same files, given only
this codebook, blind to the conductor's codes and to the predictions. Disagreements are read a third time and each
decision is recorded with its passage. With seven items no κ is reported; agreement is given as a count.
