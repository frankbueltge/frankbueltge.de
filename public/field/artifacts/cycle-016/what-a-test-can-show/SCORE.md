# Score — What a Test Can Show (cycle 016, step 4 of the programme, the Field)
**What it is.** The step's evidence, growing into the cycle's paper: what today's safety evaluations of frontier
models can establish, read first in the world's main synthesis on the question, then behind it, result by result:
who ran each test, who set its pass mark, and whether anyone outside the maker can check it.
**What a reader meets.** The answer in six lines, with its corrections dated. Then the check: how far a blind second
reading agrees, code by code, and a funnel from the tests behind the results to those an outsider could repeat and
whose source holds the result the report cites. Then the report's body as a strip of 2,323 sentences, the 201 on
evaluation standing out, and the same 201 as squares by section; the reader colours them by what each rests on
(measured, open, inferred), what its test is for, or whose evidence it cites, picks out one kind, and opens any
square to read the sentence. Then the industry lean of the evidence, the pass marks and their setters; behind the 41
measured sentences, their 58 sources as tiles in bands by whether anyone outside the maker could repeat the test,
coloured by who ran it or marked with whether the source holds the result, each opening to the source in its own words; step 3's nine losses of control beside the
Atelier's coding; the predictions fixed before each count, method and limits. Since the fourth state, beside its paper: every share against all
201 sentences on evaluation, the tests on control marked with how each repeatable one brought its behaviour about, the
test sources grouped by organisation, and the seven public instruments the report names, each cell opening to its passage.
**Material and form.** *International AI Safety Report 2026* (Open Government Licence v3.0), fetched whole and
hashed, with its own asterisk on industry-affiliated references; the 58 sources behind its measured sentences (papers,
system cards, benchmark pages, reports), each read on its own page and linked as read; step 3's nine losses of control on
the makers' own pages; the Atelier's coding of the same cases. A self-made page drawn by script with no library; the seven public
instruments the report names, each read in its own text and hashed (study 83); every number from `analyse80.py`,
`analyse81.py`, `analyse82.py` and `analyse83.py`.
