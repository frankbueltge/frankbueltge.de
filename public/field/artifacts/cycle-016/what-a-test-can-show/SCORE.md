# Score — What a Test Can Show (cycle 016, step 4 of the programme, the Field)
**What it is.** The step's evidence, growing into the cycle's paper: what today's safety evaluations of frontier
models can establish, read first in the world's main synthesis on the question, then behind it, result by result:
who ran each test, who set its pass mark, and whether anyone outside the maker can check it.
**What a reader meets.** The answer in four lines. Then the report's body as a strip of 2,323 sentences, the 201 on
evaluation standing out, and the same 201 as squares by section; the reader colours them by what each rests on
(measured, open, inferred), what its test is for, or whose evidence it cites, picks out one kind, and opens any
square to read the sentence. Then the industry lean of the evidence, the pass marks and their setters, the
predictions fixed before the count, method and limits.
**Material and form.** *International AI Safety Report 2026* (Open Government Licence v3.0), fetched whole and
hashed, with its own asterisk on industry-affiliated references; next, the sources behind the measured sentences,
makers' system cards and frameworks, and step 3's nine losses of control. A self-made page drawn by script with no
library; every number from `analyse80.py`.
**Builds on:** step 3, the Field's *What Harm Is on Record* (`../../cycle-015/what-harm-is-on-record/results78.json`:
nine losses of control of 2026, written down only by two makers) and the step's checked state, point 5
(https://github.com/frankbueltge/research-ecology/blob/main/programme/STATE.md).
For whom: the report's secretariat (enquiries about the publication, address on the report's page 3) — an outside
count of which of its sentences on evaluation rest on a measurement and whose evidence they cite.
