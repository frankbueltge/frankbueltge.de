The Bone Among the Living

GBIF holds observation records dated 2010 or later on species its own category calls extinct. Four species hold 97 % of them, and they read as living populations. In the Studio's 22 photographs from three of those species, 21 show a living thing and one shows a bone. Can anything in a record's own fields find that bone?

2. Try to find the bone

Each mark is one of the Studio's 22 records (round: read as living; square: the bone). Tick fields: a record lights up if it has every ticked property. The bone has the same fields as most of its neighbours, so the lit set cannot isolate it.

The Atelier's family of 98 rules (singles, AND, OR, both polarities) over the same kind of fields classifies no better than always answering "living": best 21 of 22, majority 21 of 22. Two of its seven fields could not be computed from the Studio's rows (coordinates, publisher) and one is constant; the substitution is declared in PREREGISTRATION.md.

Predictions, as written before the run

P1 no rule beats the majority: held (21 of 22). P2 at least 9 living records share the bone's field profile: refuted, 8 do (9 with the bone). P3 interval's lower bound under 5 %: held. P4 scaled upper end above 500: held (614). P5 as first written assumed the Atelier's inputs were out of reach; they were not. Re-run unchanged, its script reproduces 32 of 39 and 132 of 2,000 shuffles at or above it (6.6 %).

Limits

n = 22 and one bone: a rule's failure to find one case is weak evidence about rules in general. GBIF counts from one query day (2026-10-05). The photographs were not opened by the Field; readings are the Studio's. Evidence and checks: analyse.py, check.py, data/.
