The clock and the record

A tornado warning cannot change the funnel. Can it change the record of the funnel? Every warning's end is written when it is issued, and the storm does not know that clock. If warnings make people look harder, weak tornadoes should stop being recorded when the clock runs out. They don't, as far as 238 tornadoes can show.

● tornadoes that began under a warning · ● under none · dot size: count · lines: 95 % intervals · shaded: the window · dashed: the clock compared.

What was measured

All 19,631 US tornadoes of 2008–2023 (study 13) against every tornado-warning polygon as first issued. For each tornado that began inside a polygon within W minutes of that polygon's initial expiry, I asked whether it was recorded as weak (EF0 or unrated). Before: it began in the last W minutes and a warning covered it. After: it began in the first W minutes after the expiry and no warning covered it. If the warning helps write its own record, the weak share falls after the clock.

What came out

  • No step at the expiry. 53.7 % weak before, 53.8 % after (238 tornadoes). The difference is +0.1 points, and the interval (−6.4 to +6.5) excludes the drop of 5 points or more I predicted. P2 failed.
  • The placebo is not flat. Moving the clock 20 minutes earlier, both sides under a warning, the weak share still rises 3.8 points (1.7 to 6.0). Over a warning's life, later tornadoes are weaker: the storm ages. P3 failed, and it tells me the design has a slope to beat.
  • The sign wobbles with the window: −3.8 at 10 minutes, +0.1 at 20, +3.3 at 30, every interval spanning zero. P4 failed. P1 held (238 ≥ 100). One of four predictions held.
  • Read against the slope (not registered): if the weak share kept rising at the placebo's rate, about 57.5 % would be expected after the clock. 53.8 % is about 4 points below that, inside the uncertainty. A small search effect is not excluded. A large one is.
  • Descriptive: of tornadoes that began inside a polygon within 20 minutes after its expiry, 702 were still under a warning (extended or re-warned) and 238 under none. Forecasters re-warned three in four.

What it means for the question

The Atelier argued that a warning able to prevent its danger leaves a record that bounds only p(1−e), and that the tornado warning is the clean case, e = 0. This study tests the step the argument leaves open: whether the record bends toward the warning even when the storm cannot. At the one place where the warning stops and the storm goes on, the record of weak tornadoes does not detectably change. For the share of weak tornadoes, e for the record is within about ±6 points of zero. That supports studies 13, 14 and 16 scoring warnings against the reports. It does not cover the count of tornadoes: 4,498 began in the 20 minutes before the clock and 940 in the 20 after (238 of them under no warning), and that fall cannot be split between a storm that left and eyes that stopped looking.

Limits

  • The expiry is chosen near the storm's expected exit, so the after-side storms are selected as weakening or unexpected. That biases toward a higher weak share after, against my hypothesis; the placebo slope shows such drift exists.
  • Start minutes in the tornado record are often reconstructed by survey and rounded. A start dated into the warning would bend the record the same way, and the design cannot tell it apart from search.
  • 238 tornadoes are on the after side. The study can see a drop of about 6 points, not 2.
  • Orange dots left of the clock are tornadoes inside the first polygon after it was trimmed or cancelled there. They read weaker too, likely for the same reason as the after side: the forecaster judged the storm weaker. Not registered, not tested.
  • The outcome is the rating, not whether a tornado is recorded at all. The count of tornadoes recorded is the stronger test, and this design cannot identify it.
