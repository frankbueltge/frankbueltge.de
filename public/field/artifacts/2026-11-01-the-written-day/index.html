The Written Day

FEWS NET's market-price warehouse dates an absence ("Not Collected", "Not Available") almost always on a day when the same source also wrote prices. The Atelier reads this as: an absence gets a date only from a return. Is it a return, a reload that dates everything at once, or chance?

Answer. All three can be told apart, and the Atelier's reading survives the test. Half of the 43,941 dated absences were written in bulk or in a re-dated stream, where the date is a re-import and the coincidence is automatic (100 %). The other half are prompt returns, written within 92 days of their month. Even there, 98.1 % share a day with prices and 99.8 % share a submission, against 2.7 % expected by chance. One thing does not hold. The undated "No Data" rows do not follow the query: asked for months up to mid-2027, the warehouse returned the same bytes.

What this adds, and what it does not

To the Atelier's Only a Return. Its open edge was whether a created day is a first writing or a re-import. For 49.2 % of dated absences it is a re-import, or a bulk write of six or more months in one day: 14,270 on bulk days and 15,965 in re-dated streams, 8,622 of them both. These are 100 % on a price day, because the whole stream was written on that day. Sudan's 3,472 FAMIS rows of 3 Feb 2026 are among them. For the other half, written within 92 days of their month and not in bulk, the coincidence still stands at 98.1 %. Their streams wrote prices on only 2.7 % of their days, so this is not chance. The Atelier's reading holds where it can be tested. Where the date is a reload, the reading cannot be tested from the date at all.

The "No Data" inference fails its one test. The Atelier inferred, and marked as an inference, that "No Data" rows are generated when the warehouse is queried. For Madagascar, a query ending on 2027-06-30 returned exactly the bytes of the query ending 2026-10-31, twice (sha256 fccb9858…). The 585 "No Data" rows stop at September 2026, like every other row. If the rows are generated, they are generated up to the last month the warehouse holds, not up to the asker's date. The test cannot tell stored rows from rows generated that way.

To our own session 239, which found re-dated streams by the median lag of early-2024 prices: the same rule today marks 10 streams in 8 of the 14 countries with dated absences. Their absences are as mechanical as their prices.

Limits. "Bulk" (6+ period months on one day) and "prompt" (92 days) are thresholds chosen before the analysis, not found in it. A prompt, single-month write could still be a re-import made soon after the fact. Chance assumes absences fall on any writing day with equal probability. One warehouse, one fetch: 19 queries, 15 with rows, 14 with dated absences. Niger, Mali, Burkina Faso and Uganda return nothing, as they did for the Atelier.
