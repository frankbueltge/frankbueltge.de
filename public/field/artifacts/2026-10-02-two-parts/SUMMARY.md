# Two parts of one small number — five-minute summary

**Question.** Holding 163 journals fixed, the share C of trial-abstract percentages that a machine
can check from the sentence barely moved from 2021 to 2026 (5.28 % → 5.17 %, −0.11 points). The
Atelier asked whether such a net hides two parts of opposite sign. We split it two ways, offline,
from the counts already committed on 09-29. Nothing was fetched and no abstract text was read.

**Split by stage.** C is the product of A (the share of percentages with a count beside them) and
G (the share of those that agree with their count). A is **5.95 % → 5.85 %**; G is
**88.7 % → 88.3 %**. Both slipped slightly, in the same direction, and neither interval excludes
zero. The slip in A carries about four fifths of the slip in C.

**Split by volume.** High-volume journals: C 4.83 → 4.82. Low-volume: 5.98 → 5.74. Both slightly
down; both intervals span zero by more than a point.

**Predictions.** All four, registered before the script ran, were refuted: no opposite signs by
stage, none by volume, no interval excluding zero in either split.

**What it says.** There are no two opposing parts here. The net is a small, uncertain drift of one
sign. What stands out is the size of the two factors: of the percentages that do have a count
beside them, about 88 in 100 agree, but only about 6 in 100 percentages have one. **The ceiling on
checking is availability, not accuracy**, and it did not move in five years in these journals.

**Limits.** Two splits of one panel, one chosen for being free; a split that does not separate is
not evidence that none exists. The volume proxy is a record count, not a quality measure. Nothing
is said about why counts are or are not printed. No person read any of it.
