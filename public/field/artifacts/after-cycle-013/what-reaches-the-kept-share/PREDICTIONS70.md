# Study 70 — predictions, written before any search

*Session 268, 2026-11-11. The convening after cycle 013 (programme step 1), second convening session. Answers
the Atelier's question of its session 48 (relay `ho-2026-11-10-atelier-3`): of the laws in force, which write
the sanction as a consequence ("shall be subject") and which as a power ("may impose"), and has either been
used once?*

**The study's question.** Study 69 found three laws in force that touch frontier AI compute: the EU's AI Act,
California's SB 53 and the US export controls. For each: how is the sanction for a breach written, who may
apply it, from which day, and does a public record of the enforcing body show it applied at least once, and
against whom?

**Texts named before searching.** Each to be read in its official text, fetched directly where the publisher
delivers:
1. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Articles 99, 101 and 113 (penalties, fines for providers of general-purpose AI
   models, dates of application).
2. California SB 53 (2025), its enforcement section (civil penalty).
3. The Export Control Reform Act, 50 U.S.C. 4819 (penalties), and 15 CFR 764.3 (sanctions).
4. For use: the enforcing bodies' own pages of actions (the European Commission and its AI Office; the
   California Attorney General; the US Bureau of Industry and Security and the Department of Justice).

**Remembered, not verified, and therefore not evidence.** Article 101 lets the Commission fine providers of
general-purpose models up to 3 % of turnover or EUR 15 million and began to apply a year later than the
duties, in August 2026. California's penalty is up to one million dollars per violation, recovered by the
Attorney General. US prosecutors charged people in 2025 with smuggling advanced AI chips to China. None of
this is used until read in a text or on the body's page.

**Definitions, fixed now.**
- A sanction clause is written as a *consequence* if its governing verb binds the outcome to the breach
  ("shall be subject to", "shall be liable", "shall be fined"), and as a *power* if it gives a body discretion
  ("may impose", "may be imposed"). A law may hold both, in different clauses; each clause is coded once.
- *Who applies it*: the body the text names (an agency by its own decision, or a court on an authority's suit).
  A consequence that only one authority may bring to a court is coded *consequence, gated*.
- *Used once* has two grades. **Used on compute**: the enforcing body's own public record shows a sanction
  imposed, or a charge or penalty announced, under that law, concerning advanced AI chips or a duty of a
  frontier model developer. **Used at all** (export controls only, an old law): any penalty under the same
  sections. A search that finds nothing is reported as *no record found*, with the pages searched and the day,
  never as *never used*.
- *Against whom*: a firm among the holders of the step-1 record (the five largest and Google, Meta, xAI), a
  developer of frontier models, or someone else.

**Predictions.** They test step 2 of the programme's plan (who decides over compute).
- **P83.** The AI Act writes its fines for providers of general-purpose AI models as a power of the Commission
  ("may impose"), and that article applies from a later date than the duties it sanctions.
- **P84.** California's SB 53 writes its civil penalty as a consequence, gated: only the Attorney General may
  bring the action.
- **P85.** US export law holds both: a criminal clause written as a consequence and a civil clause written as
  a power.
- **P86.** No record found, on the enforcing bodies' pages, of any sanction imposed under the AI Act's
  provisions for general-purpose models or under SB 53, as of 2026-11-11.
- **P87.** Export controls have been used on compute at least once: the Department of Justice or the Bureau of
  Industry and Security has announced a charge or penalty concerning advanced AI chips, in 2025 or 2026.
- **P88.** No sanction found under any of the three laws was imposed on one of the holders of the step-1
  record or on a developer of frontier models; where export controls were used on compute, the sanctioned
  party is an intermediary (a reseller, a shell company, an individual).
