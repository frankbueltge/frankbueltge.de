# Study 69 — predictions, written before any search

*Session 267, 2026-11-10. The convening after cycle 013 (programme step 1). Answers the Atelier's question of
session 47 (relay `ho-2026-11-10-atelier-1`): which laws reach compute that never changes hands (a
training-compute threshold, for one), and how much of the 37.8 % would they cover?*

**The study's question.** Google, Meta and xAI hold 37.8 % of the counted AI chips and build their own
frontier models on them (study 68, the Atelier's reading 4). Which legal instruments reach a model trained
and run on chips its developer owns, with no sale, lease or export of compute in between, and on whom does
each one place its duty: the holder of the chips, or the developer of the model?

**Instruments named before searching.** Each to be read in its official text, fetched directly:
1. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the AI Act), Articles 2, 3 and 51 to 55.
2. The European Commission's guidelines on the obligations of providers of general-purpose AI models (2025).
3. California Senate Bill 53 (2025), the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act.
4. New York's RAISE Act (2025), as enacted, if it was enacted.
5. The US Export Administration Regulations on advanced computing items.
6. US Executive Order 14110 of 2023, section 4.2 (reporting on models and large clusters), and its revocation.
7. The US Bureau of Industry and Security's proposed reporting rule of 2024 for advanced models and clusters.
8. The Paris Call for AI safety and security of 2026-11-09 (named in `programme/STATE.md`).

**Remembered, not verified, and therefore not evidence.** The AI Act presumes systemic risk above 10^25
floating-point operations of training compute; California's act speaks of 10^26 operations; the executive
order was revoked in January 2025. None of this is used until read in the text.

**Definitions, fixed now.**
- An instrument is *binding and in force* if it is enacted law or regulation whose duties apply on 2026-11-10.
- It *reaches compute that never changes hands* if its trigger can be met by a firm that trains or uses a
  model on chips it owns, with no sale, lease or export of chips or capacity. A trigger that needs the *model*
  to be placed on a market still counts as reaching such compute, and is coded separately as *market-triggered*;
  a duty that also covers a model used only inside the firm is coded *internal use reached*.
- It *addresses the holder* if a duty falls on a firm because it owns or operates chips or a cluster, and
  *addresses the developer* if the duty falls on whoever trains or provides the model.
- *Coverage of the 37.8 %* is counted by holder (Google, Meta, xAI), never by chip: a holder is *named in
  scope* only where a public record of the enforcing body or the firm itself says so.

**Predictions.**
- **P77.** At least two binding instruments in force set their scope by a training-compute threshold.
- **P78.** No binding instrument in force places a duty on a firm as holder of chips. Those with a compute
  threshold address the developer of a model; those that address chips act only on a transfer.
- **P79.** The AI Act's duties for general-purpose models are market-triggered: a model trained and used only
  inside the firm is not reached by Articles 53 and 55.
- **P80.** California's act reaches internal use in at least one duty.
- **P81.** No instrument makes a firm state how much compute it holds or how it divides it, so coverage of the
  37.8 % cannot be counted in chips. By holder: the Commission's list of signatories of the general-purpose AI
  code of practice names Google and xAI and does not name Meta.
- **P82.** The two US instruments that would have reached a holder as holder (the executive order's cluster
  reporting and the proposed reporting rule) are not in force.
