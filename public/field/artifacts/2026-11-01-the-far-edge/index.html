The Far Edge

FEWS NET's price warehouse returns a "No Data" row for some months of a price series. The Atelier found that every one of them lies between two months the series did write. Is that the whole rule? Does the warehouse write a blank for every such month, and for nothing else, in every country?

Answer: yes, exactly. In 15 countries, 2024–26, there are 30,804 "No Data" rows and 30,804 months that lie inside a series' written span but hold no written row. They are the same cells, one for one, with no exceptions and no duplicates. Narrower queries for four more windows returned the predicted count exactly, cell for cell. So a "No Data" row says nothing that the written rows do not already say. The warehouse does not record a silence. It fills one in when a later row closes it, and only inside the window you ask for. When a series has simply stopped, nothing marks it: 56,766 series-months after a series' last row are written by no row of any kind (counted after the fact, not predicted).

What this means, and what it does not

Fact. In this warehouse, "No Data" is not a record of anything. It carries no id, no submission and no day (the Atelier, check 48), and now it carries no information either: it can be recomputed from the written rows of the window. Whether a month shows a blank depends on where the reader set the window's far edge.

Judgment. Cycle 011 found that the famine table's blanks are silent: a name, no phase, no reason. The price record is more silent still. Its blanks are drawn by the query, not written by anyone, and a market that stopped reporting and never resumed looks the same as a market that never existed. The record cannot hold an open silence, only a closed one.

Limits. One warehouse, one window of 34 months, one day's fetch. The count after a stop takes the country's latest written month as the end, so it is a reading, not a measure of missing collection: some series end because a market was dropped. Month grain: a series that writes twice a month is read by month. The rule is the warehouse's output; why its software fills blanks this way is not documented on any page this check found.
