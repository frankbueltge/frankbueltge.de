The tornadoes that came

Study 12 found that of 639 asteroid strikes the sensors kept, 6 had been seen coming; and none of the 13 warned strikes harmed anyone. This study asks the same of a danger whose warnings exist to protect people. It sets two US records side by side: every tornado the Storm Prediction Center kept from 2008 to 2023, and every tornado warning polygon the Weather Service issued. Did the tornado begin inside a warning that was already out?

Of tornadoes, began inside a warning issued before they touched down (). The asteroid record: 6 of 639.

The cell that is empty for asteroids

The Atelier read the keepers' pages of the 13 warned asteroid strikes and found no harm written on any of them: warned and harmful, 0. For tornadoes, that cell holds most of the harm. Of the tornadoes that killed someone, began inside a warning already issued, and they carry of the deaths.

What this says, and what it does not

  • Measured. About half the tornadoes since 2012 began inside a warning already out (49.7 %), against 63.1 % in 2008–2011. The fall the literature dates to 2012 has not recovered through 2023: 2017–2023 stands at 49.4 %.
  • Measured. The stronger the tornado, the likelier the warning: 51.8 % for EF0–1, 70.1 % for EF2 and stronger, 8 of 8 for EF5. The asteroid record showed the same direction (3 of 72 at 1 kt or more against 3 of 567 below) at a share a hundred times smaller.
  • Measured. 72.3 % of killer tornadoes were warned before touchdown, and they hold 85.2 % of the deaths. That is below the 75 % predicted. Warned and harmful is the common case here, not the empty one.
  • Judgment, not measured. A warning is not a prevention. The harm is concentrated where the warnings are because the strongest tornadoes are both the most warned and the most deadly. Nothing here says how many deaths warnings prevented: the record of the harm that did not happen is not in either file.
  • Not read. The warning texts. Whether they name an act to protect anyone, as the Atelier asked of the asteroid pages, is not measured here.

Method, data, sources

Tornadoes: Storm Prediction Center, 1950-2023_actual_tornadoes.csv, fetched 2026-10-14, one row per whole track (format specification, SPC, updated 2010-04-07), start minute converted from CST to UTC. Warnings: Iowa Environmental Mesonet, tornado-warning polygons with their updates, 32 half-year requests, 96,923 polygon rows of 43,819 warnings (data/warnings.tsv.gz). A tornado is warned ahead if a polygon valid at its start minute contains its start point and its warning was issued strictly before that minute. The registered rule takes each polygon as it stood (issuance and updates); the second rule takes the issuance polygon alone until its first expiry. They differ on 205 of 19,631 tornadoes. Literature: Brooks & Correia, Weather and Forecasting 33 (2018) 1501–1511, doi:10.1175/WAF-D-18-0120.1, read in full. Spot check: for the Joplin tornado of 22 May 2011 the match gives warning 31 of the Springfield office, 17 minutes ahead; the Weather Service's own service assessment says Tornado Warning #31 with 17 minutes of lead time for touchdown. Rules: PREREGISTRATION.md; code: extract.py, analyse.py, build.py; checks: check.py (16 of 16, including a second matcher written another way that agrees on 300 of 300 sampled tornadoes).
