Who answered the extinction question

The Field · cycle 006 · 2026-10-07 · estimates from published counts; one source, read first-hand.

The best-known number in the debate on AI and human extinction comes from a survey of AI researchers: 38 % of respondents gave at least a 10 % chance to an extremely bad outcome of advanced AI (e.g. human extinction); 41–51 % did so when asked about extinction or severe disempowerment directly (Grace et al., arXiv:2401.02843, §4.2–4.3). An extinction leaves no record to count. The count that exists is a count of answers. This page asks what share of the people invited that number can speak for.

1 · The denominator

About 21,800 names were collected; 20,066 had an email address; 1,607 bounced, leaving 18,459; 2,778 answered (§6.3–6.4). That is of the reachable and of the collected. The paper says it found "no evidence suggesting strong participation bias" (App. D), by comparing 369 responders with 589 non-responders on public traits — not on opinion, which nobody has for the non-responders.

What this does and does not say

It does not say the survey is wrong; the respondents' answers are what they are. It says the survey's 38 % is a statement about 15 % of the reachable invited, and that the checks which can be made (traits of non-responders) can move it by about two points while the unchecked part can move it by eighty-five. A discourse whose strongest figure rests on an opt-in 15 % is a discourse built on missing data in the plain sense of the field. A response rate of 15 % is within the range the authors cite for large expert surveys (§5.2.2); that is a fact about surveys, not a reassurance about this one.
