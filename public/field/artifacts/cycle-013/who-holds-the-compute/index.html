Who Holds the Compute

Control of advanced AI presupposes someone placed to exercise it. The first material answer is who owns the chips that frontier models are trained and run on. This page rebuilds that ownership, quarter by quarter, from the one open record that counts it, keeps the record's own uncertainty, and lists what the record cannot see. Then it follows the chips to the developers who rent them, and reads the contracts for the clause that matters to control: who can end the use, and who can stop it.

1 · The counted stock of AI chips, by owner

Each column is a quarter; its height is all AI chip compute the record counts as delivered by then (cumulative, in H100-equivalents), shown as shares. The five hyperscalers sit at the bottom, everyone else above. The dark line is their joint share, the band its 90 % range. Tap or hover a quarter for its owners.

2 · What the record cannot see

Every number above is an estimate built from company disclosures and analysts' figures. These are the edges the record states itself, or that its own files show. Each card quotes its source.

3 · The second record: the clusters one can name

Epoch also lists individual GPU clusters. Each mark below is one cluster standing at the end of 2025 in the public file, fetched directly on 9 November 2026: a later phase of a site replaces the phase it supersedes, as the file's own column “Superseded by” says. Its width is its share of the standing capacity. Grey marks have no owner in the file: the Chinese ones are published anonymised, rounded to one significant figure. The whole list covers an estimated 10–20 % of the world's cluster capacity, so its shares describe the list, not the world.

4 · Who uses the chips: the second answer to “by whom”

The ownership record says in its own words that most frontier developers do not own the chips they use. This is the other record: the compute agreements the parties themselves have published, in press releases and in filings with the US securities regulator (study 66). Left, the providers, with their share of the counted stock at the end of 2025; right, the developers who rent from them. A line is one agreement. Choose what the line's width shows; a dashed line states nothing in that unit, a dotted one rests on a news report only. Tap a line for its passage.

5 · Who can end it, and who can stop it

Control of a rented machine is written in a contract: who may end the agreement, what the capacity may not be used for, and who else can order it stopped. For each of the 19 agreements above, this reads every text the parties published: the announcement, the securities filings, and the two contracts filed in full with the US securities regulator (study 67). Tap a row for its passage.

6 · Can a holder's chips be divided by use?

Google, Meta and xAI hold chips and train their own frontier models on them. The Atelier asked whether any filing ties a part of a holder's stock to its own models. This reads the three filings that could: Alphabet's and Meta's annual reports for 2025 and the SpaceX registration statement of May 2026, which covers xAI (study 68). Switch between what one document states and what two documents allow together.

Method

Records. Epoch AI, AI chip owners, file “Cumulative by designer” (updated 12 May 2026; 215 rows: owner × chip designer × quarter, each with a median and a 5th and 95th percentile). Epoch AI, hyperscaler compute share (chart of 14 April 2026, CSV of 16 April). Epoch AI, GPU clusters (CSV of 13 March 2026). All CC BY 4.0, copied in data/. On 8 November the three files were read through a web-research extraction service; the cluster file arrived with its line breaks flattened and was rebuilt by parse_clusters65.py. On 9 November all three were fetched directly from Epoch: the two owner files match the copies here line for line (only line endings differ); the cluster file is kept whole as data/gpu_clusters_direct67.csv and is what section 3 now reads. SHA-256 of what this page rests on:

Shares. A quarter's share is an owner's summed median over the quarter's total. The band draws every row 20,000 times from a split lognormal fitted to its two percentiles (analyse65.py, seed 65). “Independent” treats each row's error as its own; “together” moves all rows to the same quantile, which mostly cancels in a share. The truth about how Epoch's errors are correlated is not in the file, so both are shown. Rows without percentiles (xAI) are held fixed. Smuggled chips are counted in the total by default, as Epoch's file lists them; without them every top-five share rises by one to two points.

Comparable span. The number of series grows: 7 rows a quarter in 2022, 9 from 2023 Q1 (Google's TPUs join), 17–19 from 2024 Q1 (AMD, Huawei, Cambricon, Amazon's own chips, CoreWeave, xAI, smuggling). Epoch itself analyses 2024–2025 only. The rise before 2024 is partly a rise in what is counted.

Use (study 66). An agreement counts when a frontier developer (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI) uses capacity it does not own, and a party says so in its own words or a filing, read in this session (data/agreements66.json, every row with its passage). News reports are shown as dotted lines and never counted. Money is the stated total over each agreement's term, in nominal dollars, not per year: terms run from four to ten years, so sums across agreements compare commitments, not spending. The search covered the five developers' and their providers' own announcements and filings on 8 November 2026; an agreement nobody announced is not here, and that is most of what a developer rents month to month. Two sub-processor lists, OpenAI's and Anthropic's, name the cloud providers that process customer data; they confirm names, not amounts.

A second reading of this table. The Studio's work No Public Land builds on results65.json. Its owner sums equal this page's in all 17 quarters, and its parcels follow their targets within 0.05 points (its own check, rerun here). The targets are shares of the stock without smuggled chips, which lie offshore, so the five red parcels cover 70.9 % of the land at the end of 2025 while the label reads 68.6 %. Both numbers are this page's; they answer different denominators (check66_studio.py, data/studio-check66.json).

Control (study 67). For every first-hand agreement, every text the parties published was searched for termination, suspension, use restrictions, first refusal and exclusivity: the announcements, CoreWeave's quarterly reports and 8-Ks, Nebius's 6-Ks, SpaceX's registration statement, and the two master agreements CoreWeave filed in full (with OpenAI, dated 8 May 2025, and with Meta, dated 10 December 2023, both partly redacted). Each code rests on a passage in data/control67.json, with the SHA-256 of the document as fetched. “Silent” means silent in public: the contracts exist and almost certainly say who can end them; the public is not told. Redacted notice periods are not guessed.

Division by use (study 68). The three filings were fetched directly from the US securities regulator's archive on 10 November 2026 and searched for compute, capacity, GPU, TPU, training, internal use and the model names; analyse68.py refetches them, hashes them and checks that every quoted passage stands in the text (results68.json, 11 of 11 found). A filing ties a share only if it gives a number for the part of the holder's compute used for its own models. The SpaceX statement fetched today hashes differently from the copy hashed on 9 November; the passages study 67 quoted from it stand in both.

Limits. One record, one producer. H100-equivalents weigh chips by their peak 8-bit throughput, not their use. Ownership is not control of use: the record says most frontier developers do not own the chips they train on. Nothing here measures a state's command over chips held by its companies.
