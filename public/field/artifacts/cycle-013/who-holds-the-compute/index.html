Who Holds the Compute

Control of advanced AI presupposes someone placed to exercise it. The first material answer is who owns the chips that frontier models are trained and run on. This page rebuilds that ownership, quarter by quarter, from the one open record that counts it, keeps the record's own uncertainty, and lists what the record cannot see.

1 · The counted stock of AI chips, by owner

Each column is a quarter; its height is all AI chip compute the record counts as delivered by then (cumulative, in H100-equivalents), shown as shares. The five hyperscalers sit at the bottom, everyone else above. The dark line is their joint share, the band its 90 % range. Tap or hover a quarter for its owners.

2 · What the record cannot see

Every number above is an estimate built from company disclosures and analysts' figures. These are the edges the record states itself, or that its own files show. Each card quotes its source.

3 · The second record: the clusters one can name

Epoch also lists individual GPU clusters. Each mark below is one cluster in the public file (459 with a stated capacity), its width its share of the listed capacity. Grey marks have no owner in the file: the Chinese ones are published anonymised, rounded to one significant figure. The whole list covers an estimated 10–20 % of the world's cluster capacity.

Method

Records. Epoch AI, AI chip owners, file “Cumulative by designer” (updated 12 May 2026; 215 rows: owner × chip designer × quarter, each with a median and a 5th and 95th percentile). Epoch AI, hyperscaler compute share (chart of 14 April 2026, CSV of 16 April). Epoch AI, GPU clusters (CSV of 13 March 2026). All CC BY 4.0, copied in data/. Direct requests from this machine were refused by its network, so all three files were read through a web-research extraction service; the cluster file arrived with its line breaks flattened and was rebuilt by parse_clusters65.py, which trusts only its first thirteen columns.

Shares. A quarter's share is an owner's summed median over the quarter's total. The band draws every row 20,000 times from a split lognormal fitted to its two percentiles (analyse65.py, seed 65). “Independent” treats each row's error as its own; “together” moves all rows to the same quantile, which mostly cancels in a share. The truth about how Epoch's errors are correlated is not in the file, so both are shown. Rows without percentiles (xAI) are held fixed. Smuggled chips are counted in the total by default, as Epoch's file lists them; without them every top-five share rises by one to two points.

Comparable span. The number of series grows: 7 rows a quarter in 2022, 9 from 2023 Q1 (Google's TPUs join), 17–19 from 2024 Q1 (AMD, Huawei, Cambricon, Amazon's own chips, CoreWeave, xAI, smuggling). Epoch itself analyses 2024–2025 only. The rise before 2024 is partly a rise in what is counted.

Limits. One record, one producer. H100-equivalents weigh chips by their peak 8-bit throughput, not their use. Ownership is not control of use: the record says most frontier developers do not own the chips they train on. Nothing here measures a state's command over chips held by its companies.
