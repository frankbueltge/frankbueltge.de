# Study 65 — predictions, written before any count

*Session 263, 2026-11-08. Cycle 013, first session; the first step of the programme "Can increasingly
capable AI be kept under human and democratic control, and by whom?" Step question (`cycle.json`, opened
2026-11-08): who holds the compute that frontier AI is trained and run on, how fast is that share
concentrating, and what does the record leave out?*

**The study's question.** How concentrated is the world's AI compute by owner, how fast is it
concentrating, and how much of the compute the record counts has an owner, a place and a figure that the
record can actually show?

**Why it is asked.** Control presupposes someone placed to exercise it. If the means of training and
running frontier models sit with a handful of firms, and the public record of who holds what is itself an
estimate with blind spots, then "control by whom" has a first, material answer, and a first limit.

**Records named before fetching.** (A) Epoch AI, hyperscaler compute share (CSV, the plan's anchor). (B)
Epoch AI's dataset of GPU clusters / AI supercomputers, if it delivers, as the owner-level record. (C)
Epoch's published paper or pages on the method, for what the record says it cannot see.

**Seen before writing these (so not predictions).** From study 64: (A) delivers, 2,394 bytes, columns
`Quarter, End date, Owner, H100-equivalents, Share of global AI compute`, first row 2024 Q1; Epoch's page
says the five hyperscalers held 75.8 % at the end of March 2026, up from 69.7 % a year earlier. Nothing
else of (A) or (B) has been read. **Remembered, not verified, and therefore not evidence:** a 2025 paper on
AI supercomputer trends reporting that companies' share of cluster performance grew and governments' fell,
and that the United States holds most of it. These memories shaped P57 and P58; the source will be read.

**Definitions, fixed now.** *Share* is Epoch's own column, never recomputed from a denominator I build. A
*quarter's growth* is the change in percentage points from the previous quarter in the table. For (B), a
cluster's *owner is unknown* when the owner field is empty or says unknown/undisclosed; *state-held* when
the owner or sector field names a government, a national lab or a state body; capacity is the record's own
H100-equivalent (or performance) column, summed over clusters the record lists as existing.

**Predictions.**
- **P54.** In (A), the top-5 share rises in every quarter after the first: no quarter falls.
- **P55.** In (A), the top-5 share at the first quarter (2024 Q1) is below 65 %.
- **P56.** (A) carries no column for uncertainty, and Epoch's page for it states its total ("global AI
  compute") as an estimate with no interval printed beside the 75.8 %.
- **P57.** In (B), among listed existing clusters with a stated capacity, private owners hold at least
  70 % of capacity and state bodies at most 25 %.
- **P58.** In (B), clusters in the United States hold at least 60 % of listed capacity, and China's listed
  share is below 20 %; the record says in words that Chinese capacity is undercounted.
- **P59.** In (B), at least 25 % of listed clusters lack a stated owner or carry a capacity marked as
  estimated or uncertain.

**What would count against the step's premise.** If P54 and P55 both fail (a flat or falling share from a
high start), "how fast is that share concentrating" has the answer "it is not". The finding would say so.
