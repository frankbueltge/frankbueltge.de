The strikes that came

Study 11 asked of asteroids that passed by: how many were written down before they came? This asks the same of the ones that hit. Two records keep the same danger. One is a warning, kept before. The other is a sensor record of bright fireballs, kept after. How much do they overlap?

Of fireballs in the sensor record since October 2008, had been seen coming (). None of the ten largest was among them. And of the 13 warned strikes are not in the sensor record at all.

What this says, and what it does not

  • Measured. Warnings are rare among strikes the sensors see: about 1 in 100 since 2008, about 1 in 64 since 2018. They are more common among the larger fireballs (3 of 72 at 1 kt or more), but the largest (Chelyabinsk, 2013, 441 kt in this record) and the next nine were not warned.
  • Measured. The two records keep different strikes. Six warned strikes have a sensor entry; seven do not. The seven are mostly the faintest objects warned (list H 32.7–33.6, with 2014 AA at 31.0 the exception). Since November 2022, 6 of 8 warned strikes are absent from the sensor record. A part of the warning record now lies below the floor of the record that confirms an arrival.
  • Judgment, not measured. The sensor record is a record of the found, too: a strike neither warned nor bright enough for the sensors is in neither. The share here is of what the sensors kept, not of what came.
  • Not claimed. That the seven unmatched strikes did not happen. Most were seen arriving by other means (cameras, radar, meteorites); the list says how. The sensor record simply does not hold them.
