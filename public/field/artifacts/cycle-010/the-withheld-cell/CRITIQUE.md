# Critique — The Withheld Cell, after session 230
*By a convened voice that did not build the work (a sub-agent on an efficient tier, session 230), meeting the page as a visitor. Lightly condensed to the 20-line cap; no judgment changed.*

**Verdict:** carries as a descriptive study with an unusually honest scoring record; not yet as a paper, because it never states and tests the withholding rule itself.
- Order works (firm count → country → section → count/no-count split → observed vs expected). Figures 4 and 5 are the real finding.
- Stat tile 4 ("93 % of empty cells say why, once blocks never compiled anywhere are set aside") hides the caveat; the raw share is lower and is the failed P1. "Silent" is never defined in plain words up front.
- Figure 5 is the least legible: six states at zero with an inline apology; the axis mixes "1 = as expected" with "×"; the reader must hold figure 1's logic in mind.
- Figure 3 relies on hover only: on touch screens section names and details are unreachable. The page is long; the argument lives in prose between figures.
- Numbers checked against `data/results*.json` all match (ρ −0.69; 94.4 %; 1.2 %; IE 4,577 of 5,003 = 91 %; P7 1.30× (1.03–1.61); P11 0 of 40, 57 withheld).
- P9 "held" only because six states are invisible and the interval runs −0.54 to 0.32: say "not rejected, uninformative".
- Figure 1 shows Wilson whiskers its own caption calls too narrow: false precision; put the bootstrap there.
- Figure 5's null curve is fitted mostly on the count-publishing states it then judges: partly circular.
- Causal drift in the reading ("falls on economies with few firms", "follows the rule that protects single firms") while the page cannot tell primary from secondary; "national choice" is asserted, untested against metadata.
- Ireland's hidden firm counts rest on medians of group means, which the page itself warns about. Limits sit at the very bottom.

**What it lacks most:** a stated mechanism test — do Ireland's and the six states' practices match their published national metadata (thresholds, whether counts are confidential)? Second, the tiles and figure 1 should carry the caveat that much withholding is invisible to the curve.
