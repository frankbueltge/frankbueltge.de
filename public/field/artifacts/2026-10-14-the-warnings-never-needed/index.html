The warnings never needed

Study 13 counted the tornadoes that came under a warning: 53.7 %. This study turns the two records round and counts from the warnings' side. Of every tornado warning the US Weather Service issued from 2008 to 2023, how many were followed by a tornado inside the warned area while the warning stood? And how does that compare with the one other warning list this cycle has measured, the impact list of JPL's Sentry, which the Studio laid out in Struck Off?

The fall in 2012 depends on what counts as needed

Under the registered rule, the share never needed fell from in 2008–2011 to in 2012–2023, as the literature reports. But the warnings did not get better at being out before the tornado. Counted strictly, a warning is needed only if a tornado began inside it after it was issued; then the share never needed moved from to , about one point. What changed is that fewer warnings were issued for each tornado, and the gain sits wholly in warnings met only by a track crossing into them or by a tornado begun before or near their issue (8.2 points of the share in 2008–2011, 11.7 in 2017–2023), not by a tornado beginning inside them after they were out. (This comparison was not registered; it is reported as found.)

Two keepers of warnings

The Sentry list keeps every asteroid that might hit the Earth within a century. The Studio counted objects that it has ever held; were struck off (JPL's own interface gives the same count tonight), and one, 2014 AA, came true: it struck the Atlantic on 2 January 2014, 22 hours before it was struck off. The two grids below are drawn the same size, 5,804 cells each; the tornado grid is filled in the proportion of its 43,819 warnings.

Tornado warnings, 2008–2023

The Sentry list, 2002–2026

Judgment. These are not the same kind of warning. A tornado warning tells people to take shelter within the hour; a place on the Sentry list says only that an impact cannot yet be ruled out. Both keepers write their warnings never needed as quietly as the ones that came true: the tornado warning simply expires, the Sentry object is moved to a table with a name and a minute. Neither record says how many warnings were needed in the sense that matters, by changing what someone did.

Method and limits

  • Records. 43,819 tornado warnings (96,928 polygon rows, the Iowa Environmental Mesonet's archive of the Weather Service's products, as fetched for study 13), and 19,631 whole-track tornadoes of the Storm Prediction Center's file, 2008–2023 (sha256 fa616e2f…5fcc, the same file as study 13, now read with its end points).
  • Registered rule. A warning is met if a tornado beginning from 20 minutes before its issue to its last polygon's end has a track (the straight line from start to end point) that meets one of the warning's polygons. The record gives a tornado's start time only, so a track is not placed in time beyond its start; the allowance admits tornadoes already on the ground when warned.
  • Checks. 14 of 14 in check.py, including a second matcher (each track sampled at 201 points, winding-number rule) that agrees on 598 of 600 warnings drawn at random; the two it misses are track-grazes between its samples. The Joplin warning of 2011 (Springfield, #31) is met under both rules.
  • Limits. One analyst. Straight tracks stand in for the real paths. The Weather Service verifies county by county against its own database, which this study does not have; the literature's "near 0.75" is a level from a figure's discussion, not a table. Tornadoes are themselves a record of what was found: a warning "never needed" may have met a tornado nobody recorded. Warnings for tornadoes in late December 2023 whose touchdowns fall after the file's end are counted as never needed.
  • Built on. The Studio's Struck Off table (works/2026-10-14-struck-off/data.json, studio repository, sha256 6ff0dccd…a03b), checked against JPL's Sentry interface (removed objects: 3,581). Study 13 of this practice for the tornado frame and its yearly warned shares.
