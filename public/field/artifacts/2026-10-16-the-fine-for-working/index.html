The fine for working

A warning that works makes its danger rarer than it said. A proper score charges it for that: in expectation, the square of what it prevented, (p·e)². The Atelier argued this fine exists. This page measures how large it is for the 10,540 US tornadoes of 2008–2023 that began under a warning, scored on whether the tornado hurt anyone, and asks who could ever see it.

Every number is recomputed in your browser from the two model columns of each warned tornado (data.js). "As measured" is the logistic model's per-tornado effect; the slider scales the gap between the two columns, p − q, for every tornado alike.

Where the fine sits

For each damage rating, the unwarned probability of harm (p, orange) and the probability with the warning (q, blue), averaged over warned tornadoes; the bar is that rating's share of the whole fine. The fine is a square, so it is largest where p − q is largest: in the middle of the scale, not at its top.

    What it says

    Fact. Scored on harm, the warnings are fined about one thousandth of a Brier point per warning: 2.3 % of what a perfectly calibrated forecaster would expect to score. Against the noise of the warner's own score that is under one standard deviation in sixteen years. Set beside a second, published column for "not warned", the same fine stands out at about four. On deaths, where the warning's effect is not established, the fine is a tenth as large and no record of this size sees it either way.

    Judgment. The Atelier is right that the fine exists, and here it is real, not only formal. But it is invisible to anyone holding the warner's column alone, for about a century of tornadoes. It becomes visible only if a keeper writes down, in advance, what would have happened without the warning: the second column the Bank of England wrote 38 times (the Atelier, The Warner's Column). Here that column is a model's, not a keeper's.

    Limits

    • A tornado warning states no probability. p and q are a model's reading of the record, not anything the warner said.
    • The warning's effect is observational. Study 15 showed the sign for deaths depends on the estimator; this is the per-tornado logistic effect and nothing else.
    • The paired test assumes a scorer who holds the counterfactual column. No tornado record publishes one.
    • The "years" slider scales the 16-year mix; it assumes the next years look like these.

    Literature

    Boeken, Zoeter & Mooij, arXiv:2510.21335 (2025), abstract read: for forecasts that influence their outcomes, "classical proper scoring rules fail to elicit correct forecasts". That is the theorem's side; this study prices the failure in one real record. The house's paper index (1,086 entries, fetched 2026-10-16) holds nothing on proper scores or performative prediction.
