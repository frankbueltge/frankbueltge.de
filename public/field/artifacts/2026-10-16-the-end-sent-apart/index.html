The end sent apart

FEMA's archive of local alerts links a warning to its later word only when the keeper sends a Cancel or an Update that names it. Study 17 counted the warnings nothing names again as silent. The Studio found that some "warnings" are themselves ends: an all-clear sent as a fresh alert, naming nothing. This study counts them in the whole archive, asks whether they close warnings the archive calls silent, and checks whether study 17 holds without them.

Recomputed in your browser from one row per warning in data.js (no text, no senders). The registered window is 24 hours; the slider shows how the answer moves with it.

What was found

1. The all-clears are real and few. By the Studio's rule, unchanged, 1,016 of the 23,154 local warnings (4.4 %, 4.1–4.7) are messages sent as a fresh alert whose shown words begin with a cancel or carry an all-clear. Inside the Studio's own dealt pool the same code finds 678 of 14,049, the Studio's count exactly. 92.2 % of them are silent themselves: nothing follows an end. They are rising: 3 in 2014, 223 in 2026 so far.

2. Study 17 holds without them. Taken out, 15,707 silent warnings remain and all 15,707 carry their own expiry; 99.4 % of first cancels still come before it; who sends an alert still explains its cancelling far better than what it warns of (η² 0.51 against 0.09). Every verdict is the same. Study 17 and the paper's study-5 lines need no correction.

3. An all-clear does follow a warning, but not twice as often as chance. Two in three all-clears (66.9 %, 64.0–69.8) come within 24 hours of a warning from the same sender, against 37.3 % for any other warning. The ratio is 1.80 (1.53–2.07): our bar of 2 was not met (P4 failed). Four in five such pairs share the event code.

4. It does not close the silent ones in particular. The preceding warning is silent for 66.6 %, which passed our bar of 60 %, but 71.0 % of all warnings are silent: the bar sat below the base rate, so that pass says nothing (a bad test of ours, named here). Read the other way, 645 of 15,707 silent warnings (4.1 %) are followed by an all-clear from their sender within a day, and so are 4.0 % of the warnings that were not silent. The end sent apart exists, and it is no more likely to follow a silence than any other fate.

Reading (judgment). At most about one silence in twenty-five has a word of ending filed apart from it, and the record does not say that word ends that warning: the pair is made by us, from sender and time. The archive's silence stays what study 17 found: an end written in advance, not an end reported afterwards.

Method and limits

Method. The warnings and their fates are study 17's corrected build, rebuilt from today's fetch cut back to the 82,754 messages study 17 read; its figures reproduce exactly (check 1). An all-clear is the Studio's rule (STARTSCANCEL or ALLCLEAR on the shown words), applied to every warning, not only to those the Studio deals. A predecessor is the nearest earlier warning, not itself an all-clear, from the same sending account within 24 hours; the chance level applies the same test to every other warning. The ratio's interval resamples accounts (2,000 draws). check.py recounts everything from data/warnings.tsv by a second route, and proves a tamper is caught.

Limits. The pairing is by sender and time; no message names the warning it ends, which is the point. The rule's word list is mechanical and catches some warnings that only mention a lifting. Daily repeated alerts sit exactly at 24 hours, so the 24 h count is sensitive to the window's edge; the slider shows it. Accounts are hashed with study 17's salt; no text and no sender is committed.

A fault found on the way. Study 17's fetch.py pages the archive by $skip in id order. Today, with the archive's count unchanged, that returned 63 messages twice and 63 never. The build's assert of 82,754 unique ids stopped the run, as it was written to; fill.py lists every id twice (both passes agree), refetches the 63 by id and drops the duplicates. Study 17's own fetches passed the same assert, so its counts are not affected.
