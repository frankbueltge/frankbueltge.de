# Commission — map the field Meridian works in, and come back with possibilities

*Written 2026-09-01 by the house, on the architect's direction (wording private), to be
executed by a **fresh session with no other context**. This is a commission, not a programme.
It is deliberately short on instructions and long on limits.*

---

## 1. Why this exists, and what went wrong before it

The Field (Meridian) works on the default question *"E2E automation of AI research"* and has
produced two artifacts in cycle 001 from its own means. Before this commission, the house
twice tried to give it a research programme — three named directions, each with a
pre-registered falsifier — and the architect rejected both attempts for the same reason:
**fixing directions before the overview exists is not research, it is administration wearing
a lab coat.** He also said plainly that he cannot currently judge where this field leads.

So the answer to "where does this lead" is not a plan. It is a map, made by someone who has
actually read the territory.

## 2. The one question

> **What is actually possible, in this field, for a machine-run research practice that works
> from public records over time — and what would be worth doing?**

That is the whole brief. Everything below is either a limit or a description of what to hand
back; none of it narrows the question, and where it seems to, the question wins.

## 3. What to come back with

**A map of possibilities, not a programme.** Concretely, one document that a non-specialist
can read and decide on:

1. **The field as it actually stands.** Not a literature list — an account of what is
   contested, what is settled, what is claimed and unverified, and who is doing what. Every
   claim traceable to a retrievable source; anything you could not retrieve, named as such.
2. **The possibilities, plural — at least five, and they must not all be of one kind.** For
   each: what it would find out, why it is not already done (or who does it and how it
   differs), what it would take to run, and **what would kill it**. Rank them by the evidence
   for them, not by taste.
3. **Explicitly among them: the interactive line.** Works that are instruments — an indie
   game, an ARG, an interactive experience where participants consent to full data collection
   and get real entertainment in exchange, and the measurement happens while they play. This
   is a **first-class branch, not an appendix.** The house means it seriously: *Exit 8* as the
   realistic model of scale (one mechanic, perfectly chosen), *Disco Elysium* as the model of
   depth, an ARG as the model of collective work. Ask what such forms can measure that no
   questionnaire can, and what already exists in that space.
4. **What it would cost**, in money, in nights, and in things the house does not have.
5. **The uncomfortable options.** If the honest answer includes possibilities this house would
   dislike — that its current instruments are redundant, that the interesting work needs a
   collaborator, that the question is occupied — those belong in the map at full strength.

## 4. The limits — the only ones

- **No wet lab, no large-scale training, no funded compute.** The practice is a nightly cloud
  session with web access and a small Cloudflare/Neon footprint.
- **Anything holding runtime state** obeys the house's six proof duties
  (`.claude/rules/runtime-and-works.md`): declared state; a nightly committed snapshot; **count,
  never identify**; declared mortality; a named cost ceiling; fail-closed. Duty 3 is also the
  data-protection floor, and it is not negotiable.
- **Human participation is allowed** — anonymous, consented, minimal-data, no vulnerable
  groups, no sensitive categories, no deception with real stakes. There is no ethics board
  here; proportionality is the standard, and where a design exceeds it, say so.
- **Nothing sends itself.** Any option requiring recruitment, invitation or submission needs a
  human act. Say which options need one and how often.
- **Every claim sourced.** No result, figure or method reconstructed from training memory.

## 5. What has already been scanned — do not redo it, do not trust it

A shallow pass (six searches, 2026-09-01) found the following. **It is a scan, not a finding,
and it carries the assumptions of whoever ran it.** Treat as leads:

- Three systems reported in *Nature* 2026 (ERA, The AI Scientist, MIRA); AI-Scientist-v2 got a
  manuscript through a workshop review; Co-Scientist (multi-agent).
- Benchmarks: MLE-bench (**submissions paused April 2026 over fairness/comparability**),
  PaperBench, CORE-Bench, RE-Bench, ResearchClawBench, and a "research lifecycle" suite.
- Critique: *Agentic AI Scientists Are Not Built For Autonomous Scientific Discovery*
  (arXiv 2605.08956) — four named causes including that benchmarks measure single-turn
  accuracy rather than closed loops.
- *Autonomous Research Agents: A Survey of AI Scientists and the Verification Gap*
  (arXiv 2608.05179, June 2026) — claims the bottleneck has moved from capability to
  **verification**: 83 % of runnable systems release code, 38 % ship reproducibility artifacts,
  the same 38 % report a novelty-verification method, and **no LLM-era system demonstrates an
  externally validated verification oracle.**
- Meta-science: agentic reproducibility assessment (2605.02651); >half of researchers using AI
  in peer review, often against guidance; an argument that AI collapses the cost of robustness
  checks, making current sensitivity standards underpowered by orders of magnitude.

**The verification gap is the lead this scan liked best. That is exactly why it must be tested
rather than adopted.** If the survey finds it occupied, overstated, or less interesting than
something else, say so — that finding is worth more than agreement.

## 6. Report where this commission is wrong

This brief encodes the assumptions of the session that wrote it. Its last section is therefore
a duty: **name what in this commission the survey contradicts** — a limit that is not real, a
framing that misdescribes the field, a possibility excluded by its wording. A commission that
comes back unamended has probably not been tested.

## 7. What this brief deliberately does not say

It names no research direction, no hypothesis, no falsifier and no schedule. It does not say
whether the centre is verification, measurement, the interactive line, or something not yet
named. Those are the map's to propose and the architect's to choose.

**Deliverable:** one document, written for a reader who is not in this field, with its sources
linked. Length as the material demands; a map that cannot be read in one sitting has failed the
reader it is for.
