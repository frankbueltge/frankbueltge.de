# Ducking — design (2026-11-01)

*Frank's word of 2026-11-01, wording private. Paraphrased: the mixing desk proposed the night before
as a first work for two Raspberry Pis is too much hardware for now and can be done digitally; make
it a lab experiment, and use the better free voices found in another project, not machine-sounding ones.*

## The work

One desk. A channel for each matter of the newest briefing, in the briefing's order, and one for
everything else on Common Ground's charts. A fader's height is its channel's share of the trailing
week; each channel is a voice reading its evidence, all running all the time. Push a fader up and it
holds only while you hold it. The sum stays whole. Let go and it falls back.

## What changed from the sketch

The sketch of 2026-10-31 had eight channels whose shares were shares of each other, so its 100 % was
a setting, and it said so. Two things replace that:

- **The denominator is the ledger.** Everything no matter named is a channel. The 100 % is everything
  the charts held, and the finding got sharper for it: the matters together are a sliver of one
  percent to about one percent, and most of them are zero in most weeks.
- **The fader is logarithmic, with the top stretched.** Otherwise a matter at 0.03 % could not be told
  from zero. Level is dB of the share taken as a power share.

Also gone: the synthesised motor noise. The only sounds are voices.

## After the first hearing

Frank heard the crowd drown everything and did not read the channel's first name, EVERYTHING ELSE
(wording private). Both were faults of the work. The channel is now THE CROWD, a caption above the
screen says what it is, and the top of the fader's travel is stretched. A transcription test showed why
it was too hard: against the open crowd a voice took about 80 % of the mix to be recovered, because
speech masks speech. A lowpass that closes on the crowd as a hand raises a matter brings that down to
about 10 %. The crowd's level still follows its share; what it costs is that the sound no longer equals
the fader exactly, and the method sheet says so.

## Measure

Common Ground only, read by Third Reading's rule (`src/lib/reading/attention.ts`), so the same matters
are counted the same way on both works. GDELT was tried as a broader second meter on 2026-11-01:
the DOC API returned a thin series and rate-limited a second request, so a second meter would have
needed a pipeline of its own. It stays out, and the method sheet states the tilt of the charts instead.

## Voices

Kokoro-82M, open weights, Apache 2.0, local, free. Spoken forms of figures are written by hand in
the round (`say`) and every reading was checked by transcribing it back. Rendered once per round by `scripts/ducking/voices.py`. A test ties the
recordings to the round's words, so the page cannot show one sentence while a voice says another.

## Where it hangs

`src/data/ducking/rounds/<round>.json` · `public/ducking/<round>/` · `src/lib/ducking/*` ·
`src/components/ducking/*` · `src/styles/ducking.css` · `/ducking`, `/werke/ducking` ·
`src/data/werke.ts` · USP audit §23. A new briefing does not reach the desk by itself: a new round is
written and rendered by hand, then `CURRENT_ROUND` moves. Until then the matters and their words stay
at the old briefing's date, the faders keep moving, and the page says which is which.

## Neighbours (audit §23: ADDED VALUE, thin)

The gesture is 31 years old: AudioStreamer (Schmandt and Mullins, 1995) raised one of three news
programmes when you turned towards it. Nothing found holds the conjunction with a committed measure.
The zero sum on the desk is arithmetic, and the page says so; the claim about attention outside the
page rests on Zhu, Eisensee and Strömberg, and is limited by Sisco and others.

## Not built

Keeping what visitors hold. It is the audit's first direction: without a mark this is AudioStreamer
with a better story. One number per channel fits the study frame's `marks` kind, but the frame serves
the practices' works and ids, and a house work would need its own entry there, a consent line and the
privacy text. Frank has not been asked yet.
