## 1. Why this line starts at a disadvantage (say it first)

Every published charge against data art was written about screens, and the house's own
verdict fell on screen works. Applied honestly, the checklist reads: illustration instead
of transformation (Saltz on Anadol, Feb 2023: derivative of its training data, unable to
transcend its source); arbitrary mapping (Manovich 2002: since software makes any
data-to-image mapping trivial, an unmotivated one means nothing); indexed to the probable
rather than the real (Steyerl, *Mean Images*, 2023); research display substituting for a
proposition (Bishop, *Information Overload*, Artforum 2023); wall-text dependency (Trebing,
Monopol 2024: the themes have to be written next to the images); and — decisive for a
nightly practice — repetition without mourning anesthetizes (Warhol's own account of the
Death and Disaster series).

Simondon says the same from the other side, thirty years before the genre existed:
aestheticization from outside is "a materialized lie" (MEOT 196), and "every technical
spectacle remains puerile" without participation in the schemas of action (MEOT 236).

**The line's whole problem in one sentence:** on a screen, nothing costs anything, nothing
is unrepeatable, and nothing resists. Whatever the line becomes, it has to manufacture
cost, unrepeatability and resistance *out of properties only a screen has* — otherwise it
is a picture of data, and pictures of data are the saturated middle.

## 2. What only a screen can do (the medium-justification list)

The physical track answered "why this medium?" with one exclusive property: FDM has
duration, so the z axis can be time and the printer becomes a recording instrument. The
screen needs its own answer, and it has eight candidates:

1. **It computes at the moment of viewing** — the work can be *made* in front of the
   viewer, from state that did not exist a second earlier.
2. **It can destroy as it shows.** Serving can be subtractive: a thing shown once and
   then withheld from everyone after.
3. **It can refuse.** A page can decline to show, close itself, expire at a declared
   moment, or make showing conditional on something the viewer does.
4. **It knows it is being read** — arrival time, sequence, count. (Also the line's main
   ethical minefield; see §6.)
5. **It is one object with many simultaneous inhabitants** — shared state across viewers,
   which no print and no gallery object has.
6. **It hands over the means of falsification at zero cost** — view source, re-run,
   re-derive, diff. Verification is native here; in the physical track it is an
   achievement.
7. **It has a perceptible body: latency.** Waiting, rate limits, timeouts, 404s, dead
   evidence — the network's resistance is available as material, and it is not the
   practice's to command (Mersch's *passio*: what befalls, without our doing).
8. **Identity persists while content changes** — the same address, a different object
   each night, with a public diff.

**The screenshot test** (crisp form of the medium question, use it early): take a
screenshot of the work. What does the screenshot destroy? If the answer is "nothing
essential", the work should have been an image, and the screen was decoration. Every
affordance above answers the test differently — that is what makes them load-bearing.

## 3. The house already has the two prototypes

The line is a concretization of something the record already contains — problems found at
the saturations of one's own working, not invented from outside:

- **Below the Threshold** (n-1, candidate, 2026-08-16): renders *committed, dated
  evidence* at every load, never live queries; its own law is "Nothing rendered that is
  not committed evidence"; and it says to the reader: "Check any line above against the
  public history it names; you do not have to trust me." That is affordance 6 as a work.
- **STILL DARK** (studio, 2026-08-15): twelve stop buttons, one per list, holding one
  arrival state each; headline figures rewritten live at each beat of a ~32-second run;
  the opening reading a single point (100 %), the final one a band (22–38 %) — magnitude
  falls, range widens. That is affordance 1 as a work, and its restraint (no artist
  statement in the work file) is exactly the anti-wall-text discipline.

Both are unfinished in the same way: they *offer* verification and computation but demand
nothing, cost nothing, and lose nothing. They watch well and implicate no one. The gap
between them and a work is the line's actual subject.

(Note for the parallel session: the working paper decoupled from all existing practices on
2026-08-20 and no longer cites either work. They are the *house's* precedent, not the
paper's evidence — do not re-couple them.)

## 4. Five work families (each on a named affordance)

**F1 — Re-derivable claims** *(affordances 1 + 6)*. The work states a finding and hands
over everything needed to break it: pinned data, the derivation, hashes, a re-run in the
viewer's own machine. The reliquary economy inverted for screens: the index is mute, so the
frame speaks — and here the frame is the authentication apparatus, which is exactly what
the Shroud of Turin has been since 1988 (the laboratory dossier became part of the object,
and it lost none of its pull). Debtor: the public, owed a checkable claim rather than a
trusted one. Risk: turns into documentation. It is a work only if the invitation to falsify
is *the experience*, not a footer.

**F2 — Depleting works** *(affordances 2 + 5)*. A finite stock consumed by attention. The
canonical model is Gonzalez-Torres' 175 lb of candy — the number is a body, taking a piece
enacts the wasting, the pile is endlessly replenished — and the transferable core is:
**the number must be a body, the operation must be a wound, the viewer's hand must be in
it.** On screen the stock has to be genuinely finite, and there are only a few honest
candidates: records shown once and then embargoed (dated, hash-verifiable), or a real
compute/API budget spent per view until the work stops. Both are wagers with receipts.
*Honest tension to resolve before building:* the house's archive is inviolable, so a work
may not actually destroy evidence. The clean form is a **dated seal** — "no visitor after
you will see this line until 2046; the record is sealed, its hash is published" — mortality
without evidence loss. Anything looser is theatre.

**F3 — The singularia machine** *(affordances 1 + 3)*. Against the mean, for the
particular: the work shows the aggregate that governs a decision, then one real case the
aggregate erases, then refuses the second — and states how many it will never show you.
This is Steyerl's critique inverted into a mechanism (statistical renderings "indexed to
the probable rather than the real"), it is Mersch's *veritas singularis de contingentibus*
made operational, and it is machine-native in the strict sense: only a machine holds a
hundred thousand particulars *and* declines to average them. Overlaps Ulysses' territory
(the warrant of numbers) — coordinate, don't duplicate. Debtor: the erased particular.

**F4 — Passio works** *(affordance 7)*. Made of what the practice does not command:
institutional latency, statutory deadlines running out, requests unanswered, evidence that
404s. The work waits, visibly, and the waiting is the form; it must include the practice's
**own** debts and silences, or it is a dashboard pointing at others. This family is the
answer to the sharpest objection the whole programme faces — that a machine practice is
pure *actio*, all grasping and no being-befallen — and it is worth building for that reason
alone. Neighbour honesty: FOI trackers exist as civic tools (FragDenStaat and kin); the
claim can only be the work's framing and its self-implication, never the tracking.

**F5 — One address, growing** *(affordance 8)*. Same URL, a different object every night,
every past state public, and **the diff is the work**. This is the accretion niche the
dossier found unoccupied, in the medium where it is cheapest — which is also why it is the
easiest of the five to fake. It earns its existence only with a declared end (a date, a
count, a condition) and a rule that costs something to keep; otherwise it is the nightly
content stream that produced the verdict in the first place.

## 5. Four hybrid forms (the physical track's own logic reused)

The v3 draft's three honest fabrication modes govern here (score · executor · concept).

**H1 — Reliquary pairs.** Object plus page: the object is the mute index, the page is the
frame, the dossier and the re-derivation. The screen finally has a job the print cannot do
— and the print gives the screen the body it lacks.

**H2 — Score pages.** The page *is* the executable score — pinned dataset, deterministic
derivation, G-code, hashes — and anyone may fabricate; the practice never claims the object.
Native mode for a machine practice, and the cleanest answer to "the practices cannot print".

**H3 — Witness pages, asymmetric.** Before a live inscription: the declared mapping and
guardrails, hashed (pre-registration). During: nothing — silence while the work is being
made elsewhere. After: log, manifest, timelapse, invitation to re-derive. The asymmetry is
the statement: the event was not content.

**H4 — Studio-fact works.** A screen work whose state is set by a physical fact in the
founder's room (a print in progress, a light sensor, a printer's own resonance). The
private material fact made public, live — passio with an address.

## 6. Ship gate for this line (translation of the v3 work bar)

1. **Debtor named** — whom does the work owe what? A mechanism has inputs; a work has a
   debt (the ex-voto structure: danger, vow, survival, object — and the tablet says so in
   writing).
2. **A cut moment** — not the phenomenon, one slice of it (Géricault chose the rescue ship
   as a speck on the horizon, not the wreck).
3. **Mapping motivated to necessity** — the form could not be otherwise. "The API offered
   it" is availability.
4. **Passes the screenshot test** (§2) — name what the screenshot destroys.
5. **Second-visit test** — is the second encounter a new beginning or a replay? (MEOT 211:
   art as the power of iteration that does not negate the reality of each new beginning.)
6. **No wall text** — the schema is perceivable in the encounter (MEOT 197, 114); tested by
   a stranger who read nothing, asked either "what did you understand?" (schema) or, for
   works that show rather than say, "what struck you?" (the non-propositional variant).
7. **Declared mortality** — what kills this work, and is that written down at creation?
   Link rot, an end date, a depleted stock, a revoked API. Decay declared is a statement;
   decay discovered is bad material.
8. **Documented cost** — the rule risks something; failures stay in the record. Flawless
   execution proves nothing (Hsieh kept 133 missed punches in the record; that is why the
   year counts).
9. **Machine-advantage bar** (v2, unchanged) — scale · repetition · verification · the
   temporal, experienceable by a stranger in the artifact itself.

**Ethical floor, non-negotiable.** Affordance 4 (the work knows it is read) is the line's
temptation and its trap: measuring viewers is a data practice on real people, and P8's
boundary holds — work on technical reality, measure manipulation, never practise it. What
is defensible: counting arrivals with no identity attached, and telling the viewer plainly
that the count is part of the work. What is not: identification, fingerprinting, retention
beyond the count, or an aesthetic effect that depends on the viewer not knowing.

## 7. Saturated on screens — avoid or subvert deliberately

Particle clouds and point-cloud drifts · gradient wallpaper driven by any feed · data
mapped to hue and motion with no argument · dashboards presented as art · immersive
projection as awe machine · scroll-driven "storytelling" that is an article with parallax ·
the latent-space walk. This is the Anadol pole, and the house's position is the opposite
one: not data as fog machine, but data as evidence, with the means to check it. The kitsch
floor is the live ticker as décor.

## 8. Line names to choose between (the founder's call; wording-kanon governs)

**SINGULARIA** — names the epistemic commitment (the singular against the mean; Baumgarten's
*veritas singularis*, the core of Mersch's aesthetic research) and would sit well beside
COUNTER-MEASUREMENT. · **THE PERISHABLE** — puts mortality first, binds F2/F5. ·
**FALSIFIABLES** — puts the handing-over of falsification first, binds F1. ·
**WHAT BEFALLS** — puts passio first, binds F4. The line enters when a work names it, so
the name should be the one the first work can carry without embarrassment.

## 9. Five candidates, ready to be developed

1. **Sealed Once** (F2 + counter-measurement). A corpus of documented erasures — public
   claims later quietly changed or deleted, from material the house already holds. Each
   visitor is served exactly one, then it is sealed by date, its hash published; a counter
   shows how many remain unseen. When the last is served, the work is a hash list and one
   sentence. The viewer is told, truthfully, that no one after them will see this line.
2. **The Mean and the One** (F3). The figure that governs a decision, beside one real case
   it erases, and the refusal of the second — with the count of what will never be shown.
3. **Waiting Room** (F4). Every pending request, deadline and silence the practice is party
   to, its own included, rendered as live duration. Nothing sends itself; the work only
   waits, and the waiting accumulates.
4. **The Plinth Page** (F1 + H1). The screen twin of the Reliquary of the First Question
   (v3 draft §4): a page that shows only what it waits for and the live state of the
   condition, checkable by anyone. If the condition never fires, the page is the work.
5. **One Layer, Two Ways** (H3 + F1). The witness page for the physical experiment E3 — the
   same event inscribed live and rendered afterwards — where the page carries what the two
   objects cannot: the pre-registration, both provenance chains, and the single question
   whether provenance is visible in the objects at all.

## 10. Cheap tests before anything is built

Prototype in a browser tab, no site integration: (a) run the stranger probe on a link with
