## 1. Consensus (`/consensus`)

**(a) Claim.** *"How much of the 'independent' news consensus is really one source, copied."*
(`src/i18n/ui.ts:162`). Mechanism, `src/components/pages/ConsensusPage.astro:129`: each day
finds the sentence run word-for-word by the most outlets, computes an "echo index" for the
day's stream, links every masthead to its source, archives daily.

**(b) Nearest neighbors.**
- [Churnalism / Churnalism US](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2013/04/churnalism-tracker-catches-journalists-copying-press-releases-wikipedia) — pastes an article, checks it against a hashed corpus of press releases/other outlets for verbatim reuse (sliding-window hashing).
- [Media Cloud](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/media-cloud/overview) (MIT/Harvard) — ingests 1B+ articles, [explicitly deduplicates repeated sentences across sources](https://medium.com/media-cloud-project/tech-brief-how-we-deduplicate-content-in-media-cloud-772cd46b6f7f) at global scale with an open API.
- ["Detecting Textual Reuse in News Stories, At Scale"](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/download/9904/2777), *International Journal of Communication* — peer-reviewed churnalism-at-scale methodology.
- [Reusing the News](https://www.reusingthenews.org) — academic n-gram text-reuse study of broadcast news, same exact-match technique.

**(c) Draft verdict: ADDED VALUE (thin).** The measurement technique — exact/near-exact
cross-outlet text-reuse to quantify churnalism — is over a decade old, institutionalized in
both a consumer tool (Churnalism) and a heavyweight research platform (Media Cloud), backed by
peer-reviewed method papers. Nothing here is a new measurement. What isn't replicated is the
*format*: a daily, public, single-headline ritual with a running scalar "echo %" and a
committed daily archive — none of the neighbors publish a daily "front page" naming one
sentence. Real but thin daylight: if either neighbor shipped that front page tomorrow, The
Consensus would be redundant overnight.

**(d) Rework direction.** Publish the *syndication-structure* classification (chain
syndication vs. scattered placement vs. mixed, TLD-share) as the distinguishing analytic
layer — none of the neighbors classify *why* outlets converged, only *that* they did.

## 2. Iceberg Theory (`/parallax`)

**(a) Claim.** *"How Wikipedia language editions differ on contested topics."*
(`src/i18n/ui.ts:157`). *"Included are sovereignty and territorial disputes drawn from
Wikipedia's own categories; nobody selects."* (`src/components/pages/ParallaxePage.astro:25`).
Method: per-claim matrix per topic — stated / contradicted / omitted per language edition; one
topic re-measured daily (example cited: Senkaku Islands).

**(b) Nearest neighbors.**
- [INFOGAP](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.384.pdf) (UBC/Johns Hopkins/UW, EMNLP 2024; [coverage](https://hub.jhu.edu/2025/01/09/finding-hidden-biases-in-wikipedias-multilingual-content)) — peer-reviewed, fact-level cross-lingual information-gap detector for Wikipedia at 2.7k-article scale; authors explicitly note it generalizes to contentious-topic argumentation across stances.
- [Omnipedia](https://collablab.northwestern.edu/pubs/CHI2012-BaoEtAl-Omnipedia.pdf) (Northwestern, CHI 2012) — visualizes shared vs. unique content across up to 25 language editions at once.
- [Manypedia](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/3939/3382) (First Monday 2013) — automated side-by-side "language points of view" comparison (now defunct).
- WikiCompare — browser plugin comparing English vs. 8 other-language editions, flags missing info.
- ["China and Japan's Wikipedia War"](https://foreignpolicy.com/2013/02/05/china-and-japans-wikipedia-war), *Foreign Policy* (2013) — journalistic deep-dive on the exact Senkaku/Diaoyu edit-war example the site cites.

**(c) Draft verdict: REDUNDANT-leaning.** INFOGAP is the embarrassing neighbor: peer-reviewed,
fact-level presence/absence detection functionally identical to the "nennt/verschweigt"
matrix, larger scale, and its authors name contested political topics as a target
application — not a hypothetical competitor. Omnipedia and Manypedia popularized "compare what
each Wikipedia language omits" as a genre over a decade ago, UI included. The Senkaku case
itself is well-trodden academically and journalistically. A visitor who knows Wikipedia-bias
research would read this as a smaller, hand-curated re-run of a solved genre.

**(d) Rework direction.** The plausible surviving differentiator is *process*, not method: a
committed, versioned, git-archived daily register with a fixed non-selected topic set, never
retconned — none of the neighbors offer that as a public, continuously-dated artifact
(INFOGAP is a paper/dataset; Omnipedia/Manypedia are dead demos). Name this explicitly as the
point, and cite INFOGAP/Omnipedia/Manypedia openly rather than let a visitor discover the
overlap unassisted.

## 3. Society (`/society`)

**(a) Claim.** *"This is Minsky's book, performed. Not a simulation inspired by The Society of
Mind (1986) but that book as working machinery: twenty-seven agents, every one small enough to
read whole, every one carrying the essay it steps out of."* (`src/pages/society/index.astro:70-76`).
The page's own "where this stands" section (lines 156–169) already names prior art it searched
and found: agent-society art (Ian Cheng's BOB, Karl Sims, Creatures), ablation-as-gesture
(Neural Interactome), and the Voyager Society of Mind CD-ROM (1994).

**(b) Nearest neighbors.**
- Voyager Company's *Society of Mind* CD-ROM (1994) — interactive hypertext edition made with Minsky's involvement, but navigable book, not executable agents (already cited by the site itself).
- [AgentSociety](https://github.com/tsinghua-fib-lab/agentsociety) (Tsinghua FIB Lab) — large LLM-agent social-simulation platform, but LLM-native agents for social science, not Minsky's small deterministic rule agents.
- [Mindstorm / NLSOM](https://github.com/metauto-ai/Mindstorm) — invokes "Societies of Mind" by name but implements it as LLMs/neural nets talking in natural language — the architectural inversion (opaque large models) of what the book and this site describe.
- ["Implementing Agents in JavaScript"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18228) — precedent for static, no-backend, browser-only multi-agent simulation as a deployment pattern, not built around Minsky's book.
- Essay-only pieces drawing K-line/LLM-memory analogies (e.g. "Society of Mind: The 50-Year-Old Blueprint for AI Agents") — commentary, not implementations.

**(c) Draft verdict: UNIQUE (provisional).** Across several search angles nothing surfaced that
is a deterministic, non-LLM, browser-executable re-implementation of Minsky's own named agents
and mechanisms (Builder, Grasp, Censor-Wreck, K-line arch-transfer) with section-level
citations to the 1986 text, testable claims, and live ablation. Today's "Society of
Mind"-branded software is almost uniformly LLM-multi-agent frameworks borrowing the name
loosely — the exact inversion the site's own copy calls out. This is the one experiment where
independent research corroborates rather than undercuts the claim.

**(d) Caveat, not a rework need.** This verdict rests on absence-of-evidence, which is weaker
than the other findings in this audit (where strong positive neighbors exist). Worth one more
pass in academic HCI/cognitive-science venues specifically (SIGCHI/AIES archives, 1990s–2000s
pedagogical Society-of-Mind simulations) before treating UNIQUE as settled.

## 4. Machine Attention (`/attention`)

**(a) Claim.** *"Its first project, The Foreknown, notarizes the world's public warnings
(GDACS, NOAA) as hashed original bytes the moment they are issued — provenance applied to the
future — and holds a hundred announced futures under watch as ticking clocks on a public
stage."* (`src/data/werke.ts:35-36`). Method sheet (`src/pages/werke/attention.astro`): every
preserved file carries source URL, UTC retrieval time and SHA-256; a resolver scores
"materialized" (with lead time) vs. "unmatched" once a warning closes.

**(b) Nearest neighbors.**
- [OpenTimestamps](https://opentimestamps.org) — open standard since ~2016, hashes any file and anchors it to Bitcoin as proof-of-existence at a point in time; used by multiple member services. Generic, no disaster focus, no resolution semantics.
- Proof of Existence / OriginStamp — the original hash-plus-blockchain-timestamp services (2013/2015) this whole mechanism descends from.
- GDACS itself + ReliefWeb, wired by third-party dashboards — GDACS already issues timestamped, population-scored, RSS/CAP bulletins; commercial/NGO dashboards already combine GDACS+ReliefWeb for real-time awareness.
- Metaculus / Good Judgment (Superforecasters) — the dominant forecast-verification ecosystems with resolution, calibration, Brier scoring — the closest analogue to the "materialized/unmatched" verdict idea, applied to crowd predictions rather than institutions.
- HDX / ReliefWeb archives — institutional, versioned disaster-report archiving, not cryptographically hashed.

**(c) Draft verdict: ADDED VALUE, narrower than framed.** The core mechanism — hash, timestamp,
prove existence — is a decade-old commodity (OpenTimestamps, Proof of Existence). Nobody needs
this project to prove a GDACS bulletin existed at a given time; GDACS's own feed already
timestamps its own issuance. "Provenance applied to the future" overclaims against that prior
art. What isn't found elsewhere: continuous SHA-256 notarization *specifically* of
institutional early-warning feeds, held as a public ledger, resolved into an explicit
materialized/unmatched taxonomy — forecast-verification methodology (Metaculus-style) applied
to institutions instead of crowds. That combination is real.

**(d) Rework direction.** Drop "provenance applied to the future" as the headline (it overclaims
against OpenTimestamps); foreground the part with no found neighbor: an open, falsifiable,
ongoing calibration/lead-time scorecard *of the warning institutions themselves* — audit the
alerting systems' track record the way Metaculus audits forecasters.

## 5. The State Before the Interface (`/observatory`)

**(a) Claim.** *"An autonomous observatory of public AI procurement in Europe. One machine runs
the whole chain — signal → candidate → investigation → evidence → verification → form → public
result — and its autonomy is itself the thing under measurement."* (`src/pages/observatory/index.astro`,
lede). Method sheet (`src/pages/werke/observatory.astro`): reads TED via the public Search API
v3, computes differences — records changed after publication, records that disappear, the same
vendor winning across borders, buyers that return.

**(b) Nearest neighbors.**
- [Transparency International EU — Integrity Watch Red Flags](https://transparency.eu/integrity-watch-red-flags) — real-time public procurement monitoring, ~40,000 EU-budget + 320,000+ national contracts across 9 countries, updated near-daily, 20 fraud/corruption risk indicators.
- [EU JRC — AI Watch / Public Sector Tech Watch](https://ai-watch.ec.europa.eu) — the European Commission's *own* observatory of AI adoption in public administration, 940+ collected use cases as of 2024 (survey/desk-research based, no longer updated).
- [Tussell — UK Public Sector AI Procurement Tracker](https://tussell.com/insights/ai-procurement-tracker) — commercial, monthly-updated tracker of AI spend/vendors in UK government, backed by a full contract database.
- Jorpex and similar TED-monitoring SaaS — commercial continuous TED ingestion with keyword/CPV filters including AI terms, built for sales leads.
- [TED Open Data Service](https://data.ted.europa.eu) — the EU's own official SPARQL/knowledge-graph layer over all TED notices, already open and reusable.

**(c) Draft verdict: REDUNDANT on the surface mission; ADDED VALUE only on method.** At "does
anyone already watch EU AI-in-government procurement," the answer is unambiguously yes,
multiple times over, at greater scale: Tussell (commercial), Transparency International EU
(near-real-time, EU+9 countries), and the JRC's own AI Watch (the Commission's own observatory
of its own public sector). The page's "before-picture that cannot be reconstructed later"
framing is weakened by Tussell and TI-EU building exactly that picture now, continuously, with
paid staff and larger datasets. Not found duplicated: SHA-256/append-only provenance manifests
per notice, an explicit six-criteria investigative gate with committed per-candidate reasoning
(including the ones that die), and full autonomy-cost logging as the stated research object —
but that's a claim about *how it's built*, not *what it uniquely sees*.

**(d) Rework direction.** Explicitly reframe away from "first/only to watch AI procurement in
Europe" (false — name Tussell, TI-EU, AI Watch on the page itself, per the site's own
"checkable" ethic) and toward what's actually unreplicated: a fully autonomous,
hash-verifiable, publicly falsifiable *investigation* pipeline, not a monitoring dashboard.

## 6. Protocol (`/protocol`)

**(a) Claim.** *"Every night a pipeline writes the minutes of the planet's session — from
twelve open, citable sources, deterministic, no LLM. Every agenda item ends the same way:
Resolution: adjourned."* (`src/data/werke.ts:168`; confirmed by `src/lib/protokoll/render.ts:60`
and `src/pages/protocol/feed.xml.ts:10`).

**(b) Nearest neighbors.**
- [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org) — 14,000+ interactive charts across poverty, climate, conflict, health, energy since 2013, Oxford-affiliated, CC BY. Dwarfs the Protocol on breadth and authority; no nightly deterministic "minutes" ritual, no stated no-LLM constraint.
- Carbon Brief "Daily Briefing" — genuinely daily (weekday) climate/energy digest, human-curated prose, climate-only.
- [Climate–Conflict–Vulnerability Index (CCVI)](https://climate-conflict.org) — fuses climate+conflict+vulnerability into one open index, quarterly cadence, not nightly.
- HDX "State of Open Humanitarian Data" reports — periodic meta-assessments of global humanitarian data availability.

**(c) Draft verdict: ADDED VALUE, driven by the literary form, not the data aggregation.** As a
"we aggregate open planetary indicators daily" pitch, this ground is heavily occupied by
better-resourced players — OWID almost certainly tracks a superset of the 12 sources with more
rigor and reach; Carbon Brief already does genuinely daily human-edited aggregation for
climate; CCVI already fuses climate+conflict. None of these neighbors do the specific thing
that makes the Protocol odd rather than derivative: a nightly, fully deterministic, no-LLM,
test-locked pipeline rendering every item through a fixed bureaucratic template ending
"Resolution: adjourned" — an artistic/procedural conceit, not a data-journalism product. No
comparable "deterministic templated minutes-of-the-world" project turned up.

**(d) Rework direction.** Stop positioning "twelve open sources, daily" as the value
proposition (invites a losing comparison to OWID); foreground the deterministic/no-LLM/
test-locked-template mechanism and the "adjourned" bureaucratic-literary device as the explicit
claim — an art piece about administrative language applied to planetary indicators, not a
data-aggregation competitor to Our World in Data.

## 8. Editorial Deadline (`/redaction`)

**(a) Claim.** *"What is quietly removed from the official public record — struck through,
checkable in two clicks."* (`src/i18n/ui.ts:170`). *"Evidence is Wayback permalinks
(before/after) — checkable yourself in two clicks. Rewriting is not cover-up; the instrument
claims no intent."* (`src/components/pages/RedactionPage.astro:100-101`).

**(b) Nearest neighbors.**
- [EDGI — Federal Environmental Web Tracker](https://envirodatagov.org/federal-environmental-web-tracker-about-page) — open-source "Scanner" automatically diffing federal webpages between dates, publishing a public dataset of 1,500+ significant changes since 2017, continuing through 2025 (632 changes in the first 100 days of the second Trump term alone, per NPR Aug 2025).
- [Sunlight Foundation / Berkman Klein — Web Integrity Project ("Gov404")](https://sunlightfoundation.com/gov404) — monitors/catalogs federal website changes/removals since 2016 with detailed before/after documentation, feeding congressional oversight and FOIA investigations.
- [changedetection.io](https://changedetection.io) — free/open-source, off-the-shelf scheduled-capture + diff + notification tool explicitly marketed for monitoring government pages.
- Security Force Monitor's Wayback CDX methodology — the identical "two captures, spot the substantive difference" evidentiary technique, documented.
- ["Visualizing changes to US federal environmental agency websites, 2016–2020"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7906373), peer-reviewed — the same change-tracking/visualization approach on the same non-authoritative Wayback source.
- [News Sniffer](https://www.newssniffer.co.uk) — world chamber's strongest living neighbour (found in the 2026-08-14 §2 seal; the 2026-08-13 pass had only declared NewsDiffs dead): actively monitoring 2.7M news articles with 5.25M captured versions, minute-fresh, open source — but a browsable per-article revision archive of a handful of UK feeds (BBC, Guardian), not a metric instrument: no daily rate, no triviality classification, no global/multilingual scale, no deletion measurement, no receipts.
- [diffengine](https://github.com/DocNow/diffengine) — Documenting the Now's RSS-diff lineage (with NYTDiff and the long-dead NewsDiffs): per-outlet bots posting edit screenshots to social accounts; curatorial, instance-by-instance, several dormant; no register, no rate, no classification.
- [MediaSpin dataset](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/42794) — ICWSM 2026: 78,910 post-publication headline-edit pairs annotated for 13 media-bias types via an LLM-plus-expert pipeline — academic proof that headline edits carry bias signal, shipped as dataset and paper, not a living public instrument; its taxonomy is a benchmark the chamber's triviality filter should cite.
- [GDELT Global Difference Graph](https://blog.gdeltproject.org/looking-back-on-four-months-of-the-global-difference-graph) — publishes the underlying recrawl data (~53.6k title rewrites/day, verified 2026-08-14) and one-off blog analyses; no living public surface, no filter, no archive of record.
- [Pew Research, "When Online Content Disappears" (2024)](https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2024/05/17/when-online-content-disappears) — the deletion-rate neighbourhood is one-off studies (Pew: 38 % of 2013's pages unreachable a decade later; Harvard LIL's 2021 NYT link-rot/content-drift study): measured once per paper, not watched daily, none against a self-committed archive that can produce the vanished text.
- ["Unpublishing the News" (IJoC 2019)](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/download/10786/2679/38967) — the unpublishing/right-to-be-forgotten literature (Dwyer's newsroom-policy research, CJR's "Into Oblivion") studies why and how newsrooms take articles down — policy ethnography and ethics, no counting instrument, no public register.

**(c) Draft verdict: ADDED VALUE (re-sealed 2026-08-14 with the world chamber; chamber 1
alone remains REDUNDANT).** For chamber 1 the 2026-08-09 reading stands unchanged: EDGI's
Scanner is the same idea at industrial scale with nine years of history, and a daily
single-pick presentation is no defensible edge. The world chamber changes the werk's
position: the gesture "watch news revisions" is occupied by a living incumbent (News
Sniffer, 2.7M articles — bigger than the 2026-08-13 pass assumed), and headline-edit bias
analysis exists academically (MediaSpin), but the instrument form appears unoccupied
worldwide: nobody publishes a daily, global, versioned-filter register of headline
reframings, and nobody publishes a daily deletion rate measured against an archive they
themselves committed before the vanishing (receipts, incl. the HTTP-451 class no census
tracks for news). Kill conditions, named: News Sniffer shipping a global daily metric
surface, or MediaSpin's pipeline going live as a public daily instrument — either would
collapse the daylight to presentation.

**(d) Rework direction.** Build the world chamber as sealed: a daily register of genuine
headline reframings (GDG `PAGE_TITLECHANGE`, versioned triviality filter with the trivial /
running-update / reframing classes, MediaSpin-aware) plus an in-house sampled deletion rate
with confidence interval and disclosed unverifiable classes (403 bot-walls), receipts from
the committed newspool — the daylight is the union: metric + register + receipts daily, at
global scale, from a self-committed archive; every neighbour holds at most one of the three.

## 9. Ghost Fleet (`/ghost-fleet`)

**(a) Claim.** *"Ships that switch off their transponder on purpose to vanish — counted where
they hide."* (`src/i18n/ui.ts:176`). The page's own disclosure: *"'Intentional' is an estimate
by Global Fishing Watch (ML), not proof."* (`src/components/pages/GhostFleetPage.astro:89-90`) —
every case links out "on Global Fishing Watch ↗."

**(b) Nearest neighbors.**
- [Global Fishing Watch — Dark Vessels research](https://globalfishingwatch.org/research-project-dark-vessels) — the peer-reviewed source (*Science Advances*, 2022) of 55,000+ identified intentional AIS-disabling events, with a public interactive map and hotspot analysis. Since Ghost Fleet's own "intentional" flag *is* GFW's ML output, this experiment is a curation layer on GFW's already-public data, not an independent measurement.
- [The Outlaw Ocean Project](https://theoutlawocean.com/podcast/the-dark-fleet) — investigative journalism running the "single vessel dark for months" narrative for years (e.g. the 110-day chase of the *Thunder*), with named vessels, crews, and months of reporting.
- [Windward AI / C4ADS "Oil and Water"](https://c4ads.org/reports/oil-and-water) — commercial/NGO maritime intelligence tracking sanctioned shadow-fleet tankers with named case studies, regularly updated.
- [Skylight](https://support.skylight.global) — nonprofit platform built specifically to detect and display dark-vessel events in near-real time, at operational enforcement scale.

**(c) Draft verdict: REDUNDANT — the most severe case in this audit, because the page's own
text names the dependency.** The "intentional" judgment is GFW's, not the site's, and every
case links out to GFW. Ghost Fleet does not independently detect dark vessels; it re-narrates a
subset of GFW's already-public data as a daily "case of the day." GFW itself already publishes
hotspot maps and press narratives; The Outlaw Ocean Project already does long-form journalism on
individual dark vessels with far more depth than a generated paragraph; Windward/C4ADS/Skylight
already do continuous surfacing at operational quality. Nothing here is measured independently.

**(d) Rework direction.** Since it cannot out-detect GFW/Skylight/Windward, the honest
differentiator would be cross-referencing GFW's dark-vessel events against a second,
independently computed public signal (marine protected area boundaries, sanctions lists,
flag-state deflagging records) to surface a compound finding GFW's own site doesn't state —
turning "GFW re-narrated" into "GFW cross-referenced with X, join logic shown." Absent that,
this is the strongest rework-or-remove candidate in the audit.

## 10. Headroom (`/headroom`)

**(a) Claim.** *"Data-center efficiency near its floor, consumption climbing — what four
hyperscalers disclose, tracked year by year."* (`src/i18n/ui.ts:181`). *"The PUE Google would

## 19. Common Ground (`/trending`)

is occupied on its own. Aggregation is a crowded genre: trend-pulse gathers more sources than
this ledger ever will, Google publishes the canonical single-source surface, and
GoogleTrendArchive has already proved the archival value of one of them. Counting AI crawlers
is a young genre with living examples too. What no neighbour holds is the union: a **daily
convergence** across independent platforms computed by a disclosed rule; a **committed,
recomputable archive** of every day as CC0 data; **machine-first delivery** (JSON, Markdown,
RSS, `llms.txt`) on a public URL; and a **self-counted machine audience** for that very page,

sixty days — in which case the convergence layer alone is a blog post, and the entry is
reworked or archived. **The arcs layer (added 2026-09-02) adds a second daylight on the same
terms.** A watched term gets a mention count per platform — Hacker News, Google News, GitHub,
arXiv, Reddit, plus Wikipedia pageviews where an article exists — over three windows, each
count backed by the titles, links and dates it was counted from, a status assigned by a
published threshold rather than a proprietary score, and every day committed so a reader can
recompute the arc instead of trusting it; beside it, the n-grams the machine noticed and nobody
promoted, published as a list, because in every commercial tracker that candidate pipeline *is*
the product. The neighbours of that half all sell the score and keep the evidence: Exploding
Topics, Glimpse and Treendly are paid dashboards over search interest with no receipts and no
archive, Google Trends gives growth without counts or sources, and TopicRadar hands the
