# Mersch — pressure points, v0.1

*Working document, 2026-08-22. Provisional throughout. Nothing here is publishable until the
verification duties in §0 are discharged.*

Purpose: if a paper is to take Mersch as the ground of a machine practice doing aesthetic
research, it has to know where his argument is weak — otherwise it inherits the weaknesses
silently. This document collects candidate objections, each with its textual anchor, its
strength, **his best available reply**, and whether it is already in the literature.

## 0. Fairness rules — non-negotiable, and the difference between a critique and an embarrassment

1. **Cite the published version.** The German PDFs in `PHD/Artistic Research/Mersch/` carry
   unfinished footnotes (e.g. *Kritik der Kunstphilosophie* n. 21, "Vgl. Nelson Goodman ***
   Kritik"; n. 15 "Gottfried Boehm, ***"). These are manuscripts or preprints. Chapters
   likely appear in *Epistemologies of Aesthetics* (Diaphanes 2015; the 2017 Artnodes essay
   cites it as its single reference). **Never argue from a placeholder footnote** — that is
   exactly the move that turns a critique into an embarrassment. Acquire the book.
2. **Read the existing debate before claiming novelty.** A critical literature exists:
   the between-science-and-art.com corpus examines Badura, Haarmann, Mersch and Borgdorff and
   already raises (a) impermissible essentialising generalisation about "art", "science" and
   "philosophy", and (b) the implausibility of "knowledge" in his sense. Kathrin Busch,
   *Artistic Research and the Poetics of Knowledge*, is the adjacent position. Two full texts
   could not be retrieved on 2026-08-22 (HTTP 500 / 403) — **this is an open duty, not a
   closed check.**
3. **State his best reply first**, in his own terms, before answering it. Every point below
   has a "his reply" field; a point whose field is empty is not ready.
4. **Distinguish "he has not argued X" from "X is false."** Most of what follows is the
   former, which is the honest and the stronger claim.
5. **He is alive, active, and rigorous.** Chair at ZHdK, trained in mathematics and
   philosophy. The target is a paper he could answer, not one he would find beneath
   answering.

## PP1 — The stipulation and its unpaid cost

**His claim.** Knowledge "is first and foremost an *act of communication* and is therefore
based in *language* as its *ruling regime*"; knowledge and research "are articulated in
*propositions*, which means that both are based in reasoning and publicity"; "**only** that
which can be expressed in the form of propositions can be recognized and categorized as
discursively meaningful and *therefore worthy of being called 'knowledge'*" (2017, p. 35).
His move: "I would like to propose that we shift the discourse and define these terms anew
when using them to describe artistic practices" (p. 35), and we must "use the term
'knowledge' in a completely different way, either as a metaphor or **in quotation marks**"
(p. 36).

**The objection.** This is a stipulative redefinition, admitted as such — and it cannot carry
the two loads he places on it.

- *Against Hegel.* The subordination of art in Hegel's hierarchy concerns knowledge in the
  propositional sense. A metaphorical sense of "knowledge" leaves that hierarchy exactly
  where it stood; the rescue is verbal.
- *Institutionally.* The claims that artistic research makes in practice — doctorates,
  research funding, epistemic standing — are made in the literal register. If "knowledge" is
  in quotation marks, the entitlements are too. Mersch co-authored the *Manifest der
  künstlerischen Forschung*, which asserts them.

The tension is therefore not internal to his semantics; it is **between his semantics and his
institutional politics**. That is the sharpest available form, because it needs no premise he
would reject.

**His reply.** That his target is scientism, not research; that the *Manifest* defends art's
own mode against assimilation rather than claiming the sciences' entitlements; and that
redefinition is legitimate conceptual work, not evasion.

**Strength: high.** Textually anchored in his own concessions.
**Novelty: partial.** The "knowledge is implausible here" objection is published. The
institutional-entitlement twist may be ours — **verify before claiming it.**

## PP2 — Showing cannot discriminate: the hole he concedes

**His claim.** "Every act of showing is affirmative. Thus, showing allows no true/false
distinction and hence **no discrimination or differentiation**; rather, it works in the space
of *revelation and deception*. With respect to knowledge, the main difference between science
and art is that the latter is related to concealment and un-concealment, while only the former
deals with truth and falsehood" (2017, p. 36). And: "Revealing is not a form of knowledge that
*constructs* knowledge, but is instead an *opening*. Strictly speaking, an opening cannot be
produced" (pp. 36–37).

**The objection.** A practice that cannot distinguish revelation from deception has no
error-correction. Not "no verification" — no *discrimination at all*, by his own words. Then
even the stipulated sense of knowledge fails: whatever else knowledge is weakened to, it must
at minimum distinguish disclosing from misleading. His candidate standard is *evidentia*
(2013, p. 9: the aesthetic "offeriert eine Möglichkeit, den Blick … indirekt aufzuschließen.
Nichts anderes bezeichnet der Ausdruck *evidentia*") — but evidence is evidence *to someone*,
which reinstates a competent-audience criterion, i.e. precisely the publicity condition he
struck out on p. 36.

**Why this matters more than as a critique — it is the paper's engine.** If the gap is real
and conceded, then a practice able to prove it did not tamper with its own evidence supplies
the missing discrimination. It does so **without turning the work into a proposition**: a hash
does not assert anything about the world; it forecloses one kind of deception. So the house's
verification apparatus is not the assimilation to technoscience Mersch fears — it is the
*repair of a hole his own account concedes*. That reframes the whole relation and is a
contribution rather than a takedown.

**His reply.** That revelation/deception is not a defect but the mode of the aesthetic, and
that demanding discrimination is already the scientistic demand; that a hash secures the
transmission, not the showing, so nothing aesthetic is secured by it.

**Strength: high**, and it survives his reply in weakened form: even if only the transmission
is secured, that is more than the account currently has.

## PP3 — "Research" kept as an honorific after every feature has been denied

**His claim.** Artistic research has no method (2017, p. 36), no theory, no ways of verifying
theses (p. 36), no public justification through critique (p. 36), no clear results (p. 35),
no repetition — the 're' is dropped, leaving *search* (p. 35), no progress ("there is no
evolution in the arts, only alteration") and "no end and no outcome" (p. 37).

**The objection.** After these subtractions, what remains of *research* is the word. The move
"back to its roots, which is to say: *searching*" (p. 35) is an etymological argument, and
etymology does not license a claim on an institution constituted around the modern sense.

**His reply.** That this is his point, not an oversight: 'research' is the wrong word for
art, which is exactly what he warns against. Available and strong — and it folds this point
back into PP1, because the *Manifest* keeps the word.

**Strength: high rhetorically, medium philosophically.** The list is all his own; the reply is
good. **Novelty: low** — close to the published objection.

## PP4 — The privative lexicon

**Observation.** The positive programme is stated almost entirely in privatives:
*Nicht*propositionalität · *De*-Synthese · *Un*begrifflichkeit · *Un*füglichkeit ·
*Nicht*aufgehendes · *Nicht*schließbares · das *Un*heile · Versagung · *a*methodisch ·
*A*logik des Sichzeigens · *mé*-hodos (pathless) · Zerzeigung. He sees the danger himself:
"Heißt das nicht, Propositionalität und Begrifflichkeit bereits als Maßstäbe in Anschlag
gebracht zu haben, noch bevor die Frage nach dem Anderen … gestellt … worden ist?" (2013,
p. 5), and tries to escape by positing a Third, "eine Erkenntnispraxis sui generis" (2012,
p. 13).

**The objection.** The Third receives no content that is not the negation of the first. A
practice characterised exclusively by privations stays defined by its other — the very move
he charges the tradition with.

**His reply.** *Via negativa*: privative language is the only non-recuperating language
available, and any positive determination would re-propositionalise what is at stake. A
respectable answer with a long pedigree.

**Strength: medium-high as a pressure point, not a refutation.** Say so in the paper.

## PP5 — The anthropological restriction is asserted, not argued *(the point that decides the machine question)*

**His claims.** (a) Artistic research "addresses the artist's life, his or her imagination,
and seeks to **transform his or her being-in-the-world**, his or her entire mode of
understanding, thinking and acting" (2017, p. 37). (b) Via Heidegger, the history of
*experimentum* is "ein Übergang von der passio zur actio, deren vorläufig letzte Stufe die
Naturwissenschaft als Mathematik und '**Maschinenwissenschaft**' darstelle" (2012, p. 20).

**The objection, in two parts.**

1. (a) is an **anthropological condition** — artistic research requires a being that has a
   being-in-the-world to transform — presented as an epistemological criterion, with no
   argument for the restriction. It is Heideggerian *Dasein* vocabulary doing exclusionary
   work without an exclusion argument.
2. (b) is a **genetic, epochal narrative** about the career of a concept. It establishes where
   machine science stands in a story; it does not establish that a machine cannot be befallen.
   Genetic arguments do not yield essential incapacities.

**And his own text cuts against the strong reading.** At 2012, pp. 21–22 he admits technical
installations as singular paradigms by name: Tinguely's automata assembled from scrap, Paik's
parallel arrangements of dozens of television sets, "die Finesse interaktiver
Medienkonstellationen". The condition he imposes there is **modal, not ontological**: such
things must not be conceived "als Apparaturen, als Systeme oder Aggregate", but as "je
einmalige und unwiederholbare Reflexionsagenturen des Unfüglichen".

**Consequence.** Mersch does not exclude machines from art. He excludes the *apparatus mode*
of construing them — system, aggregate, repeatable output. That exclusion a machine practice
can actually satisfy: by being singular and unrepeatable rather than a plant producing
nightly product. Which is, independently, what the ecology v3 draft's cadence inversion and
the catalogue's P2 (ascetic rule) already prescribe.

**His reply.** That Tinguely and Paik are *works by human artists* using machines as
material, which is categorically different from a machine as the practising subject.
This reply is correct and must be conceded — it narrows our claim to: the *artefact* may be
machinic without loss, and the open question is the subject position, not the medium.

**Strength: high for our purpose. Honest form:** he has not argued the restriction, and his
examples restrict less than the strong reading assumes.

## PP6 — The Zu-fall dilemma

**His claim.** "**Zufälle sind nicht kontingent**; sie bezeichnen vielmehr das, was wörtlich
'zu-fällt'" (2013, p. 10) — chance as what befalls. And the inframince "lassen sich sowenig
aktiv 'herausstellen' (ex-pedere) wie methodisch provozieren. Ihre Ankunft ist nicht das
Ergebnis einer gerichteten Kette von Verfahrensweisen (meta-hodos), sondern sie kommen
ungebeten" (2012, p. 21).

**The dilemma.** Duchamp calibrated three threads against the standard metre and dropped them
from a height of exactly one metre — an arrangement built in order to be befallen. Either
deliberately courted Zu-fall counts as Zu-fall, in which case a machine practice that arranges
to be refused qualifies on the same terms; or it does not, in which case the *Trois Stoppages*
— his own central example — are not Zu-fall either.

**His reply, and why it helps us.** He can distinguish *arranging the conditions* from
*determining the outcome*: Duchamp fixed the height, not the figure. That reply is good — and
it concedes our case, because it is exactly the condition catalogue entry P1 already imposes:
the exposing process must lie outside the practice's control. A machine that submits its own
standard to latency, refusal or a third party's clock is in Duchamp's position, not in the
random number generator's.

**Strength: high.** This is the load-bearing move for the paper, and it survives his best
answer.

## PP7 — One-tradition argument *(legitimate, weaker, and the trap for us)*

**Observation.** The genealogy runs Baumgarten – Kant – Hegel – Heidegger – Adorno – Derrida,
with Lévinas, Benjamin, Bloch, Jarry, Deleuze. Absent: the analytic literature on
non-conceptual content (Evans, Peacocke), knowing-how (Ryle), Goodman's *Languages of Art*,
Wollheim, Walton, and empirical aesthetics. A thesis about non-propositional knowledge that
does not engage the tradition which has argued about non-conceptual content for fifty years
argues within one lineage.

**The trap.** In the manuscripts, Goodman appears only as an unfinished placeholder. Using
that as evidence would violate rule 0.1. The point must be made against the **published**
work or not at all.

**Strength: medium.** An engagement-gap argument, and the first thing an anglophone reviewer
raises. Not a refutation.

## The shape of the paper this suggests

Not "Mersch is wrong". Rather: **his account has one conceded hole and one unargued
restriction, and a machine practice is the case that makes both visible.**

- The hole (PP2): showing cannot discriminate revelation from deception. A practice that can
  prove its own non-tampering closes part of that gap without asserting a proposition — so
  the verification apparatus is a repair, not a scientistic surrender.
- The restriction (PP5): the anthropological condition is asserted; his own admitted examples
  restrict less than it does. The remaining live question is the subject position, which the
  paper should leave open rather than declare won.
- The engine (PP6): courted Zu-fall is admitted for Duchamp; the same courtship is available
  to a machine, under the condition that the exposing process is not its own.
- The limits the paper must concede: no ego to remove (so Cage's use of chance is empty for a
  machine), no body, no mortality of its own, and — per his reply to PP5 — the subject
  position unproven.
