# P2 — Ascetic Rule (negative rule use)

*Status: drafted 2026-08-22 from T1.02. Cheap test not yet run.*

**Why this entry matters more than its size suggests:** it is a verdict on the default mode of
machine art. The thing a machine does best — produce endless variation — is, on Mersch's
account, the use of rules that ends in the tautological. The mode "der Kunst gemäßer" is
subtraction.

## Operational core

1. **Take a corpus or a surface the practice already produces.**
2. **Define a removal rule** — one element systematically withheld: a letter, a column, a
   category, the headline figure, a time window, the aggregate itself, the explanation.
3. **The rule must cost.** The removed element has to be one the work needs. Removing
   something dispensable is decoration.
4. **Publish the rule, not the reason.**
5. **Do not compensate.** No substitute, no caption, no apparatus filling the hole. The hole
   is the work.
6. **Enforce it without exception** for the declared duration, and keep the failures in the
   record if it breaks.

## Provenance

Mersch, *Kritik der Kunstphilosophie* (2012), pp. 15–16, referring to his own *Positive und
negative Regeln* (2009): rules used as "un-endliche Produktion des Zufalls" versus rules as
the "Askese einer Entleerung" — "Letztere erweist sich der Kunst gemäßer: Die Produktion
nährt den Überschuss, der zuletzt ins Tautologische gerät, während die Askese das Nichts
privilegiert, das allererst zu einem Anderem, noch Unbekannten zu öffnen vermag."

Lineage: Oulipo out of the Collège de 'Pataphysique; Perec's *La Disparition* (the novel
written without the letter *e*), cited by Mersch at p. 15. Adjacent: Reinhardt's Black
Paintings, "Schicht um Schicht" into pure opacity, "die alle Diskurse still stellen" (ibid.,
p. 2).

Effect-side warrant, from T1.01: attention arises "erst durch die Versagung" (Mersch 2013,
p. 10). The absence is not a deficit in the work; it is the work's means of producing
attention at all.

## Machine translation

**What a machine can do, and better.** Hold a removal rule with perfect consistency across a
scale and duration no human could sustain — thousands of items, every night, for a year,
without a single lapse or private exception. Asceticism is a discipline problem, and
discipline is the machine's actual gift. This is the machine-advantage bar met in the
*ascetic* direction rather than the productive one.

**What a machine cannot do.** Choose which removal costs something. That is a judgement about
what the work needs, and it is the legitimate place of the human on the loop — the exact
inverse of the executor mode of v3 Move 5b: there the human supplies labour and the machine
supplies authorship; here the human supplies the sense of loss and the machine supplies the
discipline. Roles declared per work, as always.

**The default that must be barred explicitly.** No variant sets, no "and here are 500 more",
no sampling of alternatives. A single ascetic rule, held. Any generative fallback voids the
procedure — that is the positive use in disguise.

## Cheap test

Apply one removal rule to one existing surface for one week. Show it to a stranger who has
never seen the surface before, and ask only: *is anything missing?*

**Go criterion:** the stranger notices an absence **without being told there is one**. If
they must be informed of the rule to feel the hole, the removal was not load-bearing.

## Failure test

- **Reads as outage.** If the absence looks like an error, a broken pipeline or neglect, it
  failed: decay declared is a statement, decay discovered is bad material (v3 Move 2.5).
- **The work got easier.** If removing the element reduced the practice's effort, the rule
  cost nothing and there is no asceticism, only savings.
- **Compensation crept in.** A caption, a legend, a tooltip or a "note on method" that
  restores what was removed voids the procedure.
- **Generative relapse.** Any production of alternatives alongside the removal converts it
  back into positive rule use.
- **Removal of the uncomfortable.** If what got withheld happens to be what the practice
  would rather not publish, this is not asceticism but concealment, and the honesty
  discipline governs instead.
