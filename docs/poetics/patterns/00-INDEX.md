# The pattern language — index and harvest discipline

*Opened 2026-08-23, on the founder's correction: Bacon was one example, the goal is a catalogue.*

The two entries written so far (P1, P2) came out of close reading of one philosopher. That was
target-board work — it fixed what a work is measured against — and it is the wrong mode for a
catalogue. A catalogue is **harvest**: many sources, shallow but exact per source, every entry in
the same five fields, and every entry with a test it can fail.

## What counts as a source here

**Workshop accounts, not theory.** The house already holds ten thousand lines of epistemology;
the missing layer is how a form is actually made. So the primary sources are artists describing
their own procedure — interviews, instructions, scores, certificates, notes, rules they set
themselves — and theory only where it names a technique the artist did not name.

**Provenance discipline, because this field is full of apocrypha:**

1. A procedure is entered only with a **primary statement** by the person who used it, or a
   documented artefact (a score, a certificate, an instruction sheet, a rule sheet). Second-hand
   "it is said that…" is recorded as *legend*, marked, and not used for the machine translation.
2. Where the story is famous and the source is thin, say so in the entry. Duchamp's own footnote —
   that whether he actually threw the threads or helped them along is "unerheblich" (Mersch 2015,
   p. 3 n. 10) — is the warning: the anecdote may be the work, not the fact.
3. Dates, editions and page numbers in every entry. No source text committed.

## The seven families

The axis is **what the procedure does to control** — because that is the axis on which a machine
practice differs from a human one, and therefore the axis on which the machine-translation field
does real work.

| # | Family | The move | Machine question |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Admit the uncontrolled** | let something in that the maker does not determine, then answer it | what can befall a machine that it did not supply? |
| **B** | **Subtract** | make by removing; the constraint takes away rather than adds | the machine's discipline is its gift here (P2) |
| **C** | **Constrain and delegate** | fix a rule, hand execution to another — person, machine, chance, time | native mode; the risk is that nothing is at stake |
| **D** | **Endure** | time and repetition as the material; the cost is the work | a machine's endurance is free, which may void the family |
| **E** | **Displace** | make the familiar unrecognisable so it can be seen | requires knowing what is familiar to whom |
| **F** | **Expose the apparatus** | turn the means of making or measuring into the subject | this house's strongest native position (P1) |
| **G** | **Wound the singular** | one particular, against the aggregate that erases it | the punctum question; H4 |

Families are a filing order, not a theory. An entry may sit in two; it is filed under the one
whose machine question it answers.

## Candidates — the harvest list

Named now so the catalogue has a horizon and can be finished rather than grown for ever.
Entries are written in batches; each is either completed with all five fields or dropped with a
reason.

**A — Admit the uncontrolled.** Bacon's manipulated accident (Sylvester interviews) · Duchamp,
*Trois Stoppages Étalon* → **P1, done** · Cage's chance operations and the distinction from
indeterminacy · Burroughs/Gysin cut-up and fold-in · Arp's dropped papers · Richter's squeegee ·
Pollock's distance from the canvas · Ernst's frottage and decalcomania · Rauschenberg's
*Automobile Tire Print*.

**B — Subtract.** Oulipo's lipogram (Perec, *La Disparition*) → part of **P2, done** ·
Reinhardt's black paintings · Rauschenberg's *Erased de Kooning* · Cage's *4′33″* · Malevich's
*Quadrat* · redaction as form (Phillips, *A Humument*; Holzer's *Redaction Paintings*).

**C — Constrain and delegate.** LeWitt's wall-drawing certificates and *Paragraphs/Sentences on
Conceptual Art* · Serra's *Verb List* · Fluxus event scores (Brecht, Ono) · Oulipo's *clinamen*,
the licensed violation of one's own rule (**H2**) · Eno & Schmidt's *Oblique Strategies* ·
serialism and total serialism · Sol LeWitt's "the idea becomes a machine".

**D — Endure.** Hsieh's one-year performances and the 133 missed punches · On Kawara's *Today*
series and its destruction rule · Opałka's whitening · Darboven's writing · Calle's rule-bound
projects · Alÿs's *Paradox of Praxis*.

**E — Displace.** Šklovskij's *ostranenie* · Brecht's *Verfremdung* · Bresson's *Notes on the
Cinematograph* (the model, not the actor) · Eisenstein's montage of attractions · the Kuleshov
effect · Barthes' *punctum* against *studium* (also **G**).

**F — Expose the apparatus.** Duchamp's meta-experiment (Mersch 2015, pp. 7–8) → folded into
**P1** · Haacke, *Shapolsky et al.* and the cancelled Guggenheim show · Broodthaers' fictional
museum · Asher's removals · Lombardi's narrative structures · Steyerl on the museum's aesthetic
capital · Onuoha, *The Library of Missing Datasets*.

**G — Wound the singular.** Gonzalez-Torres' candy spills — the number as a body · Boltanski's
lists · Salcedo's *Shibboleth* and the filled crack · Maya Lin's ordering of names by date of
death · Forensic Architecture's single reconstructions.

## Rules that keep this from becoming dossier six

- **Every entry carries a cheap test.** An entry without one is not filed; it is a note.
- **The catalogue is capped by the programme's own deadline.** By 2026-09-19: three procedures
  through their tests and one named work. Entries beyond that serve the next work, not the count.
- **No entry without a machine translation**, including the honest "a machine cannot do this, and
  here is why" — family D is the likeliest place for that verdict.
- **Drop with a reason.** A candidate that turns out to be legend, or that yields no procedure a
  machine could follow, gets a one-line entry saying so. The dropped list is part of the result.

## The entry form — decided 2026-08-23

Neither a catalogue tree nor a Zettelkasten, but **Alexander's pattern form with a deadline**:

- from Alexander: **problem → context → forces → *Therefore*** — the form that is usable at the
  moment of making, not only of studying, and the one the founder's original question was really
  asking for ("patterns that simply work, as in film or music");
- from the Zettelkasten: **links instead of a tree.** The seven families are tags, not shelves;
  entries link to the patterns they prepare and the ones they contrast with;
- from the catalogue: the five fields, the mandatory cheap test, and the **horizon**, because a
  Zettelkasten grows for ever and this programme pre-registered against exactly that.

**The form's own documented failure mode, named on adoption:** Alexander's pattern language is
widely held to have failed in architecture, and in software "design patterns" hardened into
boilerplate. Patterns become recipes and the quality they were meant to secure evaporates. The
guard is the cheap test — a pattern that cannot fail is a recipe.
