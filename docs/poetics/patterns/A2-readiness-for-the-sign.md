# A2 — Readiness for the Sign

*Family A, admit the uncontrolled. Drafted 2026-08-23. Pairs with — and contradicts — A1.*

---

## The problem

**A practice that decides in advance what it is looking for finds only what it already had. But
the encounter that would break that cannot be arranged: provoking it makes it yours again.**

## Context

Arises before a work exists, at the point where a practice chooses its material. It is the
pattern that governs whether a practice can be surprised at all. Where A1 works inside the making
of a single piece, A2 works on the standing condition of the practice: what it is exposed to,
and whether it is in a state to be struck.

## The forces

- **Goodwill is the enemy.** Proust "attacks what is most essential in a classical philosophy of
  the rationalist type: the presuppositions of this philosophy. The philosopher readily
  presupposes that the mind as mind, the thinker as thinker, wants the truth, loves or desires
  the truth, naturally seeks the truth. **He assumes in advance the goodwill of thinking**"
  (Deleuze, *Proust and Signs*, tr. Howard, Minnesota 2000, p. 94). And: "truths remain arbitrary
  and abstract so long as they are based on the goodwill of thinking. **Only the conventional is
  explicit**" (p. 95).
- **Truth is not willed.** "the truth is not revealed, **it is betrayed**; it is not communicated,
  it is interpreted; **it is not willed, it is involuntary**" (p. 95). "Thought is nothing without
  something that forces and does violence to it. More important than thought is '**what leads to
  thought**'" (p. 95). The leitmotif of *Time Regained* is the word **force**: "impressions that
  force us to look, encounters that force us to interpret, expressions that force us to think."
- **Not choosing is the mark of authenticity.** Proust, quoted at p. 96: the impressions' "first
  character was that **I was not free to choose them**, that they were given to me as they were.
  And I felt that **this must be the mark of their authenticity. I had not gone looking** for the
  two cobblestones of the courtyard where I had stumbled. But precisely the **fortuitous,
  inevitable** way in which the sensation had been **encountered** governed the truth of the
  past."
- **And provoking it is the failure mode, named.** "the Socratic demon, **irony**, consists in
  **anticipating the encounters**. In Socrates, the intelligence still comes before the
  encounters; **it provokes them, it instigates and organizes them**. Proust's humor is of another
  nature… **One must be endowed for the signs, ready to encounter them, one must open oneself to
  their violence. The intelligence always comes after**" (p. 101).

## Therefore

1. **Stop assuming your own goodwill.** Write down what the practice currently wants to find. That
   list is the conventional, and it is what will be found if nothing intervenes. It is not the
   material; it is the obstacle.
2. **Do not arrange the encounter.** Instigating it is the Socratic move and it returns the
   encounter to your intelligence, which "always comes before" and therefore finds only the
   possible. Build no traps.
3. **Build readiness instead of provocation.** "One must be **endowed** for the signs, **ready** to
   encounter them, one must **open oneself** to their violence" (p. 101). Readiness is standing
   exposure with no target: being reachable by what you did not ask for, in more places than you
   are looking.
4. **Let the intelligence come after.** "To think is always to interpret — to explicate, to
   develop, to decipher, to translate a sign" (p. 97). The interpretation is the work; it may not
   precede its occasion.
5. **Take the constraint as the criterion.** What you were not free to choose is the mark of
   authenticity (p. 96). Where a practice can show it did not select its material, that showing is
   evidence, not decoration.

The double symbol of the hieroglyph, and the test of the whole pattern: "**the accident of the
encounter and the necessity of thought: 'fortuitous and inevitable'**" (p. 102). Both, or neither.

## Provenance

Deleuze, *Proust et les signes* (1964; complete text 2000, tr. Richard Howard), Conclusion to
Part I, "The Image of Thought", pp. 94–102, with pp. 91–93 on apprenticeship. Primary text read.
Deleuze quotes Proust from the Pléiade edition; those quotations are at one remove and marked as
such in the entry.

Note the 1972 preface (p. xi), which is why this book belongs in this catalogue at all: the work
of art "not only interprets and not only emits signs to be interpreted; **it produces them, by
determinable procedures**. Proust himself conceives his work as **an apparatus or a machine**
capable of functioning effectively, **producing signs of different orders**, which will have an
effect on the reader." Part II is titled *The Literary Machine*; chapters 8–12 are unread and are
the next harvest.

## Machine translation

**What a machine can do, and uniquely.** Step 5 is the one place where a machine practice has an
advantage no human has. Proust's criterion of authenticity is negative — *I was not free to
choose them* — and a human can only assert it. A practice that commits its inputs with their
provenance can **show** that a given material was not selected by it: that the arrival order was
the source's, that the timestamp was not chosen, that the refusal came from outside. The mark of
authenticity becomes checkable. That is not a metaphor for passio; it is the one form of it that
survives verification.

**And the barrier is lower than it looked.** "**Voluntary and involuntary do not designate
different faculties, but rather a different exercise of the same faculties**" (p. 99). The
involuntary is not an endowment a machine lacks; it is a *mode of exercise*. Each faculty, in its
involuntary form, "discovers and attains its own limit… It **ceases to be interchangeable**"
(p. 99). What a machine would have to demonstrate is not that it possesses a special faculty, but
that one of its ordinary operations was exercised involuntarily — under constraint it did not set.

**What is genuinely open.** Step 3, readiness, has no obvious machine form. Standing exposure is
easy to fake as breadth of monitoring — and monitoring is targeted, which is provocation with
extra steps. Whether a machine practice can be *endowed for signs* rather than merely subscribed
to feeds is the honest unsolved question of this entry, and it should be carried as such rather
than answered by assertion.

## The tension with A1 — and why both are kept

Same author, seventeen years apart, and they do not agree.

| | **A1** (Bacon, 1981) | **A2** (Proust, 1964) |
|---|---|---|
| The accident | **is made** — free marks thrown by the hand | **cannot be made** — provoking it is the Socratic error |
| The skill | in the **answer**: manipulated chance, utilised accident | in the **readiness**: endowment, openness, intelligence after |
| Failure | receiving without answering | anticipating, instigating, organising the encounter |

They are not reconcilable by choosing the "later" Deleuze. Both are procedures, and a practice
must know which one it is running: **A1 asks you to make the accident and answer it; A2 forbids
making it and asks you to be reachable.**

The house's own case sits between them and gets a different verdict from each. *Twenty-Five*
takes material it did not choose (A2 satisfied, step 5 provably so) and does not answer it (A1
failed, beat 4 missing). That is a coherent reading, and it says the work is a good A2 and a poor
A1 — which is more useful than a single verdict would have been.

## Cheap test

Write the list from step 1: what does this practice currently want to find? Then check the last
three things it made against the list.

**Go criterion:** at least one of the three came from something not on the list. If all three
were on it, the practice has been exercising its faculties voluntarily throughout, and nothing has
forced it to think.

## Failure test

- **Provocation dressed as readiness.** A new feed, a new monitor, a new alert is a target. If the
  practice knows what it is watching for, it is Socratic.
- **Asserted involuntariness.** Claiming material was not chosen, without the provenance that
  shows it, fails step 5 — and this is the failure a machine practice has least excuse for.
- **Intelligence first.** If the interpretation existed before the occasion, the occasion was an
  illustration.
- **Fortuitous without inevitable.** An encounter that forces nothing is an anecdote; a necessity
  without an accident is a plan (p. 102).
