# W1 — *Twenty-Five* (design, 2026-08-23)

**Status: design proposal. Not commissioned, nothing built.** Working title; the wording gate
applies. First work candidate of the Poetics programme, derived from catalogue entry P1 (the
standard undone) with P2's ascetic condition folded in.

---

## 1. The standard

**Twenty-five battle-related deaths in a calendar year.** Below that number, an armed conflict is
not an armed conflict — it does not enter the dataset, the count of the world's wars, the
tables that governments, journalists and researchers work from.

This is the Urmeter of the work: a public measure, whose authority is real, which the practice
did not make and does not own. Duchamp took the standard metre, not his studio ruler; that
distinction is the whole difference between this and the six proposals of 2026-08-22, which
took the house's own logs as their subject.

> **Verification duty before any build.** The exact threshold, its wording, its unit of account
> (calendar year), and the definition of "battle-related" must be read out of the current UCDP
> codebook and cited to the page, not quoted from memory. If the definition has been revised,
> the revision is material to the work and belongs in it. The same duty applies to any second
> standard used for comparison (EM-DAT's ten deaths / hundred affected; GDACS alert levels;
> IPC phase thresholds).

## 2. The fall

The threshold is exact. The quantity it measures is not.

Death tolls are revised — upward, downward, months later, by sources that disagree with each
other and with themselves. So the same event crosses the line more than once, in both
directions, at times nobody chooses. An event is not a conflict at twenty-four, is one at
twenty-six, and was neither for the eleven weeks in between during which the counting had not
finished.

**That is the fall, and the practice does not supply it.** Not a random number, not a statistical
variation, not a simulated perturbation — the world's own revising, which nobody in this house
controls. It is *Zu-fall* in the only sense available to a machine practice: what befalls.
(Mersch, 2013, p. 10; and his own footnote that the purity of Duchamp's chance is "unerheblich",
2015, p. 3 n. 10.)

## 3. The form

**Three measuring sticks.** One per event. Three, and never a fourth to improve the result.

Each is a rule whose edge is not straight: its curvature is the revision history of one event's
death toll against the threshold — the line bending above and below twenty-five as the counting
changed hands and dates. The rule is mute. It measures nothing usable. It is a measure with a
curved edge, and it is the only thing the work asserts.

Beside them, the dossier — and the dossier is not documentation, it is the second half of the
work (Mersch 2015, pp. 7–8: what Duchamp staged was "das Penible jeder Versuchsanordnung, die
Genauigkeit des Settings … wie gleichermaßen die säuberliche Aufzeichnung, Archivierung und
Veröffentlichung der Wurfresultate"). Every retrieval timestamped to the second; every source
quoted with its own revision date; every query, every hash, every byte count; the calibration
stated to a precision the object cannot bear. **The apparatus is overdone until its instituting
pathos shows** — a laboratory of absolute rigour, measuring a number the world cannot state to
within a hundred.

That gap — the precision of the apparatus against the imprecision of the dead — is the wound.
Nothing in the work says so.

## 4. What the work refuses

- **It does not update.** Fixed at three events, on a stated date, and never again. If a toll is
  revised the day after publication, the rule stays as it was and the revision is not shown.
  The work is an attempt without return (Mersch 2013, p. 7); a live instrument is the opposite
  of one.
- **It does not correct.** Where sources contradict, both stay. No reconciliation, no best
  estimate, no "most reliable source". Averaging is the identity procedure the work exists
  against.
- **It names no headline figure.** There is no "X events were miscounted". The aggregate is what
  erases the singular; supplying one would undo the work in its own last line.
- **It does not explain itself.** No wall text. A curved measuring rule beside a threshold and a
  dossier of absurd exactitude either shows what it shows or fails.

## 5. The debtor

The people below the line — those whose deaths did not reach twenty-five, and those whose deaths
reached it late. Named where the sources name them; counted where they do not. The work owes
them the only thing it can give: that the arbitrariness of the line be visible as a shape rather
than argued as a claim.

## 6. The v3 work bar, checked

| # | Requirement | This work |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Debtor** | Those below the threshold, and those admitted late. Named with the work. |
| 2 | **A cut moment** | Not "thresholds are arbitrary" — one slice: the interval in which one named event was not a conflict. Three such intervals, no more. |
| 3 | **Mapping motivated to necessity** | The curvature *is* the revision history; it could not be otherwise. A bar chart of the same data would be availability, not necessity. |
| 4 | **No wall text** | §4, last point. Tested by the stranger probe (§8). |
| 5 | **Iteration, not imitation** | Three fixed instances, dated, never updated. Its uniqueness mode is declared at creation: mortal, and killed by its own refusal to track. |
| 6 | **Documented cost** | The failed retrievals, the sources that would not answer, the tolls that could not be established stay in the dossier. Flawless execution proves nothing. |
| 7 | **Machine-advantage bar** | Only a machine holds the full revision history of many sources at many timestamps *and* can prove it did not tamper with any of them. A human could assemble one such history; not three, not to the second, not verifiably. |

## 7. Mersch's nine criteria (T1.01 §4), checked honestly

| # | Criterion | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Attempt without return | **Yes** — §4, first point. The refusal to update is the irreversibility. |
| 2 | Initiates resistance | **Yes** — the contradictions between sources are kept, not resolved. |
| 3 | Undecidability held open | **Yes** — whether the event was a conflict is left undecided, because it was. |
| 4 | Shows rather than says | **Intended, untested.** This is the criterion the work can most easily fail, and the stranger probe is the only judge. |
| 5 | Weight to the singular | **Yes** — three named events, no aggregate. |
| 6 | Knowledge in the *Zwischenraum* | **Yes** — the work is made of the gap between an exact measure and an inexact quantity. |
| 7 | Works through *Versagung* | **Yes** — the rule refuses to measure; that refusal is what produces attention. |
| 8 | Chance as *Zu-fall* | **Yes** — §2. Nothing is randomised; the revising befalls the work. |
| 9 | Produces a *Sprung* | **Unknown.** Not decidable by design. |

And the criterion from the *Manifest* (p. 18): **intervention**. This work does intervene — it
touches the datasets that governments and press use, and it does so in the register those
datasets claim for themselves. Whether it reaches anyone is the open question, and it is the
right open question.

## 8. Cheap tests, before anything is built

1. **The stranger probe.** Two people who have read nothing are shown the three rules and the
   threshold. Ask what struck them (the non-propositional variant, v3 Move 4b). **Go criterion:**
   at least one names the mismatch between the exactness of the apparatus and the vagueness of
   the count, without being told there is one.
2. **The screenshot test.** Take a screenshot. What does it destroy? Write the answer down before
   building. *Current honest answer: little.* This may be a print or a score work rather than a
   screen work — the fifth line is screen **and hybrid** works, and forcing it onto a screen
   would be availability, not necessity. Decide after the probe, not before.
3. **The data probe.** Can three events be found whose tolls actually crossed the threshold in
   both directions, with citable revision histories? **If not, the work does not exist.** This is
   the gate: one day of work, and it fails cleanly.
4. **Pre-registration.** The three events, the sources, the retrieval dates and the mapping are
   fixed and hashed *before* the first rule is drawn — not as a scientific credential (Mersch,
   2012, p. 20: works are their own hypothesis) but so that the choice of events cannot be
   tuned to the result afterwards.

## 9. Failure tests

- **Simulable fall.** If the revision history could have been predicted or modelled by the
  practice, it was variance and not *Zu-fall*.
- **The rule reads as a chart.** If the curved edge is taken for a data visualisation, the work
  has landed in the second misunderstanding of the *Manifest* (p. 17, where "Datenvisualisierung"
  is named among the things that block the view). This is the sharpest risk the work carries.
- **Discreet apparatus.** If the dossier reads as method documentation rather than as staged
  rigour, P1's step 6 was skipped.
- **Instrument creep.** If anyone can use the result to measure anything about the world, the
  work has become an instrument and inherits an instrument's obligations instead of the work bar.
- **Moralising.** If the work tells the reader what to feel about the line, it has said what it
  should have shown.

## 10. What must be decided by a human, and why

Which three events. That is not a data question; it is the judgement about which loss is worth
making a rule out of, and P2's machine translation already places exactly this kind of choice
with the human on the loop — the machine supplies the discipline, the human supplies the sense
of what the removal costs. Roles declared with the work, per v3 Move 5b.
