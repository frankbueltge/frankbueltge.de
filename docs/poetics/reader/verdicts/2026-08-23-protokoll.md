# Protocol — read 2026-08-23

## Seen, before reading
A dark dashboard. A date in display type, "PROTOCOL · DATA" above it, a subtitle about "measured figures of the agenda", then thirteen identical cards: a label (Atmosphere, Sea ice, Ocean, Fire, Seismicity, Attendance, Displacement, Food, Price of money, Energy, Conflict, Attention), a large number with a unit, an arrow "vs year ago", a small sparkline, a one-sentence caption and a source line with an "as of" date. One card says "Fire: 0 detections" under a sparkline that plainly has readings. One card is a person's name with a view count. Below the grid a box titled "Losses" reports that nothing happened this week and that this was "entered into the record"; then a collapsed disclosure "Official wording", then a paragraph comparing the page to Our World in Data and Carbon Brief, then a footer line that calls the page "an instrument". A stranger takes this for a personal planetary-indicators dashboard, a one-man OWID, with one small mono line of bureaucratic voice ("Resolution: adjourned") that reads as a wink. What it is *about*, beyond "numbers about the world, nightly", cannot be read off the surface; the minutes it claims to be are folded shut behind a triangle.

## Verdict: SAYS
A dashboard that tells you in one mono line that it is minutes, keeps the minutes themselves under a collapsed disclosure, and then confesses in its own footer that it is an instrument — the conceit is the caption, the cards are the work, and the cards are a dashboard.

## Thesis 12, answered (Manifest, p. 51)
- **How it happens:** A nightly pipeline renders thirteen open feeds through one card template and one mono summary line; the minutes proper are collapsed by default.
- **What it stakes:** Nothing that cannot be re-run tomorrow; the only risk taken is a stale source, and the page treats that as a footnote.
- **What it brings to appearance:** Trend lines and big numbers — a dashboard; the administrative voice appears only as text.
- **How it thematises itself:** Twice and contradictorily — "an instrument" in the footer, "a literary-procedural device" in the paragraph above it.
- **Radicality of the stake:** Low; no taboo touched, no schranke brushed; the bureaucratic tone is a wink, not an unconditionality.

## Misunderstandings (Manifest, pp. 13–17)
- Outputdenken (p. 13): "method and sources disclosed, source code open", "Git is the archive" offered in the footer as the page's credential.
- Research display (p. 17): thirteen data visualisations bespickt with comments, literally; the "artistic unrest" is one sentence under a triangle.
- Niche occupation (p. 17): a dashboard with a bureaucratic caption is a miniature shift in the fabric of the hundred-times-shown dashboard.

## Cover the text
Thirteen cards with large numbers, arrows, sparklines, and a grey box. It still shows something: that CO₂ climbs, Arctic ice falls, a conflict count oscillates weekly, one sparkline spikes twice. It shows a dashboard. Nothing of minutes, session, agenda, resolution, or record survives; the claim the house makes for the work is entirely sentence.

## One move
Open "Official wording" and delete everything above it: let the minutes be the page — one bureaucratic paragraph per item, "Attendance: 8,310,838,000", the name of the day's most-viewed person as an agenda item, the fire feed's 0 left standing as what was entered — and drop the sparklines; the template has to be seen swallowing the world, not folded under a triangle.
