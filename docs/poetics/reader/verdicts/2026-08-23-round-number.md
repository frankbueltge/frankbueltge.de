## Seen, before reading
A dark page titled "Round Numbers", subtitled as a test that claims to spot faked numbers and how often it is wrong, dated today. A box labelled "THE DEFENDANT OF THE DAY" holds a series called "Synthetic — rounded (tampered)", tagged synthetic control, n = 4,330, and the line "The Benford verdict: acceptable" — the accused, declared tampered in its own name, walks free. Beneath it a bar chart of the digits 1 to 9 falling from tall to short, each bar with a thin cap sitting almost exactly on its top. Then a huge "0%": how often the test calls clean data of this size suspicious — followed by a caption that calls the defendant "a large, clean sample", although the label two inches above says tampered. Further down, four other series with false-positive percentages (0, 51, 38, 45), a reading list of three papers that concedes the insight is fifteen years old, a disclaimer that "this instrument indicts the tool", and a footer line: "An instrument — method and sources disclosed, source code open." I would take it for a daily statistics dashboard about Benford's law wearing a courtroom costume, which on this particular day acquits a rigged witness and calls it clean.

## Verdict: INSTRUMENT
A false-positive meter for Benford's law that put on a judge's wig, named the day's data "the defendant", and on the day of reading acquitted the tampered series, printed 0 % where the shelf promised "about half", and captioned the tampered defendant "clean" — an instrument whose costume slipped, and it should take the costume off.

## Thesis 12, answered (Manifest, p. 51)
- **How it happens:** a nightly script scores five series against a first-digit expectation and prints a false-positive rate.
- **What it stakes:** nothing of its own; the Benford test's reputation, which the literature on its own reading list spent fifteen years ago.
- **What it brings to appearance:** a number (today 0 %) and a bar chart shown a hundred times before.
- **How it thematises itself:** twice as "an instrument", once as a trial — and the trial wording is the part that misfires.
- **Radicality of the stake:** none; no taboo touched, the disclaimer makes sure of it.

## Misunderstandings (Manifest, pp. 13–17)
- Outputdenken: "method and sources disclosed, source code open", "a standalone, queryable artifact" — offered as the credential (p. 13).
- Research display: a data visualisation with commentary, a reading list and a method sheet, and nothing to see without them (p. 17).
- Niche occupation: by its own admission — the insight is "15 years old", what stays open is running the known curve "against contemporary administrative series nobody has tested this way" (p. 17).

## Cover the text
Nine falling bars with thin caps, a large "0%", four small grey percentages. The falling bars are the Benford figure from every textbook; nothing shows that a stranger could name, and even today's contradiction — tampered, yet "clean" — exists only in the sentences.
