# Correction — read 2026-08-23

## Seen, before reading
A dark page, a one-word title, and a sentence addressed to "you": a jobs number was inflated and has been quietly cut. Beneath it a very large "1.25M", then a row of red bars hanging below a line like icicles, nearly all pointing down, two small ones at the far right pointing up. Then "92%", a short paragraph, a capitalised caption "THERE IS NO FINAL NUMBER", a section called "NEAREST NEIGHBORS" naming two Federal Reserve tools and conceding that one of them does almost the same thing, a source line, a "Method sheet" link, and a footer sentence: "An instrument — method and sources disclosed, source code open." A stranger takes it for a data-journalism explainer: US employment figures get revised downward, here is by how much. What it is about is legible in a second; what it is — beyond a chart with a thesis attached — is not.

## Verdict: INSTRUMENT
A revision gauge that says "instrument" in its footer and "you" in its headline — the bars are honest, the word "quietly" is borrowed from a polemic the page has not written, and the one thing it says an official tool cannot do is the one thing it has not done.

## Thesis 12, answered (Manifest, p. 51)
- **How it happens:** two vintages subtracted, one bar per month, two headline numbers, a caption.
- **What it stakes:** nothing of the maker's; the revisions are the agency's risk, the page only carries them.
- **What it brings to appearance:** that first reports run high — a fact published by the revisers themselves, since 1990.
- **How it thematises itself:** "An instrument", in the footer, below a headline that speaks in a work's voice.
- **Radicality of the stake:** none; no taboo touched, no barrier, a known correction re-told.

## Misunderstandings (Manifest, pp. 13–17)
- Outputdenken (p. 13): "method and sources disclosed, source code open" offered as the page's closing credential — the house's honesty, presented as the page's merit.
- Research display (p. 17): a "Nearest Neighbors" section on the work itself, conceding the SF Fed does "almost exactly what this page does" — the display explaining why it is not the display.
- Niche (p. 17): a first-vs-latest chart the Philadelphia and San Francisco Feds have run for decades, re-set on a dark ground with a second-person headline.

## Cover the text
A row of red bars hanging from a line, deepening toward the middle, two stubs at the right pointing up. It shows a series that is mostly negative, then less so. Not jobs, not inflation, not "you", not quiet. Something is still shown — a drop — but the drop needs every sentence to become a correction.

## One move
None: it is an instrument, and it should say so plainly — at the top, in place of "the jobs number you reacted to"; the "consequences layer" it says it "builds toward" would be the work, and until it exists this is the gauge.
