# Bycatch — read 2026-08-23

## Seen, before reading
A dark page with a one-word title, "Bycatch", and a subtitle about what reading a scholarly article "catches along the way". Beneath it a boxed FAQ ("What does this measure?") explaining that a machine opens journal pages and watches whether the article's DOI is sent to third parties before consent. Then a headline in the manner of a report: 40 of 50 publisher pages refuse the measurement, with a list of five publishers, four of them "10/10 blocked". Further down: a two-bar chart (7 vs 0), eleven near-identical indented rows reading "ReadCube · plain text · query string / Evidence", a table of company names with counts, a line chart that is mostly empty with the caption "Gaps stay gaps", a list of academic neighbours, and a footer that says "An instrument — method and sources disclosed". A stranger takes it for a privacy audit dashboard maintained by one person: a weekly tracker census of academic publishers. What it is about is clear from the surface; the title is the only thing on the page that is not a report.

## Verdict: INSTRUMENT
A census that says so at the bottom in small type and, at the top in large type, borrows a work's best line — "the refusal is the finding" — then files the refusal as a row count instead of letting forty shut doors appear.

## Thesis 12, answered (Manifest, p. 51)
- **How it happens:** A headless browser opens fifty pages weekly, clicks nothing, and records where the DOI goes; a human browser repeats it from a flat once.
- **What it stakes:** Compute minutes and a claim about five publishers; nothing of the maker's, nothing irreversible.
- **What it brings to appearance:** That the industry of measuring readers refuses to be measured — and then only as the sentence that says so.
- **How it thematises itself:** Explicitly and in prose ("the apparatus is the differentiator, not the finding"; "An instrument"), never in its form.
- **Radicality of the stake:** Low; it touches no taboo the cited neighbours did not touch in 2018–2020, and admits as much (Manifest, p. 17).

## Misunderstandings (Manifest, pp. 13–17)
- Outputdenken (p. 13): "tamper-proof even when hashed", collapsible "Evidence", "This page loads zero third-party requests" are offered as credentials; for an instrument that is its trade, not a flaw — it becomes the misunderstanding only where the page reaches for a work's verdict.
- Research display (p. 17): a datavisualisation census with commentary is exactly the description; permitted here because the page does not claim artistic unrest.
- Niche (p. 17): the page states in its own words that the finding is known since 2018–2020 and only the apparatus is new — a "Miniaturverschiebung" confessed, which for an instrument is honest and for a work would be fatal.

## Cover the text
Left: one word, "Bycatch"; a bar of 7 against a bar of 0; five rows with right-aligned monospace numbers; eleven indented rows of identical shape; a table of seven names with small integers; a near-empty line chart between two dates. The eleven identical rows and the empty chart faintly show repetition and absence — that is the closest the page comes to showing — but none of it says what was refused, who refused it, or why a reader should care; the title is the only thing that still points at a catch.

## One move
None: it is an instrument, and it already says so — the one correction is to stop the headline from wearing a work's line: either delete "it is the finding" or let the forty refusals stand as forty blank frames with nothing written in them.
