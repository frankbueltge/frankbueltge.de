# Society — read 2026-08-23

## Seen, before reading
A dark page headed "EXPERIMENT · STAGE 6 — ROOM · STARTED 2026-08-05", a title "Society", a subtitle "a mind of mindless parts", a Minsky epigraph, and then four paragraphs of prose before a single pixel moves. Below the fold, two panels: a schematic of five ochre blocks on a line with one block lifted, a crosshair cursor, labels "the tower site" and "the arch site"; beside it a near-empty box with one sentence, "A morning. The society wakes wanting to play." Further down, a wiring diagram of coloured dots in labelled bands (appetites, builders, body, senses, B-brain) with faint lines, a card showing one agent as five lines of pseudocode and a "silence this agent" button, a thirty-cell grid of book chapters mostly marked "asleep", and then a long tail: "Under proof", "What the attachment does not show", "Where this stands", "Nearest neighbors", a growth record with pull-request numbers and commit hashes. A stranger would take it for a pedagogical browser demo of Minsky's book with a lab notebook stapled underneath — a cognitive-architecture diagram that builds a toy tower. From the first screen alone you cannot see the work; you are told about it.

## Verdict: SAYS
Minsky performed, then annotated until the performance is the footnote: the one thing here that would show — a tower rising under no hand that can build, and never rising again once a dot goes dark — is told to you in paragraph four, proven in a test section, and only then allowed to happen.

## Thesis 12, answered (Manifest, p. 51)
- **How it happens:** Twenty-seven rules run deterministically in a browser; a pointer is read as motion or sign; a button removes one rule; prose narrates before, during and after.
- **What it stakes:** Nothing that cannot be reloaded — the page says so itself; what is staked is a claim about minds, not a loss.
- **What it brings to appearance:** A tower built where no single rule can build, and a hand that goes once to the wrong place — a small, real appearance of a whole exceeding its parts.
- **How it thematises itself:** As verified: tests, corrected citations, hashes, prior-art concessions; the B-brain and the single-voiced scribe are the only self-thematisation in the work's own medium.
- **Radicality of the stake:** Low — the taboo it touches (kill a part of a mind, mourn it) is undone at reload and pre-announced as a test; the page's own section names the mercy as a lie and keeps it.

## Misunderstandings (Manifest, pp. 13–17)
- **Outputdenken** (p. 13): "Under proof", "claims that fail a build when they stop being true", PR numbers and commit hashes on the public page — verifiability offered as the work's credential.
- **Research display** (p. 17): a data-visualisation of the roster, a nearest-neighbour section, a prior-art file, a growth record — roughly nine hundred words stand between the stranger and the first movement.
- **Niche occupation** (p. 17): the page concedes agent societies, ablation and the interactive book as borrowed and claims to "win by composition, not mechanism" — which is the definition of a shift in an already-shown fabric; what survives is the book actually running.

## Cover the text
What remains: five ochre blocks on a line, a crosshair, a tower that rises and sometimes falls; coloured dots in bands with lines that light; a grid of thirty cells, most dim; one button. The tower rising under no visible builder still shows something, and a dot going dark followed by the tower never rising again still shows loss — that much survives. Gone with the sentences: that this is a mind, that the dots are Minsky, that the detour is memory, that the dimming is a dream, and the whole of the mourning, which is a line of text. The block world shows; the mind is said.

## One move
Remove the reload. Let a silenced agent stay silent for everyone who comes after you that day, let the morning you wounded stay wounded until the date changes, and delete every sentence above the block world — then the room is the work and this page is its archive.
