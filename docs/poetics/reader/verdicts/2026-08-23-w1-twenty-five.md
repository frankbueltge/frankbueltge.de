# Twenty-Five (W1, score work, not published) — read 2026-08-23

## Seen, before reading
A black page, a title, and a German preamble that tells me to look at the rulers before I read — a work that opens by issuing viewing instructions. One English sentence in a code box: "Twenty-five battle-related deaths in a calendar year." Below it, three pairs of white bars with black tick marks, the ticks irregular — a dense cluster on the left, a lone mark in the middle, one at the far right — and in each pair the second bar carries the same marks plus one or two more. Tiny labels: 24.1, 25.1. I would take them for rulers that have lost most of their graduations, or for a before/after of a barcode; I would guess, from the sentence, that the marks are years and that something crossed a line. What was admitted, where, and why, I could not say from the surface; the page continues for four more screens to tell me.

## Verdict: SAYS
Six rules that show a standard re-ruled without explanation, bolted to a page that the score itself appoints as the speaking half — the work concedes criterion 4 in its own constitution and then proves the concession.

## Thesis 12, answered (Manifest, p. 51)
- **How it happens:** four pinned CSV versions are diffed and the year columns of three dyads drawn as ticks on one-metre rules, in before/after pairs.
- **What it stakes:** nothing of its own — three rows of someone else's changelog; the practice admits it only read what had already happened.
- **What it brings to appearance:** a metre with holes, and its twin with one more mark — a standard altered after the fact, with no reason on record.
- **How it thematises itself:** by splitting into a mute half and a speaking half and writing the split into the score; the scroll then continues with the house's own Mersch scorecard beneath.
- **Radicality of the stake:** low — no taboo touched, no institution addressed, the dead present as a column; the cost named ("das Werk wartet auf den Briefkasten") is proposed, not yet paid.

## Misunderstandings (Manifest, pp. 13–17)
- **Outputdenken** (p. 13): "Provenienz — nachrechenbar", SHA-256s, byte counts and a byte-identical re-derivation are offered as the work's credential; that is the house's honesty, not the work's aesthetics.
- **Research display** (p. 17): the page is a data table, a changelog count and a disclaiming paragraph; the work names its own sharpest risk — "liest sich als Chart" — and the three tick-rows do read as small multiples; the self-audit beneath (commentary, not the work) is the display's commentary layer sitting on the same scroll.
- **Niche** (p. 17): the tampered rule is an old conceptual gesture applied to a not-yet-tried dataset; the before/after pair is the one shift that is the work's own.

## Cover the text
Six white bars, black ticks at irregular intervals, in three pairs; the second bar of each pair has one or two ticks the first lacks; labels 24.1, 25.1. What remains shows a standard that has been re-ruled — that much survives without a word. It does not show war, death, twenty-five, a record, or an absent reason; every one of those is on the page, and the score knew it when it called the rules mute.

## One move
Cut the page to its one sentence and the three version numbers, and let the added tick on each second rule be cut later and by another hand than the rest — so that the admission shows as an intrusion into the standard instead of being told as a row in a table.
