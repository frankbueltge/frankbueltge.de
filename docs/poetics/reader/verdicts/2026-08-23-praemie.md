# Policy — read 2026-08-23

## Seen, before reading
A dark page on a personal site, set like the first page of an insurance contract: "Insurance policy — The Present. Policyholder: future generations. Insurer: the market. Policy no. 1. Valid: continuous, non-terminable." Under it a dashboard: a large number, 279.3, labelled INSURANCE PRICE INDEX, "+179.3 % since 1998", and a chart in which grey spikes (disaster cost per year) sit under a smooth rising line (premium index). Then six numbered clauses, § 1 to § 6 — peril, premium, claims history, settlement, exclusion, deductible — each a sentence or two of climate-and-insurance figures with a mono-type source line, retrieval timestamp to the second, and licence. A "Nearest neighbors" section names three other places that publish the same series. The page closes "This policy was not signed. It is in force. Premium due.", then a version string, a "Method sheet" link, and a footer line calling itself "An instrument — method and sources disclosed, source code open." A stranger takes it for exactly what it looks like: a climate-insurance dashboard dressed as a contract, the joke legible at once — the market insures the present against the catastrophe it priced in, and sends the bill to people who have not been born.

## Verdict: SAYS
A dashboard in a contract's clothing: every clause is a caption to a number that three named neighbours already publish, and the page signs itself "instrument" in the footer to be excused from the work its header claims to be.

## Thesis 12, answered (Manifest, p. 51)
- **How it happens:** A pipeline fetches four public series each night and pours them into six clauses of a mock contract.
- **What it stakes:** Nothing the house cannot regenerate tomorrow; the risk is borne, as § 6 says, by someone else.
- **What it brings to appearance:** A rising number — which the page itself admits is "already public and current at Deep Sky."
- **How it thematises itself:** It names its neighbours, versions itself, and signs off as "an instrument" — self-description in place of self-exposure.
- **Radicality of the stake:** Low; a contract that binds "future generations" touches no taboo, because no one present is asked to sign.

## Misunderstandings (Manifest, pp. 13–17)
- Outputdenken (p. 13): retrieval timestamps, licence notes, DOI, "Fassung 0.1.0 · Schema 1", "source code open" — offered on the surface as credentials.
- Research display (p. 17): a data visualisation with numbered commentary, plus a "Method sheet" the form cannot do without.
- Niche occupation (p. 17): the house's own neighbours note concedes the finding is shown elsewhere; what remains is a legal-document skin laid over a dashboard — a miniature shift in a fabric shown a hundred times.

## Cover the text
A number (279.3), a grey spiked area, a rising line, two year labels, a legend, and the site chrome. It still shows that something rose. It does not show a policy, a policyholder, an insurer, an exclusion or a deductible: the entire conceit — the only thing the house claims as its own daylight — lives in the sentences, and goes with them.

## One move
Strip the dashboard and let the contract be the whole page: no chart, no source lines, no index, a named policyholder instead of "future generations", and a premium that falls due from the reader who opened it — the form is the one thing here that could show, and at present it is the caption of a chart.
