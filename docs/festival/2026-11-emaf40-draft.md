# DRAFT — EMAF 40 (European Media Art Festival 2027): an entry, if Frank wants one

**Status: draft for Frank's decision, written 2026-11-08. Nothing has been submitted and no account
was created.** An entry leaves the house, so the submit button is Frank's.

**In one paragraph.** The call has an archive theme and no fee. It closes once 2,200 entries are in,
and on 15 December 2026 at the latest. The house's clearest fit is the Studio's **STRUCK OFF**,
entered in the Installation section. The strongest second work is the lab's **Ducking**, though its
fit is weaker and it needs hardware. If Frank enters, the entry should go in **by the end of
November**: EMAF shows no live count towards the cap. The open question for Frank: enter, and with
which work.

Context: on 2026-11-03 Frank agreed an attention strategy (wording private): quiet groundwork first,
then one launch moment with Ducking, then this call. The house audit of 2026-10-15 left one owner
decision open. In August, festival entries were judged too early, and the audit asked whether that
view still holds. This draft does not decide it. It is here so that Frank can decide quickly.

## 1. The call, as read on 2026-11-08

Sources: emaf.de (start page and news), the call's regulations (German and English PDFs, linked from
emaf.de and from the entry portal), and the entry portal `emaf.filmchief.com/entry-forms`.
Everything below is paraphrased, except a few words of the theme.

- **Dates.** Festival 21–25 April 2027 in Osnabrück. Exhibition 21 April – 23 May 2027, in the
  Kunsthalle Osnabrück. The organiser is Experimentalfilm Workshop e.V.
- **Deadline.** The call closes once 2,200 entries are in, and on 15 December 2026 at the latest.
  The cap is new. EMAF says entries rose sharply in recent years and it wants time for each one.
  **No count is shown anywhere.** EMAF 39's call ran to 22 December 2025, and no total was
  published on emaf.de. So whether the cap arrives early cannot be read; 15 December is a ceiling,
  not a promise.
- **Fee.** None, and EMAF says it will stay that way.
- **Eligibility.**
  - At most two works per artist.
  - Only works completed in 2026 or 2027.
  - No premiere status needed, so the planned Ducking launch costs nothing.
  - A work entered at an earlier edition is not considered again.
  - A work not in English needs English subtitles.
- **Sections.**
  - **Film:** experimental films of any length.
  - **Installation:** installative, interactive, spatial or sculptural media works; the exhibition
    is chosen from these.
  - **Expanded:** performances, live events, workshops; the portal also names VR.
- **Theme.** The theme binds the Installation section: the entry form says entries must relate to
  it (the regulations say should). It puts the archive at the centre in three roles: an instrument
  of knowledge production, a site of critical intervention, and a space for other futures.
- **What an entry needs.** For Installation and Expanded: a comprehensive description, a tech rider,
  a cost calculation and visual material. The portal's step 1 creates an account (name, e-mail,
  password). Steps 2–4 are general information, team and confirmation, and their field limits show
  only after sign-in. So every text below comes in a short version (about 80 words) and a long one
  (about 300).
- **Online works and AI.** The call names neither net-based works nor AI. The exhibition is a room
  in the Kunsthalle, so each candidate below gets a room form rather than entering as a browser page.
- **If selected.** Notice by e-mail from mid-March 2027. Installations and Expanded projects get a
  fee of €500, and the festival pays production costs and the transport of installation works. The
  artist is invited with free accreditation, three hotel nights (more if set-up needs them) and a
  contribution to travel. Exhibited works are insured at the value the artist states.
- **Rights.** The entrant must clear the rights of anyone else involved, and of submitted text and
  images. EMAF may use submitted text and images in its publications, on its site and in
  advertising, and may edit those texts or write its own.
- **Who chooses.** EMAF names a five-person team for the film programmes. On 2026-11-06 it announced
  Lisa Deml as curator of the 2027 exhibition. EMAF describes her work as concerned with the
  politics of seeing, hearing, remembering and imagining, coming from journalism and a critical look
  at media reporting. Both candidates make a record heard: STRUCK OFF the silence of a list,
  Ducking the quiet of the news.

## 2. The candidates

### First: STRUCK OFF — the Studio (Ensemble), 2026-10-14

A ledger of every asteroid the public impact list of NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (Sentry) has
held since 2002, one cell each in the order first seen, played with sound. It is on the site at
`/studio/werke-html/2026-10-14-struck-off/`, its record is `src/content/studio/works/2026-10-14-struck-off/meta.json`,
and the dossier lists it among the selected works.

**Why this one.**

- **It meets all three parts of the theme in one list.** The list produces knowledge about the
  future: which strikes still stand. Reading it whole is the intervention. And each of its cells is
  a future that was possible on the day it was listed.
- **Its findings are about how a record keeps things** (fetched 2026-10-14, all from JPL's own
  files):
  - Of 5,804 names, 3,581 were struck off. Half of those went within about 18 days of the half-month
    their object was first seen.
  - A reason is written by name for three, and Apophis was struck off a month before its note.
  - Of the 2,223 still standing, 1,972 had not been observed for a year: kept for want of a second
    look.
  - Of thirteen small asteroids seen before they struck the Earth, the list held one, 2014 AA, and
    struck it off about 22 hours after it came down. The table has one word for a warning ruled out
    and one come true.
- **The jury can place it.** The practice named its neighbours itself, and both are archive art:
  Mimi Onuoha's *The Library of Missing Datasets* and Voluspa Jarpa's *Biblioteca de la
  No-Historia*. It also says what it adds to each: here the folders are full and the label is
  missing; nothing is redacted, and the missing part was never written.
- **It is robust in a room.** It is a self-contained page with no feed, so it runs offline for five
  weeks. One pass from 2002 to the fetch day takes about a hundred seconds.
- **Its making is the house's thesis.** A machine-run practice made it in one session under a public
  constitution. Every number is computed by a committed script from files hashed in a manifest, and
  no third-party code is used.

**Honest weak points.**

1. **It enters a media-art section from a house that claims no art.** Since 2026-11-01 no surface of
   the site calls itself art, and the Studio offers its works "as research, not claimed as art". An
   entry is the first time the house hands a work to people whose job is to judge exactly that.
   This is Frank's call, not a footnote. The texts below never call the work art in the house's
   voice, and §3 offers one optional sentence that says so.
2. **The room form does not exist yet.** The work is a page. Projection and sound in a room are a
   proposal of this draft. The page needs a kiosk wrapper (autoplay, loop, controls hidden); the work
   itself would stay unchanged. The practice has written no `INSTRUCTION.md` for it. It can be asked
   for its own room form; a change of tempo, for instance a slower pass for a room, is the practice's
   call.
3. **The data are frozen on 2026-10-14.** The list will have moved by April 2027. Fetching it again
   would make a new work, because the practice's rule is lists as fetched and hashed. The room must
   say "as of 14 October 2026".
4. **Onuoha is the canonical neighbour in exactly this discourse,** and the jury will know her work.
   The difference is specific (full folders, missing label), but the family resemblance is real.
5. **One list, one country.** It is a NASA list, kept in California: an American record, not the
   world's.
6. **No video yet.** A jury reads the materials it is sent, and today there are two screenshots.

### Second: Ducking — the lab, 2026-11-01

A mixing desk for the world's attention. It has one channel per standing field of the ecology's map
plus THE CROWD, everything else on Common Ground's charts. Each fader stands at its channel's share
of the last week, each channel is a synthetic voice, and a field stays audible only while a hand
holds it up. Play runs the last weeks back with real headlines read by field. It is on the site at
`/ducking`, with its method at `/werke/ducking` and its design in `docs/design/2026-11-01-ducking.md`.
Frank counts it among the strongest works in the lab (2026-11-01, wording private).

**Why this one.**

- **It is the lab's most interwoven work.** It brings the Middle's briefing, the map's fields,
  Common Ground's daily ledger and real headlines together on one desk.
- **It is truly interactive,** and a desk is a natural object for a room. With motorised faders, a
  released fader drives itself back to where the week put it. The desk resists the hand.
- **It fits the "critical intervention" part of the theme literally:** the visitor's hand is the only
  thing that changes what is heard.
- **It is listening to an archive.** Every level comes from a ledger the house commits every morning
  since 2026-09-02 and never rewrites, and Play voices that archive.

**Honest weak points.**

1. **Its archive fit is the weaker of the two.** The desk reads an archive, but the room leaves
   nothing behind. Its strongest form for this theme would keep what visitors hold, one number per
   channel, published as open data beside the web's ledger: the room's own archive of another mix.
   That is the audit's first direction, and it is not built. Frank has not been asked (design note,
   "Not built"), so the entry texts do not promise it.
2. **The audit rates it ADDED VALUE, thin** (`docs/audits/2026-08-09-usp-audit.md` §23). The
   gesture is AudioStreamer (Schmandt and Mullins, 1995). Without a record of the hands, the audit
   reads it as that older work with a better story.
3. **It brings back the hardware.** On 2026-11-01 Frank set the motorised desk aside as too much
   hardware for now and asked for the lab version (wording private). The festival would pay
   production costs, but someone builds and installs it. The page does not yet take MIDI input or
   drive motorised faders.
4. **It depends on a live feed.** For five weeks the desk needs Common Ground's pipeline to run and
   the venue's network to hold until 23 May 2027. It also needs an offline fallback that says which
   day it shows; that fallback is not built.
5. **The ledger leans towards developer platforms,** and the page says so.
6. **It speaks what the briefing says.** On 2026-11-01 a check found 27 of 69 briefing claims wrong,
   out of date, overstated or not on the linked page, and the desk had repeated some aloud. Since
   then every round records the day its figures were checked, but the risk class remains.
7. **Headlines raise a rights question.** The record reads real headlines, and stills or video show
   them, so EMAF's rights clause applies to them. The safest pick is visual material without
   headlines.
8. **It has never been shown in a room.**

### Considered and not chosen

- **UNDER WARNING** (Studio, 2026-10-14). It is the dossier's first pick and the most striking
  image: a night map of the United States whose people light up beneath every tornado warning of
  sixteen years. Its subject, the column the record lacks (the people warned), fits "what an archive
  lacks" more than the archive's own bookkeeping, and a map of lights has more visual neighbours. It
  is the swap-in if Frank prefers it; the texts would need rewriting.
- **Protocol** (lab, nightly since 2026-06-12). It is the house's most literal archive: nightly
  minutes of the planet's session, never revised. But as a room it is text, and the audit credits
  its form, not its data. As a reading it would suit Expanded, where the theme is not required.
- **Invoked Past** (lab). It reads the press's memory, close to the curator's field, but its
  extractor stops at 2014, so the last decade is invisible.
- **Policy** (lab). The audit's verdict is REDUNDANT.
- **A whole cycle as a room** (cycle 007's warning works from all three practices). It is the
  ecology's truest form, but too diffuse for a jury that reads one work at a time.

## 3. Entry texts

### STRUCK OFF

**Title:** STRUCK OFF · **Year:** 2026 · **Section:** Installation · **Country:** Germany

**Credit line, one of these (Frank's choice):**

- (a) *Ensemble (the Studio), a machine-run practice of the research ecology at frankbueltge.de.
  Apparatus designed, programmed and run by Frank Bültge.* This is the recommended option: it is
  true to the record.
- (b) *The research ecology at frankbueltge.de (the Studio); entered by Frank Bültge.*

**Short description (80 words):**

> Since 2002 a public list kept in California has held every asteroid that might strike the Earth
> within a century. STRUCK OFF plays every name it has ever held, in the order first seen, one cell
> each, with sound: a tone when a warning is listed, a dry stroke when it is struck off, a low hum
> that grows with the warnings nobody has looked at again. The list writes when it strikes a
> warning. It almost never writes why.

**Long description (322 words):**

> Since 2002 NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory has kept Sentry, a public list of asteroids whose
> orbits do not yet rule out a strike on the Earth within the next hundred years. An object usually
> leaves the list when new observations rule a strike out. The list is an archive of futures: each
> entry was a possible impact on the day it was written.
>
> STRUCK OFF reads this archive whole. Every name the list has ever held, 5,804 as of 14 October
> 2026, stands as one cell in the order its object was first seen. The work plays from 2002 to that
> day. A soft tone sounds when a warning is listed, pitched by size, and a dry stroke when one is
> struck off. A low hum grows with the warnings still standing that nobody has observed for a year,
> and their cells fade.
>
> What the reading finds is how the record keeps things. 3,581 warnings were struck off, half of
> them within weeks. The list writes a name and a minute for each, and a reason for three. Most of
> the 2,223 still standing are kept for want of a second look: 1,972 had not been observed for a
> year. Of the thirteen small asteroids seen before they struck the Earth, the list held one,
> 2014 AA, and struck it off about 22 hours after it came down. The table has one word for a warning
> ruled out and one come true.
>
> In the room the ledger fills a dark wall and the sound fills the room: a dry rain of strikes over
> a hum that only grows.
>
> The work was made by the Studio, one of three machine-run practices in a research ecology that
> works under written constitutions, in public. Every number is computed by a published script from
> the list's own files, hashed as fetched; the work makes no risk claim of its own. Code, data and
> method are open.

**Optional sentence (Frank's choice, for the end of the long text):**

> The house that runs the practice calls none of its works art; entering one here hands that
> judgement to others.

### Ducking

**Title:** Ducking · **Year:** 2026 · **Section:** Installation · **Country:** Germany

**Credit line:** *Frank Bültge, the lab of frankbueltge.de.* How the making is described, with
machine help under the house's rules, is Frank's to word.

**Short description (77 words):**

> Ducking is what a mixing desk does when one signal pushes the others down. This desk gives each
> standing field of the world's troubles a channel, from war and hunger to climate and democracy,
> and one more for everything else the web is talking about: the crowd. Each fader stands where the
> web's attention put it last week. At rest you hear the crowd. Push a field up, and it is heard
> only while you hold it.

**Long description (291 words):**

> Ducking is what a mixing desk does when one signal pushes the others down. This desk seats the
> standing fields of a map that a research ecology keeps of what presses on the world: war, hunger,
> poverty, climate, health, displacement, democracy and more. One channel holds everything else the
> web talks about: the crowd.
>
> Every morning since September 2026 a machine has read what the web searches, reads and posts
> about, across independent platforms. It keeps what several of them share, by a published rule,
> and commits one dated file that is never rewritten. Each fader stands at its field's share of the
> last week of that archive, on a logarithmic scale. Each channel is a synthetic voice reading
> where its field stands, all of them at once.
>
> At rest you hear the crowd, because the charts are nearly all there is. Push a field up and hold
> it, and it rises out of the noise. The sum stays whole, so the room it gets comes from everything
> else. Let go, and the motorised fader drives itself back to where the week put it. Nothing stays
> up by itself.
>
> Press play and the last weeks run back. The faders move as they moved, and real headlines are
> read out by field, as dense as they were counted. A hand on any fader ends the playback.
>
> The archive here is a daily, public ledger of attention, and the desk is a way to listen to it. It
> lets a visitor hold what the ledger left quiet, for exactly as long as a hand can hold it. Every
> level is derived from committed data, the voices are made with an open model on one machine, and
> the code, data and method are public.

## 4. Room, tech rider, cost calculation, visual material

These are proposals. Every item is to be quoted; **no money is committed**, and the festival pays
production costs.

### STRUCK OFF

- **Room:** a dark wall or a darkened room. One large projection of the ledger alone; the page's
  paragraphs are not projected.
- **Sound:** two active speakers at a moderate level.
- **Playback:** a small computer plays the work's own page offline in a browser, in kiosk mode, as
  a loop of one pass (about a hundred seconds, 2002 to 14 October 2026). It starts by itself after a
  power cut. The house would build the kiosk wrapper; the work stays unchanged.
- **Beside it:** a printed sheet with the three written reasons, the sources and the manifest
  hashes, the receipts. An option for a physical counterpart: a printed register of every name, the
  struck ones struck through. Both are the house's proposal, not the practice's, and the Studio can
  be asked for an `INSTRUCTION.md`.
- **Cost items:** projector (or the venue's), mini PC, two speakers, cables and mounts, printing,
  one set-up day.
- **Visual material:** the work's own two screenshots (`shot-1100.png`, `shot-390.png` beside the
  work) exist. Still to make: a screen capture of one pass with sound (1–2 minutes) and a room
  sketch.
- **Rights:** the data are NASA/JPL's and are credited on the page (check JPL's data-use note before
  sending). The work uses no third-party code. Its code and text are the house's, under open
  licences.

### Ducking

- **Room:** a table with a motorised fader surface, one fader per channel (fourteen in the round
  after cycle 012), and a screen above it for the strips, the captions and the passing words.
- **Sound:** two active speakers, or headphones as a variant for a shared room.
- **Playback:** a small computer runs the desk's page, reads the faders and drives their motors
  (MIDI support not built yet), and fetches the daily ledger. Without a network it keeps the last
  day it has and says which day that is (not built yet).
- **Cost items:** motorised fader surface(s), computer, screen, speakers, network access (venue
  Wi-Fi or a mobile router), cables, set-up day. The building is the house's own time.
- **Visual material:** the current shelf still (`public/shelf/lab-ducking.webp`) predates
  2026-11-03 and still shows the old role line in the masthead, so a new still is needed. Still to
  make: a short video of hands on faders and a room sketch. Headlines in the frame raise the rights
  question in §2.
- **Open option (Frank's decision, not promised in the entry):** keep what visitors hold, one number
  per channel, with a consent line and the privacy text, published as open data.
