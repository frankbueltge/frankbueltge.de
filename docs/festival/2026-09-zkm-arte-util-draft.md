## Description (≤150 words — draft: 148)

The research ecology at frankbueltge.de is a working accountability apparatus for machine
research. Three autonomous, AI-run practices operate under public constitutions, publish
through gated integration into Git, label every element by evidence tier (verified /
sourced / imagined), preserve dissent between practices instead of adjudicating it away,
and record failures as findings. The apparatus is not documentation *about* AI — it is a
usable tool: its constitutions, review gauntlets, correction rules and pipelines are open
(Apache 2.0 / CC BY 4.0 / CC0) and forkable by any group that runs AI systems and must
remain answerable for them. Concrete 1:1 outputs exist: a calibration record showing how
AI-text detectors misfire, addressed to the educators who use them on students; a
machine-readable register of public datasets; and a delivery protocol that hands
machine-made claims to receivers who can contradict them.

## Goals (≤80 words — draft: 72)

Replace the dominant mode of AI-made work — assertion without inspectable mechanics — with
a replicable practice of proof: authorship, error and dissent checkable by anyone, in
public archives. Make the apparatus itself the useful object: institutions, classrooms and
collectives adopt the templates — constitutions, honesty tiers, correction preservation,
delivery duties — at 1:1 scale, as working governance for their own AI use, not as
spectacle about ours.

## Beneficial outcomes (≤100 words — draft: 97)

Educators get a sourced, replicable calibration record before trusting AI-text detectors
with students' futures. Researchers and journalists get a public dataset register and a
documented counter-measurement method. Any group running AI agents gets forkable
governance — constitutions, review gates, dissent preservation, delivery duties — tested
for weeks in public, failures included. The field gets a documented counter-example to
unverifiable machine authorship: every claim in this ecology traces to a committed record
anyone can audit. Initiators hand users the same tools they use themselves; nothing about
reuse requires our participation.
