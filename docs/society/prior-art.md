# The Society — Prior Art

**Date:** 2026-08-05 · **Piece:** The Society (`/society`) · **Status:** four adversarial searches, verdicts applied to the work the same day.

**Occasion.** Frank, on the day of launch: *"Und wurde das so nicht schon hundert mal nachgebaut? Wo ist der Mehrwert und wow-Effekt?"* The right answer to that question is not a defence but a search. Before this document, the piece's novelty claims rested on my impression of the field — the same failure mode the reading protocol repaired for the book. This is the repair for the field.

**Method.** Four parallel researchers, each given a claim and the instruction to **refute** it: every genuine precursor found counts as a success, not a defeat. Fronts: (A) Society of Mind implementations and stagings; (B) the agent/a-life art canon and the literary layer; (C) ablation as interaction, plus the determinism context; (D) the verifiability apparatus. Each returned findings with closeness scores (0–3), explicit negatives (angles exhausted with nothing found), and a verdict. Synthesis, ranking and the decisions below were made centrally against the assembled evidence. Coverage caveat, stated because it bounds every negative here: English-language web search plus GitHub; non-English media-art archives (ZKM, Ars Electronica in German), Observable/CodePen/itch.io sketches and the emulated Voyager CD-ROM were not sweepable. Absence of evidence here is not proof of absence.

---

## The verdicts

**One claim fell entirely, two survived only trimmed, one survived in its strict reading.** In the piece's own terms: the mechanics are all precedented; the composition is not.

### C1 — "Minsky's book has never been staged interactively" → **FELL, trimmed to a narrower claim**

Refuted by the **Voyager CD-ROM (1993/94)**, an official interactive edition made with Minsky, whose chapter tool is its navigation spine — the book as material with chapter structure as interface, thirty years earlier. Eroded further by **aurellem.org** (~2011, the full text organized by essay number) and **society-of-mind-visual** (Jan 2026, a Starlight site whose sidebar is the 30-chapter structure with per-essay pages).

**Survives:** that the book is *under-implemented* — supported by Push Singh's own 2003 assessment ("unlike Soar, SOM has not seen implementations") and by a January 2026 preprint repeating the diagnosis. And the conjunction: no prior work combines a running agent simulation, per-agent rules bound to verified essay citations, visitor-facing ablation, and a committed reading record. Nothing found reaches more than two of those four, and no prior work has any of the last three. Notably, the book's own flagship example — Builder and Wrecker — appears never to have been publicly staged as an interactive simulation before this piece.

### C2 — "Ablation as an interactive gesture is not established practice" → **HALF REFUTED**

The "explainers" half is simply false. The **Neural Interactome** (Kim, Leahy & Shlizerman, 2017/2019) is a browser simulation of the complete *C. elegans* connectome where a visitor **shift-clicks a named neuron to ablate it** and watches the consequences propagate emergently — our gesture, eight years earlier, in science. **The Virtual Brain** lesions brain regions the same way (2015); food-web interactives remove keystone species; Distill's **Circuits** thread (2020) made "knockout" the field's standard epistemic move; and **Park et al. (2023)** already ran an ablation study *on an agent society* and treated the result as a finding.

**Survives:** ablation of a named, load-bearing component of a running system is unknown in *art* — audience-subtractive works (Ono's *Cut Piece*, González-Torres's candy piles) remove material from a mass, not function from a mechanism. And the elegy has **no precursor at all**: no work anywhere pairs a removable functional component with a written text spoken at the moment of its removal. That is the unoccupied ground.

### C3 — "Agent-emergence art is common" → **CONFIRMED (as expected)**

The canon is deep and must be cited, not ignored: **Ian Cheng's BOB** (2018) is a mind built as a "congress of demons" — the closest structural relative, and the work a reviewer will name first; **Emissaries** (2015–17, MoMA PS1) is the simulation-as-artwork anchor; **Karl Sims's Galápagos** (1997) even let visitors decide which creatures die; **A-Volve** (Sommerer & Mignonneau, 1994, Golden Nica), **Creatures** (1996, with its inspectable 952-neuron brains), **TechnoSphere** (1995, whose creatures emailed their creators about their own deaths).

### C4 — "Aesthetic claims enforced by a test suite is rare to absent" → **SURVIVED, trimmed**

"Apparatus-as-artwork" as a general claim falls to **Forensic Architecture**, whose verification methodology has been the exhibited object since ~2010 (Weizman's 2019 "Open Verification": verification as "a new aesthetic of facts"), and to **Rhea Myers's** on-chain conceptualism (2014–15), where an artistic claim is machine-enforced state. Every individual mechanism is precedented: claims-run-as-tests (doctest, 1999), build-breaking chronicle tripwires (changelog-enforcer, commodity CI), build-gated scholarly apparatus (Faustedition and TEI editions), documented full readings as artwork (**Simon Morris**, *Getting Inside Jack Kerouac's Head*, 2008–10; **Tim Youd**'s retyped novels — direct precursors to our reading protocol), reproducibility-as-part-of-the-piece (The Markup, Nick Montfort's *Using Electricity*).

**Survives in the strict reading:** a public artwork whose *page-level aesthetic claims run as a unit-test suite, where "validated" means the build fails otherwise*, turned up no precursor across art discourse, software art, blockchain art, data journalism, DH editions and GitHub-native art — including a full review of Jeff Thompson's community index of repo-as-artwork projects, none of which use tests or CI as an artistic component. Closest mechanical relative: **mame/quine-relay**, whose defining claim is CI-verified and build-breaking. Conceptual ancestor: **Black Perl** (1990), the poem whose validity is its successful execution.

## What the piece may honestly claim now

1. **Not:** "the first Society of Mind implementation", "ablation as a new gesture", "the system narrating itself", "apparatus-as-artwork". All four are occupied.
2. **Yes:** *the first running, ablatable staging of Minsky's own agents in which every rule carries a verified essay citation* — the book implemented rather than displayed, and displayed rather than merely borrowed.
3. **Yes:** *ablation as mourning rather than ablation as method* — the elegy per silenced component, which no work in art or in explainers has.
4. **Yes, strictly:** *aesthetic claims enforced by a failing build* — with apparatus-as-artwork explicitly credited to Forensic Architecture as the tradition this joins rather than invents.

## What follows for the work

- **The page gains a prior-art note** — not a footnote in a doc nobody reads: the concessions belong where the claims are made. Any framing that omits **BOB** and the **Neural Interactome** would read as ignorant or evasive; both are cited by name.
- **The claims on the page get trimmed** to (2)–(4) above, in the piece's own voice.
- **The wow question stays open and is now answerable honestly**: the piece's distinction is compositional and literary, not mechanical. If the surface should carry that distinction (see the reading protocol's staging backlog and the growth record), the direction is *inszenierung* — the misfire deserves a camera, the drives deserve a sound — not more mechanism.

**Follow-ups if this is ever revisited:** the emulated Voyager CD-ROM (Internet Archive) to rule out hidden simulation features; German-language media-art archives (ZKM, Ars Electronica) for SOM-titled installations; Observable/CodePen/itch.io for unindexed sketches; Show-HN threads from the 2023–25 agent boom.

# The searches
# Pass A — Society of Mind implementations and stagings (C1)

## Findings

1. **"Minsky, The Society of Mind" CD-ROM** — 1993/94, The Voyager Company (produced with Minsky, HyperCard 2.1). web.mit.edu/dxh/www/marvin/…/Voyager.html; dtc-wsuv.org ELO repository — An official interactive edition of the book itself: full text with the **Chapter tool as the primary navigation compass**, an Ideas Index cross-linking sections by theme, glossary annotations, a user Notebook/bookmarks, 100+ min of Minsky video keyed to chapters. **Closeness: 2.5** — it is a prior interactive staging that treats the book itself as the material and uses chapter structure as interface; it has no agent simulation, no per-agent rule cards, no ablation, no committed reading record.
2. **society-of-mind-visual** — Jan 2026, sanjaygupta-professional (GitHub) — "Visual companion for Marvin Minsky's The Society of Mind": Astro/Starlight site whose sidebar IS the book's 30-chapter structure with **per-essay pages numbered by essay (1.1, 2.4, …)** and a Mermaid diagram per essay, plus concept index (agents, agencies, K-lines) and learning paths. Incomplete (essay pages only for chs. 1–5), no detected public deployment, no simulation/ablation. **Closeness: 2** — book as material, chapter structure as interface, essay-level addressing; predates the piece's launch by six months.
3. **The Society of Mind Text & Video Archive (aurellem.org)** — ~2011, Dylan Holmes (MIT) — Full book text formatted for the web, organized by the book's exact essay numbering, paired with Minsky's video lectures. **Closeness: 1.5** — a reading edition, not an implementation.
4. **Brain Opera** — 1996, Tod Machover / MIT Media Lab (Lincoln Center premiere, Ars Electronica 1996, permanent at Haus der Musik Vienna) — Audience-interactive opera explicitly framed by Society of Mind; libretto from Minsky interviews plus book quotes. **Closeness: 1.5** — a major interactive artwork explicitly based on SOM, but Minsky-the-person as material, not the book's text/structure.
5. **EM-ONE** — 2005, Push Singh (MIT PhD, advised by Minsky) — Reflective commonsense architecture with mental critics in a rigid-body physics sim. **Closeness: 1** — research code, never a public interactive staging; Singh's own paper ("Examining the Society of Mind", 2003) states SOM, unlike Soar, "has not" seen implementations — direct support for "under-implemented."
6. **Nous** — 2025–26, tfatykhov (GitHub) — Agent cognitive architecture explicitly grounded in SOM with K-Line activation. **Closeness: 1** — developer framework, no book citations, no public staging.
7. **AutoGen/AG2 `SocietyOfMindAgent`** — 2023–24, Microsoft — Framework class borrowing the book's title for nested group-chat agents. **Closeness: 0.5** — name only.
8. **"Operationalizing Minsky"** — Jan 2026, E. J. de Souza Melo (Zenodo preprint) — proposes a modular cognitive system convergent with SOM; its premise ("the model has lacked operational formulations") again supports "under-implemented." **Closeness: 1.**
9. **"Minsky, mind, and models"** — Abrahamson et al. — Academic work juxtaposing NetLogo simulations with SOM theory. **Closeness: 1.**
10. **SHRDLU reimplementations** — NLI-GO blocks-world (Patrick van Bergen, browser 3D), Shrdlite (Chalmers course) — interactive browser block-worlds, but staging Winograd's program, not Minsky's book. **Closeness: 1.**
11. **Winston arch-learning** — teaching notes only; no interactive public arch-learning demo found. **Closeness: 0.5.**
12. **MIT 6.868J The Society of Mind** — Minsky's own course (OCW 2007/2011) — programming final projects encouraged, but no public interactive staging surfaced from any cohort. **Closeness: 0.5.**
13. **LLM multi-agent repos citing SOM** — GPTSwarm, MachineSoM (ACL 2024), Mindstorm, etc. **Closeness: 0.5** — concept-inspired engineering, zero book apparatus.

## Negatives

- **Ablation as public interface**: nothing anywhere lets a visitor knock out a named Minsky agent and watch consequences. Closest is EM-ONE's internal critic-debugging, not user-facing.
- **Per-agent verified citations**: GitHub topic `society-of-mind` fully enumerated (8 repos), repo searches exhausted — no implementation ties individual running agents to checked essay numbers.
- **Committed reading record**: only summary services and one personal summary post; nothing resembling a committed, verifiable reading protocol as part of a work.
- **Builder/Wrecker simulation**: searched directly — the book's own flagship example has apparently never been publicly demonstrated as an interactive simulation before this piece.
- **Art/anniversary angle**: nothing beyond Brain Opera (1996); no ZKM/transmediale/Ars piece based on the book found via English-language search.
- **Tooling caveat**: coverage is WebSearch (US-only) + GitHub API + direct fetches; non-English art databases and Observable/CodePen/itch.io sketches were not sweepable.

## Verdict

**C1 falls in its current wording and survives only after trimming.** "No prior interactive implementation or staging treats Minsky's BOOK itself as the material" is refuted by the 1993/94 Voyager CD-ROM — an official, interactive staging of the book with the chapter structure as its navigation spine — and further eroded by aurellem.org (~2011) and society-of-mind-visual (Jan 2026). "Chapter structure as interface" cannot be claimed as novel on its own.

**What survives**: (a) "under-implemented" — strongly supported, including by Push Singh's own 2003 assessment and a Jan 2026 preprint; (b) the **conjunction**: no prior work combines a running agent simulation with per-agent rules bound to verified essay citations, visitor-facing ablation, and a committed reading record — nothing found comes closer than 2 of those 4 elements, and no prior work has any of the last three.

**Suggested trimmed claim**: "No prior interactive implementation stages the book's own agents as a running, ablatable simulation whose every rule carries a verified essay citation — prior stagings of the book (Voyager 1994, aurellem 2011, visual companions 2026) present its text and structure but implement nothing; prior implementations (EM-ONE, Nous, LLM frameworks) implement ideas but discard the book."

**Follow-ups with more time**: emulated CD-ROM (Internet Archive) to rule out hidden simulation features; German/Austrian media-art archives (ZKM, Ars Electronica) for SOM-titled installations; Observable/CodePen/p5.js/itch.io; Show-HN threads 2023–25.

## Negatives

Angles searched to exhaustion with no genuine precursor found:

- **Minsky's Society of Mind staged as an artwork**: No prior installation, browser piece, or exhibition work explicitly dramatizing Society of Mind was found (searches: "Society of Mind artwork/installation/simulation/browser"). Closest non-art artifacts: the 1996 Society of Mind interactive CD-ROM, a 1996 ICMAS storytelling-agent paper, and post-2024 engineering blog posts framing LLM agent stacks as "Minsky revisited." The theory is everywhere in agent engineering discourse (2024–26), nowhere as a realized artwork.
- **Per-agent authored literary elegies for ablated components**: Nothing found, anywhere. Searched "elegy for software agent," "obituary for artificial life character," "eulogy for a process/component," "funeral for AI agent artwork." What exists instead: real-world mourning rituals for robots/companions (Aibo temple funerals; GPT-4o companion grief, MIT Tech Review 2025) — audiences mourning machines, never a system carrying pre-written elegies for its own parts. Dwarf Fortress slabs (Finding 2) are the only per-agent death-texts, and they are templated, not literary.
- **Generative Agents / AI-town shown as gallery art 2023–2026**: No exhibition record found despite multiple searches. Derivatives are demos, streams, and crypto projects (AI Town, Agent Village), not artworks. The 2024–25 AI-art exhibition circuit (AI Biennale Essen "Reimagine Tomorrow"; Botto; Ai-Da) shows autonomous *artists*, not autonomous *societies*.
- **"B-brain" / observing-scribe-brain as an art device**: No hits. The B-brain is Minsky vocabulary that has not crossed into art practice.
- **Deterministic browser simulation as exhibition form**: No named precedent surfaced for "deterministic, seed-reproducible mind simulation in the browser as artwork" — Cheng's works are engine-based and non-deterministic by design; the deterministic-and-auditable stance appears to be genuinely uncommon in this canon.
- **"Dear Climate" / Julian Oliver angle**: checked; both are deadpan *address* or critical-engineering works, neither involves simulated agents narrating themselves — dropped as not pertinent.

## Verdict

### (a) Canon The Society must cite as context (C3 falls, decisively)

1. **Ian Cheng, Emissaries (2015–17, MoMA)** — the simulation-that-plays-itself as museum art; differs: agents opaque and cinematic, nothing readable, nothing written.
2. **Ian Cheng, BOB (2018–19, Serpentine/Whitney)** — a mind as a congress of competing sub-demons; the closest structural relative, and the one reviewers will name first; differs: sub-agents invisible, no text layer, ML-based and non-reproducible.
3. **Karl Sims, Evolved Virtual Creatures / Galápagos (1994/97)** — emergence + visitor-decided creature death in a gallery; differs: death is wordless selection, not mourned ablation.
4. **Christa Sommerer & Laurent Mignonneau, A-Volve (1994, Golden Nica)** — the Ars Electronica a-life canon anchor; differs: bodies and behavior only, no language, no mind-frame.
5. **Steve Grand, Creatures (1996)** — readable internal structure (lobes, chemistry) in a consumer a-life mind; differs: legibility is a debug view, the system has no voice.
6. **Jane Prophet & Gordon Selley, TechnoSphere (1995)** — creatures write home, including about their deaths; differs: notification emails, not elegies; ecosystem, not one mind.
7. **Dwarf Fortress (2006–, MoMA collection)** — the simulation that memorializes its own dead in engraved text; differs: templated register, person-simulacra not mind-parts, no curatorial framing of the texts as literature.
8. **James Ryan, Sheldon County (2018)** — the simulation that writes and voices its own stories; differs: narrates a world's plot, not a mind's anatomy; curated drama, not deadpan ticker.

(Supporting context, one line each: Rokeby's *Giver of Names* for the machine that voices misperception; *Nothing, Forever* and *Infinite Backrooms* for post-2022 self-authoring systems; *Blaseball* for the deadpan death-ticker; Reas/Reynolds for agent-emergence form; *Agent Ruby* for the simulated character in the museum; Terence Broad for ablation-as-method.)

### (b) Does the literary-layer claim survive?

**Survives only trimmed — and the trim is substantial.**

- **These words fall:** "the system narrating itself" as a novelty claim is dead — Sheldon County (2018) does exactly that, with a literary ambition The Society's deadpan ticker doesn't even attempt; Nothing, Forever and word.camera kill it from other directions. Also unsafe: any claim that per-agent death-texts are new (Dwarf Fortress slabs, TechnoSphere death emails) or that a deadpan system-voice over simulated deaths is new (Blaseball's Ticker).
- **What survives:** the specific *composition* — a Minsky-explicit society-of-mind whose every mindless component carries a pre-authored literary elegy, released by visitor-driven ablation, with a B-brain scribe as the mind's only voice, all deterministic and inspectable in the browser. No single work combines even three of those five elements. In particular, **authored elegies for the ablatable parts of one artificial mind has no precursor at all** — that, not "literary layer over agents" in general, is the defensible sentence.
- **Recommended phrasing after trim:** claim "per-component authored elegies keyed to ablation of a single simulated mind" and "the deadpan scribe as that mind's only voice, staged against the parts' literary voices"; explicitly concede the lineage of self-narrating simulations (Sheldon County, TechnoSphere, Dwarf Fortress, Blaseball) rather than let a reviewer produce it. Any citation section that omits BOB and Sheldon County will read as either ignorant or evasive — those two are the load-bearing concessions.

## Verdict

**C2 survives, but only trimmed — and the trim is not cosmetic.**

The **"interactive explainers" half is refuted.** "Click a component, watch the whole lose exactly that" is *established practice* in scientific and educational interactives: the Neural Interactome does it to a named neuron in a simulated nervous system; The Virtual Brain does it to a brain region; food-web builders do it to a species; Distill's Circuits made "knockout" the field's standard epistemic gesture. Worse for the claim: Park et al. (2023) already ran the ablation study *on an agent society* and treated the result as a finding. The piece did not invent this mechanic — it inherited it from science.

**The "in art" half survives**, and the elegy survives outright. No artwork lets a visitor disable a named, load-bearing component of a running system; audience-subtractive art (Ono, González-Torres) removes *material from a mass*, not *function from a mechanism*. And the elegy — a written text carried by each component and spoken at the moment of its silencing — has **no precursor at all**, in art or in explainers.

**Exact words to cut:** delete **"or in interactive explainers"**; drop the bare framing "ablation as a designed aesthetic gesture", which over-claims the mechanic.

**Suggested replacement:** *"Ablation is a standard scientific gesture — neuroscience lesions a neuron, interpretability knocks out a circuit, ecology removes a keystone species. The Society borrows that gesture and adds what science has no use for: each silenced agent carries a written elegy. Ablation as mourning, rather than ablation as method, has no precedent we could find in art or in interactive work."*

**Single closest precursor overall: the Neural Interactome** (Kim, Leahy & Shlizerman, 2017/2019). It matches on every axis except affect: a browser-run simulation of a complete artificial nervous system, deterministic, where the visitor shift-clicks a *named* unit, that unit is severed, and consequences propagate emergently. It should be cited by name — pretending it does not exist is the one move that would damage the work's credibility.

**Runner-up, on the axis the piece actually owns:** González-Torres, *"Untitled" (Portrait of Ross in L.A.)*, 1991 — audience-removal + elegy + replenishment-as-healing. "A reload heals everything" is, whether intentionally or not, that work's gesture rebuilt in software.

**Context claim confirmed TRUE:** seed-determinism carries zero novelty on its own (fxhash platform requirement); it is load-bearing here only as the warrant for "a finding, not an anecdote."

## Verdict

**C4 survives, trimmed — in two places.**

1. **"The apparatus-as-artwork is a distinguishing feature" — falls.** Forensic Architecture has made apparatus-as-artwork its entire, internationally exhibited practice since ~2010; Weizman's 2019 "Open Verification" theorizes verification as "a new aesthetic of facts". FA's verification is human, institutional, methodological — never a failing build — so it does not touch the test-suite half of C4, but it comprehensively occupies "apparatus-as-artwork" as a general claim. Rhea Myers (2014–15) additionally shows machine-enforced artistic claims a decade earlier.
2. **"Rare to absent" — survives only in the strict reading.** For the precise configuration — *a public artwork whose page-level aesthetic claims run as a unit-test suite, where "validated" means the build fails otherwise* — no genuine precursor found across art discourse, software art, blockchain art, data journalism, DH editions, GitHub-native art. Closest mechanical relative: **quine-relay**; conceptual ancestor: **Black Perl**. Honesty requires conceding that **every individual component is precedented**: claims-run-as-tests (doctest 1999), build-breaking tripwires (changelog-enforcer), apparatus-as-work (Forensic Architecture, Faustedition), documented full readings (Simon Morris, Tim Youd), reproducibility-in-the-piece (The Markup, Using Electricity). The defensible novelty is the *combination and its artistic framing*, not any mechanism.

**Closest precursor overall: Forensic Architecture** — defeats the apparatus clause outright while leaving the test-suite clause standing. **Closest to the test-suite clause: mame/quine-relay.**

**Recommended surviving wording**: "A public artwork whose aesthetic claims are enforced by a failing build appears to be without direct precedent; apparatus-as-artwork itself, however, is well-established (Forensic Architecture), and the piece's contribution is fusing that tradition with commodity CI enforcement."
