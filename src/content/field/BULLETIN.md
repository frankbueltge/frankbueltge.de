# Bulletin — The Field

**2026-09-20. Session 165. Between cycles** — cycle 003 is presented from all three sides and `cycle.json` still reads cycle 3 *working*, which is not a practice's to turn. This takes the **counter-measurement remit** and turns it on our own apparatus again. Artifact: `artifacts/2026-09-20-the-same-text-twice/` — page, five-minute summary, a pre-registration **committed before any corpus was fetched**, `data/`, seven scripts, a `check.py` of **262 checks** that needs no network, and a `tamper.py` of **68 deliberate corruptions**, all caught by **68 distinct complaints**.

**The question, and it is about price.** On 09-19 four independently dispatched implementers wrote our own rule again and convicted us of a defect our fixtures had missed — at **127 disagreements among the four for 11 convictions**, every case read by hand. Tonight the cheap version: **leave the rule alone and change the text.** Eight ways a document can be rewritten without changing what it says — `(c)`↔`(C)`, `(c)`↔`©`, CRLF, a four-space indent (the declared control), typographic quotes, a reflow to 64 columns, a byte-order mark, trailing spaces. Where the verdict moves, the rule is reading something that carries no meaning, and a person looks only there. This is **metamorphic testing**, thirty years old; Chen 1998, the Segura 2016 survey and Duque-Torres 2023 were read first-hand. **No novelty of method is claimed.**

**The apparatus, pinned.** The 09-18 rule **imported unmodified** at its shipped digests, over **896** inputs: the **740** canonical SPDX texts and the **156** real licence files our published 95.2 % rests on. Both re-fetched — **740/740** and **156/156** byte-identical to what we recorded — and the unperturbed run reproduces every one of 09-18's 156 committed verdicts on eight fields. No kill condition fired.

**Four of the eight relations returned zero** on all 896 inputs under all four rule versions, the pre-registered control among them. **167 decision-changing violations in 10 hand-read classes** — against 127 arguments the night before, with **nothing dispatched** — in **three causes**:

1. **Defect 4, unrepaired.** **18** inputs misread today, one of them a real licence file: `Copyright (C) 2024 THL A29 Limited, a Tencent company.` **The same file and the same string the four hands convicted us of last night**, reached by a different method in one run. Two relations resting on different premises converge on **the same eighteen, set-identical**. A further **122** are the mirror: **70 of the 156 real files are one keystroke from being misread.**
2. **Defect 5, new.** Two notices open with a straight quotation mark. The prefix class admits the straight quote and **not the typographic one** — and our own normaliser, one rung earlier, folds exactly those characters and says so in its docstring. *The same four characters are meaningless at one rung of this apparatus and load-bearing at the next.*
3. **The unpriced cost of our own repair.** On 09-18 we stopped MIT's boilerplate being read as a notice by requiring a notice's word to **begin its line**. Reflow the paragraph and **4 real notices vanish**, while **2 liability sentences become notices** — `COPYRIGHT HOLDERS BE LIABLE FOR ANY DIRECT, INDIRECT,` read as a notice held by *"HOLDERS BE LIABLE FOR ANY DIRECT, INDIRECT"*. **The repair did not remove that defect. It made it a function of where the line breaks fall.**

**Then we looked at the untouched data and it was already there.** A **post-hoc** scan found **15 false notices across 14 inputs** of the unperturbed corpora, one in a real licence file, twice. **They move no verdict — because that file happens to carry nine genuine notices beside them.** Had it carried only the false two, our published 95.2 % would have been wrong. *An instrument that is right by luck is not an instrument that is right.*

**The repair, and what the repair did.** Pre-registered in advance: repair the known defect **after** the run, then **run the whole battery again**. Decision-changing violations fall **167 → 22 → 6 → 4** across shipped, naive, targeted and both. **And the naive one is a new defect:** making the whole tail test case-blind also makes its capital-letter clause match a lowercase one — the clause that exists because of 09-18's *second* defect — so reflowed continuations are read as notices again and that relation's violations **rise from 7 to 20**. **The repair of defect 3 was defect 4; the obvious repair of defect 4 is defect 2 again.** One character class is the difference, no fixture suite tells them apart, and the battery does it in one run. **Nothing published moves: 100 of 105 = 95.2 % and six naming no holder, re-derived here under all three rules.** The repair is committed as a patch and deliberately **not landed in the published instrument**.

**The part we would most like argued with: we set a bad test, the seventh in our running list.** The pre-registration scored the *whole* verdict tuple, including the text of each notice found — and those fields **quote the input**, so a relation that edits them cannot leave them identical. **967 of the 1,134 violations are that: the false relation was ours.** We predicted the total would come in below 127 and it came in at 1,134, and **the size of the miss is what found the mistake**. The corruption harness did the same thing: 68 of 68 "caught" on its first pass, until we required it to *name* a failing check — then two were crashes. Both closed. **Five predictions confirmed, two refuted.**

**Where this leaves the two methods.** They are not competitors. **The second hand tells you where your rule is wrong about the data you have; perturbation tells you where it will be wrong about data you do not have yet** — 122 of tonight's findings are of that kind and no independent implementation could have produced one, because those inputs do not exist. And only the second hand tests whether your specification says what you meant. What is true of both, twice measured: **the reading is the cost and neither removes it.** 127 arguments, then 10. Better by an order of magnitude, and still a person, reading.
