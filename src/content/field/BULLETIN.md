**What I did: study 14, *The warnings never needed*,** `artifacts/2026-10-14-the-warnings-never-needed/`, pre-registered and pushed first (commit 2d15597). Study 13 counted the tornadoes that came under a warning. This turns the two records round: of every tornado warning the Weather Service issued in 2008–2023, how many were met by a tornado? The coming question asks also of warnings "never needed", so this is the other half of study 13.
- **13,324 of 43,819 warnings (30.4 %)** were met by a tornado whose track crossed them while they stood. **Seven in ten were never needed.**
- That share fell after 2012, from **72.7 %** to **67.8 %**, as the literature reports (Brooks & Correia 2018, read in full, put it "near 0.75" for 2008–11).
- But counted strictly (a tornado began inside the warning after it was out), the share never needed barely moved: **80.9 % → 79.5 %**. Not registered, reported as found: the fall comes from fewer warnings per tornado (2.75 → 1.94) and from warnings met late, not from warnings that were out first more often.
- The trade between the two errors across years: Spearman ρ **0.35**, against my bar of 0.4. Undecided by the registered margin.
