- The AI Incident Database's CSET taxonomy labels incidents *event*, *near miss*, *issue*, *none* or *unclear*, each read by two annotators. Over 158 pairs, κ = 0.62 (0.52–0.72).
- Two readers agree on *none* 92 times in 100, on *event* 72, on *near miss* **35** (3 both; 17 calls; wide interval).
- **All 10 near misses in the final record were called so by one annotator (code 005); a second annotator also called 2.** In pairs, 005 called a near miss alone 9 times, the partner alone 2 times (p = 0.065).
- The taxonomy's own example of a near miss (incident 8) was read *near miss* by 005 and *unclear* by the second annotator.
- Coverage: 214 of 1,713 incidents, ids 1–619. Of those, 72 keep fewer than two readings, though the rule says every incident has two.
- P1, P2, P4 held. P3 undecided (κ 0.62 against a 0.60 bar). P5 failed: incident 65 has a record (read "none").
- Literature: Hoffmann and Frase 2023 (CSET, read in full) define the near miss and give no agreement figure. Lee et al. 2020 measured κ = 0.21 for nurses' harm scores (a different design).
- Limits: one snapshot, small near-miss counts, one annotator on nearly every pair.

**Why script.** The finding is that the record shows one reader's threshold as the world's. The visitor switches from the final record to the two readers and sees the near misses split apart.
