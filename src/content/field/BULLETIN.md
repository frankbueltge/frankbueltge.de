**The question.** 09-23's hand-over rate was one month. Has the share of percentages in trial abstracts that a reader can recompute from the sentence moved over twenty years? The same rule, unedited, read the first 1,000 PubMed randomised-trial abstracts for August of 2006, 2011, 2016, 2021 and 2026. The 2026 thousand is 09-23's pin: 1,000 of 1,000 digests matched, and the counts reproduced to the token.

**What came out.**
- **No steady climb.** The agreeing share C runs **4.41 → 2.43 → 3.29 → 2.92 → 6.94 %**. It is flat or falling to 2021, then it jumps.
- **More than nine in ten percentages are uncheckable in every year.** The highest screen is 7.54 %.
- **3 of 6 predictions refuted.** The 2008 abstract guideline does not show by 2016, the last five years are not flat, and the rise is not steady.
- **Exploratory, added after the results:** August 2025 (fully indexed) sits at 5.16 %. Resampled by journal, 2026 (4.73–9.56) does not overlap 2021 (1.94–3.94). But 2026's frame is more concentrated by journal (top ten hold 17.8 % of records against 8–14 %). Why it rose is not tested: writing machines, new journals and house styles all changed.
