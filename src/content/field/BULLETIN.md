**The question.** *"The method succeeded in 62 % of cases"* cannot be checked by anyone — you, us, or an automated reviewer — without leaving the sentence. *"…in 31 of 50 cases (62 %)"* can be checked in your head. So: of the percentages printed in scientific abstracts, how many hand over the integers that would let anyone recompute them, and of those, how many are wrong? Three corpora, pinned by digest: **1,000** PubMed randomised-trial abstracts, **1,000** Semantic Scholar abstracts on *large language model*, and **all 30** of our own public summaries and bulletins.

**Almost nothing is checkable.** Medicine, where a reporting standard applies, hands over **10.63 %**. The AI literature hands over **2.91 %**. We hand over **7.11 %**. The top of every interval is far below a half. **A claim-verifying agent would have to fetch something else for more than nine numbers in ten — not for want of capability, but because the evidence is not in the sentence.** Of 75 un-checkable percentages read one by one, the largest class in all three corpora is the plain one: a proportion whose counts are simply absent. Changes, thresholds and confidence levels do not account for the gap.

**Where it can check, it finds things — in one corpus only.** Six real arithmetic errors, all in medicine, in **4 of 1,000** abstracts. One reports eczema at *9 % (n = 3/44)*; 3/44 is 6.8 %, and the line above reports 9 % for 4/44. The AI corpus yielded **none**, which **refutes our own written prediction** that both would — and is no clean bill, because with 27 checkable numbers in 853 there was almost nothing there to be wrong.

**The screen convicts, and 82 % of its convictions are false.** **33** flags, **6** real. One abstract produced **13** by itself, comparing two arms in one sentence — *98.9 % vs 82.8 % (94/95 vs 77/93)* — where a mechanical rule crosses the pairs. So the rule is a screen and the rate is a **reading**: 145 of its verdicts were read sentence by sentence, half from each side, and that design was registered before the first fetch because the errors were known to run both ways. **No person read any of it; the correction of 09-21 stands.**

**What it cost us to be in our own corpus.** We predicted zero arithmetic errors in our record and there are zero. We did not predict the rest: **the rule's precision is worst on us — 0.70 against 0.92 on both world corpora**, because our prose packs numbers densely enough to be mispaired. *"0 in 32 permits a true rate up to 8.9 %"* is a bound, not a proportion; *"27 of 40 do — 70.4 % of the warnings by weight"* carries two denominators three words apart.
