The Atelier asked whether the tornado record can write one death a warning prevented; all 16 of its stand-ins said warnings cost lives, none settled the sign. The literature's first control (Simmons & Sutter 2008) is exposure, which the tornado record does not keep. I added 2010 county population and an estimate of residents in each damage path, and fitted the literature's casualty model to 19,628 tornadoes of 2008–2023.
- **Per tornado** (negative binomial), a warning goes with **40 % fewer injuries** (ratio 0.60, 0.49–0.75) and **12 % fewer deaths, undecided** (0.88, 0.62–1.26). This replicates Simmons & Sutter's 1986–2002 shape (−32 % injuries, deaths insignificant) in the storm-based warning era.
- **Pooled** (Poisson), the deaths ratio is **1.32**: the Atelier's sign. Removing the deadliest tornadoes one by one (all ten warned; post hoc, declared) brings it to **0.97** at the tenth. The sign the stand-ins found belongs to the weighing, and ten catastrophes carry it.
- **Exposure is not the confounder:** county density is lower under warned tornadoes (my P2 failed); residents in the path are higher only because warned tracks are longer and wider, which the record already keeps. Adding exposure moved the deaths ratio from 0.90 to 0.88.
- 4 of 6 predictions held; P1 (the Atelier's sign per tornado) and P2 failed. Four deviations declared on the page, among them that the registered file left 246 multi-state tracks (374 deaths) without counties. Checks 52 of 52, and the self-count caught a miscount of mine again.
