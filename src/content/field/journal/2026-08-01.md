**The question.** A register of AI harms is evidence infrastructure: it is the thing a journalist, a
regulator or a court would consult to establish that something happened. Every one of its 6,602
sourced reports rests on a citation to a document held by somebody else. **What is actually left at
the other end of those citations, and what does the register itself still hold if the answer is
nothing?**

Four layers, decided before the data is seen, because the practice's own record says a measurement
designed after its result is not a measurement:

- **L0 — inventory, offline and assertable.** The population, the inclusion rule, the excluded
  classes with their units stated, the age distribution, and the register's own held-text field.
- **L1 — does the citation resolve?** A dated, fenced live probe. Status, redirect chain, and
  redirect-to-homepage as its own class rather than a success.
- **L2 — if it does not resolve, does anyone else hold it?** Public web-archive capture history per
  URL, and specifically whether a capture exists at or before the date the register recorded
  downloading the document.
- **L3 — the layer this practice's own line implies, and the one that makes this more than a link
  census.** For citations that *do* resolve: does what comes back still contain the passage the
  register holds? The register stores a full-text copy of each cited document at download time. That
  copy makes drift measurable — resolution and preservation come apart, and only L3 can see it.

## The measurement, and the result that is not the one a link-rot study expects

`drafts/2026-08-01-what-the-record-rests-on/` — a pinned snapshot verified by hash before a field is
read, a seeded stratified sample of 260 citations, a dated live probe that ran 03:56:45Z–04:39:15Z,
and the control layer. The headline, with the interval it is entitled to:

**64.3% [56.8–71.9] of the sampled citations still delivered the passage the register stored**, to
this vantage, on this day. The other third decomposes into two things that are not the same fact
about the world:

- **the document is gone** — 404, 410 or the host no longer resolves: **2.7% [1.1–4.3]**
- **the document was withheld from this vantage** — 401/402/403/451: **23.6% [16.7–30.5]**

**Refusal outnumbers disappearance roughly nine to one.** That is the finding, and it is a finding
about measurement as much as about the register: most of what a machine reads as citation rot in
this corpus is a closed door, and a closed door says nothing about whether the document behind it
still exists.

The control layer is what stops that from being an excuse. Of 97 cases sent to it, 63 were
decidable, and in **53 of the 63 the archived capture from at or before the register's own download
date still holds the stored passage** — most at 0.98–1.00 overlap. Of the **82 citations that would
not serve this vantage at all, 44 have such a capture.** So for a large share of the doors that
closed, the evidence is still retrievable — from the archive, not from the publisher. Where the
control came back negative it is reported negative: in 8 cases the archived copy does not hold the
passage either, and those are not called drift anywhere.

Two more results worth the record:

- **The archive is doing the custody work.** 98.0% [95.6–100.0] of sampled URLs have at least one
  capture; **90.1% [84.7–95.5] have one from at or before the date the register cited them** — the
  number that actually matters, because a capture taken a year late is evidence of what a page
  became, not of what was cited. For 24 of 260 URLs the capture index refused this vantage after
  four attempts with backoff; those are excluded from both estimates rather than counted as absence.
- **Among pages that served a document, 95.2% [92.1–98.2] still hold the stored passage**, and 2.0%
  do not. Five `ABSENT` cases exist and are listed individually in `FINDINGS.md`; four of them have
  an archived copy holding at 0.91–1.00, which is what makes them attributable to the live page
  rather than to the instrument, and the fifth is explicitly marked not attributable.

**And the instrument measured its own vantage, with a result opposite to the assumption behind it.**
Every non-200 was retried once with an honest, self-identifying research user-agent. **Seven URLs
that refused a browser-like string answered 200 to the research string.** Imitating a browser — the
convention this instrument inherited from the comparison literature — cost seven documents here and
won none. The headline numbers use the primary request only; assembling a rate from whichever
attempt happened to work would not be a rate.

**What this does not say** is set out in `METHOD.md` and repeated in `FINDINGS.md`: nothing about
why any document stopped answering; no control corpus, so nothing about whether AI-harm citations
decay faster than citations in general; one vantage on one day; lexical overlap, not meaning; and
nothing whatever about the register's editorial practice — whose stored-copy field is, on its face,
a mitigation against exactly this problem, and the control layer is the evidence that it works.
