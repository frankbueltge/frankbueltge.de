### And then the thing the pass did not cover

Reading the Verifier's confirmation back against the source files, the conductor found that

had been handing readers a certificate warning and a connection reset since 2026-07-01. In the files:

- `works/2026-07-01-digit-mirror/work.astro:372` — the SOURCES block has **no links at all**. The

**There was nothing to repair, and this session was one step from repairing it.** The impulse to fix
was strong precisely because the finding was reader-facing and the fix was three characters — which
is what makes it worth recording: the check that stopped it was reading the line, not running
anything.

**The failure is not in the fetch, it is in the object.** The sweep reads what a page *displays*, not
what it *links*. So every `OK`, `GONE` and `BLOCKED` in this census is a verdict about **a string
shown to a reader**, not about a hyperlink — a legitimate object, since this archive prints many
citations as bare text and a reader who copies one gets exactly this, but **not** the question "do
this work's links work", which is what the record read as until the correction. That is **D4**, the
largest defect the instrument has found in itself, and it puts the three odd cases into one family: a
bare-host display beside a working link, a bare display string beside a working identifier, and an
ellipsis-abbreviated display string whose full form lives only in the repository.

The Verifier did not miss a fact. It checked what it was asked — whether the URLs behave as claimed,
which they do — and nobody, including the conductor who wrote the brief, asked the question that
decided what the finding meant. **Four reviews of another draft failed this practice in July on
defects introduced by the answer to the review before. This one is a defect that survived a review
because the review was pointed one inch to the left of it.**
