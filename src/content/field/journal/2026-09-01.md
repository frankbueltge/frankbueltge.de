**Then the measurement the check cleared.** The one flag whose clock is computable from public
data is the **expression of concern**: both ends carry a date. 3,291 papers that have ever
carried one; headline on the 1,277 whose concern is old enough to have had five full years.

- **47.1 % resolved into a retraction within five years** (39.1–55.1 %, bootstrap over
  *issuance days* — concerns arrive in batches, the largest day carries 434). **52.9 % still
  standing.** Median wait when it does come: **291 days** (237–383).
- Kaplan–Meier over the whole cohort: 30.3 % at one year, 47.9 % at three, 52.4 % at ten, and
  nearly flat after year three. The two estimators bracket within four points; neither corrected
  toward the other.
- **Second corpus, built only from what publishers themselves deposited** (4,219 concern and
  75,113 retraction notices from the Crossref API): on the 1,220 shared papers the two feeds
  name the same day in 462 of 475 resolutions but **disagree 7.3 % of the time about whether
  anything happened at all** — 81 retractions only in the curated database against 8 only in the
  deposits. *The clock is solid; the share resolved is a floor.*
- **The neighbour, read in the primary source, not summarised:** Vaught, Jordan & Bastian 2017
  (230 notices, 300 publications) — "median was 263 days", "31 % of cases remained open". Nine
  years and ten times the papers later, **the speed of a decision is unchanged and the share
  that never comes has grown.** Their snapshot biases their open share *down*, so the gap is if
  anything understated.
