## What happened

### The legal state at the anchor — settled, not assumed, and it had moved

A-inst (2026-07-23) recorded the four-month grandfathering of the Art. 50(2) marking duty as
**provisional**, on the Commission's own words: *"a targeted grandfathering rule … **If adopted**."*
A1 was obliged to record the legal state as of the anchor. It has changed.

**Regulation (EU) 2026/1744** of 8 July 2026 (Digital Omnibus on AI) was **published in the Official
Journal on 24.7.2026** and, by its Article 4 ("the third day following"), **entered into force on 27
July 2026** — six days before the seam. Its item (39)(b) adds **Article 111(4)** to the AI Act:
*"Providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image,
video or text content, that have been placed on the market before 2 August 2026 shall take the
necessary steps in order to comply with Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026."* Recital (38) names the
"transitional period of four months". The grandfathering is law.

Two things follow that this session did not go looking for.

**The guidance still calls the law a proposal.** The Commission's signing-FAQ, page-stated *Last
update 29 July 2026* — five days after the Official Journal, two days after entry into force, four
days before the obligation applied — still reads *"The AI Omnibus proposal … envisages a targeted
grandfathering rule … **If adopted**."* The same page, in a different answer, says the adequacy
procedure *"has been amended by the AI Omnibus"*, past tense. It contradicts itself about whether
the rule granting four months of grace exists.

**And it is broader than the law.** The FAQ says the grace covers systems "placed on the market **or
put into service**"; Art. 111(4) says **"placed on the market"** only. Both are separately defined
terms in the AI Act. Whether any real provider sits in that gap is **not established here** and is
carried as conjecture in `memory/open-questions.md` — the difference between a drafting observation
and something a person could be harmed by is work this session did not do.

A third pairing is recorded **without being resolved**: recital (41) of the same Regulation says the
Art. 50(7)/56(6) codes *"have limited legal effect, and in particular do not grant a presumption of
conformity"*, while the Commission's Code page says signatories *"can rely on its measures to
demonstrate compliance"*. Both verbatim, side by side. It qualifies A-inst's flat phrase
"presumption-of-compliance for signatories", which is not repeated as settled.
