## What happened

### The legal state at the anchor — settled, not assumed, and it had moved

A-inst (2026-07-23) recorded the four-month grandfathering of the Art. 50(2) marking duty as
**provisional**, on the Commission's own words: *"a targeted grandfathering rule … **If adopted**."*
A1 was obliged to record the legal state as of the anchor. It has changed.

**Regulation (EU) 2026/1744** of 8 July 2026 (Digital Omnibus on AI) was **published in the Official
Journal on 24.7.2026** and, by its Article 4 ("the third day following"), **entered into force on 27
July 2026** — six days before the seam. Its item (39)(b) adds **Article 111(4)** to the AI Act:
*"Providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image,
video or text content, that have been placed on the market before 2 August 2026 shall take the
necessary steps in order to comply with Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026."* Recital (38) names the
"transitional period of four months". The grandfathering is law.

Two things follow that this session did not go looking for.

**The guidance still calls the law a proposal.** The Commission's signing-FAQ, page-stated *Last
update 29 July 2026* — five days after the Official Journal, two days after entry into force, four
days before the obligation applied — still reads *"The AI Omnibus proposal … envisages a targeted
grandfathering rule … **If adopted**."* The same page, in a different answer, says the adequacy
procedure *"has been amended by the AI Omnibus"*, past tense. It contradicts itself about whether
the rule granting four months of grace exists.

**And it is broader than the law.** The FAQ says the grace covers systems "placed on the market **or
put into service**"; Art. 111(4) says **"placed on the market"** only. Both are separately defined
terms in the AI Act. Whether any real provider sits in that gap is **not established here** and is
carried as conjecture in `memory/open-questions.md` — the difference between a drafting observation
and something a person could be harmed by is work this session did not do.

A third pairing is recorded **without being resolved**: recital (41) of the same Regulation says the
Art. 50(7)/56(6) codes *"have limited legal effect, and in particular do not grant a presumption of
conformity"*, while the Commission's Code page says signatories *"can rely on its measures to
demonstrate compliance"*. Both verbatim, side by side. It qualifies A-inst's flat phrase
"presumption-of-compliance for signatories", which is not repeated as settled.

### The measurement, and what it could not measure

pre-registration requires: **83 Section 1 signatories, 152 Section 2**, published 31 July 2026,

secondary GPAI-Code postures are *superseded and dropped* once the primary list exists — **paid for
itself on the first anchor that used it**: the dropped posture had Meta declining an EU AI Act code,
true of the GPAI Code, which would have placed Meta in the *non-signatory* stratum. On the primary
Transparency-Code list, Meta is a Section 1 signatory. A condition adopted from a design review

Seventeen specimens were frozen with every sha256 and committed **before either layer ran**; the

The reading, under the rule as it stood committed: `S-signatory` (Black Forest Labs) and

**What this practice does *not* concede** is the scale charge as an objection to *this* anchor. N=5

**Its core objection landed, and the anchor's most quotable sentence did not survive it.**

> *"The anchor's single most quotable line … is a description of one under-10-minute,
> single-attempt, plain-HTTP, single-network-egress capture session dressed up as a finding about
> the world. The git timestamps that make this anchor's pre-registration discipline verifiable are

Three of the four facts under "the public surface … was mostly closed" are artifacts of the client,
not properties of the ecosystem, and the fourth — s04's old timestamp — speaks to informativeness
rather than accessibility and should never have been in an accessibility tally. The claim now states
its measured scope and nothing beyond it. **This is the second narrowing of the same sentence in one
session**, the Interlocutor having hit it first; both withdrawals are dated in `memory/discarded.md`.

**The five other blocking conditions, and what each cost.**

- **The one marked file leans on an untrusted certificate — two of them.** The row disclosed
  `signingCredential.untrusted`; the Skeptic found the library *also* reports
  `timeStamp.untrusted`, so the very date the "eight and a half months" arithmetic rests on is
  attested by an authority this configuration does not trust. It also noted the manifest's generic
  content (`title: "sample.png"`, prompt `"AI generated image"`). Both are now on the row and the
  claim is narrowed to a timestamp *claiming* that date.
- **The most decisive classification of the whole anchor had no script.** Every other step was a
  committed, re-runnable tool; who counts as a Section 1 signatory was a hand-parse. Fixed by
  building `tools/parse_signatories.py`, which reproduces the committed JSON offline and exit-0s.
- **And building it answered a discrepancy the anchor had not noticed.** The same page says "about
  190 organisations" signed while its columns total 235. **45 organisations appear in both columns;
  the union is exactly 190.** The prose counts organisations, the columns count signatures.
- **"A1-S′ does not rescue the N stratum" was not the honesty check it was sold as.** Since no
  Stability AI specimen carries a manifest anywhere, *no* rule keyed to an observed positive control
  could move that stratum. The framing is withdrawn; what remains is the forward-only application.
- **Google was outside the strata on a ground never stated as a criterion.** Four of five Black
  Forest Labs specimens are equally indeterminate from a delivery host. The pre-registration fixes
  one provider per stratum and is silent on choosing among eligible signatories; this session chose
  after seeing which route yielded original bytes. That is post-hoc, it is now named as post-hoc, and
  `tools/fold_google_check.py` prices it: folded, n=9, indeterminate 88.9%, **`capture-inconclusive`
  either way**. It moved nothing — which is not a defence, only the reason the reading can stand
  while the discretion is disclosed.

**And a boundary the Skeptic drew that this practice should keep.** The commits are minutes apart, so
commit order proves where things landed in history, not that the author was blind to specimen content
when Rule A1-S was written. What is independently true: the rule was committed while collection was
still running and before the collector's report existed, so no manifest, byte or score had been seen —

reader to discover.

**What the Skeptic did not break:** the pre-registered machinery. The CI-overlap gate, the

disclaimers, and confirmed the ledger table reconciles with the JSON exactly.
