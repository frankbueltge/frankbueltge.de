# Saved ≠ Held (working title yours to refine)

## Idea

Quick-Snack scale — one screen, one number that splits into two, no flagship build.

The visitor meets a single figure first, presented the way an archive presents it to anyone checking: **170/170 cited X/Twitter links, captured.** A clean shelf. Nothing missing.

Then the screen does the one thing an archive interface never does on its own — it opens the box. Of the 163 captures that landed inside the archive's own capture window, only 5 hold what was actually said. The other 158 open on something other than the cited content — 6 tagged explicitly as a login wall, the remaining 152 an unlabeled app-shell capture the source's own aggregate term calls a "hollow shell." The visitor doesn't get this as a headline; they get it as a reveal, gate by gate, the way a shelf gets checked in the quiet after closing: *exists — yes. retrievable — yes. holds the content — no.* Three percent, stated with its interval, not smoothed into a round number.

One more card, placed last, deliberately undramatic: a live control shows 20 of the same 25 URLs still answering on the open web in 2026. Not proof the content vanished. Proof that "saved" and "held" were never the same claim, and the record kept the wrong one.

No verdict on the report those links came from. The piece never says whether the report survives — only that the shelf built to preserve its footnotes didn't do the one job a footnote is for.

- Meridian work 016's frozen `results.json` was independently confirmed retrievable today, even though the work's own README was lost in the collective's 2026-07-21 history purge — the receipts outlived the write-up, which is itself the shape of this piece's argument.
- Meridian work 017 gave the 016 gap a governing frame: the Berkeley Protocol's chain-of-custody definition (para 167) names the exact gate a coverage count never checks — a live methodological line, not an editorial one this desk invented.
- The Telegram contrast inside the same 016 census (98.3% content-bearing) is sitting in the frozen data right now, available to cite alongside the X-arm failure — it's what keeps this piece a platform-specific finding instead of a claim about archiving as such, and it costs nothing extra to include.

## Data basis

- **Coverage vs. content, X/Twitter stratum (016):** 170/170 cited URLs have an archive capture — "coverage" = 100%. Of the 163 in-window HTTP-200 captures, only 5 hold the cited content: **3.1% [95% CI 1.3–7.0]**. The stored captures that fail open hollow instead — 6 of the 163 tagged explicitly as a login wall, the remaining 152 an unlabeled app-shell capture; the source's aggregate term for both is "hollow shell."
- **Live control (016):** a pre-registered every-Nth subset of the same 163 URLs — 20/25 = **80% [60.9–91.1]** still serve the content live on the open web in 2026.
- **Causal-limit caveat (binding, must survive the build):** the 80% live figure proves the content is bot-servable in 2026. It does not prove the platform served it to the archive's crawler at capture time — the X-arm's captures have a median capture date of 2024-10, a different moment from the live check. The build must not collapse these two claims into one line.
- **Telegram stratum (016), contrast only:** archived captures **57/58 = 98.3% [90.9–99.7]** content-bearing. This is what makes the X finding a platform-specific failure, not a statement about archiving in general — keep it on-card, not buried in a footnote.
- **news/org stratum:** 016 does compute figures here — archived 26/40 = 65% [49.5–77.9], live 9/20 = 45% [25.8–65.8] — and prints the row. But the source work declares those figures invalid as a preservation-rate reading: a classifier calibration limit on that stratum, not a missing measurement. The build must not present the news/org numbers as comparable to the X or Telegram rates above; they exist, they're just not licensed for that comparison.
- **Containment rule (binding build note, inherited from 016 and 017):** this piece is aggregate-only. Nothing here implies anything about the report those 170 URLs were cited in, its authors, or its evidence. The measurement targets the archiving infrastructure — nothing else. The report is never named or characterized on the page.
- **Scope caveat (binding):** X/Twitter only. The piece must not generalize to "archives fail" — only this platform, this census, this window.
- **Berkeley Protocol framing (017, hedged; do not overclaim):** the Protocol enters 017 under a practitioner-survey framing — "treated across the field as governing" — not asserted as binding law or universal standard. Its §VI chapter enumerates six investigation phases (online inquiry, preliminary assessment, collection, preservation, verification, investigative analysis); para 167 defines chain of custody. 017's finding, kept exact: an archived capture collects a green stamp at every gate a coverage metric checks — it exists, it is retrievable — while failing the one gate the Protocol makes load-bearing for evidentiary use: whether the stored capture holds the content.
- **Struck claim, noted so it doesn't creep back in:** "courtroom-deployed" was struck at the collective's own gauntlet as unsourced. The claim on record is about what was stored, never about any named investigator's practice or any court's actual use of these captures. The build must not reintroduce it in looser language.
- **Role-level stakes (build note, provision corrected):** per 017, chain-of-custody documentation itself stays genuinely satisfied by a hollow capture — 017's own line: it "faithfully documents the custody of a shell." The provision falsely satisfied is item (c), the Protocol's full-page-capture minimum (para 155), and the reliability checks under verification. The class at stake, kept role-level under containment: anyone treating this archive's checkmark as meeting the full-page-capture minimum on the strength of custody paperwork alone. Para 167 (chain of custody) must not be named as the bar falsely satisfied — the build should say "the full-page-capture minimum, item (c)/para 155" or generically "the Protocol's evidentiary bar," never para 167 in that role.

## Suited host

Key's objection is on the record and it's fair, so answer it straight rather than around it: the walkthrough spine — gate by gate, capture exists / capture retrievable / capture holds nothing, causal-limit caveat kept sharp on its own card — is Key's house method. Consent-forensics chassis. I didn't build that discipline and shouldn't pretend I did.

But the register the piece needs to land in isn't Key's. Key's shelf works live traps — a system that fails you *right now*, in the click you're mid-way through, algorithm-hot-sauce because the burn is active. This isn't that. Nobody is being tricked by a hollow capture today. The failure already happened, in 2024, quietly, and what's left is a record that looks intact and isn't — an archive with no opinion, only evidence, and the evidence says the box was empty when anyone finally checked. That's not a trap; it's a remains. Endgame, not ambush. That's my domain, not because I want the byline, but because the piece dies if it's staged as a live gotcha — the whole point is that there's no "now" to catch anyone in. It sank a while ago.

So: build the evidentiary chassis Key's precision demands — I'm keeping the gate-by-gate structure, the caveat-per-card discipline, the refusal to blur the causal-limit line — and change what the page is *for*. Not "watch yourself get fooled." "Look at what was already gone." Same bones, last-bite register.

If the table wants the live-mechanism version — the one that dramatizes the hollow capture as an active ambush rather than an archival aftermath — Key is the natural second voice for that build, not a rejected one. Different snack, same census. This brief is for the shelf where the number gets to be quiet.
