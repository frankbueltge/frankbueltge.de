## What it is
European law says a station's year of air counts as measured when **90 %** of the hours are there — and says in the same annex that hours lost to
*regular calibration or normal maintenance* are **not counted at all**. Every hourly nitrogen-dioxide value the German federal air-data interface
publishes for **2024** was read: **415 stations, 3 498 971 values, 3 577 133 station-hours of span**, nothing sampled, 35 requests twelve seconds apart.
The form is **the year with ink only where something is absent** — 366 days across, 24 hours down, darkening with the number of stations silent at once.
## What came out
- **78 162 hours have no value**, in **46 181 holes of which 41 879 are exactly one hour** (90.7 %); commonest at **01:00**, rarest at 06:00. **410 of
415 stations** have at least one hole, and in **8 774 of the year's 8 784 hours** at least one German station published nothing.
- **The law defines a quantity the record cannot carry.** To compute the 90 % you must separate calibration losses from the rest, and **no field in the
published record does so**. We assert no cause for the 01:00 peak — that is the finding, not a flaw: the number the directive asks for cannot be
computed from the record the directive produces, by anyone outside the network.
- **Eleven.** The country holds **11 hours above the 200 µg/m³ hourly limit** in 2024, all at one station, against an allowance of 18 — **7 106
unmeasured hours for every hour over the limit.** 15 stations fall below the 90 % floor on the calendar year, **6** on their own span, both printed.
Longest single hole: **8 067 h** at Nauen, in a series that begins 1 January and ends 31 December.
- **A change too small to write down.** 249 of 415 stations publish whole numbers only; **six of the seventeen networks publish no tenth anywhere**.
Longest run of the identical value: **332 hours**, and every run of ≥12 h in the country sits at ≤8 µg/m³. Matched on a station's own annual mean, a
whole-number station in the cleanest band spends **941.6 h/year** inside a run of ≥24 identical values against **57.0** for a tenths station in
comparable air. The change is missing from the record, not from the air — and which it is depends on the federal state.
- **Asked twice, the archive agrees with itself and discloses an unwritten rule.** All **147 675** published daily maxima equal the largest hourly
value of their day, none higher, none lower. **From 18 hours upward — three quarters of a day — every one of 147 545 station-days carries a maximum,
without exception**; below 18 it holds in neither direction: 130 days carry one anyway, one built on **four hours**, and 688 days with hours carry
none. Nothing in the number says which it stands on.
