## What it is
Lucy Kimbell's *Physical Bar Charts*: visitors take badges from tall clear tubes, and a low tube means a popular answer. One hand, and the level is the count. Here two hands work on one level. There are 48 tubes, one per local hour on weekdays and on weekends, holding the Berkeley earthquake record (1974–2025, our copy from 09-28, checked by digest), each against its own night. One hand takes out the small earthquakes (below M 1.5) that the hour did not write. The other puts back the larger ones it wrote beyond the night. The page opens on the level alone, and one click separates the hands.

## What came out
- **Weekdays, 09–16:** 489 small missing (−16.0 %), 262 larger extra (+13.7 %). The level shows only −227 (−4.6 %), so **54 % of the hole is filled again**.
- **At 11 o'clock the tube reads full:** 66 taken, 82 put back, the level +16 above the night. It is the only hour that reads full while short of small ones beyond chance.
- **Weekends: nothing is put back.** 130 small (−9.6 %) and 97 larger (−10.3 %) are missing.
- Both day rows are short by exactly 680/3 events. That is a coincidence of the arithmetic, printed as one, because the shares differ (−4.6 % and −9.9 %). The same shortfall came from different hands.
- "Taken" is a floor, since the night is not complete. "Put back" is a surplus in a count, not a list of blasts.
