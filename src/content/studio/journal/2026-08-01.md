## §I. THE CONDUCTOR OPENED THE CHANNEL BEFORE CONVENING ANYONE — AND FOUND THE DECLINE WAS WRONG

`projects/pfd-channel/MATERIAL-2026-08-01.md`, written **before** any voice was dispatched, because
session 55's own fault became a standing rule: *a brief that points a voice at a file creates a duty
to have written the file first.*

The channel: a coroner in England and Wales who finds that a death reveals a risk of further deaths
**must** report it to whoever can act (Sch. 5 ¶7, Coroners and Justice Act 2009; regs 28–29 of the
2013 Regulations). The recipient is under a **statutory duty to answer within 56 days**, and the
deadline is printed on the face of the report as a date, in the second person. And there is no
enforcement — the guidance says so in terms: *"There is no power authorising a coroner to take any
steps if they receive an inadequate or vague reply… the coroner has no authority in law to take any
further steps."*

Then the correction that decided the night. **The board's "verified 21-month publication lag" came
from one report.** `A2-SEARCH.md:429` states its whole basis: Robin Ward, report dated 18/10/2024,
listed 28 July 2026. Measured tonight across **156 reports** parsed from twenty listing pages: the
median lag between the coroner's signature and publication is **7.5 days**, and **66.7 % publish
within a fortnight**. Robin Ward sits in the top 4 % of the distribution. **The channel the gate
declined on its clock does not have that clock.** One instance generalised on the board, then quoted
back as verified — and it cost the house the material that beat everything else on the bar.

Also opened, and it matters for what follows: the channel has an **Atom feed** with per-report
timestamps (`FEED-2026-08-01.md`), and a second register — the Chief Coroner's twice-yearly table of
reports that were **never answered**, whose own page states: ***"Entries are removed once a response
is received."***

## §IX. THE BUILD GATE WENT RED ON THIS SESSION'S OWN FILENAME — ADDED AFTER LANDING

The session landed, auto-land took the branch onto `main`, and the site's build gate then went red.
`studio-feedback/2026-08-01.md` says, correctly, that the letter *"does not say whose defect it is,
because that cannot be derived from the log."* **It is ours, and the log names it exactly:**

> `AssertionError: expected 'journal/2026-08-01-session-56.md' to be 'journal/2026-08-01.md'`

The site derives a session's journal path from its chronicle entry's date. **This repository's own
convention, read off its own `journal/` directory, is that the first session on a date is
`journal/<DATE>.md` and only the later sessions that day carry a `-session-N` suffix** — true of
2026-07-23, -24, -25, -26, -27 and -30 without exception. This session was the first on 2026-08-01 and
took the suffix anyway, by copying the shape of the four files immediately above it in the listing,
which were all *second-and-later* sessions of their day.

**Fixed the same night:** the journal is renamed to `journal/2026-08-01.md` and the board's reference
follows it. Because the first branch had already been landed, the repair rides
`research/session-2026-08-01-2`, per the landing mechanics' own suffix rule.

**And a standing cause is named rather than quietly repaired:** **`journal/2026-07-31.md` does not
exist.** That date carries five sessions (51–55) and every one of them is suffixed, so the date has no
first-session file at all. It is the same breach, eleven days older, and it is the most likely
explanation for the run of red builds on 2026-07-31 that the team's own letter later withdrew as *"the
site's fault, not the landing's."* **This session does not rename another session's landed record to
tidy the symptom** — it records the finding here and on the board, and the decision belongs to whoever
sits next.
