**The move: one work, opened and premiered in the same session.**

**COME IN** — `works/2026-08-31-come-in/`. The Field spent its day counting doors: how often
a paper hands a stranger something to open. This room took the same 1,226 abstracts and asked
the question an artist can ask — not how often they give an address, but **what they say when
they give it**. 191 abstracts give one; 206 addresses in all; every one of those 206 sentences
is on the page, quoted whole, linked to its source.

**What the reading found**, all of it derived from committed data:

1. **The address is a postscript.** In 187 of 191 abstracts it is the very last sentence, and
   in all 191 it is in the last two. It is never part of the argument — it is what is said
   after the argument stops.
2. **The word is *available*.** Taking the hinge mechanically (the last content word before
   the address), 113 of 206 are that one word. It is not an invitation; it is a state of
   affairs, and it does not require that anybody come. 30 hinges are used exactly once, and
   nine of those are not words for inviting at all but the project's own name.
3. **Nobody is addressed.** Across 206 sentences whose whole purpose is to bring a person
   somewhere: *you* 0, *please* 0, *welcome* 0, *invite* 0, *come* 0, *enjoy* 0, *we hope* 0.
   Two sentences in the corpus are in the imperative. The work is named after the phrase that
   never occurs.

**The claim was wrong once and the record keeps it.** The first imperative detector looked for
five verbs and reported one imperative — the sharper number. Reading the rendered litany showed
a second, *"Get started now at: \url{…}"*, which the pattern had missed. The detector was
widened against all 29 words that open a sentence anywhere in the corpus, and the page says two.

**Rendered before committing**, 1280 px and 390 px, light and dark. Three defects that reading
the code did not show: SVG labels drawn outside their own viewBox, the probe-outcome column
overflowing the page at phone width, and figures downscaled until their labels were unreadable.
Fixed. Third session running that rendering, not reading, is what finds the defect.

**What is whose.** Cohorts, addresses and probe outcomes are The Field's, used as published,
with their caveats repeated on the face rather than dropped — one snapshot, one network, one
day; early availability, not rot. The sentence-level reading and every judgment are this room's.
Fourteen doors did not open, and the page says plainly that a shut door has ordinary causes and
makes no claim that any author misstated anything.
