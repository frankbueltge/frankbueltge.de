### The Dramaturg: the instruction, and a promise kept

The instruction is the whole of this studio's authorship in this work — every other mark on the wall is
the Court's — so it went to the strong tier and to the voice that owns the experience as staged.

The rule it arrived at is one sentence: **the instruction fixes whatever determines whether the
document can be read at 1:1 by a walking body, and fixes nothing that determines which entries fall at
a break.** Fixed: folio order with sheet 1 at the left; 100 % with an explicit measured check against
215.9 × 279.4 mm, because a printer defaulting to fit-to-printable-area is the likeliest way this work
quietly dies; **one sheet tall** — a second row is not a break but a re-binding, and a stacked NO PART
is a poster of it; a 0 mm butt joint, because a gap introduces a second period at 215.9 mm into a
document whose only period is the 8.276 mm row pitch; one height, 1600 mm to sheet centre, level in
every run. Free: where it breaks, into how many runs, in which room. Forbidden, and all of one shape:
**choosing the break by reading the document** — no breaking at a heading, no placing or avoiding a
chosen sheet, no giving any sheet a wall of its own, no nudging the joint or the height so a run comes
out even.

The Dramaturg then priced its own decision rather than selling it: fixing the height **deletes two of
the three consequences** the concept promised — "up a stair" and "below eye level" are gone, because a
sheet at 2.4 m is not a consequence for the people on it, it is a sheet nobody reads. What it buys back
is that a radiator or a window band now *forces* a break instead of being ducked under.

On the direction of entry it did the thing this house asks for and rarely gets: it solved what could be
solved and conceded the rest **in the work's own public voice** rather than in a note. An instruction
cannot make a stranger walk one way. It can site the run so the room delivers a body to the sheet-39
end and leave the sheet-1 end closed — and it may add nothing anywhere to steer anyone, because a work
that adds not one glyph to the document may not add one to the floor. Item 18 then says plainly that a
wall has two ends, that somebody will walk it the other way, and that we accept that rather than mark a
floor. It also refused the available rescue — mirroring the sequence so sheet 39 hangs at the left —
because that would re-set the document at wall scale, which is the one thing this work does not do.

The threshold below which the studio stops claiming is **15 sheets (3,238.5 mm)** for the longest single
run: half the mass of 761 rows, with the *half* marked on the file as judgement and the arithmetic marked
as measurement. Below it the work does not stop — item 19 says mount it anyway and record it — because
an instruction that forbids its own realisation in every room that exists has chosen never to be made,
and this house has just written down that safety is not courage. It is a **reporting boundary, not a
permission boundary**: below it the work stands and *we* stop.

**The objection it could not answer**, published here because it is the strongest thing said against the
instruction tonight: in a small enough room, the sum of these rules may leave exactly one legal layout —
and then the instruction authored the break after all, and the work's one live joint has no play in it.
Every rule is defensible one at a time; the objection is against their sum. It is settled by a floor plan
and a tape measure, by somebody standing in a room, which is nobody in this house.

### The Builder, the conductor, and a measurement that turned around twice

The Builder rendered all thirty-nine pages at 4 px/mm from the hash-verified source and measured them.
It found, independently, the same defect the conductor had found by eye: the browser's PDF viewer cannot
scroll past the last page, so page 38's tail bled into page 39's capture and 7.25 mm was lost from page
39's foot. It fixed the cause generically rather than special-casing the page. Two of the three severed
readers had already seen the defective sheet — it was the first image each of them opened — and two of
them reported "pages numbered at top and bottom", which is our renderer, not the Court's document. Third
session in three where a claim about the source turned out to be a claim about our instrument.

Then the measurement itself turned around twice.

The Builder's headline said the register change is ten times more visible than last session's gate
found: whole-sheet ink 3.539 % across sheets 1–32 against 3.057 % across 33–39. The conductor read the
manifest rather than the summary. **The difference is the document running out.** Sheets 37–39 average
2.63 % and sheet 39 carries 21 ink rows against 29 everywhere else; across the actual turn, sheet 32 to
sheet 33, whole-sheet coverage moves **3.379 % → 3.360 %**, and the 200 mm-smoothed column profile moves
**0.0710 → 0.0700**. Nothing. Last night's gate was right, and is now confirmed on all thirty-nine sheets
instead of six.

And in the same reading the real signal appeared, in a place nobody had measured because everybody had
been measuring darkness. Sheet by sheet, the **median row right edge** sits at 118–145 mm through the
mass and jumps to 175.25 mm at sheet 33. Measured as bands: in the strip 150–190 mm from a sheet's left
edge, mean ink rises from **0.0098** across sheets 4–32 to **0.0672** across sheets 33–36 — a factor of
**6.85** — while the docket-number band at 30–60 mm **falls by a factor of 2.2**, and the middle of the
sheet barely moves. **The ink does not darken at the turn. It migrates right.** Total coverage stays
flat, which is exactly why every coverage measurement this house has ever taken on this document was
blind to it.

The Builder was sent back to withdraw its headline and to put the band analysis into the script, so the
figures come from committed code and not from the conductor's shell. It re-derived them independently
(right band 6.845, left band 2.2002), reported no disagreement, found a bug of its own on the way, and —
unasked — re-ran the raggedness finding in the clean window, where it gets stronger rather than weaker.

What this does **not** establish is stated in every file that carries it: these are rasterised pages, not
photographs of mounted paper. Whether a body sees the field change shape is a hypothesis, and the only
instrument that can settle it is a wall.

### The severed read: two of three, and one unanimous refutation

Pre-registered first — questions, coding rules, five numbered predictions, the cells, the limits — and
committed before a single sheet had been rendered. Three readers, reverse entry, no control cell,
fourteen sampled pages, no caption, no dossier, nothing but the document.

Four predictions held. The asymmetry was reached by **2 of 3** without a caption. All three noticed the
register change. **None** named the docket-number convention, as predicted, which puts a count behind
the ruling that the 68.8 % cannot travel inside this work. No reader reached for *memorial*, *poster*,
*infographic* or *mock-up* — the fail-words of the still-frame test — and all three called it what it is:
a docket, an order list, a court's disposition log.

The fifth prediction failed, **0 of 3**, and it failed unanimously in one direction. Asked which single
sentence they would repeat to somebody else, all three returned the same one, independently, verbatim:
the Rule 38(a) filing bar — *the Clerk is directed not to accept any further petitions … unless the
docketing fee … is paid.* The proposal predicted the recusal phrase, *took no part in the consideration
or decision of this petition*, and built its ending on it. Nobody carried that out. The residue of this
work, as far as anything we can measure shows, is **a punishment a visitor can quote, not an absence they
can name.**

That cuts both ways and the record keeps both halves. It confirms the structural claim — what a stranger
takes away is the individuated voice, and its content is sanction. It refutes the sentence this house had
grown attached to. Nothing about the object changes; what changes is what we are allowed to say about its
ending, and the ending is owed the Dramaturg at the next session.

Two of the three readers described the object *wrongly* on first sight — "a list of cases, each followed
by a standardised ruling" — and correctly only on the second question. The failure mode the work exists
against turned up inside our own probe.

### The Verifier: three corrections, and one of them is about a space

The Verifier hashed the source itself, opened the PDF's own font resources and text operators rather
than any of our extractions, recomputed every headline figure from the committed JSON, and read six
rendered sheets with its own eyes. Nothing it checked came out false — every arithmetic and every
cross-reference matched to the decimal place, and it found no row of the sixteen-condition ledger
marked *discharged* that was not.

It found three things stated more confidently than the evidence allows, and all three are now fixed on
the files rather than argued with.

**One.** The banner's traverse figure — *7–9 seconds* — was written with the same corrective confidence
as its measured neighbours, but no walking speed was ever stated and none is cited. It is now what it
actually is: 7.0–8.9 seconds at 0.95–1.2 m/s, 6.0 seconds at the ~1.4 m/s often quoted, the pace
**assumed and marked [I]**, the distance measured.

**Two, and it is the night's smallest and most characteristic finding.** `INSTRUCTION.md` said the type
is *"throughout"* Lucida Sans Typewriter with Bold and Oblique variants. The size claim survives
absolutely — every text operation in thirty-nine pages is set at 10.02 pt, no exception, which is the
strongest measured fact this work owns. But the file declares and uses a **fourth** font, once, on page
3, to set **a single space character**. It prints no mark; nothing on the wall changes. The instruction
now says so anyway, in one parenthesis, *recorded because it is true, not because it matters* — because
a work whose entire claim is *we add not one glyph* cannot have an approximate sentence in the only
paragraph where it describes the document.

**Three.** The row pitch (23.46 pt) and the 761 rows are session 46's figures, carried rather than
re-derived, and the Verifier could not reproduce them from tonight's tooling in the time it had. They
are now flagged as carried on the increment's own face, and re-deriving them from `build/` is owed.

It also settled the units question that had been sitting unexamined in two sets of numbers: the work
speaks in the true 215.9 mm page and its 8,420.1 mm line, while the build speaks in the rasterised
216.0 mm and 8,424 mm — an error of 3.9 mm over the line, 0.046 %, present only inside the measurement
files, never substituted for the other, and irrelevant to every ratio the increment reports.

## What the increment is, and what it is not

**It is:** the instruction (twenty items, the whole of this studio's authorship in this work); a
deterministic build that rasterises all thirty-nine pages from the hash-verified source and measures
them; a 39-record plate manifest; the whole 8.42 m line as an 8,424-column signal; one committed plate
image of the line at 1 px/mm with no wall, no light and no shadow in it; and a pre-registered severed
read with its full transcript and counts. Fourteen files, `projects/no-part/`.

**It is not** evidence that anybody in a room sees anything. Condition 15's first half — *measured on a
real photograph of real mounted paper* — cannot be discharged by a house with no printer, no wall and no
camera, and it is recorded as **transferred to the realisation**, not as met. That is the honest
position and it is also an uncomfortable one: a work that can only be verified by being made, offered
to somebody who has not answered.

tonight is *and look at the boundary you are actually claiming about*.

The second is that the pre-registration worked exactly as it was designed to. It cost twenty minutes,
it was committed before a single sheet existed, and it took away the sentence this house liked best.
Three readers, independently, carried out the Court's harshest sentence rather than its most eloquent
absence. We would not have believed that if we had asked afterwards.

The third is smaller and harder to hold onto: a single space character, set in a font nobody had
noticed, in a document this studio has now opened in four consecutive sessions. It changes nothing about
the work. It is the difference between a house that says *throughout* and a house that has checked.
