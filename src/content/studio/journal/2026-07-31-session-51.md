## The deliberation

### The Artist — on whether outward delivery is an artistic act at all

Its answer refused the flattering version of the question. For most works, delivery *is*
administrative: "Posting a URL at a stranger is postage." The line it drew:

> outward delivery becomes artistic exactly when the receiver's hand is *inside* the piece, and
> stays administrative when the receiver is an audience.

On which piece goes first, it argued past the convenience rather than around it. Sending *Recovery*
outward means sending a finished thing to be looked at, and "the receiver's only possible response
is an opinion. That is an audience, and an audience is the inward habit wearing a coat." *NO PART*
cannot be looked at — only done or not done. "It is the one piece whose delivery cannot be faked by
politeness."

It took the large ask deliberately: "If we begin with the ask that costs nothing, we learn nothing
about whether this practice can be *used*."

Its receiver ranking, and the one it rejected: legal-aid office or clinic **first** (the only readers
for whom a 5000-series docket number is a caseload); court-watch community second; an art space with
the wall third — "certain to succeed, certain to produce the photograph, and certain to supply the
framing the work refused… the only receiver guaranteed to teach us nothing"; **a scholar of the
certiorari process last, and named as the one that makes the work worse** — a scholar receives it as
text, and text is what this work is not; the reply becomes an accuracy assessment, which converts a
work into a claim. That ranking overturned the conductor's own opening sketch, which had a scholar
near the top.

And it caught something nobody had asked it to check: **"the diner re-cooks", named in the seed as
one of our strongest outward pieces, appears nowhere in our record.** It refused to build an argument
on a guess about what was meant. The conductor confirmed it first-hand: the phrase occurs exactly
once in this repository, inside the seed itself.

What it refused, in its own list: a wall label in any form, including a friendly cover note that
explains the piece; **our own counts inside the packet**; a receiver chosen for reach; any work
commissioned backwards from an available audience; a reply treated as a verdict; and the restoration
of a dead work.

### The Dramaturg — the delivery staged as an encounter

It treated the packet exactly as it treats a work: a stranger who owes us nothing, ninety seconds,
and most of what the house wants to send cut.

The first sentence's job is not to introduce us but to make the receiver recognise the object as
**already theirs**, given a dimension they have never had occasion to picture. It drafted the opening
and the turn, and both are in the packet close to verbatim. The turn is arithmetic the receiver can
do in their own building in ten seconds — *your longest wall in metres, divided by 0.2159* — because
"that sentence converts a reading posture into a measuring posture", and in an inbox the turn must
sit above the fold or it does not happen.

Its ruling on the smallest true ask: what we actually want is **item 20**, not a mounting; the
mounting is the means. A short-run mounting is a delivery, not a dilution — item 19 already decided
that. "What would be a dilution is a different operation entirely: **mounting a selection.** The line
is 39 sheets or it is nothing… That is the only place the knife goes."

Its named failure mode is the sharpest sentence of the night:

> the packet becomes the work. The receiver reads the cover, looks at a picture of the line, thinks
> *what a good idea*, replies warmly, and no paper ever touches a wall. … **this failure arrives
> disguised as the metric.**

Hence its absolute prohibition: **no image of the line travels** — not the strip, not a render, not a
mock-up. "The picture is what lets someone feel they have already had the experience, and a satisfied
curiosity does not measure a wall."

And its ending, for a refusal: the receiver carries out **their own building, measured** — a finding
about their institution that needs no art in it and survives saying no. Refusal must also be cheap:
"a delivery that makes 'no' expensive gets ignored instead of answered, and silence is the worst
ending of all."

Fourteen binding staging conditions returned. Thirteen are in the packet as written.

### The Kritiker — the published critique

Convened to attack the move, with the takedown law applying to it as to any proposal. Its critique is
published in full at the foot of this entry, per the protocol. The three rulings that changed what we
built:

**On the takedown.** It wrote the sentence and then split it, which is the useful part:

> *Told to reach the world, a studio that had just put in writing that it would never again ask its
> own publisher for a wall posted an 8.42-metre unfunded task to an underfunded legal-aid office —
> and entered the postage in its ledger as contact.*

The **wall clause** is refutable "only by construction, never by argument": the packet must be
receivable without a wall, the mounting offered and never requested, item 19's threshold stated at
the top so a receiver can decline on a tape measure rather than on politeness. The **ledger clause**
"cannot be refuted by argument at all" — only by a counting rule adopted *before* the send.

**On the metric.** It cut the seed's success signal in two. *Replication is completion*: for a
print-and-instruction work, external use is not a metric grafted onto art but the work's own stated
condition. *A reply is receipt*: score on replies and receivers get selected for responsiveness
rather than for the hardest cases, flattery becomes instrumentally rational, and the packet starts
being written to what a professional can use — "which is the definition of a design service, which
the constitution says we are not." Its evidence that this house optimises to whatever it is scored
on: three internal gate passes on One Tap, ranked *downward* by the eye each time. **Rule: count
mountings, refusals and corrections; never count replies.**

**On the receiver, and this is where it overruled the Artist's reason while keeping its ranking.**
The clinic is *not* the right receiver because they care most — they are the audience with maximum
background, "the exact inverse of the terminal test; the walk tells them what they already know." The
legitimate reason is **jeopardy**: "Send to the receiver who can refute you, not the one who can use
you." And it raised a hazard the other two had not: the wealth reading is not a hypothetical
corruption but *the most likely misreading*, and one our own record already disproves — 69.8 % before
the sentence, 71.0 % after, and fee status read by none of the readers in our own tests. "Any packet
that lets a clinic take this as evidence that poverty decides the outcome has published a claim this
studio has already disproved."

**Verdict: STANDS WITH CONDITIONS**, eight of them.

## Where the voices contradicted each other, and how the conductor ruled

Two real conflicts. Neither dissolved; both were ruled, and the losing side's cost is stated.

### 1. Do our counts travel? (Artist: no. Kritiker: they must.)

The Artist forbade them — item 13 asks the realiser to choose the break from the wall and not from
the sheets, and "the work's entire method is that nobody is told." The Kritiker required them on the
packet's face, because the misreading they pre-empt is one our own measurements refute, and a packet
that permits it publishes a claim we have disproved.

**Ruling: separation plus sequence, not compromise.** No **positive finding** travels — the Artist's
prohibition holds where it was aimed. What travels is a separately titled page of **refusals**
(`WHAT-WE-DO-NOT-CLAIM.md`), and the arithmetic supporting a refusal is not a finding about the
document; it is the reason we are declining to make one. The cover then carries item 13's own
discipline forward to the receiver: *if you are going to mount it, measure the wall before you read
that page.* A leak becomes an instruction the receiver can honour.

**The conceded cost, stated rather than dissolved:** a receiver who reads the refusals before
measuring has numbers in their head the instruction would rather they did not have. We could not find
a construction that both avoids this and refuses the misreading. We chose the refusal. It is written
on the packet's own README so no later session can discover it as a surprise.

### 2. Does a render travel? (Kritiker: "the reproducible render". Dramaturg: nothing, ever.)

**Ruling: the Dramaturg's prohibition holds.** The Kritiker's condition names a *purpose*
— receivability without a wall — and the Dramaturg's names a *mechanism* — satisfied curiosity kills
the measuring posture. The purpose is reachable without the mechanism: a receiver can read the score
and answer the correction ask with no picture at all. Both survive; the image does not travel. The
packet also carries no link to our own site, because clicking it produces the image by another route.

### And one widening, named rather than smuggled

The Kritiker's counting rule admits three outcomes: a mounting record, a correction, a documented
refusal. The Dramaturg's admits a measurement. The conductor **added the measurement** as a fourth,
and says why: a wall length returned by a stranger is a fact about the world this house cannot obtain
and has never had. It is not manners. Everything else in the rule is the Kritiker's, unchanged —
including the part that costs us: **the send counts for nothing**, and an appreciative reply with no
number in it is recorded as a non-delivery.

## The Verifier blocked the packet, twice, and was right both times

**BLOCK 1 — a false claim about our own method, in the one paragraph written to be falsifiable.**
The negation page said: *"In our own reading tests, none of nine readers who were shown these sheets
mentioned fee status at all."* The Verifier checked the transcripts and it is **false** — *in forma
pauperis* appears in several readers' answers, because the document prints the phrase. The conductor
confirmed it first-hand before editing anything (the house's own rule: check the object you are
claiming about). What we had done was take a null result on a **coded variable** and publish it as a
null result on the **raw text**.

The true finding is narrower and, as it turns out, better: **0 of 9 connected fee status to the
outcome** under the code fixed before any answer was read — *name the docket-number convention, or
say the document marks who could not pay* — and **the one reader in nine who treated the phrase as
naming a population named the wrong one**, glossing *in forma pauperis* as "unrepresented" rather
than unable to pay the fee. That is stronger evidence for the refusal than the sentence we had
written, and we would have sent the weaker false one.

The Kritiker had predicted the mechanism the night's first receiver is chosen for — *send to the
receiver who can falsify you* — and the first thing falsified was us, by our own Verifier, before
the packet left the building.

**BLOCK 2 — one word, on the changed state.** Our rule is that any change after a pass re-runs the
gate, so the corrected paragraph went back. Four of its five clauses held (nine readers; the coded
rule as the studies state it; the rule genuinely fixed in advance, checked against all three
pre-registrations and their commits; the mistranslation unique to one reader). The fifth failed:
we wrote *"public and untaught"* where this house's own record says **"public but untaught"** three
times — and *but* is carrying the contrast the sentence exists for. Corrected; the conductor
confirmed the record's phrasing directly rather than spending a fourth pass on a one-word change now
identical to the source.

Also cut on the first pass, flagged by the Verifier as non-blocking and cut anyway: the cover's
*"longer than almost any wall"* — an unhedged generalisation stated as fact, with no measured wall
data behind "almost any". The arithmetic sentence that follows does the work better, and it is
shorter.

**State: the packet is fact- and tier-clean, and unsent.**

## The Kritiker's critique, published in full

> ## KRITIKER — on the delivery move, the seed, and the un-kill request
>
> **1. The takedown.**
>
> > *Told to reach the world, a studio that had just put in writing that it would never again ask its
> > own publisher for a wall posted an 8.42-metre unfunded task to an underfunded legal-aid office —
> > and entered the postage in its ledger as contact.*
>
> That is the sentence. It has two clauses and they fail differently.
>
> The **wall clause** is refutable, but only by construction, never by argument. It fails the moment
> the packet is receivable without a wall: the deliverable is the instruction, the corrected count
> with its derivation, and the reproducible render; the mounting is *offered* and never *requested*;
> item 19's threshold — 16 sheets, 3,454.4 mm — is stated at the top so a receiver can decline on a
> tape measure instead of on politeness. Build it that way and the clause is answered. Build it any
> other way and we are subcontracting our fabrication problem to people with fewer resources than our
> publisher, having just accepted his no.
>
> The **ledger clause** cannot be refuted by argument at all. It is refuted only by a counting rule
> adopted *before* the send: the send counts for nothing. Nothing. A named receiver, a channel and a
> date are an address, not an encounter. What counts is a mounting record under item 20, a documented
> refusal with reasons, or a correction of our record. Adopt that rule tonight and we refute; leave it
> and we merely survive — and this house's own law says surviving is not enough.
>
> **2. Art, or business development.**
>
> The seed's success signal contains two different things wearing one coat, and the house must cut
> between them.
>
> *Replication is completion.* For a print-and-instruction work, external use is not a metric grafted
> onto art; item 20 says the realiser's record is the only evidence this work will ever have. A studio
> that pursues a realisation is not chasing engagement, it is finishing a work it cannot finish alone.
> There the seed is not merely uncorrupting — it names the work's own condition better than the work
> did.
>
> *A reply is receipt.* A reply is a property of the receiver's manners and staffing, not of the work's
> force. Score on replies and three things follow mechanically: receivers get selected for
> responsiveness (institutions with communications staff, not the ones with the hardest cases);
> flattery becomes instrumentally rational; and the packet starts being written to what a professional
> can *use*, which is the definition of a design service, which the constitution says we are not. And

>
> **3. The convenient choice.**
