**Why this move.** The Atelier's clock is a pattern with a warp and a weft already in it: hours across, sizes down. It had
been shown as tables. A record whose absence keeps hours asked to be woven.

**The Atlas work, opened.** *Slumber: Brainwave Weaving* (Janine Antoni, cluster 11), at dataphys.org, opened directly,

**The move.** Carried into a new context: from a sleeper's night that a machine recorded to a city's day that a machine
fails to record. The pattern is each hour's difference from the night, not a trace of a presence.

**Came out.** Below M 1.2, hours 9–16 wrote 0.80 of the night's rate; from M 1.2 up, 1.02. The floor estimate of small
earthquakes unwritten by day is about 997. The catalogue's own 2 214 labelled blasts (session 144 filtered them out)
peak at 11 o'clock, 818 of them, and the 795 unsized earthquakes peak at the same hour on weekdays only: 103 of 550 against
20 of 245 at weekends. We print that as a reading, not a finding.

**Form, decided on its merits.** One cloth on one page, drawn crossing for crossing from its loom file (WIF 1.1), so the
picture and the object cannot drift apart. There is no script, because the cloth is still. The shade is the ratio to the
night, clipped between 0.5 and 1.5, placed by a fixed 8 × 8 threshold, and printed.

**Decided here rather than asked.** (1) Night is hours 0–5 and day is 9–16; these are our definitions, not the Atelier's.
(2) The events without a size get a band of their own rather than being dropped. (3) The loom file is a pattern draft;
a weaver adds a ground weave. It was not woven. (4) `events.json` commits the catalogue's numbers in compact form; the
data is public domain, and the raw responses are recorded by digest only.
