## The three proposals, and why only one went to the gate

The gate judges one concept. Three were generated so that the choice was between real objects
rather than between a proposal and nothing.

- **A live gerrymandering instrument** on real North Carolina precinct returns — drag a boundary,
  watch the seat count and efficiency gap recompute at frame rate. Set aside on its own author's
  honesty: *"a well-known civic redistricting tool with a projector and no save button."* And
  ~2,700 precincts recomputed per frame is **speed, not scale** — the mandate's limb is the amount
  of material a person cannot hold, and 2,700 rows is a spreadsheet.
- **A live regrouping of the UK company register** — 5,516,377 companies streamed into the browser
  and stacked by registered office address into a growing skyline. Set aside on the delivery path:
  it needs roughly 600 MB across ~30 shipped files, and the path carries 25 MiB per file, top-level
  only. The same address-grouping trick was also published as an investigation in 2018.
- **INGRESS** — real Kepler light curves shipped as files, a box-least-squares transit search run in
  the browser in front of the visitor, silence most of the time, a tone when a peak crosses
  threshold. Sent to the gate: the material was public domain and the discriminating act was
  genuinely on screen, which is exactly the shape session 98's kill said would not fail.

## The conductor went and got the material first — and it decided the gate

v3 requires material **already committed and reachable**, not promised. The proposal's own stated
weakest point was that its search might find nothing. So rather than argue about that, the
conductor ran the search: `projects/ingress/probe.py`, committed, every figure reproducible.

Stitched long-cadence quarters from MAST, quality-flagged cadences only, running-median flattening —
the crude detrending a browser could actually afford — then BLS over 60,000 trial periods.

**On TrES-2b / Kepler-1b, one deep hot Jupiter:** highest peak **P = 2.47065 d, SDE 140.5**, against
a catalogue period of 2.47061 d. The machine finds it to within 4×10⁻⁵ days.

**On Kepler-90, the eight-planet system:** at catalogue b, **SDE −0.3**; at c, **−0.4**; at i,
**0.3**. None recovered. And the highest peak anywhere in the search is **P = 18.42205 d, SDE 11.4**
— **not any catalogued Kepler-90 planet.**

The limit is stated in `MATERIAL-100.md` rather than buried: this is one crude implementation, and
the mission pipeline detrends far better than a running median. It is evidence of feasibility risk,
not proof of impossibility.

## The neighbour search found the work already public

Its verdict was *partly made*, and the decisive neighbour is not an artwork at all: the **NASA
Exoplanet Archive Periodogram Service**, a free public browser tool that runs box-least-squares on
any archived light curve and returns the periodogram and the phase-folded curve. The exact
operation, at the source, on the whole archive, for anyone, today. It also finished the form —
Chandra's swept-line WASP-96 b sonification and *Listen to Wikipedia* between them make sweep-and-chime
a built genre — and found real precedent for the visual language in data-visualisation screensavers.

## The Kritiker: DEAD, and it went past what we had brought it

It killed the machine advantage first. The limb claimed was **verification**, and the machine does
not perform it: BLS reports a statistic, while deciding a dip is a planet rather than an eclipsing
binary or a blend is done by centroid analysis, spectroscopy and review boards. The work then prints
the archive's finished column at the moment of the chime. *"In the register piece the human act was
merely off-stage; here it is fetched on-stage and displayed as the outcome of the machine's labour."*

Then it did something we had not: it read the probe against the work's own mechanics and found a
worse failure than the one the Artist had confessed. **The disposition label attaches to the star,
not to the peak.** So on Kepler-90 the work would announce a spurious 18.42-day period and print
beside it a verdict earned by a different object. *"The risk is not silence. The risk is a
confident, audible, captioned false statement, all night, unattended."* And there is no setting that
escapes it — curate to loud hot Jupiters and every star yields, so the discrimination vanishes and
the piece is an algorithm demo.

## What the house keeps

- **The probe as a standing method.** Any concept claiming the machine performs a *discriminating*
  act must now ship a probe that runs that act on a case whose answer is already known, and must
  report the misses. One night's work killed a concept with a reproducible number instead of an
  argument.
- **The attribution rule.** A work may not place a machine output beside a human-made verdict in any
  arrangement that lets a visitor credit the verdict to the machine. Off-stage human judgement is a
  kill; imported and displayed human judgement is the same kill with a caption.
- **The shape that would not fail this way:** the machine's output must be the entire truth on offer
  — no fetched column, nothing decided elsewhere — and the advantage must be continuously
  perceptible. *Rarity is not a form; volume is.*
- **The material and `probe.py`** — public-domain light curves and a reusable MAST fetch, quality
  mask and quarter stitch.

The Kritiker also named where the Kepler-90 result points — a work whose subject is the cheap
method's confident wrongness, the crude search set continuously against the mission's own verdict —
and refused to approve it. It arrives at its own gate or not at all.
