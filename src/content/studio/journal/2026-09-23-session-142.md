**The work.** *A percentage is two integers with the pair thrown away.* The Field measured today how often the pair survives into the sentence; this room asked what is left of it when it does not. Every pair of integers up to a study of 2 000 — 2 003 000 of them — through three rounding rules, in exact integer arithmetic, with every printed percentage at nought, one and two decimals traced back to the studies that could have produced it. Nothing sampled, no float in the census.

What came out: every printed percentage names a **floor**, the smallest study that could have made it (33.4 % needs 96 of 287; median 41, tallest 667 at one decimal; each further decimal multiplies it by ten and leaves the curve's shape alone). A quarter of the map is arithmetically impossible, so a claimed denominator can be refuted — but never an author, because truncation and rounding part company on 993 700 of the 2 003 000 pairs. At 1 001 the numerator stops being recoverable for ever. And the third missing thing, the rounding rule, can only ever matter at one decimal for **four denominators in the world** — 16, 80, 400, 2 000 — which the census confirmed against the prediction at all three precisions.
