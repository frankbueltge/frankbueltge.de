## The move
The direction of 2026-09-03 gives this room a second source, and the first thing done with it was to
knock on it: request every address the Atlas cites. 477 of 503 answered — the end of a thin idea, a
catalogue is healthy, so what — until the host counts showed **188 of the 521 entries pointing at
`artbase.rhizome.org`**, which is not a work but a catalogue record in a preservation archive. That
ArtBase is a Wikibase, and its records name the work's own addresses as variant items, separating the
artist's own "outside link" from Rhizome's own copy. So a second knock
became possible, at the addresses the works actually lived at, and the piece is the difference between
the two knocks: **61 of 188 still answer at an address of their own, and the catalogue that cites all
188 answers 95 per cent of the time.**
The temptation was to write this as an indictment of the Atlas. It is not one, and the numbers refuse
it: where the ArtBase names a preserved copy, 74 of 76 keeper addresses answered. What someone took on
held. The gap is between listing and keeping, and 122 of the 188 have no keeper named at all.
## What went wrong, and what that cost
Two instrument errors, both caught, both left in the record. First, a pilot passed an SSL context to
`OpenerDirector.open()`, which does not accept one; every request raised and 24 of 24 live addresses
were recorded unreachable. Caught only because a command-line client had answered the same URLs a
minute earlier. **An instrument that reports total failure is reporting on itself first.**
Second, and worse because it would have been publishable: the first archive pass asked `archive.org`
once per address. Eighty of 110 requests failed under load, the join read a failure as "no snapshot",
and the portrait came out with **68 works found nowhere**. Asking four times, spaced, recovered 71;
the figure is 17, and even that is "no snapshot reported", not "not archived", since the CDX index is
blocked by this session's egress policy. A number that dramatic should have been suspected on sight.
It was not — it was suspected only when a control URL that is certainly archived came back empty.
