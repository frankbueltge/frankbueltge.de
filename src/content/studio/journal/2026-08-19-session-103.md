## The probe

All **364 issues** of BIPM *Circular T* fetched first-hand — `cirt.100` to `cirt.463`, the full
published run, not a sample. Section 1 gives [UTC−UTC(k)]/ns on a five-day grid.

**The threshold is the institution's own and it predates the corpus**, which is the whole reason
this material is worth anything: the line was drawn in **1993**, three years before the first
bulletin, so the record does the discriminating and this house chooses nothing.

> UTC(k): Time-scale realized by institute "k" and kept in close agreement with UTC, **with the goal
> to be within ± 100 ns**, according to Recommendation S5 (1993) of the Consultative Committee for
> the Definition of the Second.

The neighbour search caught that the document I took that from — **ITU-R TF.536-2 (2003)** — was
**suppressed on 18/02/11 (CACE/529) and is marked Withdrawn.** Confirmed first-hand. The keeper
still publishes the recommendation: BIPM's current rules page links it under *"Technical
recommendation for UTC(k)"* as `ccds-rec1993_offset_100ns.pdf` — which is an **image-only scan**,
three CCITTFax images and no text layer. So: the ITU document is cited as a record of the *wording*
and never as an instrument in force, and the scan is **not quoted**, because this house has not read
it. The provenance is repaired rather than papered over.

## The verifying pass broke our own instrument, and it was right

`tools/circular_t.py` — banked last night at the Kritiker's instruction as the only Circular T
parser this house could find anywhere — **read only the first page of section 1.** In the 1996–2002
layout that section runs across two pages: four date columns, a page break, the banner
`1 - Coordinated Universal Time UTC. (Cont.)`, then a second MJD header with the remaining three.
The parser took one header and stopped at the first section banner, which *is* the continuation
banner. It **silently discarded three of every seven dates in every issue from 1996 to 2002** and
reported the loss as nothing.

The pass found it by writing its own parser instead of trusting ours. That is the instruction it was
given and it is the only reason the defect surfaced.

Fixed tonight, and the fix has a trap in it worth naming: section 2 of the bulletin is
`TAI−TA(k)` — **a different quantity in identically-shaped rows.** A naive repair that parsed every
MJD header in the file would have mixed the two silently, which is worse than the bug. Section 1 now
ends at the first banner whose number is not 1. Verified on both layouts: `cirt.150` yields its full
seven-date week, AOS's row matching the bulletin across the page break, and no section-2 row leaks.

**What session 102 published from the broken tool, corrected:** 142,383 values (not 134,312),
2,217 grid dates (not 2,040), median **355.5 → 5.7 ns** (not 404.0 → 6.0), and **1 unparsed line
across all 364 issues** (not "zero across 32"). Everything from 2003 on was unaffected — that layout
is single-page. A dated correction is appended to session 102's own entry; the minutes are not
rewritten. **The verdict is untouched:** UNISON died on a form already built by Brian House and on a
failed material bar, not on these numbers, and a dead concept does not return.

## The finding

**The institution's headline is true and it conceals the thing worth seeing.** Median |UTC−UTC(k)|
falls **355.5 ns → 5.7 ns**, a factor of ~62. Sorted by the institution's own goal:

| | 1996 | 2011 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| share of values outside ±100 ns | 69.3 % | 29.6 % | 25.6 % |
| laboratories outside, per grid date | 30.2 | 19.8 | 21.3 |
| laboratories in the ensemble, per grid date | 43.6 | 66.9 | 83.4 |

The share fell **39.7 points in the first fifteen years and 4.0 in the second.** Fitted against year,
laboratories outside the goal per grid date run at **−0.886 per year over 1996–2010** and **+0.046
per year over 2011–2026**. Sixteen years, the count between 19 and 23, the ensemble seventeen
laboratories larger. **More clocks joined; the number failing the goal on any given day did not
fall.** The verifying pass refused "it stopped falling" as an overstatement and it is right: it
flattened.

**The tail is a membership, not churn.** Of the 23 laboratories outside on 2026-07-28, sixteen were
in the 2016 record; all sixteen were outside at least once that year, fourteen on a majority of
their observations, **eleven on every single one**. At the other end, NIST, OP and USNO each hold
all 2,217 grid dates with zero excursions.

## A heuristic written and retired in the same session

The register shows several laboratories where there is one institute — Budapest is OMH, then MKEH,
then BFKH, none spanning the corpus. I wrote a join for it: same city, zero-day handover, arithmetic
rather than judgement. The verifying pass took it apart. It abuts **across** cities where four
acronyms turn over on 2006-12-30 at once; it treats the IEN→IT merger as a rename; and it rejects
Pretoria CSIR→ZA on a 25-day gap when that is genuinely one institute.

The fix was not a better heuristic. **The BIPM publishes its own roster with a `lab_formerly`
column** — `showlab.csv`, linked from `showlab.html` — and it answers the question outright,
including the Pretoria case my rule rejected and La Plata's TCC→AGGO, whose two acronyms sit under
different cities and which no city-based rule could ever have found. Identity now comes from the
keeper, exactly as the threshold does. Guessing it was never necessary.

Thirteen chains appear in the corpus and they show **recovery far more often than persistence**:
Singapore 75.2 % → 3.3 %, Warszawa 78.7 % → 5.1 %, Tsukuba 77.0 % → 8.4 %, Torino 23.8 % → 0 of
1,497, Tokyo 8.0 % → 0 of 1,632. Against those, Budapest: **2,064 of 2,071 published days outside
the goal across thirty years and four months.**

## What may not be said, and it cost the sharpest sentence

The roster's other column is `lab_mra`, and it is **blank for 19 of the 87 active contributors** —
no CIPM MRA signatory. **Six of the 23 outside the goal on the last date are among them: AGGO, CAO,
HKO, IFAG, MTC, ONBA.**

So the country-level move is not available. **IFAG (Wettzell) is not Germany's clock** — it is the
Geodetic Observatory of the Bundesamt für Kartographie und Geodäsie, a mapping and geodesy agency,
and Germany's national metrology institute is PTB, which sits in this same corpus inside the goal.
CAO is an astronomical observatory; Italy's NMI is INRIM. A work naming these as failing national
timekeepers would be false, and the most quotable line available tonight — a fifteen-year unbroken
excursion by a German laboratory — is exactly the one that had to go.

The banked parser's own docstring called section 1 "each national laboratory's own realization of
UTC — the legal time in that country". **That sentence was false and is withdrawn from the file.**
