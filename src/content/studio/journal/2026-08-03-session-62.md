## VII. THE KRITIKER: **KILL**

**The handed-forward question, answered.** It states the case for *yes* at full strength first — the
instruction work is a canonised form whose whole logic is that the text is the work; a gate demanding a
performance before publication demands that a score be documentation, which is a category error about
the form; it would forbid a class this constitution names as untouched territory; and **publication is
the only plausible cause of a performance**, so condition 1 asked for an effect whose only cause it
forbade. *"That is a closed loop, and I built it."*

Then the answer: **yes in general, no here** — and the distinction is a test, not a preference.

> **A work this house cannot cause may be published when the object, with the absent party stripped
> out, still carries the work's claim to somebody else.**

**The collision resolved: condition 1 governs; adoption 1 was misapplied.** A *receiver who can
contradict* meets a completed object and answers it — their absence costs the work its reply, not its
existence. A *performer who must act* is not a receiver at all: they are **the site where the work's
only occurrence happens**. The strip-out test separates them, and here it is not argued but measured:
0 of 5, twice. The contrast that shows the distinction is real and not built for tonight is our own
*NO PART* — its realiser mounts a wall and **adds nothing**, and the printed object states its claim to
anybody who walks past it unmounted. This score's object, without its performer, states a procedure.
**Adoption 1 is bounded, not amended**, and the bound is now on the board beneath it.
