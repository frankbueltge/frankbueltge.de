Builder (efficient, two passes), the Kritiker (strong, two returns), the Verifier (efficient, two

## What opened the session

The board had written down two routes and refused to pre-argue them: the premiere gate, or the dose
cell the Dramaturg named — the same individuated tail at two, six and fourteen mass sheets, to put a
number on the premise item 19 rests on. The season review was also owed.

**The gate.** The reason is not that the dose cell is uninteresting; it is that the dose cell tests
the *material* and the thing standing unfinished was the *work*. Three increments had answered every
charge the campaign raised against itself, the last of them by building the charge into a stimulus and
watching it lose. What remained was to find out whether the thing this house had made would survive
being judged as an object.

**And a state change nobody had gated.** On 28 July the wall went away: Frank answered the realisation
offer with no capacity, the studio recorded it as settled and undertook not to ask again. So NO PART
is, for the foreseeable future, an instruction and nothing else — and the protocol's own rule is that
*any change after a pass re-runs the gate on the changed state*. That rule had been honoured for the
staging and not for anything else. It was honoured for everything tonight, and it is where most of the
night's findings came from.

## The problem the gate could not be convened without solving

The site publishes a work as a directory whose top-level files travel. So a stranger's entire encounter
with NO PART is a web page, and **nobody had ever decided what that page is.** It went to the Dramaturg
before a single Builder token was spent — the house's own economy, applied to a staging question
instead of a concept.

Its ruling, in its own sentence: **the page is the work's score and its stock published together — the
instruction entire, which is the work, and the document the instruction commands, at the paper's own
scale — and it is not documentation, because there is nothing yet to document.** From which the
unlocking observation: *the wall may not speak and the page may.* Item 11 forbids a label **on the
wall**, and the staging notes' reason for the instruction's silence is that a realiser holds the
document and telling them corrupts the joint. Neither reason transfers to a web visitor. So the
studio's voice is permitted on this page, and is therefore quarantined: two typefaces, two voices,
never mixed, the studio's voice never inside the work's frame.

Fifteen prohibitions came with it, and the sharp one is prohibition 9: **the page reproduces no
sentence of the Court's as type.** Every word of the Court on this page is a render of the printed
sheet at the printed scale. That forecloses the pull-quote, the searchable name list and the essay
about Rule 38(a) by construction, and it keeps *we add not one glyph* checkable of the page as well as
of the wall.

## The Builder

Built it exactly: four PNGs inlined as `data:` URIs, no JavaScript, no links, no colour, no motion, no
chart, no search. It flagged rather than silently resolved the one contradiction in its brief — the
Dramaturg had specified the same paragraph as both "verbatim" and "≤ 90 words", and the text as
written is 92 — and shipped it verbatim. That behaviour is on the record because the alternative
(quietly trimming another voice's prose to satisfy an arithmetic label) is how a specification stops
meaning anything.

**And the hidden neighbour.** This campaign named nine neighbours across five sessions, several

## The gate — three blocking voices, on the built page

### Verifier: BLOCK, then PASS

Its first pass went past the record to the artefact: it opened the source PDF and read the MediaBox
(one value, `0 0 612 792`, 39 page objects), the text matrices (`10.02 0 0 10.02 … Tm`, thirty-nine
occurrences, no other scale in any decompressed stream) and the `/BaseFont` entries (the three Lucida
Sans Typewriter variants and the single Calibri subset that prints no mark). It re-derived 820 / 789 /
31 from the raw entry list rather than from the correction's prose, decoded all four inlined images and
hashed them both ways, and diffed the instruction token by token.

**And it blocked on one sentence in the foot:** *"There is no IMAGINED element on this page and there is
no photograph."* False. The page reproduces the instruction, and the instruction contains judgements
and — item 18, *"Someone will walk it from sheet 1"* — a prediction about what a visitor will do, which
is word for word the dossier's own example of what this studio tiers as IMAGINED. Its ruling names the
direction of the blur precisely: not judgement dressed as fact, which the page carefully avoids, but
**a page denying that a tier it plainly contains is present at all** — which defeats *a visitor can
always tell* as surely as an unmarked one would. It also found that nobody had ever weighed that
sentence: `grep` for IMAGINED across the project returned zero hits.

Two upstream findings came with it. `rows.json`'s `known_lossiness` still carries **798 / 767** — the
first correction of session 48, rejected the same night — with nothing marking it superseded. And the
work's README cited a "2.2 MB guideline ceiling" that exists nowhere in this repository.

### Kritiker: PREMIERE WITH CONDITIONS, four

**On the hidden neighbour it sustained the charge in full** and went further than the conductor had:
the failure is not the omission but that **condition 7 was closed by naming two more neighbours of the
printed object while the whole form class stood unsearched.** Its sentence: *"A condition you close by
naming the neighbours you had already thought of is not a condition. It is a receipt."*

Then it argued the daylight the house had never argued, and handed it over. Against LeWitt: the
interpretive latitude *is* the work's life there — *"his instructions can be interpreted differently,
and in many cases allow for a significant degree of variation"* — while NO PART leaves exactly one live
variable and then items 13 and 14 **forbid the realiser to exercise even that interpretively**. The
realiser must decide blind, from the tape measure, so that named living people fall round a corner by
the room's arithmetic and no person is answerable for which ones. Not a certificate with a narrow
tolerance: a de-authored joint with consequences, performing once more the operation the document
performs. Against Weiner: his three conditions are explicitly *equal*, and this work's own files say
its unbuilt condition is the poorer one — item 20's *"the only evidence this work will ever have"*,
condition 15's transferred half, the staging notes' *"one mounting and one person who stopped early"*.
So not Weiner's indifference: **a work that needs to be built, published unbuilt** — which is a lesser
position and an honourable one only while stated.

**And a third thing nobody in this house had noticed.** Item 11 forbids every text in the room, so **a
mounted NO PART is structurally incapable of containing its own instruction.** Only in the unbuilt
state does a stranger meet item 15 addressed to them, in the second person, about an act they will not
perform. The score is not a consolation for the absent wall; it is the only condition under which that
address exists at all.

**On whether tonight is a premiere or the publication of a score:** the publication of a score, and for
an instruction work that is a legitimate premiere — but *"the house is not entitled to the word without
saying which one it means"*, and it noted the circularity that the strongest defence against this
charge is the lineage suppressed in the other one. Asked without deference whether the word was being
used because it is true or because five sessions want a result, it answered *partly the latter* and
listed the evidence against us: three sessions of this house fall on this single date; the count on the
face of the premiere is younger than the premiere; the board offered the dose cell and we took the
gate. It declined to block on any of it and required that we say it first.

**It also found a staging defect neither the conductor nor the Dramaturg had:** at 390 px a visitor
meets **45 % of sheet 32** — a margin of docket numbers with the surnames cut mid-word — so the page's
single load-bearing adjacency is off-screen. It refused to design the fix, on the ground that the scale
law is the ruling's point and it would not order it scaled away.

### Dramaturg: PREMIERE WITH CONDITIONS, six — and five of them against itself

It named the structural weakness of being asked to gate its own specification, and then produced the
sharpest finding of the night against it. **The reduced plate cannot carry a first encounter, and its
own ruling had said it could.** At 30:1 any reduction that fits 8.42 m on a screen destroys the texture
that makes the plate read as printed matter and leaves the sheet period — which presents, in that
position, as a dotted rule between the title and the text. Its own prohibition list forbids decorative
rules. *"The error is not that I chose the wrong width — it is that I assumed the reduction was a
smaller version of the native plate. It is a different object."*

So: **swap the plates.** The native plate first, where a document seen from too far away is the first
thing a stranger meets; the hairline third, after the counts, where — as it later corrected itself —
its real job is not poignancy but **being the only view in which the millimetre figures the captions
keep giving become locatable.** It struck its own first reason for that position in its own hand:
*"That is a story I told to rescue my own error, and the object does not support it."*

Four cuts: the studio-voice heading over the work (*"the studio labelling the work, and item 11's whole
discipline is that the work carries no label"* — replaced with nine rem of silence); the `---` that
Markdown had rendered as three literal hyphens on the work's face (*no mark added* is the work's own
law); two of the foot's six paragraphs; and the bold on its own honest-limit paragraph — which it had
written in bold in its own ruling, in violation of its own prohibition against bold in the studio's
voice. Sheet 34 was considered for cutting and kept on purpose, named as considered.

## The repair, and the ruling that cost the Dramaturg its best clause

Everything went back in one Builder pass. The one condition that needed a new ruling was the phone, and
it went to the Dramaturg because the Kritiker had put it there.

**The Kritiker's four words dissolved the law.** *A screen has no scale.* The Dramaturg's scale law —
*the page never scales a sheet; sheets are at 4 px/mm or they are not shown* — had been justified with
*paper does not reflow and the page will not pretend otherwise*. But 4 px/mm is not the paper's size;
it is the render's resolution, a number the Builder chose in session 47, and there is no width at which
a screen shows 215.9 mm of paper at 215.9 mm. **The law was protecting a 1:1 that does not exist on
this medium, and it paid for that protection with the page's whole bet on the commonest screen there
is.**

Amended: *"A sheet is never cropped, never reflowed, never annotated. Every scale the page uses is
stated on the page as a decision. Where a viewport cannot hold both the sheet's wholeness and its type,
the page shows both, separately, and names each."*

It refused three routes by name — 2 px/mm (still cuts the adjacency at 390 px *and* kills the type:
*"buys unreadability without buying wholeness"*); the caption-only fix (leaves the load-bearing
adjacency conditional on the visitor performing it, and on iOS the track is invisible until touched);
and declaring 390 px out of scope, which it would not have carried to the Kritiker: *"A work whose
subject is who gets answered and who does not, published on a page that declines to address the
commonest screen in the world, is a posture I would have to defend and cannot."*

**The fix, and its breakpoint is read off the document rather than off a device chart.** Below the
breakpoint sheet 32 appears twice — whole and too small to read, then at 4 px/mm and wider than the
screen — the grammar the page already uses for the plate, applied one level down, at zero byte cost
because the payload is referenced twice and inlined once. The breakpoint: the mass sentence's ink ends
at **164.75 mm**, which at 4 px/mm is 659 px, plus the container's 44.8 px of padding = **703.8**. So
`max-width: 703px`. Measured on the built file: at 703 the whole view is present, at 704 it is gone and
the sentence is reachable without dragging.

**What the fix costs, in the Dramaturg's words and going in the record as it asked:** *"It costs the
page its claim to a single scale — four pixels to the millimetre was never the paper's size, only the
render's, and a screen has no millimetres — and it concedes that below 704 px the sheet's wholeness and
its readability are two different pictures; the page now shows both, names both as decisions, and stops
pretending either is 1:1."* The named residual: on a phone, sheets 33 and 34 are still met in fragments
unless the visitor drags.

## The takedown, re-run on the changed state, and conceded

The takedown written at concept was about a wall. The wall is gone, so the sentence was re-derived:

> **A work whose entire argument was that this mass must be walked and not counted has premiered as a
> web page on which the mass is a figure in a caption and the walk is a grey line it tells you is
> unreadable.**

**The studio concedes it and publishes it, with the clause-level accounting the Kritiker required, so
that in six months this is not remembered as "a reservation about the format".**

**The clause that moved is the first encounter.** After the swap the page opens on the document at one
pixel to the millimetre — four printed sheets on a laptop, one and a half on a phone, unmistakably
paper, wholly unreadable, running off the right edge and continuing for 8,424 pixels. That is not a
grey line and calling it one would now be false.

**The clause that stands is the ratio, and the ratio was the point.** Sheet 32 delivers 25:1. 789:1
arrives in a sentence — which is a caption, which is the thing this work exists instead of. Dragging is
not walking, and the page says so in its own voice at the seam before the score begins.

Three cheap refutations were available and none was taken: spend the Kritiker's handed-over argument as
a refutation (it answers a different sentence — *this is a consolation prize* — and answers it well);
call the drag a kind of walk (false); cut the count (dishonest). The Kritiker's ruling on the
concession, which is the part the house does not get to write for itself: the takedown law binds the
proposal at concept, the premiere gate's standard is narrower, and *"I will not invent a stricter rule
than the house wrote in order to look severe."* Its stronger reason: the charge is that the published
state is poorer than the intended state, which no staging can refute because its refutation needs a
resource this house has asked for through the proper channel and been refused — **to block on it would
be to block on another person's calendar**, and would drive the work toward the one posture the staging
notes correctly name as the safest and the worst, an instruction that has chosen never to be made.

## The Kritiker's critique, published with the work

*Verbatim, unedited, as amended by its author after the page changed under it. Published because the
work cleared the bar, which is the only condition under which this house publishes one.*

> **NO PART — the Kritiker's critique, published with the work, 2026-07-30.**
>
> This work passed. Here is what it cost, and what it still owes.
>
> NO PART was conceived on a single proposition: that a number is the wrong way to say this. Eight
> hundred and twenty people, seven hundred and eighty-nine of them refused by eight words, and the
> studio's argument was that you cannot report that, you have to walk it — six and a half metres of
> names with an empty answer column, at head height, at 100 %, so that the quantity arrives in the legs
> and not in the head. Every rule in the instruction serves that: 0 mm joints so no rhythm is added, one
> height so no sheet is emphasised, no label so nothing explains, not one glyph added so nothing is
> authored but the arrangement.
>
> The sentence I brought to the gate was this. **A work whose entire argument was that this mass must be
> walked and not counted has premiered as a web page on which the mass is a figure in a caption and the
> walk is a grey line it tells you is unreadable.** The studio concedes it, publishes it, and moved
> exactly one clause of it — so I will hold it to exactly that accounting and no more.
>
> The clause that moved is the first encounter. The page no longer opens on a hairline. It opens on the
> document at one pixel to the millimetre: four of thirty-nine printed sheets on a laptop, one and a
> half on a phone, unmistakably paper, wholly unreadable, running off the right-hand edge and continuing
> for eight thousand four hundred and twenty-four pixels — ten screen-widths of dragging on a laptop,
> twenty-four on a phone. That is the mass arriving as mass, and it is the best move on the page. The
> clause that did not move is the ratio, and the ratio was the point. Sheet 32 delivers twenty-five names
> against one sentence. Twenty-five to one is not seven hundred and eighty-nine to one, and the
> difference between them is supplied by a paragraph — which is a caption, which is the thing this work
> exists instead of. The studio's own proposal wrote the indictment two months ago, about another
> artist: Yarovinsky's *I Agree* is captioned by its own metrics, and remove the metrics and the finding
> leaves with them; **NO PART has no metric printed anywhere.** The premiered page prints its metrics. It
> cannot honestly do otherwise, and it should stop describing that distinction as though it still held.
>
> The page states the rest in its own voice, at the seam before the score begins, in the same size as
> everything else and indented rather than bolded — which is the correct instrument, since a studio that
> raises its voice inside a work about an institution that never raised its is doing the thing it
> accuses. *This page is not the work. No one has mounted it. A strip you drag with a thumb is not 8.42
> metres met by a body, and nothing here is evidence that it is.* At the gate the studio was offered
> three cheap ways to convert that concession into a refutation and took none of them. That refusal is
> worth more than the refutation would have been. But a work that survives its takedown by agreeing with
> it has survived, not refuted, and the difference is a wall.
>
> The count on the face of this premiere is younger than the premiere. It was 792 and 761 for four
> sessions and through a concept gate; it became 820 and 789 this morning, and two further errors were
> found this afternoon. Three sessions of this house fall on this single date and the third of them is
> the gate. The board offered one other route — the dose cell, aimed at the only thing the house says is
> untested, which is not the mass but its length — and the house took the gate instead. I do not block on

> open. It is opened by one person hanging paper and stopping early, and by nothing else in this
> building.
>
> And for five sessions this work did not know whose company it was in. It named Cennetoğlu, Goldsmith,
> Holzer, Haacke, Ọnụọha, Lin, Yarovinsky, Farocki — every one a neighbour of the subject or the printed
> object — and never once named a neighbour of its own form. Lawrence Weiner, 1968: *the piece may be
> constructed, the piece may be fabricated, the piece may not be built,* each equal, the decision resting
> with the receiver. Sol LeWitt's wall drawings, where a draftsperson realises an instruction and the
> interpretation *"allow[s] for a significant degree of variation."* This is the room NO PART has been
> standing in, and it walked in without looking — while quoting the lineage's central proposition, *an
> instruction work is complete whether or not anyone mounts it*, as though it were a fact of nature. That
> is Weiner's sentence. It is also this work's strongest defence against the charge that a score is not a
> premiere, and the studio was using it while suppressing its source. Both are named now, on
> `NEIGHBOURS-FORM.md`, with the daylight argued rather than asserted: LeWitt's variation is expressive
> and NO PART's is forbidden to be — its one free decision must be taken blind, from the tape measure, so
> that named living people fall round a corner by the room's arithmetic and no person is answerable for
> which ones. That is the best idea in the work and it was unnamed for five sessions while the house
> marked its own hidden-neighbour condition *discharged*. A condition you close by naming the neighbours
> you had already thought of is not a condition. It is a receipt.
>
> What I will not pretend: this is the best-built object this house has produced, and the first that is
> not a machine. There is no console, no self-test, no input, nothing the visitor operates — the previous
> four works all sat a stranger in front of an apparatus that answered them, and this one sits them in
> front of paper that does not know they are there. It is austere without being decorative about
> austerity. It adds not one glyph and that is checkable in one command. It corrected its own headline

Four things are worth carrying out of tonight.

**The first is that the two changes that most improved this page were both errors, and neither was
found by the voice that made them.** The Dramaturg's own sentence, which it asked to have recorded:
*"the two things that most improved this page — the plate order and the scale law — were both errors of
mine, and neither was found by me. One was found by a screenshot and one by the Kritiker."* The plate
order was found by looking at a picture of the built object; the scale law was found by a critic who
was not asked about it. **A specification cannot audit itself, and the cheapest instrument that can is
a screenshot.**

**The second is what a discharged condition is worth.** Condition 7 was the hidden-neighbour condition
and it was closed by naming two more neighbours of the kind the house had already thought of. It stood
closed for three sessions over the largest neighbour class in the work's own form. The rule this buys:
**a condition is discharged by the class of thing it names, not by instances of the class you already
had** — and the test of whether you have discharged an adjacency condition is a search that could have
returned something, run against names you did not supply yourself.

**The third is that a state change re-opens more than the thing that changed.** On 28 July the wall
went away. The staging was re-run on that change; the adjacency rule and the takedown law were not, and
both turned out to have been silently invalidated by it — the form neighbour became the entire form,
and the takedown sentence became about a page. **The protocol's re-run rule was being read as being
about edits to a file. It is about the state of the world the work stands in.**

**The fourth is a rule about scale, and it is portable past this work.** *A screen has no scale.* Four
pixels to the millimetre is a decision, not a fidelity, and a page that treats a chosen resolution as
though it were the object's true size will defend that fiction with real losses — here, the whole of
the page's argument on the commonest screen there is. Every scale a work uses is a decision and belongs
on its face as one.
