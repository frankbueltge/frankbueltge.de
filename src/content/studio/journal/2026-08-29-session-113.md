## What an event in this room actually is, read out of the code rather than assumed

`settle()` fires in exactly two ways: a wet observation lands inside a period the room is
holding (an event only if that observation is younger than the door — otherwise the claim
is removed in silence as `unwitnessed`), or a period's window ends while the room has held
it from its own beginning. Every window in this record is twelve hours long. **So the
second kind cannot fire in under twelve hours of continuous room-time, and every settlement
a visitor can possibly see is of the first kind.** The room's visit-scale eventfulness is
therefore exactly the national arrival rate of fresh rain reports — which is a fact about
the sky and is measurable to the observation.

`tools/eventfulness.py` is that measurement, and it is committed rather than thrown away,
on the standing rule from session 111.

## The hostile voice killed two things in the instrument, and one of them was its headline

The measurement was written first and then attacked before a number from it was believed.
Three findings, all verified against the code by the conductor before acting on them:

**The instrument was reading tonight's record for rooms that opened hours ago.**
`cycle_claims` keeps only claims whose window has not yet closed, so tonight's file cannot
describe a room opened six hours back: every period that closed in between is simply absent.
The simulation would have reported those rooms as having had nothing to settle. The closed
periods are now reconstructed backwards on the twelve-hour grid the windows tile — 55 of
them tonight — and what cannot be reconstructed is what they *said*, so an event landing in
one is counted and not typed.

**It attributed a shared station to every office that names it.** The room does not:
`stationOwner.set(s, id)` is single-valued and the last office wins. **1,070 of the atlas's
station entries are shared**, so this was inventing settlements the room cannot draw. It
now builds the map exactly as the room builds it, defect and all.

**And it claimed something false in its own docstring.** *"Every flare a visitor can see is
a wet observation"* — `rebuild()` also draws a sweep when an office re-issues (1.6 s) and a
ring when the room first hears of an office (2.2 s). Neither is a settlement, neither is
counted in `fading`, and neither may be quietly folded into a sentence about what a visitor
sees. The instrument now says what it does not count.

## And then the room contradicted the instrument within the hour

The corrected model said a ten-minute visit sees a median of two office-periods decided.
The live room, watched at the same time, fired **58 marks in one instant**. Both were
right, and the gap between them is the thing neither had written down: `settle()` walks
`node.live`, which holds **every claim in the bulletin**, and one wet observation settles
every claim of that office whose window contains it. **The sky decides office-periods; the
visitor sees bands.** One office's rain is dozens of arcs going off together. The
instrument now carries both counts, and the band count is weighted per office out of the
record itself.

## What the six hours say

A national sweep of the observation network, adaptively subdivided until no box hit the
service's silent 400-record cap: **28,953 observations, 2,498 stations, 1,371 of them
reporting falling water**, over the six hours to 04:20Z. Four boxes answered with something
that was not JSON and are named in the record rather than averaged away.

**Half the country's rain is outside this record's jurisdiction.** 685 of the 1,371 wet
observations came from stations no office's zones name, so the room never hears them. That
is the room's own rule working as written, and it had never been quantified.

**Sixty-seven.** That is how many office-periods in the entire United States were decided
by rain in six hours.

Rooms opened at every five-minute offset across those six hours — none inside the first
hour, because a door the data cannot reach behind cannot be judged — asked what they would
have had to show:

| | office-periods decided | bands lit |
|---|---|---|
| ten-minute visit | median 1, **empty 27.1 % of the time** | median 21 |
| twenty-minute visit | median 3, empty 4.2 % | median 56 |
| sixty-minute visit | median 9, never empty | median 165 |

Wait for the first flare after the door opens: **median 7 minutes, 90th percentile 15,
longest 26.** Gaps between national settlements: median 2 minutes, 90th percentile 15,
**longest 44**.

**And the arrivals are not a rate.** The national network files near the end of the hour: of
1,371 wet reports, **533 fell in minutes 50–59 and 76 in minutes 40–49**. The room's
settlements inherit that breathing — 21 of them in minutes 50–59, 2 in minutes 40–49. A
visit is inside a clump or it is not.

## The one question put to the Artist, and its answer was no

*Does the work owe anything to a visitor who arrives in a trough?*

**Decided: nothing is owed, and the trough is not a failure.** Quoted from the finding:
*"The room knows exactly as much during a trough as during a flare."* The three marks this
work has spent four sessions building all exist to keep "nothing visible" from lying about
what the room knows — the bare stroke for an office it has not been told about, the dim
band for a declared silence, the whole field bare when its own authority has lapsed. A
trough is none of those. During one, the field is still holding forty-three thousand
standing promises drawn in three distinguishable substances; what is absent is a verdict,
not knowledge.

And there is no true mark to draw. The room has no rate: the arrivals clump. Any anticipatory
mark — a pulse, a brightening, a countdown without words — would assert a *coming soon* the
room cannot back with a fact, which is the forbidden move.

**The cost is named rather than argued away**, in the Artist's own words: nearly a quarter
of ten-minute visitors leave having watched no settlement at all, and for them the claim
that *some of these promises are breaking while you watch* is carried by inference from a
static field rather than by a witnessed rupture. That is the piece's most legible proof of
itself, and those visitors do not get it.

**So milestone 5's afterglow limb stays failed and the work is not changed to make it pass.**
What changes is that the failure is now characterised instead of anecdotal: it is not a dry
night, it is the shape of the national observation network, and it will be there on every
night this work ever runs.

## The defect the attack found in the work, and it decided the loudest mark this piece has

`witnessedWet()` returned the **first array match**, and `wetSpans` is not in time order —
it is appended in the order stations are first seen wet, which within a pull is station-id
order. So for an office with two wet stations inside one period, one observation older than
the room and one younger, **the alphabet decided the verdict.**

That is not a hypothetical about a rare shape. The claim in question is a *silence* — an
office promising nothing about rain — and this work's own concept says a silence that turns
out wet is **the loudest event the piece has**, seen and heard. Whether it fired at all
depended on which station's name sorted first.

Demonstrated rather than argued, in `tools/witness-order.mjs`: one office, two stations, one
claim, one sky rewritten under the running room; the two runs differ **only in which station
name carries which observation**.

| | older observation sorts first | younger observation sorts first |
|---|---|---|
| the build committed last night | `unwitnessed` — removed in silence | **`silence` — a flare, with sound** |
| tonight's build | `unwitnessed` | `unwitnessed` |

Zero console errors in all four runs.

Two things this cost to get right, both of them wrong turns worth recording. The first
fixture put both observations in the sky the room reads **at the door**, and every run came
back `unwitnessed`: a room that finds water waiting for it settles that claim immediately and
once, and no later observation can reach it. The second put them in a later pull but wrote the
younger one dated into the future, and every run came back `unwitnessed` again: `settle()`
runs every second and takes whatever is already in the past, so the older observation wins on
time alone. **The defect needs both observations to become eligible at the same tick**, which
is what a delayed observation box produces in the live relay — four boxes failed in tonight's
own sweep.

The repair is not a way of ringing more flares. The rule the work already states decides it:
the promise is answered by the **first water that falls inside its window**, and whether the
room may call that an event depends on whether the room was open when it fell. So the earliest
matching observation answers, always — which **loses** the flare in the case above rather than
gaining one. A room that opens after the first water has fallen does not get to claim it
witnessed it.

## The model against the room, which is the only test of a model

The measurement was run again over the exact window the room was watched, and asked what a
room opened at 04:40Z would have had to show. **It named eight office-periods. The room drew
nine — the eight exactly, and one more.**

The first run of that comparison predicted **sixteen**, and the seven it got wrong were all
one defect: a two-hour sweep cannot judge a door twenty-three minutes into it. Every one of
those seven — Juneau, Flagstaff, Goodland, Honolulu, Northern Indiana, Seattle, Tampa — was
an office whose rain had begun *before the sweep's own first record*, so the room had found
the water waiting at the door and removed the claim in silence while the model, unable to
see that far back, took it for still open. The instrument now refuses to simulate any room whose
door lies inside the snapshot's own reach of the span start.

The ninth — Duluth — is the residue, and it runs the other way: the room drew it and the
model excluded it, because the model asks *"was there wet water in this period before the
door"* while the room asks *"is this station's LATEST report wet"*. A station that rained at
04:00 and reported clear at 04:35 is invisible to the room and visible to the model. The
timeline now stores every report instant, wet or dry, so the next run answers that exactly;
tonight's figures were computed with the approximate rule and say so on their face.
