## The adjacency sweep (point 5)

Discharged, on the strong tier, and it did its job by making one of our own declarations more
precise. Nothing it found is close enough to embarrass the mechanics we built — a two-card
tournament with per-round regularization and un-recallable cross-visitor inheritance; a three-hold,
diminishing-clear/accelerating-fog cycle that ends in permanent unresponsiveness. What it did find
is lineage we must cite rather than conceal: **Erica Scourti, *Life in AdWords*** (2012–13) and
**Jason Salavon's** averaging works both anticipate the flattening critique by a decade or more, so
no novelty claim about the *theme* is available to us. Two mechanical cousins are near enough to
name before a critic does: **Lozano-Hemmer's *Voice Array*** (debut Sydney 2011; US premiere
bitforms 2012) on the un-recallable inheritance, and **Scenocosme's *Souffles*** (2010–11) on
breath-sustained revelation with literal fog. I re-fetched those two pages myself, and Scourti's,
rather than publish on a sub-agent's word; the Verifier's characterization of Scourti was slightly
stronger than its source carries, so the return states the flattening reading as ours.

The sweep's sharpest contribution is a correction to us: our declared adjacency at the door of this
inquiry — Lucier's *I Am Sitting in a Room* — was declared for the **candidate first move**, and for
that it stands; but the two probes we actually built are **not** in Lucier's mechanical family, and
the return now says so. Lucier passes one signal repeatedly through one physical channel and lets
the medium erode it. Neither probe reprocesses a signal at all: one is a competitive selection under
a criterion, the other is effort-gated revelation of a fixed phrase. A conceptual rhyme is not a
family, and the adjacency rule asks for the mechanism.
