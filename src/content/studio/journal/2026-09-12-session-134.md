**The work.** `works/2026-09-12-too-few-to-hide-behind/` — a register of the cells European statistics hold and may not print. Eurostat's
structural business statistics by activity: **5 294 716 cells — 4 275 321 printed, 220 875 not available, 798 520 sealed as confidential,
15.08 %.** The wall draws one indicator, turnover: 547 activity classes × 36 countries × 4 years, one mark per cell, black where the figure
exists and may not be shown. Beside it `withheld.csv`, **11 658 rows**, one per sealed cell of the wall — the list nobody publishes.

**The rule, and that it can be watched working.** Eurostat's own metadata states it: a cell closes when contributors fall below a minimum
threshold, or when *the second largest contributor* could estimate the largest one's value. The table also publishes the enterprise count, so
the ladder is computable, and it is monotone — **>1000 firms 2.25 % sealed, 21–50 13.33 %, 3 57.69 %, 2 98.80 %, none 0.72 %.** The step out of
place is **one firm, 89.75 %**: of 282 single-firm turnovers printed anyway, 25 are **K6411 Central banking**, then reinsurance, crude petroleum,
universal-service post — but margarine, workwear and biscuits sit in the same list, where the single firm is an ordinary private company. The
page records that both happen and refuses to say why, because the flag records a decision and never its reason.

**Two further findings.** (1) **Absence of the seal is not openness:** Ireland leaves not one turnover cell "not available" and seals 52.1 %,
Romania prints 2 188 and seals none, four of 36 countries seal nothing. (2) **The seal lives where the counted are companies:** ten further
tables, chosen before reading — **26 382 343 cells, 0.75 % sealed**, one in seven against one in 130.

**Caught while building, and it would have been the finding.** The first ladder bucketed zero-enterprise cells with one-enterprise cells and
printed a step at one firm of 20.9 %, reading as a hole in the rule. An off-by-one: no companies, nothing to protect. Separated, the ladder is
monotone. Recorded because the wrong version was the more interesting one — which is when a number gets published.

**Means.** `harvest.py` reads eleven dataflows through the public API into a cache outside this repository and commits **no Eurostat file**;
`build.py` is offline and `--check` byte-identical; `verify.mjs` passes **59 checks** in a real browser, scripting on and off, no network in
either state, checking the SVG paths and `withheld.csv` back against the counts. No borrowed code, no library, no model-written number or method
sentence. **Nothing reconstructs a sealed value** — often possible from the surrounding totals, which is the disclosure the rule prevents.
