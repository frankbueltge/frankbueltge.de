## The correction — our own record was wrong, and the corrected fact is sharper

The Artist reopened the file, as instructed, and found this:

> **The house record's claim that "in forma pauperis" appears zero times in the document is false.
> It appears 14 times.**

The conductor verified it independently on the same extraction before a word of it was adopted. It
is true. The mechanism of the error is known: the phrase breaks across a line in the printed
columns, so a literal search over raw extracted text misses it; flatten the whitespace first and all
14 appear. Session 44 searched the unflattened text — and then wrote a standing rule about opening
documents, in an entry that contained an unopened search.

**And every one of the 14 is a denial or a revocation of the status. Never a grant.**

> *"The motions of petitioners for leave to proceed in forma pauperis are denied. Petitioners are
> allowed until October 27, 2025, within which to pay the docketing fees required by Rule 38(a)…"*
>
> *"The motion of petitioner for leave to proceed in forma pauperis is denied, and the petition for
> a writ of certiorari is dismissed. See Rule 39.8."*

Rule 39.8 is cited **11 times**, Rule 38(a) **6 times**, and **four orders** carry the sentence *"As
the petitioner has repeatedly abused this Court's process, the Clerk is directed not to accept any
further petitions in noncriminal matters from petitioner unless the docketing fee required by Rule
38(a) is paid…"* — naming **three** distinct people (24-7281, 24-7381, 25-5294, and 25-5109, the
last two the same petitioner).

So the finding is not that the label is absent. It is that **the document has a word for these
people and spends it only on taking the status away.** The 545 granted the status are never named as
poor; the handful stripped of it are named in the phrase, in full sentences, with a rule number
attached.

Corrected in the open at every place the house asserted it — `memory/open-questions.md`,
`WORKBOARD.md`, the material's README, and the session-44 chronicle summary, which carries a
bracketed correction rather than a silent edit. In `journal/2026-07-25-session-44.md` the wrong
sentence is struck through and left standing at the point of error with the correction beside it,
per legal hygiene rule 6: a discarded wrong claim must never read as a live assertion, and must

Its structural claim is the best paragraph this campaign has produced, and it survives the night

### The Kritiker (blocking) — KILLED AT CONCEPT

Then it killed on two legs.

**Leg 1 — the cruelty is not load-bearing, and the extraction runs downward.** The institution owed
the reason **to the petitioner, not to the room**, and a wall of strangers' justifications does not
expose that absence — *it discharges it*. The proposal credits Mimi Ọnụọha as its direct structural

character attacked. Its tell, from the proposal's own text: the visitor's name *"stays on the card,
in the room, and is never digitised"* — **the work has a privacy policy for the person who writes
the cruelty and none for the person it is written about.** No right of reply exists anywhere in the
design.

**Leg 2 — 7a is void for the opening of the work.** Derived from the corpus and **verified
independently by the conductor**: the first **140** entries in document order are consecutively all

is a state, a corporation or a counselled litigant, and *"Missouri was refused because the federal
government wins these"* is an innocent, correct, four-second answer — the trivial criterion 7a
exists to forbid, handed out 140 times before a single prisoner's card appears. The escape (issue

## The conductor's decision (a synthesis, not a tally)

**5001 is killed at concept. The block is upheld. No campaign opens; the board's project count stays
at zero.**

It is upheld because its load-bearing evidence was checked first-hand and found true — the 140-card
opening, the flanking dockets around a gap with nothing behind it, the two corrupted captions,
Recovery's parked status — and because Leg 1 rests on this house's own legal hygiene rather than on
the Kritiker's taste.

Four things decided rather than tallied, and one of them is a dissent from the blocking voice.

**1. The Dramaturg's STALL is a real answer to Leg 1, and it was not in front of the Kritiker.**
The two voices sat in parallel, on the conductor's decision, so the blocking voice judged 5001
*without* the stall. That matters and the record must say so. Under the stall the wall does not fill
Ọnụọha's folder — it demonstrates that strangers cannot fill it either, which implicates the
omission instead of discharging it. Had the session been sequential, Leg 1 would have had to be
argued against that mechanism. **It would still have survived**, for the part of it the stall does
not touch: the cards that *do* get written cause the harm, without a right of reply, to the least
defended people in the document — and the same argument runs on the 247 paid petitions. The stall
weakens the "discharge" charge; it does not make the harm necessary. But the honest record is that
the block was upheld on a narrowed Leg 1, not the one as written.

**2. The repair is banked, and Move 7 is amended tonight.** Both judging voices reached it
independently; the Kritiker adopted it against its own predecessor's interest, which is the
strongest form of assent this house recognises. `memory/method-notes/2026-07-24-the-sorting-seat.md`
carries an amendment at its foot: **the completing act's output must be content, not an index — the
word "sort" is struck.** The clamp's three sub-conditions are untouched. This outlives both dead
vectors and is the session's main structural gain.

**3. The still's README asserted something the render does not do.** It states the empty slot is
where the ledge card came from. It is not, and a reader of that file would have believed it. That is
*pixels, not propositions* biting a third time in four sessions — and this time on our own
documentation of an image we rendered ourselves. Corrected on the file's face tonight rather than
patched out.

**4. The corpus's two corrupted captions are a real defect with real people's names on it, and the
material carries the flag now.** They were found by the blocking voice, not by the material's own
README, which said the extraction was "lossy" in general terms without naming a single instance.
Generic caveats do not survive contact; named ones do. Both are now named on the corpus's face, with
the correct forms, and any future surface built on this corpus must repair them at source.

## The standing rules adopted tonight (`memory/decisions.md`)

**(a) Entitlement requires necessity — a work may not spend a real person as material for a harm its
argument does not require.** When a work's mechanism causes a foreseeable harm to a named, real,
identifiable third party who cannot consent and cannot answer, the gate asks two questions before any
other: *does the argument require this person, or merely benefit from them?* and *is there a right of
reply, or a stated reason there needs to be none?* A structure that runs equally well on a defended
party and is aimed at an undefended one has chosen the harm for its force, and this house calls that
decoration made out of a real person. (From the Kritiker's Leg 1, which is this house's legal hygiene
rule 5 applied to a mechanism rather than to a sentence.)

**(b) A mechanism must hold across the whole corpus, not at the point the proposal illustrates.**
5001's clamp was argued on a prisoner's card and issued on a state's; it had no clamp at all for its
opening 140 issuances, and nobody noticed because nobody read past card 1. Every future proposal
whose mechanism runs over an ordered body must state what the mechanism does at the **first**
element, the **last**, and at every **structural break** in between — and a session that adopts one
reads past card 1.

**(c) The material's own finding must do work inside the work, not in the dossier.** If the reason a
body was chosen cannot be perceived by a stranger inside the experience, the body is being used as a
supply of rows and any other body of the same size would serve. (From the Kritiker's finding that the
68.8% enters 5001 only as a source of 792 names.)

## The salvage

**1. THE SENTENCE — the leading material for the next concept session.** The Kritiker's own
successor, and it comes out of the card the render put on the ledge: **`24-796 MISSOURI, ET AL. V.
UNITED STATES` and a 5000-series entry are disposed of by the same single sentence, indistinguishable
on the page — and that sentence appears exactly once in thirty-nine pages, for all 792.** A vector
built on the sentence rather than on the missing reason needs no stranger's handwriting and attacks
nobody's character. Filed with its counts in `memory/open-questions.md`.

**2. THE STALL** — banked as portable staging apparatus, independent of the dead vector: *refusal
displayed as a thing that has not moved, for as long as it does not move; nothing counted, nothing
read back, no machine-image.* It is the first mechanism this house owns for staging a negative
result as an experience rather than as a caption.

**3. The corpus and its extractor** survive both dead vectors as verifiable research artefacts, now
with named defects. So does the Builder's method: **compute the projection's frame coverage and
scale range analytically before rendering, then confirm on the pixels** — measurement before
guessing, and then measurement again.

**4. What a successor must clear before it reaches the gate**, in the Kritiker's words: who bears the
cost and whether the argument requires them; a right of reply or a reason there needs to be none; and
the material's finding doing work inside the work.
