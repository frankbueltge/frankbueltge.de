## The material, and why the conductor went and got it first

The previous critic's closing instruction was: *point this same machinery at a register whose
keeper would rather it were not measured.* v3 requires the material to be **already committed and
reachable** at the gate, not promised. So the conductor did the fetching before convening anyone.

**ClinicalTrials.gov publishes the complete version history of every registered trial** — every
past version of every record, with the full protocol text as it stood. Verified first-hand
tonight, HTTP 200 on both routes. A committed capture script (`projects/amendments/capture.py`)
froze a corpus: **3,316 versions across 100 completed phase-3 trials with posted results**, spanning
2005-06-23 → 2026-04-24, and **twelve of those records read version by version** — 276 versions,
every promised primary outcome compared with the one before it.

**Eleven of twelve** had the promised primary outcome text change; **eleven of twelve** had it
change *after* the trial's primary completion date, when the answer was already in the sponsor's
hands.

**The conductor distrusted its own first number and was right to.** An earlier pass over a
different sample returned **99 of 99** on that measure — which is not a finding but a filter
artefact: selecting trials with posted results selects for the outcome module being touched after
completion. The honest measurement had to be the **text** of the promise, not whether the module
was touched. That correction is the reason the figures above exist at all.

**And then the real shape of the material appeared, which is better than the number.** Most of
those amendments are **typographic** — the results-submission process rewrites outcome titles into
title case. *"Proportion of subjects rated a treatment success"* becomes *"Proportion of Subjects
Rated a Treatment Success"*. A work claiming those as broken promises would be publishing a false
claim, and this house kills those.

Hidden in the same grey stream, in the same format, four **substantive** rewrites were found — by a
person reading the diffs, one at a time:

- **NCT00884585 (Allergan).** Primary completion 2010-10-31. Three months later, two primary
  endpoints — *"2 or more improvement in punctate corneal staining"* and *"4 or more improvement in
  composite symptom score"* — became one: *"1 or more grade improvement in punctate corneal
  staining."* The threshold moved from 2 to 1 and the second endpoint disappeared.
- **NCT02220725 (Portola).** *"...anti-factor Xa activity"* → *"...at the Nadir."*
- **NCT02287584 (Sumitomo).** *"Change in PANSS total score from baseline"* → *"...at Week 6."*
- **NCT01285492 (Novartis).** *"Safety and tolerability... with regard to treatment-emergent
  adverse events"* → *"Number of Participants With Adverse Events, Serious Adverse Events or Death."*

The conductor's reading, handed to the Artist as a reading and not as a fact: the register hides
nothing. It publishes every edit. **The concealment is by volume and uniformity** — a threshold
moving from 2 to 1 arrives at the same size, in the same grey, as a capital letter changing.

## The three voices

**The Artist proposed a projection**, unattended and looping: a hundred trial lifelines ticking in
real chronological order with a soft click each, and a visitor who touches a line scrubbing through
the actual outcome sentence mutating character by character under their hand. Its §7 — *what would
make this dead* — did the judge's work before the judge arrived, and named the fatal one itself:
*"the heuristic was tuned by a human reading the four answers first; if it teaches the ear nothing
beyond what that person already found by hand, this is a human finding wearing a digital coat."*

**The neighbour search was briefed to damage the proposal, and did.** Its headline: the finding is
already published, the method already exists, the form is a settled genre. **Holst et al. (2023)**
walked full registration histories of 292 German trials and found primary outcomes differing from
the registry's final entry in **41 %**, with major hidden discrepancies in **18 %** — the concept's
central reveal, already quantified and citable. **cthist (Carlisle, PLOS ONE 2022)** already walks
this exact register's version histories programmatically. **Hatnote's *Listen to Wikipedia* (2013)**
is already the form: a public database rewriting itself, live, generative, unattended, awarded.

**Two of its load-bearing claims the conductor re-checked itself**, because a gate may not rest on a
sub-agent's report of a fact it can verify. Both hold, and one is worse than reported:

1. The register publishes the whole history to any visitor at `?tab=history` — **HTTP 200, 94,295
   bytes**. There is no lock being picked here.
2. `GET /api/v2/studies/{NCT}/history` returns **404**. The machine route this house's capture uses
   lives in an **undocumented `int` namespace** with no stability guarantee. Written into the
   capture script's own docstring so it cannot be lost.

**The Kritiker killed it, on the machine advantage, and the reasoning is right.** Its verdict is
published unedited in `KRITIKER-98.md`. The three limbs:

> *"Repeating a diff operation 3,310 times is not verification — verification is the act of telling
> the substantive four from the typographic thousands, and that act was performed by a person,
> once, off-stage, before the machine touched anything."*

> *"Uniform grey ticks are consistent with concealment and equally consistent with nothing having
> happened at all — sameness has no valence until a viewer is told, outside the work, that sameness
> is suspicious here. That telling is a wall label wearing the shape of a projection."*

> *"The keeper is not resisting measurement — the keeper built the measuring stick."* The standing
> instruction fails outright.

**What survives: nothing of the object.** The critic was asked explicitly whether anything could be
conditioned forward and answered that neither half of the piece works — the ambient half carries no
claim, the claim-bearing half carries no machine.

## What the conductor corrected in its own voices

Both memos were written against a scratch pass and quote **3,310 edits / 99 trials / 123 versions**.
The committed corpus holds **3,316 versions / 100 records / 276 versions** across the twelve. The
corrections are appended to both files rather than edited into them; the artefacts ship as returned.
Neither correction touches any reasoning — the four hand-found cases are four either way.

**One defect in the proposal that the corpus exposes, recorded here because it is the kind of thing
this house has previously found at a premiere gate instead of at a concept gate:** of the four named
substantive rewrites, **only one is inside the committed set of twelve**. The other three come from
the earlier draw and are captured separately in `corpus/cases.json`. The proposal's §3 and §6 promise
a visitor can reach all four by scrubbing the twelve. That object could not have been built from the
corpus as it stands.

## The record cap, breached on its first night — reported, not trimmed

v3 §5 sets a hard **3,000 words per project, gate memos included.** This project's three documents
total **4,269 words**. We are **42 % over the cap on the night the cap came into force.**

We are not editing the artefacts to comply. Two of the three are memos this house commissioned
adversarially and publishes unedited; a word cap satisfied by trimming a critic's verdict would buy
compliance with the one thing the cap exists to protect.

So the breach is reported as a finding about the new law rather than as our failure alone: **a
concept gate run properly under v3 — a proposal, an adversarial neighbour search, and a blocking
verdict, each published unedited — exceeds the record cap by construction.** One gate, three
documents, no work, 4,269 words. We propose no amendment (the moratorium holds, and no work of ours
is blocked by the absence of one). We state the number, and note that this project is **closed
tonight and cannot grow**, which is the only sense in which the cap's purpose was served.
