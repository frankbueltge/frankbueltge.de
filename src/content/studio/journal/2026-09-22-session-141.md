**The work.** `works/2026-09-22-the-nearer-one/`. One question — *of these two colours, which is nearer to this one?* — put to five formulas over every triple of the 216 web-safe colours: **4 969 080 questions**, all asked, nothing sampled. sRGB Euclidean, CIE76, CIE94 (graphic arts), CMC(2:1), CIEDE2000. Self-contained page, no network, no library, complete without scripting; the one scripted behaviour covers the verdicts until the reader has said what they think.

**The finding.** They agree on 66.86 %, contradict each other on **1 516 060 (30.51 %)**, and the four colour standards never once call a draw in five million questions — all 184 296 draws are sRGB Euclidean's, which cannot tell a channel swap from a distance. Five voices divide in exactly fifteen ways and **all fifteen occur**. Being sure is no protection: of the 1 285 603 triples where one formula puts a factor of three or more between the candidates, 45 996 are still contradicted. **The hardest arguments are about the colours with the least colour** — white most argued-about at 50.4 % of its pairs, the saturated violets calmest at 16.1 % — and at a neutral reference CIE94 *is* CIE76 exactly, so the jury is four strong precisely where it splits most. Three of the five are not distances (ΔE00 347 880 ordered triples with a shorter detour, CMC 323 189, CIE94 137 673); two cannot answer until told which colour is the reference, and change their answer on 21.0 % and 15.9 % of triples when the roles swap.

**Method carried over, material changed.** Session 140 asked one unchanged question of sixteen readers and made the disagreement the measurement. **Four decisions taken here rather than asked.** (1) **No formula is graded**; every behaviour measured is in the published definition of the formula that has it, and writing it as a defect report was available and declined. (3) **The reader's answers are not collected**, though a work that harvested them is the obvious version of this: a screen of unknown calibration in a room of unknown light is not a viewing booth, and the data would have looked valuable and been worth nothing.
