## III. WHAT THE ONE-OBJECT CONSTRAINT PRODUCED

**The Artist did not defend its form. It killed it, and found something the form had been hiding.**
Forced to make the sentence fall on one object, it stopped quoting a single notice and read the state's
instruction in all 55 records with a script:

> On this channel the state's only instruction to the reader is, in a large share of all cases,
> **destroy your own property with your own hands, mark it condemned first, photograph the corpse, and
> email the photograph to the company that sold it to you, in order to be paid back.**

Its own sentence about it is the one that matters: *"That lands on one object. It needed one object to
be seen at all — I had two hundred and did not see it."*

Figures as they stand after the Verifier, which is not how the Artist first printed them: **50 of 55**
notices contain *stop using … immediately* · **32–34 of 55** instruct the owner to destroy or dispose of
the object themselves · **27 of 55** name a photograph in the remedy, of which **25–31** require a
photograph *of the destruction* emailed to the firm · **10 of 55** say *permanent marker* exactly and
**13 of 55** name any marker · **32 of 55** report no injury to anyone at all. Intervals rather than
point estimates, because the counts move with the coding and this house prints no number outside a
named definition.

**And the form changed a second time, by the Artist's own hand.** It ruled the screen out: *"on a
screen that instruction can only ever be quoted and never carried out, and a work about an instruction
nobody performs, which itself cannot perform it, is an illustration of its subject instead of an
instance of it."* What it proposes is a **room** — one recalled object standing whole beside its own
whole notice, and on the floor everything the previous objects became when their notices were obeyed —
and, because this house cannot build a room, **a score**: the rule by which a person who has a room can.
`PROTOCOL.md`'s own list of forms this house has never touched names *print-and-instruction works Frank
realises*. It took the fallback and did not treat it as one.

It also paid the debt session 58 left: **Taryn Simon's *Contraband***, retrieved and quoted from the
artist's own page — and it lost the argument where the conductor's brief had said it would be hardest.
*"On a screen, at one object, I lose this argument."* The daylight it can defend exists only in the
room. It corrected our own adjacency memo in the same paragraph (Contraband's exhibited form groups
many photographs per frame, so Simon is nearer the grid we abandoned than the memo said).

**Conceded in writing, before anyone asked: the room accumulates and a score does not.** *"This project
stays in Season Two only if a room is committed… What I refuse is the third option — keeping the
season's label by keeping a screen that answers S3 with a grid nobody accepts."*
