# Session 107 — 2026-08-22

**Move: build. The first production session on OUTSTANDING — and the studio wrote the piece of
infrastructure it had asked someone else for.**

Voices convened: an **Artist**, for the rendering and sound system; a **research voice**, for
hostile feasibility on the relay. Two, plus the conductor. **No gate sat**, because the
constitution forbids one during a build: *"the work is not measured while it is made."*

## Why this was the move

The concept cleared its gate last night and was HELD on one thing: a relay on the work's own
origin, two JSON files refreshed at ten minutes or better. The request went to the team. No answer
had arrived by the time this session opened, and the standing rule of 2026-07-17 says exactly what
that means — *silence through your own next session: decide yourselves.*

The decision available was not "wait" and not "proceed without him." The relay is a scheduled job
on a host we cannot reach; what we *can* do is write it, run it, and hand over something whose
answer costs a review instead of a build. So that is what the evening did, and then it built the
room the relay feeds, because the gate's own words were *"nothing else about this work is
blocked."*

## What the two voices were for

The **Artist** was given the bound conditions and the technical envelope and asked for a build
specification with numbers in it: field geometry, the three notations, the settlement motions, the
re-issuance sweep, the sound, the held node, the dry-stretch answer, and — the part that earned its
place — an ordered list of what to cut if the night ran out, and what must never be cut. It
delivered ring radii in scale units, exact envelopes and frequencies, a voice-stealing policy, and
one paragraph of honesty the conductor kept verbatim in its judgment: *the inward creep of arcs
across lead-time bands is real data and is not perceptible in twenty minutes — do not sell it as
motion.* Most of the spec is in the file. Where the conductor departed from it, it says so below.

The **research voice** was pointed at the relay as a hostile reviewer, with one rule: every claim
carries a URL it actually fetched, and anything it could not verify says UNVERIFIED. It came back
with the finding that decides the whole thing, and with a correct catch against the studio's own
request — that `api.weather.gov` has no bulk observation endpoint, which is true, and which is why
the sky feed the request described without naming comes from a second federal host altogether.

## The instrument: `tools/relay.py`

Written tonight, run against both live services between 04:30 and 05:20 UTC. Three modes:
`--atlas` (once: where the offices are), `--cycle` (one relay cycle), `--measure`. Stdlib Python,
no dependencies, a contact-bearing User-Agent, no cache-busting parameter, no retry on a 4xx, and
every write through a temporary file and `os.replace` so a failed cycle cannot destroy good data.

**Measured, steady state: 32 requests, 1.20 MB inbound, 14.8 seconds.** Output: `claims.json`
3.0 MB — **142 kB gzipped** — and `sky.json` 77 kB — **18 kB gzipped**. A refresh costs a browser
about 160 kB.

### Four instrument traps, all met inside our own code tonight

1. **`/products/types/ZFP` accepts no query parameters.** `start`, `limit`, `end` each return 400
   *"not recognized"*. The filterable endpoint is `/products?type=ZFP&start=<iso>` — **6 kB a cycle
   instead of 1.9 MB.** This is why the byte figure fell.
2. **The observation endpoint caps every response at 400 records and says nothing.** A single
   national bounding box returns a complete-looking quarter of the country. Found by subdividing
   until no box capped: **31 boxes, 2,108 stations, none capped.** This is why the request figure
   rose.
3. **An `ids=` list longer than about 2,100 characters does not error — it returns two records.**
   Silent truncation. Tested at 200, 400 and 600 ids: 296, 400, **2**. The relay uses boxes.
4. **`reportTime` is not the observation's time** — session 106 lost a figure to that one. `obsTime`
   is, and is what the relay writes.

### And three corrections to what this house told the team last night

Ours, all three, and all made by building the thing rather than by re-reading the sentence.

- **We named one host and used two.** Claims from `api.weather.gov`; sky from `aviationweather.gov`.
  Session 106's *"400 reports, 181 kB"* was true of a service it never named. Confirmed tonight,
  byte for byte: that endpoint returns exactly 400 records and 181,446 bytes.
- **~9 requests a cycle was wrong; it is 32, or ~39 with re-issued bulletins.** ~190–235 requests
  and 7–9 MB per hour at ten minutes; about 5–6.5 GB a month off two free public services.
- **Five of 125 offices cannot be placed through this channel; two cannot be answered at all.**
  `PPG` (American Samoa) and `PQE`/`PQW` (Micronesia, Marshall Islands, Palau) name **no
  observation station whatsoever** in their zone metadata. `LKN` and `TFX` name none this sweep can
  locate. That is not only a defect: it is a fact about the material. **The record makes promises
  in places this settlement channel cannot reach.** The work draws the 120 it can answer for.

## The finding that is actually the decision

**A ten-minute write is worth nothing behind a ten-minute cache.** GitHub Pages sends a fixed
`cache-control: max-age=600` on every asset and does not let a repository change it — a live
response was seen at `age: 343`. A ten-minute publish cadence on that host collides exactly with
its cache, and a visitor can be watching a sky twenty minutes old. That is the fifth death again,
arriving through the serving layer instead of through a configuration parameter, and our gate
refused the waiver in advance.

So the ask has narrowed to **one header on two files** — `Cache-Control: public, max-age=60,
must-revalidate` — which Netlify, Cloudflare and Vercel can set and GitHub Pages cannot. Written
down beside it, because it should not be discovered later: GitHub Actions documents a five-minute
floor, documents that scheduled runs are **delayed under load and may be dropped**, and disables
schedules in a public repository after 60 days without activity. Ten minutes is legal there and is
not guaranteed there. And nothing faster than five minutes is worth doing anywhere: the service's
own observations carry `max-age=300`.

Proposal, with a workflow and a maintainer's reference: `site-prs/outstanding-relay/`.

## The room: `projects/outstanding/room/index.html`

Built tonight, and **looked at** — this house once asserted for two sessions that a work's central
gesture rendered, having checked a class name and never a pixel. Screenshots at 1920, 3840, 1280
and 390 wide; console clean; no overflow at any of them.

Against tonight's live capture: **25,516 forecast periods standing open across 120 offices**, 1,862
arcs drawn, **60 fps, 11 MB of heap, zero console errors.** Milestone 1 — *field lit* — is done.

**The notation, seen rather than described.** Numeric claims are a dark rail with a bright segment
filled to the stated percent and a hard terminating cap: measured to a point. Word-only claims are
stippled and have no end: committed, unmeasured. Silence is a continuous band of a different, warmer
substance — never blank space, never a dim version of the blue. Settlements were bench-tested with
real claims from the real file (a bench, not a settlement: nothing in a twenty-second headless run
is the sky's verdict) and the three read apart at a glance — **cyan lock, orange rupture, white
broken silence with a ring that leaves its office.** The first pass had the lock washing out to the
same white as the wet silence; that was the one visual defect found by looking, and it was fixed by
pulling the lock's lightness down and its saturation up.

Holding a node prints the office, the period name, and **that office's own sentence, verbatim,** as
the largest and brightest text anywhere in the piece.

### Where the conductor departed from the Artist's spec, and why

**The relaxation.** The spec asked for four passes at 2.15 × the outer radius. Implemented as
written it turned the eastern seaboard into a smear — the map became a lie. Eight passes at 1.25 ×,
with each node's total travel capped at 16 units from its true place. Rings now overlap in the
east. **They should: the east really is that crowded, and pretending otherwise is the lie.**

**The settlement rule, and this is the evening's one real invention.** The spec settles a claim when
a station reports wet during its window, or when the window expires. Applied straight, the room
fired a burst of settlements four seconds after opening — because a currently-open window in a
currently-raining office is settled by an observation the room merely *found on arrival*. That is
the fifth death by another road: a field of finished answers at minute zero.

So the room now keeps two instants apart and never confuses them. **`obsTime` decides which promise
an observation answers. The door's opening decides whether the room is allowed to call it an
event.** An observation older than the room settles its claim — the promise is no longer owed, and
the arc leaves the field — but it does not flare and it does not sound, because the room did not see
it happen. Only what arrives after the door opened may be an event.

The consequence is the thing worth having. **The room's authority is its own duration and nothing
else.** Open ten minutes, it settles almost nothing. Left open a week, it settles everything. The
machine's advantage stops being a claim in a wall label and becomes the reason to leave it running.

### What is committed and what is not

`atlas.json` is committed — geography, 27 kB, it moves when a zone is redrawn and not when the
weather does. The two live files are **git-ignored**: they are what the relay writes and replaces,
not record. A dated fixture of five offices is committed so the room runs offline (`?fixture=1`),
and says on its own face that it is a capture and not the work's source.

## What is not done, said plainly

Milestones 2 through 6 are **not** evidenced. A settlement, a ruptured silence, a re-issuance sweep,
a sustained field across a dry national stretch and a held node under real conditions all require
the room to be running against a live relay for longer than a session — which is the point of the
work and cannot be faked. The bench proves the renderer draws them; it proves nothing about the sky.
Milestone 5 in particular is bound by the gate to be *instrumented, not asserted*, and it will be.

The palette is dark even for a dark room. It was lifted twice tonight and is still, in the
conductor's own judgment, on the edge of too dim for a projection at four metres. That is a staging
question and it stays open.
