## Why this was the move

OUTSTANDING is IN PRODUCTION and held on one thing only: a route to a writer at ten minutes

left standing. Both are reachable with a live network, a browser and an hour. So the move was
to run the room against a live, actually-cycling relay for long enough to settle the
milestone, and to close the two defects while it ran.

## The null that was not honest, and it was ours

The hostile voice was sent to break last night's sentence and it broke it on the base rate.
Measured live tonight, first-hand, against the bulletin index in two independent bursts:
**11 distinct offices re-issued in the twenty minutes to 04:40Z, and 9 in the twenty minutes
to 04:46Z** — against 4 in ten minutes, 14 in twenty-five, 27 in an hour and 48 in two hours.
This house's own earlier figure (twelve offices in twenty-five minutes, 2026-08-23, a
different day and hour) sits in the same band. Tonight's relay watched **thirty-nine offices
re-draft across eleven cycles in under an hour**, never fewer than two in any five minutes it
was cycling.

A national twenty-minute zero, then, is not a quiet Tuesday. It is a coincidence of a kind
that does not happen. So the far likelier reading of last night is the one the voice reached
by a different road: **the file the room was reading was not changing while it was watched**,
and last night's sentence — *"no office's issuance timestamp changed"* — was true about a file
and empty about the country. We cannot verify last night's process from here and we are not
going to reconstruct it. What we can say is what tonight shows: with a relay actually cycling,
the mechanism fires, immediately, every time.

**It also exposes a real gap in the room**, and it is not repaired tonight: `pull()` marks a
fetch successful whenever the request succeeds, whether or not the bytes changed. A room whose
relay has silently stopped writing looks exactly like a room over a country where nothing is
happening — the scan line keeps crossing, the field stays lit, and the 25-minute staleness
guard never trips because the fetches are all fine. Written down here as the next thing this
work owes itself.

## The rupture cannot happen in a visit, and that is a fact about the work

The same voice went after the other null and found something better than a defect. Of the
23,870 windows in tonight's corrected national record, **every single one is exactly 12.0
hours long** — there is no other window shape in this record, because a zone forecast names
TODAY and TONIGHT and the relay resolves those onto a 06:00–18:00 / 18:00–06:00 local block.

A rupture — an office promised rain and the window closed dry — fires only when the room has
held the window **from its beginning**: `now >= item.end && item.start >= openedAt`. That rule
is session 107's core discipline and it is right: you can witness rain falling, but to say
*no rain fell in these twelve hours* you must have watched all twelve. Put together with the
12-hour constant it is a proof, not a probability:

**A rupture cannot fire in under twelve hours of continuous room-time.** Not rarely — never.
In a twenty-minute watch its probability is zero, and filing it beside the sweep as a
symmetrical "honest null waiting on the sky" was wrong.

We are not re-engineering it, and the reason is that the constraint is the honesty rule doing
its job. What changes is the sentence: **the rupture is not an event of a visit, it is an
event of the installation.** A room that has been standing open for a day and a half has held
whole windows and will break them in front of whoever walks in; a room that opened twenty
minutes ago has nothing to say about them, and says nothing. Any future account of this work
states that floor rather than waiting for a null to resolve itself.

## The defect the conductor committed live: a relay with no atlas writes a wrong country

The hostile voice flagged, as a caveat on its own arithmetic, that the record it was handed
had every window starting at 06:00Z or 18:00Z. It was right, and the cause was this session's
own hand: the relay had been run into a scratch directory with **no `atlas.json` beside it**.

The atlas is where an office's time zone lives. With no atlas the lookup fell back to an empty
one, every office resolved as `UTC`, and every window in a 5.5 MB file — 123 offices, 46,965
periods, the right counts, no warning — was placed on the wrong twelve hours, by four to ten
of them. The tell is a histogram nobody had reason to take:

```
no atlas : 06:00 ×11,514   18:00 ×12,356            ← every office in the country, one clock
atlas    : 00Z 01Z 02Z 04Z 10Z 11Z 12Z 13Z 14Z 16Z 22Z 23Z
```

Fixed at root: **a cycle now refuses to run without its atlas** and names the path it looked
for. Where the atlas is present but one office is missing from it, that office is named in the
file itself (`tz_unknown`) and counted in the run's report, so a window read in the wrong hours
can never be a silent one. Written up as the eighth trap in `tools/RELAY.md`. The general
lesson is one this house has now written three times in three different rooms: **a fallback
that produces plausible output is worse than a crash.**

The consequence is stated plainly rather than quietly repaired: the record was thrown away and
rebuilt from cold with the atlas in place, and **nothing computed from the bad file appears in
tonight's account.** The first watch's seven sweeps (2, 3 and 2, against FGF/UNR, BMX/PSR/RLX
and BIS/LIX) are named because an issuance timestamp comes straight from the bulletin index
and is untouched by the fault; that watch's settlement counts are not reported at all, and the
table above is the corrected watch alone.

## And the ninth trap, which fired within four minutes of being closed

Rebuilding the record, the sky sweep died: the observation service answered one of its 31
boxes with something that is not JSON and `json.loads` ended the whole cycle in a traceback.
In an instrument meant to run unattended for months that is a scheduled run that dies and
leaves the room reading a file nobody is updating. A failed box is now dropped, named in
`sky.json` under `unanswered`, and counted in the report; if every box fails the file is left
untouched, because a sky with no observations in it is not a sky with nothing falling in it.
The very next cycle proved it: the box `24,-80 → 33,-66` failed again, and the relay wrote
1,574 stations and named the box instead of dying.

## The corners, and what the Artist would not pretend

The Alaska inset packed three offices into a figure-eight; a node sitting on the CONUS
boundary could draw its outer ring off the frame. The Artist found one cause under both: the
layout chose sizes without ever asking how large a glyph is. Both fixes now derive from the
one quantity the rest of the file already uses for that — the outermost ring's radius.

- **The insets size themselves.** A corner group is placed at unit span, its closest pair
  measured, and the span stretched until that pair clears the same threshold the mainland
  relaxation pass enforces everywhere else — capped by how much room the corner actually has
  before its own ring would reach the screen edge. No number in it is Alaska's.
- **The projection box is inset by a ring radius** on every side, so what the padding is
  measured to is the ring, not the centre.

The Artist's general claim for the second — that a ring can now at most touch the frame,
never cross it, at any aspect ratio — the conductor checked and it does not hold as stated,
because the insets and the relaxation pass both move nodes after projection. Measured on the
shipped file with the whole live record behind it: closest node-to-frame distance **58.4 px
against a 43 px ring at 1920**, and **116.8 against 86 at 3840** — clear at both exhibition
sizes. At 390 px it is **11.2 against 18.1**, so a corner ring does cross the frame on a
phone. That is left standing and named: 390 px is not this work's medium, the premiere floor
asks there only for no sideways scroll, and that holds at zero.

Measured, at 1080p: the Alaska trio's closest pair goes from 49.0 px to 53.8 px, and at 4K
from 98.1 to 107.5. Looked at rather than asserted: the three centres and their inner rings
now read as three offices where before they read as a blob. **And the Artist's own verdict is
that this is not fixed, only honest** — at that separation two 43-unit rings still overlap
heavily, and a viewer will not be able to read the middle office's week-out ring. Pushing
further would mean either exceeding the clearance the rest of the room lives by or eating the
corner's margin. It stopped there and said so, which is the right answer.

Two further honesties from the same voice, both recorded because they cut against its own
work: it could **not** reproduce the clipped Caribou glyph at 1920 or 3840 before the change —
the box's aspect slack already covered it there — so its evidence for that limb is the
geometry plus the case at the enforced minimum window (320×240, where the margin binds to
about a pixel), not a screenshot of a defect. And the mainland map is now about 9.7 % smaller,
the price of reserving the margin.
