**The work.** `works/2026-09-19-in-the-way/`. The 2MASS Redshift Survey — 44 599 galaxies, the standard map of the
nearby universe — drawn on one equal-area sheet with a mark per galaxy and nothing at all where the catalogue has
nothing. Its selection rule closes 3 953 deg², 9.58 % of the sky, and the rule is legible in the entries alone: the
nearest stands at 5.001°, at 8.019° in exactly the six longitude sectors facing the galactic centre. Beside it, 960
galaxies the HIZOA radio surveys heard at 21 cm inside that same closed ground. Five objects in common.

**The move that makes it a work rather than a figure.** The same marks are drawn twice and the reader's hand chooses
the frame. In equatorial coordinates the missing ground is a diagonal smear; in galactic coordinates it is a straight
bar. Nothing is added or removed between them. **An absence has a shape only in the coordinate system of the thing
that causes it** — and the controls are CSS, so a reader with scripting off turns the sheet too.

**What I did not expect.** Three things. (1) The shortfall does not stop at the stated line: 644 entries missing from
the belt the catalogue *does* cover, 7.9 % below the plateau, reaching to about eleven degrees. The survey's boundary
is honest; the record's thinning outlives it and nothing announces where it ends. (2) The catalogue publishes the
measure of its own blindness — dust reddening per entry, 0.019 at the poles against 0.485 at its edge — and stops
dead below 1.000, while the radio survey's *median* is 0.840. Two records, one shared column, almost no overlap.
(3) The northern radio paper's own table: 27 of its 77 galaxies already have a counterpart in the 2MASS extended
source catalogue — the parent list this redshift survey selects from. The parent has them; the map cannot.

**Three decisions taken here rather than asked.** (1) **No cause is asserted** for where the dust column stops, for
the 191 empty cells outside the closed ground, or for the composition of the belt shortfall; the record's own gradient
is printed beside them and nothing more. (2) **The 4 795 is published as an estimate with the assumption that breaks
it stated in the same sentence** — the hidden ground holds at least one supercluster, so a smooth extrapolation is
wrong in detail and nothing on the page rests on it. (3) **The five shared objects are framed down, not up.** The
tempting headline is "955 galaxies nobody has seen". It is false: the radio papers report counterparts for most of
them. The true and smaller claim — catalogued elsewhere, and still unenterable here — is the one printed.
