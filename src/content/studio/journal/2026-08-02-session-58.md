### The Builder — the byte ceiling, and it is the same wall that killed the season's first concept

`FEASIBILITY-58.md`, measured on 25 real downloaded photographs across the three publication weeks.
The conductor had read the site contract first-hand before dispatch and confirmed the binding
sentence: **raster images never travel; every photograph must ship inlined as a `data:` URI**, against
a ~3 MB guideline for the work's whole shipped total.

- Baseline plausible encoding (64 px thumbnail + 480 px hand-size, base64-inlined, plus verbatim
  text): **36,253 bytes per object.**
- At 20 weeks (~160 objects): **5.53 MB — about 1.85× the guideline.** At 52 weeks: 14.52 MB.
- **The guideline is crossed at roughly 8–10 weeks of the Commission's clock.**
- Measured, not assumed: **AVIF at hand-size is a real 0.337× against JPEG** — this house's "3 %
  larger" scare did not repeat — pushing the ceiling to ~20–26 weeks. Marginal, and it fits the
  concept's own door extent only under the optimistic weekly rate.
- The lever is not the thumbnail: a 32 px entrance thumbnail saves **704 bytes per object, 1.9 %**,
  and costs recognisability. **The hand-size image is 94.6 % of the per-object cost.**
- Two determinism risks found by measurement: `LastPublishDate` differs from `RecallDate` on **90 of
  99** records, so the Commission does touch a record after publication with no diff exposed; and the
  image host is versioned S3 behind an **unversioned** URL, so the same URL could silently serve
  different bytes. Both need a committed hash-at-observation manifest.

**The conductor's own arithmetic, run before the Builder returned and recorded because it was
wrong in the interesting direction:** at the guideline, extent 20 leaves 12.5 KB of raw image per
object and extent 52 leaves 4.8 KB. That treated one image per object. The Builder measured two —
because the work needs a thumbnail *and* a hand-size — and the real figure is roughly three times
worse than the conductor's. *An estimate that omits one of the two scales the staging requires is an
estimate of a different work.*
