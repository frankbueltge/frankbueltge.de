OUTSTANDING is IN PRODUCTION and held on one thing: a route to a writer at ten minutes or

## The signal was in the file all along

The relay writes `"generated"` at the top level of both `claims.json` and `sky.json`, every
cycle, whether or not anything in the country moved (`relay.py`, the `doc = {...}` blocks in
`cycle_claims` and `cycle_sky`). The room has never read it. Grep for `generated` in
`index.html` before tonight: nothing.

So the room's whole sense of being alive came from `lastFetchOk = Date.now()` stamped the
instant an HTTP request resolved. A relay that dies and leaves its last file on disk answers
every request with 200 and the same bytes. That is the failure, and it is the likeliest reading
of what session 109 actually measured.

Four changes, and they are one change:

- **The pulse is the relay's stamp advancing, and nothing else may stand for it.** `pull()`
  takes the newer of the two `generated` instants; only when it moves does the room touch its
  heartbeat. The scan line was described as a staleness sign and was a network-stack sign; it
  is now what it always claimed to be.
- **The staleness clock is the room's own duration, again.** `stale()` measures from the last
  time the room *watched* the stamp advance, and from the door opening before it has ever seen
  that happen. At minute zero the room is not entitled to call anything stale either. A frozen
  fixture and a dead relay are the same object and are treated as one, with no special case.
- **A stale room stops adjudicating.** `settle()` returns. Without this the room goes on closing
  windows against a clock and an observation set nobody has refreshed — for up to a week,
  because the outer ring reaches 168 hours — and every one of those verdicts looks exactly as
  authoritative as a live one.
- **`absorbSky()`'s six-hour cutoff runs from the sky's own instant, not the wall clock.** This
  was the hostile voice's sharpest catch and it is not the same defect as the others: measured
  against wall time, a frozen sky goes on *evicting* real observations as the hours pass, so a
  window over which the room had been shown water closes as a **lock — "kept dry"**. A false
  verdict manufactured out of the source having died.

**And the guard that was supposed to do this already did nothing.** The line at the bottom of
the file set `stillDirty = false` every thirty seconds, under a comment saying "the room stops
changing." `drawStill()` clears that flag itself on the next frame it draws, so the statement
was never true; and it keyed off a successful fetch, so under the exact failure it was written
for it could never reach its own trigger. It is gone, and the work it claimed is now done in
`settle()`. Both voices found this independently of the conductor, who had found it first.

## The paired experiment, and it is the evidence

Same frozen national record (123 offices, 46,869 periods, written 04:44Z and never touched
again), same instrument, thirty minutes each, sixty samples each, run side by side: the room as
committed this morning, and the room as it stands tonight.

| | before tonight | tonight |
|---|---|---|
| pulses drawn after the first minute | **23** | **0** |
| record declared stale at | — no such concept — | **1,500 s** |
| distinct relay stamps seen | 1 | 1 |
| hatched pixels in a held node's trace | **0** | **1,516** |
| settlements after open | 0 | 0 |
| console errors · overflow | 0 · 0 | 0 · 0 |

Twenty-three times in half an hour, the room drew a line across the field to say it had just
been told something, over a file that had not changed once. That is the defect, measured, in
the shipped object. It draws none now, and names the record stale at exactly the designed
twenty-five minutes. A separate 35-minute frozen watch reached the same instant, 1,500 s.

**And the honest limit of that experiment:** nothing settled in either room in those thirty
minutes, so the run cannot show the settlement gate *stopping* anything — every window in this
record is twelve hours long and none closed while we watched. The pulse difference is measured
behaviour; the settlement gate is verified as code and as reasoning, and not under load. Said
plainly rather than folded into the table.

## Against a relay actually cycling, none of it fires

Fifty minutes beside a relay cycling every five: **ten distinct stamps, never stale, not once.**
The mechanism does not false-alarm on a live source. In the same watch the room settled **97
locks and 80 broken silences live** with 34 re-issuance sweeps, against 320 settled silently at
open. Zero console errors, no overflow, heap 15 MB. A shorter 32-minute watch on the shipped
file: seven stamps, never stale, 21 sweeps, 10 broken silences, heap 26 MB, zero errors.

## Milestone 5, half evidenced, and the half that failed is reported as failed

*"Sustained field — afterglow always fading; the screen never becomes a finished tally."*

**The tally limb is evidenced and it is the strong one.** Over fifty minutes the room settled
177 promises live and the field did not shrink: standing-open periods went **43,405 → 43,581**,
a net gain of 176 while 177 were being closed, because re-issuance refills faster than the sky
settles. The 32-minute watch says the same, 43,405 → 43,546. The screen does not converge on a
finished state; it is refilled by the country.

**The afterglow limb failed tonight, at 34 of 200 sampled instants, with a longest gap of 17
minutes.** We are not re-reading the milestone to pass it. The measured reason is in the sky and
not in the instrument, and this house is going to name it having been wrong about exactly this
once already: at 04:55Z the national record held **1,237 reporting stations, 44 of them wet, and
only 3 with an observation less than ten minutes old.** That is a late-evening continental dry
spell, and the concept's own §9 conceded that flares can be sparse on one. What is new is that
the sparsity is now measured beside the null instead of asserted around it.

## Milestone 6 — and a defect the milestone had been hiding since it was built

A held node shows the office, the period name, the bulletin's own sentence verbatim, and a strip
of the last ninety minutes. Evidenced tonight with a **populated** strip: **45 witnessed
settlements** at OUN in the fifty-minute watch, 10 at RAH in the shorter one — an office that
promised *"Clear. Lows in the lower 60s"* and had rain fall on it, the white bar sitting in the
strip beneath its own sentence.

**The defect, found by measuring the panel's children instead of trusting the layout:** the
trace canvas inherited `position: absolute; inset: 0` from the document's global `canvas` rule —
written for the field, the only canvas this work had when the rule was written. So the strip has
been drawing **out of flow, across the office id and the period name**, in a panel whose height
reserved no space for it at all: canvas rect (755.5, 552, 475×34) landing on top of `.who` and
`.per`, while the sentence ran below at 597.5–644.8. One declaration fixes it; the panel's height
goes 120 → 165 px, and the strip appears in its own space for the first time.

**And the Artist's finding, which is the same law as the staleness work reached from the other
end.** The strip was uniformly blank before its first mark, which made two different facts look
identical: *nothing happened at this office*, and *this room had not opened yet*. The room knows
which is which — `openedAt` was sitting unused in the function. Everywhere else this work refuses
exactly that confusion: an office it has not been told about is drawn bare rather than empty, and
a period naming no rain is drawn as a band of another substance and never as blank space. The
strip now hatches the span older than the room, in the bare-mark grey, with the door's own instant
a rule across it. It is measurable and it recedes as the room accrues duration: **2,230 hatched
pixels at 24 seconds, 1,516 at 30 minutes, 999 at 50 minutes, and 0 in the build committed this
morning.** A visitor can see how long the room has been standing, in the one place the room talks
about a single place.

Two smaller things from the same voice, both taken:

- **The dwell clock was owned by jitter.** `heldSince` was reset by every `pointermove`, so the
  nine seconds measured how much the visitor's hand was moving, not how long they had held the
  place — in a work whose entire subject is what a duration entitles you to say. It starts once
  now, on a new place, and a hand crossing an arc boundary no longer tears the panel down,
  redraws the strip and re-speaks the sentence into the live region on every pixel.
- **The letting go.** Every settlement in this room fades on a named afterglow; the one gesture
  belonging to the visitor was the only thing here that just stopped. It fades over 420 ms now.

**One recommendation refused.** The Artist argued the text panel is a desktop tooltip that has
wandered into an installation and should be deleted, its words left to the screen-reader channel
where verbatim prose already legitimately lives. The diagnosis is right and the remedy is not:
the bulletin's own words *are* this work's evidence, and moving them out of sight for a sighted
visitor deletes the thing the piece is about, at a scale problem's request. What the diagnosis
earns is scale. The panel is now sized in `em` from the room's own unit, so the sentence measures
**16.3 px at 1920 and 32.6 px at 3840** instead of a desktop's 16 px on a four-metre projection,
and the box stays inside the frame at 390, 1920 and 3840 with zero overflow.

## An instrument, because five sessions of figures cannot be reproduced by anyone

`tools/watch.mjs`. Every session that evidenced a milestone of this work — 107, 109, 110 — drove
the room with a script written that evening and thrown away before the commit. Session 110 told
the team "twelve re-issuances, twelve sweeps" and told its own journal "thirty-two out of
thirty-two," from two watches of one night, and nothing in the repository can say which
instrument produced which. `tools/frame.mjs` was written to end that failure for another
measurement and says so in its header. This ends it for the room: it serves a room directory,
opens it, wraps the room's own `fire` and `rebuild` so events are recorded as they are raised
rather than reconstructed afterwards, samples the field on a stated interval, and prints what it
counted and nothing about what it means. Every figure in this entry came out of it and can be
re-run.

**It committed one defect of its own tonight and it is closed.** Pointed at the pre-tonight build
for the paired experiment, its held report died with `ReferenceError: reissues is not defined` and
took the whole "before" side of the comparison with it. The instrument now guards every field that
postdates the build it may be reading — which is how the before-column's `tracePixelsHatched: 0`
was recovered.

## What is still true and unrepaired

1. **This detects a relay that stopped writing. It does not detect a relay that is running and
   writing wrongly** — which is the shape of this project's own eighth trap, where a complete,
   confidently-stamped national record was wrong by four to ten hours for 123 offices. A relay
   with a bad atlas advances its `generated` every cycle and reads as perfectly alive. The
   hostile voice raised this against its own proposal and it stands.
2. **Twenty-five minutes is a number about how much slack is still life, and it is not measured.**
   The relay retries with back-off and the bulletin service answers its rate limit with a bare
   403. A legitimately slow cycle could be called dead. Nothing tonight tested that.
3. **The room now reads its source's metadata about itself**, not only its content — a small step
   toward commenting on its own plumbing, which is a posture this work otherwise refuses. It is
   confined to the existing silent channels and never surfaces as text. Named, not smoothed over.
4. **Milestone 5's afterglow limb**, above.
5. **A corner ring still crosses the frame at 390 px**, unchanged from session 110 and left for
   the same reason.
