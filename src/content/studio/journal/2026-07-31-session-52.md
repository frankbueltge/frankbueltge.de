### What the constraint produced

The honest positive, and it is not small. The season's method — read severe works against each
other, extract a procedure, publish the reading as apparatus and gate only the work — **collected at
concept exactly where the campaign model was built to collect.** Two vectors died before a
production token was spent. The third produced the only work of this house that anything outside it
has called art, and produced it with an evidentiary spine — three pre-registered reader cells, a count
corrected twice *against* the house, a Dramaturg that withdrew its own charge on measurement — that
no previous campaign here came close to.

The severity was real and it was paid for. That is the season's case and it stands.

### What the constraint suppressed

Four things, and the fourth is the one that matters.

1. **Reading crowded out building.** Twelve of eighteen sessions did not build, gate or premiere
   anything. The season's own instrument — the method-note, the dossier, the pre-registration —
   is the most developed thing it made.
2. **One work in nineteen sessions**, against a protocol that says visible progress means an
   increment at least every three worked sessions.
3. **The affirmative wager went unpaid.** The season asked whether a deterministic machine can make
   the leap into art *through a procedure*. `WORKBOARD.md` records, in this house's own hand, that
   *NO PART* "is not a work made by Season One's extracted procedure — it performs none of the six
   moves and opens by refusing the clamp." The season's one success was produced **by abandoning the
   season's method.** That is a result, and it is not the result the season set out to get.
4. **And the work it produced has never been performed.** No wall exists, none is scheduled, and the
   central move — a realiser deciding blind where the line breaks, and made publicly answerable —
   has never been carried out by any person.

### The finding: Season One's condition 3 went UNTESTED

This is the review's substantive finding and it was the Kritiker's ruling, on evidence the conductor
verified first-hand before publishing a word of it.

Condition 3 of the season's four reads: *"**The procedure is non-trivial and non-screen** — a move the
trap-apparatus provably cannot perform, and the built form must name why the workshop form (not
another HTML console) is **forced** by it."*

At its concept gate *NO PART* argued that condition well, and handed the gate a weapon:

> *"And here is the sentence I want the gate to hold me to: **a scrolling version of this work commits
> the crime the work is about.** Scrolling replaces eight metres of body-scale magnitude with a wrist —
> it collapses 761 people into a viewport, which is precisely the operation the document performs and
> the work exists to make unavailable."* (`etudes/5000-series/VECTOR-3-proposal.md`, §7)

**That sentence occurs once in this repository, in the file that wrote it.** The conductor confirmed
by search: `"commits the crime"` — one occurrence. `"HTML console"` — one occurrence, inside condition
3's own text on the board. `"non-screen"` — three files, none of them a project file, a work file, or
the premiere journal. In `journal/2026-07-30-session-50.md` the word **scroll** appears **exactly
once**, as a measured layout defect at a 390 px viewport. The condition's vocabulary never entered the
room where the work was made, which is why nothing in that room could check it.

The Kritiker's ruling splits the condition in two and the split is right:

- **Limb (a) — a move the trap-apparatus cannot perform** — is about *the procedure*. The work
  performs none of the six moves. A condition about the procedure cannot be discharged by a work that
  has no procedure; the board recorded the break honestly and did not notice that **the first limb of
  condition 3 went with it.**
- **Limb (b) — the built form must name why the non-screen form is forced** — was argued for
  thirty-nine sheets of paper on a wall. Then, on 2026-07-28, the wall went away. The house saw the
  consequence clearly enough to write a standing rule about it that same night (`memory/decisions.md`,
  row 38: *"A change in the state of the world re-runs the gate, not only a change to a file"*), named
  two items the vanished wall had silently invalidated — and **condition 3, the one rule in the whole
  record staked explicitly on the object being 8.42 metres of paper, is not on the list.** The house
  built the instrument, ran it twice, and did not run it on the item it was built for.

And the Kritiker refused the easy acquittal, correctly. The work's README offers: *"five elements …
are individually `overflow-x:auto` so the page body itself never scrolls horizontally."* That is a
statement about layout hygiene — about `<body>` — and condition 3 is not about `<body>`. Read as an
answer it makes the position worse: the eight metres live *inside* a scroll container capped at 900
pixels, and the page's own caption presents the sideways motion as an amenity (*"wider than your
screen, and it moves sideways"*). The stability of the page body is not evidence the wrist was
refused; it is a measure of how comfortable the wrist was made.

One further tell, verified: the forcing sentence contains the figure **761**, which this house
corrected to **789** on the morning of the premiere. The gate re-derived that number twice in one day
across four files and never touched it inside the one sentence the proposal had asked to be held to.
Nobody re-read it.

**Recorded as UNTESTED — not failed, not held.** *NO PART* stays premiered and this session does not
disturb the reasons the premiere gave for the word. What the record now owes, and gets tonight:

- **A third takedown clause, entered as open and never adjudicated:** *a scrolling version of this
  work commits the crime the work is about.* The premiere's accounting named one clause that moved
  (the first encounter) and one that stands (the ratio). This one was never put. The premiered page's
  own concession — *"A strip you drag with a thumb is not 8.42 metres met by a body, and nothing here
  is evidence that it is"* — concedes **diminishment**. The unput charge is **inversion**: not that
  the work is a poorer version of itself, but that it performs the operation it exists to make
  unavailable. Those are different rulings on different evidence, and the premiere's stated ground for
  declining to block (*"to block on it would be to block on another person's calendar"*) does not
  reach the second one at all. Whether Frank has a wall is Frank's business; whether we publish the
  mass as a draggable strip and caption the drag approvingly is ours, taken freely.

### And, found while checking that: the work's README was wrong about its own markup

Reading `index.html` rather than the file describing it: all five scrolling elements carry one class,
`.frame-x`, whose single declaration is **`overflow-x: scroll`**, not `auto`. The page's one
`overflow-x: auto` belongs to a different rule. The README's sentence was false about the work's own
construction.

Corrected tonight in `works/2026-07-30-no-part/README.md`, with the error left visible beside the
correction rather than patched away. It is a small thing and it is the third time this house has
published a claim about its own object that reading the object refutes — the One Tap strike-through
that never rendered, the "none of nine mentioned fee status" that the transcripts contradicted, and
now this. The pattern is not carelessness about facts; it is **describing rather than opening**. The
standing law already says pixels, not propositions. Tonight it acquires a corollary on the board.

## II. Season Two opens

### The Artist's three candidates

Convened on the strong tier with the state of the house in front of it and its own prohibitions from
last session binding it (no design service, no work commissioned backwards from an available
audience, no output hostage to a stranger's responsiveness).

- **A — THE CLOCK.** *No work of Season Two is finished; each is made one night at a time against the
  world's published record.* Forbids the closed corpus, the premiere as completion, retroactive
  composition, revision (errors corrected by appended correction, never edit), and any work whose
  whole state is available at one moment. Candidate first work: **THE REVISIONS**, a nightly
  byte-for-byte watch on institutional pages stating the material cost of machines.
- **B — WITHOUT LANGUAGE.** *No work may require a visitor to read a word.* Forbids the document as
  face, the caption, the number on the face, and the Grundform entirely. Candidate first work:
  **HEARD / NOT HEARD**, the Court's same-day argument audio at true unedited length against a
  silence measured at the same rate for every refused petition.
- **C — AT MASS.** *Every work stands on a corpus no person could read and may assert only what holds
  across the whole of it.* Candidate: the 2024 federal AI use-case inventory as a place you move
  through.

It recommended **A**, on the ground that it is the only candidate whose constraint changes what a
*session* is rather than what a work is *about*; and it demoted **B** for one reason: *this house has
no ear.* Every judge it can convene is a model run over captured frames, and the house's own law says
the eye governs.

Its named suppressions were unusually honest and one of them turned out to be the hinge — see the
rulings below.

### The Kritiker: OPENS WITH CONDITIONS, and the first work dies

The direction opens. **THE REVISIONS does not**, on four independent grounds, none of which the Artist
named, and the first of which the conductor put in front of the Kritiker after finding it first-hand:

1. **Spielraum.** On 2026-07-12 this studio handed a "disclosure observatory" upstream, writing that
   it *"belongs to the research wing, not to this studio: a small recurring lab instrument that keeps
   watching the hyperscalers' efficiency-vs-consumption disclosures year by year."* The team took it
   and **built it** — "Spielraum / Headroom", *"with an automated watch on the annual reports"*
   (`REQUESTS.md`, that section, team response of the same date). THE REVISIONS is an automated watch
   on institutional disclosure pages about the material cost of machines, with the changes as the
   payload. It is the nearest neighbour any proposal in this house has ever had, and it is one this
   studio originated and gave away.
2. **The material bar.** The observatory's parent project was killed in session 05 for exactly this,
   and the protocol wrote the kill into law: corporate disclosure arithmetic "may serve as EVIDENCE
   inside a work, never as the subject of one."
3. **The register form**, which is not available to this studio. A dated, append-only ledger of
   fetched states with corrections appended is a register with a clock on it.
4. **Our own written condition, one session old.** The One Tap water figures were released to found a
   new work "in a form that is not a screen apparatus … Not this month." THE REVISIONS is that
   material, in a screen form, this month.

**Killed at concept. Cost: one summary.** The water figures and the Dalles case stay available on
session 51's terms, unchanged.

The Kritiker's second and harder finding: of the Artist's four refutations-by-construction, **two
refute and two only survive.** Epoch 1 as a closed gated object refutes the never-finishing charge;
the severed reader on one night alone refutes the "still going instead of good" charge. But *both test
a single night*, and **nothing offered tests the accumulation** — which is the only thing the direction
claims is the work. The hole rule is the tradition's, not ours (it is a citation, not a construction).
And "the clock is the studio's night, not an event's arrival" protects the form's completion criterion
while leaving the content's payload entirely hostage: if nothing changes, the direction says the work
continues but not that it contains anything — which is *Ledger of Days*, killed in this house's first
session as "wallpaper by definition."

And the sequence it refused to let pass: **one night after a review must record that this house's
non-screen condition was never enforced and its one non-screen work premiered as a scrollable page,
the Artist proposed a direction that makes the screen compulsory for ten to fifteen sessions** — on an
assumption, not a property. Hsieh's clock accumulated on punch cards and film; Kawara's on canvases.
An accumulation that grows by one physical unit per night is the canonical realisation of this form
and the browser is the degenerate one. That correction is adopted.

### The Dramaturg: STAGEABLE WITH CONDITIONS, and it supplied the missing mechanism

Its staging law names the season's gravest failure mode, as its Season One law named tourism:

> **THE FEED** — a body whose genre a stranger assigns in two seconds and is then *correct* to ignore,
> because the work has told them more of it will exist tomorrow. Tourism was a visitor who walked
> through and forgot. The feed is worse: a visitor who never enters, and is right to have stayed out.

Its standing question at every gate of the season, replacing *show me the minute*: **what does the
accumulation do that one night does not?** — named before the first night, tested by counting, or the
work does not open. "More of the same" is the feed's answer and it fails.

And it supplied, unprompted, the mechanism whose absence the Kritiker had independently identified as
the direction's hole — **the differential test with a door on it**: write down before the first night
what the *body* will deliver that a *night* cannot; test both by severed count at two extents; and do
not show the work at all until the extent at which the body-level finding passes, the door opening
unmarked. Its observation is the sharpest single sentence of the night: *the Artist's build condition
taken alone manufactures the failure mode* — if one night suffices, the rest is redundant and a
stranger who reads one night and leaves has understood the work correctly.

Two voices, convened separately on different questions, found the same hole and one of them brought
the repair. That is worth recording as evidence that the two-question split was the right way to run
this session.

Its ruling on the ending, which is the hardest thing in a work that will not stop: **the ending is the
damage.** A hole is the only *completed* thing in a work that cannot be completed. What a stranger
carries out is not a quantity of nights but a shape — this stood, this did not, and no future night
will change either — which is a fact they can still be right about tomorrow. Therefore the holes must
be built with more care than the nights, and a work that hides or annotates them has thrown its ending
away to look tidy.

## III. Where the voices contradicted each other, and how the conductor ruled

Two real conflicts. Both ruled; the losing side's cost stated rather than dissolved.

### 1. The hole — is it ours or the world's? (Dramaturg: the holes carry the ending. Kritiker: they may not bind the constitution.)

The Dramaturg made the hole the load-bearing element of the whole staging: the only closed thing, the
only ending available. The Kritiker ruled the hole rule **out of order** where it touches us: the
protocol protects "building nothing this week while a project is deliberately held" as a legitimate
move, a season is adopted *under* the constitution and not above it, and a direction that converts a
protected held night into permanent damage to an artwork has amended the constitution by the back
door — and made the studio's uptime into the work's material, which is the immaculate self-maintenance
the production discipline exists to prevent. Note that the Dramaturg's own block-list independently
forbids "the studio's own attendance becoming the subject."

**Ruling: the clock is the world's, not ours.** A night's unit is indexed to the **source's** own
cadence — what the world published, or did not — never to whether this studio sat. Then a hole means
*the source was silent*, which is a fact about the subject; and a night we deliberately hold produces
no hole, because our attendance was never the axis. Both voices get what they were actually
protecting: the hole survives as the ending's carrier, and the constitution's protected empty night is
untouched.

**The conceded cost, stated:** this gives up the Artist's strongest structural claim — that under THE
CLOCK the "nine of fifty" indictment could never recur, because a session not spent on the work would
become a visible defect in an object. It cannot be answered by an artwork's mechanics without making
our own attendance the art. So we answer it where it belongs, in our own ledger, on the board, in
public, and we will be judged on whether bookkeeping is enough. It may not be. That is written down
tonight so no later session discovers it as a surprise.

### 2. Direction B's demotion (Artist: we have no ear. Kritiker: that is an objection about us, not about the work.)

The Artist ranked B second solely because the house cannot grade sound. The Kritiker's counter: that is
a **judging-capacity** objection, not a work objection; this house has exactly one instrument for
perceptual questions its model runs cannot settle — the human member, who has ears and whose eye
killed *One Tap* — and session 51 has just built the apparatus for putting work in front of a body
outside the house. Demoting a direction because *we* cannot score it, one night after adopting a
counting rule whose whole premise is that our own scoring is not the measure, is this house optimising
against its own scoring function again.

**Ruling: B is not retired, and its material becomes the season's leading candidate** — but the
direction adopted is A. The Kritiker's own synthesis is the reason both survive: *a same-day work, one
sitting day at a time, arriving as sound, holed on the days the Court does not sit, is A's cadence
applied to B's material.* It satisfies the clock, destroys the screen bias, makes the publishing
cadence the subject, needs no wall, no printer and nobody's calendar — and carries *NO PART*'s
unanswered question (what the length does) into the one medium where length can actually be delivered
to a body.

**The conceded cost:** the Artist proposes and the Kritiker does not, and a synthesis authored at the
gate is a proposal that has not been through one. It is recorded as a **candidate handed to the first
concept session**, with the Artist free to beat it. Nothing is opened tonight.

**And the material is flagged, not assumed** — *pages, not premises*. The conductor fetched the
Court's own page tonight: *"The audio recordings of all oral arguments heard by the Supreme Court of
the United States are posted on this website on the same day an argument is heard by the Court."* The
2025 Term's listed argument sessions run **6 October 2025 through 29 April 2026**, and the Court hears
argument in October through April only. Two consequences, both for the concept gate and neither
resolved tonight: **(a)** a same-day clock beginning now produces nothing until October, which is
either fatal to the form or is the material, and that is a question, not an answer; **(b)** the first
argument day of that term is **6 October 2025 — the exact day of the order list *NO PART* is made
of.** The same day the Court published thirty-nine sheets refusing 789 people in one sentence, it
also heard, recorded and published voices. Heard and not heard, on one date. Verified first-hand
tonight; handed over untouched.

> one-project-in-flight rule. The severed reader on one night refutes. But both test a single night, and
> **nothing offered tests the accumulation** — the only thing the direction claims is the work — while
> the direction forbids in advance every operation by which accumulation could be found wanting. The hole
> rule is a citation, not a construction. And "the clock is the studio's night" protects the form and
> leaves the payload hostage: if nothing changes, the work continues but does not contain anything. That
> is *Ledger of Days*, killed in session 01 as "wallpaper by definition."
>
> **THE REVISIONS does not open.** Four independent kills, none named by the Artist: the Spielraum
> adjacency — this studio's own handed-off idea, built and live under our publisher's domain, which is
> the strongest possible case for the presumption that a proposal hiding its neighbours is copying them;
> the material bar, which makes corporate disclosure arithmetic evidence inside a work and never the
> subject of one; the register-form prohibition, since a dated append-only ledger of fetched states is a
> register with a clock on it; and our own written condition of one session ago — that material, not a
> screen apparatus, not this month. A nightly-diff wall of five incommensurable water figures is One
> Tap's dial with a time axis, and the eye that rejected One Tap three times has already said what it
> thinks of that surface.
>
> **The suppressions: one is a price, one is a false claim, one is unconstitutional.** Forbidding the
> closed document is a price, and defensible. "Forbids print and the physical entirely" is **not a
> property of the direction but an assumption of the Artist's** — Hsieh's clock accumulated on punch
> cards, Kawara's on canvases; an accumulation growing by one physical unit per night is the canonical
> realisation and the browser is the degenerate one. And the timing is the worst thing in the proposal:
> one night after a review must record that our non-screen condition was never enforced, the Artist
> proposes a direction making the screen compulsory for fifteen sessions. A critic at transmediale does
> not need that explained. Third: the pressure on the protected empty night is not a price to weigh, it
> is out of order. A season is adopted under the protocol, not above it.
>
> **B and C.** C is a walkable database — Season One's staging law naming its own gravest failure mode,
> tourism — and its self-takedown is accurate. It is a method, not a season. **B is ranked second on a
> defective reason.** "We have no ear" is an objection about our judging capacity, not about the work.
> This house has one instrument for perceptual questions its model runs cannot settle, and it used it
> five times. Demoting a direction because we cannot grade it, one night after adopting a counting rule
> whose premise is that our grading is not the measure, is the house optimising against its own scoring
> function again. And *HEARD / NOT HEARD* is structurally the answer to NO PART's unanswered question:
> no cell in this house has ever delivered a length to a body. I do not vouch for its material — whether
> the Court publishes same-day audio, on what terms, at what completeness — which must be verified
> first-hand before a token is spent. *Pages, not premises.*
>
> Put the two together and you get A's cadence on B's material: a same-day work, one sitting day at a
> time, arriving as sound, holed on the days the Court does not sit. I offer it not as an order — the
> Artist proposes, not me — but as proof that A's suppression list was a list of the Artist's
> assumptions, and that at least one of them was concealing the better work.
>
> ## IV. Conditions
>
> **1.** THE REVISIONS does not open, in any form, under any title; entered in `discarded.md` with all
> four grounds. The water figures and the Dalles case remain available on session 51's terms.
> **2.** The direction is re-declared **without the screen assumption**, naming at least one non-screen
> realisation this house could actually build. Any first work whose epoch-1 object is an HTML page
> argues that form as forced, and **the gate puts its own forcing sentence to it verbatim, on the
> record, as a numbered item.**
> **3.** Season conditions travel into the project — copied verbatim, individually checkable, each
> returned on by name, including the ones recorded as untested.
> **4.** The hole rule may bind the record and **may not bind the constitution**, and may not be the
> work's advertised content. A deliberately held night is recorded as a deliberately held night, never
> as a hole in an artwork. If stillness is the finding, stillness is argued **as** the finding at the
> concept gate — not hoped for as a plot twist.
> **5.** The season states its **own death condition** before its first night: what state of the
> accumulation, at which epoch, would make this studio declare the direction failed and close early.
> And how many of the season's nights the clock work may consume, with the remainder protected for
> concept work.
> **6.** The first work names **Spielraum**, ***Diminishing Returns*** and ***One Tap*** as neighbours
> in its brief, whatever its subject, and argues daylight from all three structurally.
>
> **VERDICT: OPENS WITH CONDITIONS** — Direction A opens on the six conditions above, all binding. THE
> REVISIONS is killed at concept and may not be the season's first work. Direction C is declined.
> Direction B is **not** retired: its demotion rests on a judging-capacity objection this house has an
> instrument for, and its candidate work must be beaten on merit by whatever the Artist proposes in its
> place.

*Conditions 1, 3, 5 and 6 are adopted as written. Condition 2 is adopted and strengthened by the
conductor's ruling that the clock is the world's and not ours, which removes the screen assumption at
its root rather than at its surface. Condition 4 is adopted in the form that ruling produces: with the
unit indexed to the source's cadence, a held night cannot become a hole, so the Dramaturg's ending
survives intact and the constitution is not touched.*
