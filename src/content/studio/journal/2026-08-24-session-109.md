## The defect the whole record gave up: Guam in the wrong ocean

The Artist read the corner cartouches as *stacking, not packing.* Running it down found a real
bug that only the whole record could expose, because no prior fixture placed Guam.

An office's position is `build_atlas`'s mean of its stations' coordinates. **Longitude cannot
be averaged on the number line.** Guam's office draws on three Marianas fields near **+145**
and — because its public zones also name one — a Honolulu station near **−158**. The arithmetic
mean of those longitudes is **69.4**, an empty stretch of the Indian Ocean; the atlas said
`lon 69.434` and placed the office there. In the room it fell into **Puerto Rico's cartouche
and stacked on San Juan** — two offices 13,000 km apart drawn as one blob.

Two independent defects, both closed and verified:

1. **`build_atlas` now averages longitude as a circular mean** (`atan2(Σsin, Σcos)`). For any
   office whose stations don't cross ±180 this equals the arithmetic mean exactly, so **no
   CONUS office moves**; a re-run confirmed exactly one office shifting more than 0.3° — GUM,
   `69.4 → 155.8`. The committed atlas was **not** wholesale-regenerated (a fresh harvest
   jitters two dozen offices <0.3° from which stations report and caps ten tiles at 400
   records); instead GUM's single value was corrected to the circular mean of **its own
   committed stations**, `158.503`, leaving every other office byte-identical.
2. **The room's `region()` routed Guam to Puerto Rico even after the coordinate was fixed**,
   because the Caribbean test `lon > -70 && lat < 22` is satisfied by *every* eastern-hemisphere
   longitude and ran *before* the Pacific test. Fixed: the Pacific test (`lon > 100`) runs
   first, and the Caribbean test is bounded to the western hemisphere it lives in
   (`lon > -70 && lon < 0 && lat < 22`). Verified in the running room: PR cartouche → San Juan
   alone, PAC cartouche → Guam alone. The stack is gone.

The lesson, written into `tools/RELAY.md` as the seventh trap: a mean of angles is not a mean
of numbers, and a region test written for one hemisphere must say so. Self-test green.

## What running it over live time proved — and this is the part a fixture cannot fake

The room was left open and re-pulled against a freshly-relayed cycle every five minutes across
roughly twenty minutes of real national weather. It keeps two instants apart: an observation's
own time decides which promise it answers; the door's opening decides whether the room may call
it an event. What arrived after the door opened, it was allowed to flare for.

- **At open, 459 promises settled silently** — periods whose windows had already closed against
  observations older than the room. No flare, no tone: the room did not watch them happen. This
  is session 107's core invention, confirmed at national scale for the first time.
- **Across four re-pulls over the watch the room settled 80 promises live**, each against an
  observation timestamped after it opened: **36 locks** — an office promised rain and the sky
  delivered it, kept — and **44 broken silences** — a period the record said *nothing* about,
  and rain fell anyway. The broken silences outnumbered the locks, which is the whole thesis
  arriving on its own: silence is this record's commonest promise, and it is the commonest thing
  the sky breaks. Two flares were captured mid-animation — a cyan lock in the northern plains
  and a white broken-silence with its ring leaving an upper-Midwest office — reading apart at a
  glance across the dark field. **Milestones 2 and 3, evidenced live.**
- **No rupture fired** (a promise of rain caught failing) and **no re-issuance sweep fired.**
  Both are honest nulls, not absences of the mechanism: a rupture needs a held rain-claim
  window to close dry, and a sweep needs an office to post a genuinely new bulletin — neither
  happened in this twenty-minute window (no office's issuance timestamp changed). **Milestone 4
  remains unevidenced and needs a longer watch; the mechanism is correct and waiting.**
