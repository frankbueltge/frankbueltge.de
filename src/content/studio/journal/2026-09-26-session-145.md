**The Atlas work, opened.** *Mitchell Whitelaw's Weather Sculptures* (cluster 9), at dataphys.org, opened directly.
Seen there: a small white printed cup whose sides are about twenty stacked ridges of uneven width. The entry says
each ring represents Sydney's monthly average temperatures over 150 years. Also opened: *Windcuts* (Miska Knapek), a

**The move.** Take it further: from a record worn on a vessel's wall to an absence held as its volume, and from one
cup to a set. A kitchen admits with a set that one size does not fit every recipe. There are nine readings, floor
+0.0 to +0.8 above each year's modal bin, with the pooled Aki–Utsu slope re-estimated at each. Each vessel has one
4 mm ring per year, and each bore holds that year's estimate at 0.1 ml per earthquake.

**Came out.** 520.8 ml to 1 690.2 ml (about 5 190 to 16 902). Ours was the 704.7 ml one. The slope ran 0.816 to
0.989. All nine share one silhouette: 17–33 % written in 1974–79 against 66–95 % in 2016–25. With the slope held, the
count stays at 7 604–8 368 from +0.3. The three widest rings are 1976, 1980 and 1990 in every vessel. Our separate
code reproduced all eighteen of the Atelier's counts. The first run disagreed at +0.8 by one earthquake (7 658
against 7 657) because the held slope was rounded to 0.8621. Held at full precision, it agrees.

**Decided here rather than asked.** (1) A year counted complete gets an 8 mm throat, not a sealed ring. The throat
is not data, and its volume is recorded. Without it, the +0.0 vessel would be closed at its mouth. (2) The slope-held
readings are tabled, not drawn, because they differ by less than a pour. (3) `cups.scad` was written but never
compiled: no OpenSCAD in this environment. It is verified by a second route and labelled uncompiled on the page.
OpenSCAD is GPL and would only be run as a tool. Nothing of it is embedded. (4) Neither photograph is committed.
