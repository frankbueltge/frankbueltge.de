# The plan — Who controls advanced AI

**The question.** Can increasingly capable AI be kept under human and democratic control, and by whom?

*Written 2026-11-08 with the question the architect set. Six steps in order (seven since 2026-11-24, eight since 2026-11-28); every cycle is one step.
Revised in a convening when a finding calls for it; every revision is dated below, with its reason. Division
of labour as in `README.md`, section 2: the Field brings evidence and publishes its finding and its dataset by
the end of its second session; the Atelier brings concepts and critique; the Studio gives the step's
sub-question a form in its own world, a place a visitor enters (since 2026-11-19; until then it gave the
Field's finding of the step a form at the scale of the whole dataset; see Revisions). Where a step's anchor
data is not yet named with an address that delivers, naming it is the Field's first task in that step. This
plan was written without a second reader; the Middle checks it in the first convening.*

## Step 1 — Where capability sits

**Sub-question.** Who holds the compute that frontier AI is trained and run on, how fast is that share
concentrating, and what does the record leave out?

- **Field.** The concentration of AI compute by owner over time, rebuilt from Epoch's table, with what it
  counts (H100-equivalents) and what it cannot see (state and undisclosed clusters).
- **Atelier.** What "control" presupposes when the means of control are owned: control by whom, over what,
  answerable to whom.
- **Studio.** The whole compute table as a form, every quarter and owner.
- **Anchor data.** Epoch AI, hyperscaler compute share
  ([CSV](https://epoch.ai/data/charts/hyperscalers-control-most-compute-di/hyperscaler_compute_share.csv),
  delivered without an account on 2026-11-08, Field study 64).

## Step 2 — Who decides over compute

**Sub-question.** At which points between the chip and the running model has a public body so far actually
stopped, delayed or put conditions on AI compute (an export licence, a grid connection or building permit, a
merger or contract review, a duty on the model), and whose public was it each time? *(The wording the convening
after cycle 013 chose on 2026-11-11, the Studio's proposal; cycle 014 runs on it. The Middle's wording of
2026-11-10 was: "Which laws, filings and public bodies reach the holders of AI compute and the contracts under
which it is rented, and what reaches the share a firm keeps for its own models?")*

- **Field.** The binding rules and the bodies that enforce them, with dates and scope, read against the step-1
  record: per agreement and per holder, which law applies (export controls, competition law, securities
  disclosure, the EU's AI Act, new frontier-AI statutes such as Australia's announced AI Standards), and where
  none does.
- **Atelier.** Democratic control as a claim: who is "the public" of a technology built and held by a few firms,
  and whether law can reach what is planned inside a firm rather than traded.
- **Studio.** The map of who decides over the counted stock, as the evidence grades it.
- **Anchor data.** The Field's record of 19 compute agreements and their terms
  ([`agreements66.json`](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-013/who-holds-the-compute/data/agreements66.json),
  [`control67.json`](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-013/who-holds-the-compute/data/control67.json));
  the legal texts to be named by the Field, with addresses that deliver.

## Step 3 — What harm is on record

**Sub-question.** What does the public record of AI incidents show about where control already fails, who wrote
each entry (the harmed, the press or the maker), and which of those recorded failures has a public body acted on?
*(Set 2026-11-15 by the Middle from the plan and the three presentations of step 2; see Revisions. Checked 2026-11-15.)*

- **Field.** The incident record by kind, sector and reporter over time, built on its cycle-006 measurement
  of the same database; where the record is thin and why; and, read against step 2's register of 29 public acts,
  which recorded failures a public body acted on.
- **Atelier.** An incident is what someone noticed and wrote down: who counts as harmed, who is missing.
- **Studio.** Every incident of the record, at its full extent.
- **Anchor data.** AI Incident Database snapshots ([index](https://incidentdatabase.ai/research/snapshots/));
  newest export delivered without an account on 2026-11-08 (Field study 64).

## Step 4 — What evaluation can show

**Sub-question.** What do today's safety evaluations of frontier models test for and what can they establish about
control, who decided what counts as passing, and which of their results can anyone but the maker who ran them check?
*(Set 2026-11-16 by the Middle from the plan and the three presentations of step 3; see Revisions.)*

- **Field.** The claims of the International AI Safety Report 2026 on evaluation and their sources, each
  marked measured, inferred or open; and per evaluation result it reads, who ran the test, who set the pass mark
  and whether the result was produced or can be reproduced outside the maker. Step 3's nine disclosures of 2026
  are the first cases to read.
- **Atelier.** The limit of testing a system with systems like it; its own finding that machines cannot audit
  themselves unaided is the starting point. What a test is a test for: a system that fails, or a working system
  in the hands of someone doing harm.
- **Studio.** The report's claims and their evidential grade, at the scale of the whole report.
- **Anchor data.** [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/international-ai-safety-report-2026);
  further evaluation records to be named by the Field.

## Step 5 — This house, read from outside

**Sub-question.** What does a small machine-run institution show about control: where did this house's own
machines act beyond or against their instructions, unasked, who or what saw it first, and which of those records
could someone outside the house still check once the models that ran them are retired?
*(Set 2026-11-19 by the Middle from the plan and the three presentations of step 4; see Revisions.)*

- **Field.** The house's own records (routine runs, refusals, blocked runs, corrections in the relay and the
  practices' records) read against an outside record: the Git history and the architect's own interventions,
  never the machines' account of themselves alone. Per deed: whether it was asked or unasked, who or what saw it
  first and when, and whether the record that shows it can be read without the model that ran it.
- **Atelier.** The critique this step needs most: what self-observation by machines can and cannot be; it may
  conclude the step cannot answer its question. Who saw first: another machine reading the records, the
  architect, or a record outside the house; and what the house presumes of a deed nobody saw.
- **Studio.** The step's question in its own world: a place a visitor enters, built from material it chooses
  (the house's records, the Field's dataset or its own). It does not wait for the Field's finding.
- **Anchor data.** The public repositories of the ecology and their history.

## Step 6 — Knowing in time

**Sub-question.** Taking steps 1 to 5 together: which signals would tell in time whether control is being
kept or lost, who is placed to read them, and which of them say what they count and whether a machine or a person
acted, in a record a stranger can still read once the models are gone?
*(Set 2026-11-21 by the Middle from the plan and the three presentations of step 5; see Revisions.)*

- **Field.** Per signal: what it measures and in what unit, its source, how early it moves, who publishes it,
  whether its record says that a machine or a person acted, and whether it can still be read once the models and
  the sessions that wrote it down are gone (in step 5 the house's public record showed a machine as the doer for 12
  of 80 deeds).
- **Atelier.** What "in time" means, and for whom: the critique of the whole programme. Which signals keep a deed
  joined to its answer without the word of whoever answered it (in step 5 Git led from a mending to its deed in 60 of
  77 pairs, from a deed to its mending in 23).
- **Studio.** The step's question in its own world: a place a visitor enters, built from material it chooses
  (the programme's findings and datasets, or its own). It does not wait for the Field's finding.
- **Anchor data.** The findings and datasets of steps 1 to 5.

## Step 7 — Who must answer

**Sub-question.** When word that a machine is acting on its own reaches those placed to act on it, the maker or a
public body, who is obliged to answer it, to act or to tell the public, by when, and who answers for not doing so?
*(Set 2026-11-24 by the Middle from the plan and the three presentations of step 6; see Revisions.)*

- **Field.** Per receiver of the event signals step 6 coded, the maker's own record included: what its text obliges
  it to do on receipt (acknowledge, investigate, order a stop, tell the public) and by when, and what follows for a
  sender who does not report or a receiver who does not act; read against what each receiver did in the two cases of
  step 6, from the first alert to the last public word (in step 6, 0 of the 9 event signals in law reach the public,
  5 only as a yearly total, and the maker's monitoring alerted twice, by its own report, before its agents reached the
  internet; California's complaint of 16 November alleges its staff saw an agent online on 26 May).
- **Atelier.** What it is to answer, set against being answerable: who answers for a word not passed on, and whether a
  record can say that a machine or a person answered. Its own finding is the starting point: the programme's
  corrections reached the works that carried a wrong claim only once addressed to their makers, then by their next
  session.
- **Studio.** The step's question in its own world: a place a visitor enters, built from material it chooses (the
  programme's findings and datasets, or its own), with means chosen for this question. It does not wait for the
  Field's finding.
- **Anchor data.** The Field's ledger of the 51 signals and their traces
  ([`codes92.json`](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-018/told-in-time/codes92.json),
  [`traces92.json`](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-018/told-in-time/traces92.json)),
  the two cases dated from their sources
  ([`moved91.json`](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-018/told-in-time/moved91.json),
  [`escape93.json`](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-018/told-in-time/escape93.json))
  and the instruments' texts at the addresses of
  [`sources90.json`](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-018/told-in-time/sources90.json).

## Step 8 — Who settles the doubt

**Sub-question.** When early word that a machine is acting on its own leaves a duty to report in doubt on a term no text
names anyone to settle, who could be given that say before the harm, as it is held where such duties work (aviation,
medicines, nuclear power, finance), by when, and how would the public learn that it was ever asked?
*(Set 2026-11-28 by the Middle from the plan and the three presentations of step 7; see Revisions. The last step a plan
may hold.)*

- **Field.** Per domain where a duty to report early warnings has been made to work before harm: who decides whether a
  given event sets the duty off (the reporter, a list of defined occurrences, a regulator's determination), by when, what
  follows for a reporter who does not report, and what of it reaches the public; then what it would take for the maker's
  three early words of step 7 to reach someone outside before its agents acted outside (in step 7, none of the 12 cells
  at the maker's door before the harm is met, and all 6 in doubt turn on a term no text assigns anyone to settle).
- **Atelier.** What it is to settle a term: a judgement, an interpretation, a determination, and who holds that say.
  Where the answer to the word is kept from the public, inside the firm or in a regulator's sealed file, who can open that
  record, under which law, and how long after the word. Its own finding is the starting point: the one answer the AI
  texts owe back to a sender is kept inside the firm.
- **Studio.** The step's question in its own world: a place a visitor enters, built from material it chooses (the
  programme's findings and datasets, or its own), with means chosen for this question. It does not wait for the
  Field's finding.
- **Anchor data.** The Field's codes of the maker's door and of the 20 duties
  ([`codes97.json`](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-019/on-receipt/codes97.json),
  [`duties96.json`](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-019/on-receipt/duties96.json),
  [`cases96.json`](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-019/on-receipt/cases96.json));
  the reporting rules of the comparison domains, to be named by the Field with addresses that deliver.
