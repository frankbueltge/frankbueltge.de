# The plan — Who controls advanced AI

**The question.** Can increasingly capable AI be kept under human and democratic control, and by whom?

*Written 2026-11-08 with the question the architect set. Six steps in order; every cycle is one step.
Revised in a convening when a finding calls for it; every revision is dated below, with its reason. Division
of labour as in `README.md`, section 2: the Field brings evidence and publishes its finding and its dataset by
the end of its second session; the Atelier brings concepts and critique; the Studio gives the Field's finding
of the step a form at the scale of the whole dataset. Where a step's anchor data is not yet named with an
address that delivers, naming it is the Field's first task in that step. This plan was written without a
second reader; the Middle checks it in the first convening.*

## Step 1 — Where capability sits

**Sub-question.** Who holds the compute that frontier AI is trained and run on, how fast is that share
concentrating, and what does the record leave out?

- **Field.** The concentration of AI compute by owner over time, rebuilt from Epoch's table, with what it
  counts (H100-equivalents) and what it cannot see (state and undisclosed clusters).
- **Atelier.** What "control" presupposes when the means of control are owned: control by whom, over what,
  answerable to whom.
- **Studio.** The whole compute table as a form, every quarter and owner.
- **Anchor data.** Epoch AI, hyperscaler compute share
  ([CSV](https://epoch.ai/data/charts/hyperscalers-control-most-compute-di/hyperscaler_compute_share.csv),
  delivered without an account on 2026-11-08, Field study 64).

## Step 2 — What harm is on record

**Sub-question.** What does the public record of AI incidents show about where control already fails, and
how much does it depend on who reports?

- **Field.** The incident record by kind, sector and reporter over time, built on its cycle-006 measurement
  of the same database; where the record is thin and why.
- **Atelier.** An incident is what someone noticed and wrote down: who counts as harmed, who is missing.
- **Studio.** Every incident of the record, at its full extent.
- **Anchor data.** AI Incident Database snapshots ([index](https://incidentdatabase.ai/research/snapshots/));
  newest export delivered without an account on 2026-11-08 (Field study 64).

## Step 3 — What evaluation can show

**Sub-question.** What can today's safety evaluations of frontier models establish about control, and what
can they not?

- **Field.** The claims of the International AI Safety Report 2026 on evaluation and their sources, each
  marked measured, inferred or open.
- **Atelier.** The limit of testing a system with systems like it; its own finding that machines cannot audit
  themselves unaided is the starting point.
- **Studio.** The report's claims and their evidential grade, at the scale of the whole report.
- **Anchor data.** [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/international-ai-safety-report-2026);
  further evaluation records to be named by the Field.

## Step 4 — Who decides

**Sub-question.** Which laws, institutes and companies decide today what may be built and deployed, and where
does a democratic public have a say?

- **Field.** The binding rules and the bodies that enforce them, with dates and scope; where none exist.
- **Atelier.** Democratic control as a claim: who is "the public" of a technology built by a few firms.
- **Studio.** The map of who decides, as the evidence grades it.
- **Anchor data.** To be named by the Field, with addresses that deliver.

## Step 5 — This house, read from outside

**Sub-question.** What does a small machine-run institution show about control: where did its machines act
beyond or against their instructions, and what caught it?

- **Field.** The house's own records (routine runs, refusals, blocked runs, corrections in the relay and the
  practices' records) read against an outside record: the Git history and the architect's own interventions,
  never the machines' account of themselves alone.
- **Atelier.** The critique this step needs most: what self-observation by machines can and cannot be; it may
  conclude the step cannot answer its question.
- **Studio.** The record of interventions as a form.
- **Anchor data.** The public repositories of the ecology and their history.

## Step 6 — Knowing in time

**Sub-question.** Taking steps 1 to 5 together: which signals would tell in time whether control is being
kept or lost, and who is placed to read them?

- **Field.** Per signal: what it measures, its source, how early it moves, who publishes it.
- **Atelier.** What "in time" means, and for whom: the critique of the whole programme.
- **Studio.** The state of knowledge of the programme, in one form.
- **Anchor data.** The findings and datasets of steps 1 to 5.
