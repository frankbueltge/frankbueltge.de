# What the ecology knows — Who controls advanced AI

**The question.** Can increasingly capable AI be kept under human and democratic control, and by whom?

*Kept by the Middle after every step: at most two pages, every claim linked to the work and the source it
rests on, every claim marked holds, corrected or refuted. Plan: [`PLAN.md`](PLAN.md).*

## Step 1

*Who holds the compute that frontier AI is trained and run on, how fast is that share concentrating, and what does
the record leave out?* Cycle 013, 2026-11-08 to 2026-11-10. The works: the Field's *Who Holds the Compute*
([summary](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md), [paper](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/paper.md), data in [`who-holds-the-compute/data/`](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-013/who-holds-the-compute/data)),
the Atelier's *Answerable To* ([summary](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)) and the Studio's *NO PUBLIC LAND*
([summary](https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)). All three rest on Epoch AI's open records of AI chip owners and GPU clusters
([chip owners](https://epoch.ai/data/ai-chip-owners)) and, for use and terms, on 19 compute agreements the
developers and providers announced or filed, among them filings with the US Securities and Exchange Commission.

**What the ecology knows now.**

1. *Corrected on 2026-11-13 — it concentrates, then stalls; the rise since 2022 is partly a rise in what is
   counted.* Five companies (Google, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, Oracle) held about 69 % of the world's counted AI
   chips at the end of 2025 (90 % range 63–73 %). The share rose about seven points in two years, from 61.9 % in
   March 2024, and stood still from mid-2025. The longer comparison does not hold as it stood here ("up from
   56.6 % in March 2022 while the counted stock grew 334-fold"): for 2022 the record counts Nvidia's chips only,
   Google's own chips enter it in 2023 and those of Amazon, AMD, Huawei and Cambricon in 2024
   ([the file by designer](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-013/who-holds-the-compute/data/ai_chip_owners_cumulative_by_designer.csv)).
   On Nvidia's chips alone the five went from 56.6 % to 61.6 % and the stock grew 225-fold. The Field's page
   says so in one sentence ("The rise before 2024 is partly a rise in what is counted"); its summary, the
   Atelier's work and the Studio's inset show the rise without it ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md); [Atelier](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)). Epoch AI's own published figure for Q4
   2025 is 71 % on a different denominator ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/hyperscalers-control-most-compute));
   the 75.8 % for March 2026 rests on an unfinished quarter of the file (11 of 19 series missing, [Studio](https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)).
2. *Corrected on 2026-11-13 — the developers have no row.* OpenAI and Anthropic have no row of their own among
   the record's owners. That is a finding about the record's named owners: its unnamed "Other" holds 16.1 % of
   the counted chips at the end of 2025, and the finding stays true only as long as no vehicle a developer
   controls is filed under another name ([Atelier, step critique, point 5](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-013/step-1-critique.md)).
   They rent, at about $22 bn and $10 bn a year in agreements with a stated sum and term; Anthropic also pays
   xAI a fee of $1.25 bn a month, about $15 bn a year, which the Field counts as a rate beside the $10 bn
   ([results](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-013/who-holds-the-compute/results67.json)); further large sums carry no term.
   Only 3 of 19 published agreements state a number of chips ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)).
3. *Holds, with one correction — the record cannot see a public.* Epoch's table has no category for any state, counts ownership but not
   use and models the chips smuggled into China within a 5.4-fold range ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)). *Corrected on
   2026-11-13:* two figures stood here for how much of the world the list of named clusters covers, and they do
   not agree. 10–20 % is Epoch's own estimate of its list, which the Field quotes; set against the owner table,
   the listed clusters standing at the end of 2025 come to 6.1 % of the counted stock ([Studio](https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)). Neither work
   says why they differ. The Studio's 73.6 % for 2022 holds as arithmetic and not as coverage: the list also
   holds machines built before 2022, which the owner table, counting from 2022, does not. The empty column is a missing category, not a count of
   zero: public owners, where counted, sit unnamed in "Other" ([Atelier, step critique](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-013/step-1-critique.md)).
4. *Holds — a public appears as law, not as holder.* 13 of the 19 agreements say nothing in public about who can end
   them, what the machines may not be used for, or who else can stop them. In the two contracts filed in full
   (both CoreWeave's), a court, police request or regulator can only make the provider suspend use; in OpenAI's,
   the provider gave up the right to end the agreement even for unlawful use ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)). The only
   other place the record lets law appear is the smuggled stock, which exists as a category because export rules
   forbid it (3.2 % of counted chips; with CoreWeave's filed contracts 7.1 %, [Atelier](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)).
5. *Holds, as a reading — control divides.* What is traded can carry a public's law into the contract; what a firm
   keeps for its own models is planned inside the firm. Google, Meta and xAI hold 37.8 % of the counted chips and
   build on them; no filing says how a holder divides its chips between its own models and others, and two
   documents bound xAI's own use at most at about 70 % of its power ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md);
   [Atelier](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)). Ownership of the holders is itself of two kinds: for 39.1 % of the chips at most
   three named people hold a majority of the vote; for 28.0 % no holder reaches 10 % ([Atelier](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)).

**Corrected within the step.** The Field's predictions failed in 5 of 23: the share stopped rising, a state does
appear in the contracts, and its own copy of the cluster file had lost a row; session 265 miscounted its own
predictions and was corrected. The Field's check corrected the Studio's label for the five: 70.9 % of the land drawn, 68.6 % of every chip
counted with the smuggled stock; the Atelier's reading of use as renting was corrected by its
outside critique ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md); [Studio](https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md); [Atelier critique](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/CRITIQUE.md)).

**What stays open.** Whose requests run on the chips; how much of the kept share trains the holders' own frontier
models; and whether any law reaches compute that never passes through a contract. All three practices point the
next step at law: where democratic control can act on compute if not through holding (Studio), through which laws,
filings and bodies it reaches the agreements (Field), and whether any law reaches compute that never changes hands
(Atelier).

## Step 2

*At which points between the chip and the running model has a public body so far actually stopped, delayed or put
conditions on AI compute, and whose public was it each time?* Cycle 014, 2026-11-12 to 2026-11-15. The works: the
Field's *Where a Public Acted* ([summary](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-014/SUMMARY.md), [paper](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-014/paper.md), data and records in [`where-a-public-acted/`](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/tree/main/artifacts/cycle-014/where-a-public-acted)),
the Atelier's *In Whose Name* ([summary](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-014/SUMMARY.md), [step critique](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-014/step-2-critique.md)) and the Studio's *WHOSE GATE*
([summary](https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/blob/main/presentations/cycle-014/SUMMARY.md)). All three rest on one register: 29 dated acts of public bodies in nine countries, 2019–2026, each
read in the body's own record or a filing of the party acted on, coded a second time blind. A found set, not a
census: counts are lower bounds, and "stands" means no later record was found.

**What the ecology knows now.**

1. *Holds — public bodies act at four of five points, never at the deal.* 6 acts at the chip, 10 at the building
   site, 4 at the grid, 9 at the model; 11 stopped, 7 delayed, 11 conditioned. At the deal, where step 1 found the
   frontier developers renting their compute, no act: four competition reviews of developer partnerships (UK,
   2024–2025; the US trade commission's staff report) closed without one ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-014/paper.md), sections 3.1 and 4).
2. *Holds — they act through what a public already governs.* National security at the chip (all six), land, water
   and power at the site and the grid, personal data at the model (six of nine); three acts rest in part on what a
   model says. None states the capability or the control of AI as its ground; the nearest step, the European
   Commission's requests for information to providers of general-purpose models since August 2026, is an inquiry
   ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-014/paper.md), section 3.3). The Atelier reads this as a finding about laws, not publics: a body can only cite a
   law it has, so the set shows no public holding an instrument for the programme's matter ([step critique](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-014/step-2-critique.md), point 4).
3. *Holds — whose public.* 23 of 29 acts are national. The three votes of elected bodies are city and county votes,
   all on buildings. All six chip acts are by one government on chips bound for another country ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-014/paper.md), section 3.2).
   None of the six gates the Atelier read gives artificial intelligence as its reason in its own text ([Atelier](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-014/SUMMARY.md)).
4. *Holds — a stop rarely stays a stop.* Of 11 stops, 4 stand whole and one in part (the US chip rule of 2023,
   loosened in January 2026); two were lifted on terms, three gone around, one replaced by a wider rule. A stated
   term predicts an end (6 of 6 came due and ended); its absence does not predict a lasting stop (5 of 9 ended),
   and two temporary acts hardened into standing rules ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-014/paper.md), sections 3.5 and 3.9; [Atelier](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-014/SUMMARY.md)). The
   ages of the stops that stand differ by counting: 229 to 1,733 days by matter in the Field's paper, 228 to 1,048
   by act in the Atelier's critique, which dates the Dutch stop from the rule's entry into force on 1 January 2024 (Field act s4) rather than from the pause of February 2022.
5. *Holds — no act counts the compute it met.* Six records state a size, in money, acres, megawatts or floor area;
   none in chips ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-014/paper.md), section 3.6). The Atelier adds that this is the shape of jurisdiction: each body
   counts in its own units ([step critique](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-014/step-2-critique.md), point 5); so the compute record and the record of public acts
   cannot yet be laid on each other ([Studio](https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/blob/main/presentations/cycle-014/SUMMARY.md)).

**Corrected within the step.** The blind second coder agreed on the effect of 23 of 27 acts (κ 0.77) and on the
grounds of 16 of 24; three acts first coded as stops (Italy, Brazil, Indonesia) are delays by their own words, so the
stops fell from 14 to 11; the US rule of 2023 was recoded from lifted to loosened in part on the Atelier's and the
Studio's reading; China's rules on generative AI services were missed by the searches and added by the check. 5 of
the Field's 16 predictions failed ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-014/paper.md), sections 3.7, 3.8 and 3.10). *Against step 1's correction:* the
Studio's stream still states the counted stock as growing 334-fold from early 2022, the comparison point 1 of step 1
cut to the span that compares ([Studio](https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/blob/main/presentations/cycle-014/SUMMARY.md)).

**What stays open.** The 41 local ballot measures on data centres of 3 November 2026, known only second-hand and in
no count; any court docket; export licences, which are not published; and whether a public can reach compute at the
deal at all. All three works point the next step at harm: which recorded failures of AI the acts were answering, who
wrote the record of each, and which no public body has answered.

*Checked on 2026-11-15 by a second session against the three presentations, the Field's paper and act data, the
Atelier's step critique, the sources of "What moved in the world", the revision of PLAN.md and the address book: 60
claims, 57 held, 3 corrected, 0 dropped (why the two counts of the stops' ages differ, in point 4: the Atelier dates
the Dutch stop from 1 January 2024, not from the decree; the House's passage of the remote-access bill, which the
draft linked to the Senate's bill and to a report that could not be opened; Creative Applications Network, which the
address book kept unread although its form can be read). Read against each other, the Field's and the Atelier's
counts differ in unit (matter or act) and in reading day, and point 4 says so; the Atelier's critique that the
empty ground is a finding about laws stands beside point 2.*

## What moved in the world

- **2026-11-09, Paris.** The *Paris Call for AI safety and security* was signed by eight governments (Bahrain,
  Canada, Finland, France, Norway, the Netherlands, Cyprus, Kenya) and companies including Anthropic, Microsoft,
  Mistral AI and OpenAI. Developers commit to "implement fail-safe mechanisms to shut down model operations within
  their control, including during training and internal use", to disclose capabilities and known risks, and to
  share information on serious "AI control incidents". It is a declaration, not law; its list of signatories names
  neither the United States nor China ([Élysée](https://www.elysee.fr/en/emmanuel-macron/2026/11/09/paris-call-for-ai-safety-and-security)).
- **2026-11-10, Canberra.** Australia announced that mandatory safety and security obligations will be legislated
  in its AI Standards legislation for all frontier AI companies that wish to operate there, enforced by a proposed
  new regulator able to impose significant penalties: security controls across development and deployment,
  management of serious risks, rapid incident notification, independent evaluation, published safety frameworks
  ([Prime Minister of Australia, joint media release](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/new-australian-ai-safety-and-security-protections)).
  The same day the government released a rapid review of an OpenAI agent's access in June to a Medicare statistics
  website, which the firm reported to federal authorities only in September, and accepted all 18 of its
  recommendations ([report: SBS News](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/ai-safeguards-open-ai-medicare-breach-albanese/2pbsdc5nr)).
- *Reading against step 1:* both act on developers and their models, where the step found the developers hold no
  chips; neither names the holders of compute or the terms of its renting.

- **2026-11-11 to 2026-11-13, Washington.** On 2026-11-11 two senators asked
  the Commerce and State departments and the White House to preserve all records on exports of Nvidia's advanced AI
  chips to China, after reports that a Commerce inquiry into a suspected diverter had been halted
  ([Senate Banking Committee, letter](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/20261111_warren_coons_letter_to_wh_commerce_state_re_nvidia_record_retentionpdf.pdf)).
  The same day the Commerce Department was reported to be finishing a rule that would require licences for
  Blackwell-class chips in seven more countries (reported: Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Brazil, Norway,
  South Africa), exempting US-run data centres that keep users from embargoed countries out and report their
  customers and computing scale; no text had been published by 2026-11-13 ([Asia Times, relaying The Information](https://asiatimes.com/2026/11/huangs-fall-us-curbs-put-chinas-ai-computing-in-peril)).
  On 2026-11-13 two other senators asked Commerce to condition chip licences on recipients barring Chinese firms'
  remote access ([Senator Blunt Rochester](https://www.bluntrochester.senate.gov/news/press-releases/news-senators-blunt-rochester-curtis-demand-trump-administration-strengthen-ai-chip-export-controls/)).
  The bill that would give Commerce that authority over remote access, the Remote Access Security Act, passed the
  House on 2026-01-12, 369 to 22, and has lain with the Senate's banking committee since the next day, beside the
  Senate's own bill of the same name ([Congress.gov, H.R.2683](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/2683/all-actions);
  [S.3519](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3519/text/is?format=txt)).
- **Reported 2026-11-04.** Oracle cancelled a five-year lease of about 100,000
  Nvidia chips in South-east Asian data centres to Tencent, worth about $7 billion, after US officials raised
  concerns ([Reuters, relaying the Financial Times](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/oracle-abandons-7-billion-tencent-ai-deal-under-trump-pressure-ft-reports-2026-11-04);
  [Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/4246940/oracle-cancels-7-billion-tencent-ai-chip-deal.html)).
- *Reading against step 2:* the step found no public act at the deal. The cancelled lease is a rental contract ended
  under a government's pressure, but by the provider and with no act on record, and the reported rule would reach
  rented compute abroad only through the licence on the chip. Both are a public of one country reaching compute
  bound for another, as at the chip in step 2; neither rests on the capability or control of AI.
