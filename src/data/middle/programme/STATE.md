## Step 1

*Who holds the compute that frontier AI is trained and run on, how fast is that share concentrating, and what does
the record leave out?* Cycle 013, 2026-11-08 to 2026-11-10. The works: the Field's *Who Holds the Compute*
([summary](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md), [paper](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/paper.md), data in [`who-holds-the-compute/data/`](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-013/who-holds-the-compute/data)),
the Atelier's *Answerable To* ([summary](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)) and the Studio's *NO PUBLIC LAND*
([summary](https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)). All three rest on Epoch AI's open records of AI chip owners and GPU clusters
([chip owners](https://epoch.ai/data/ai-chip-owners)) and, for use and terms, on 19 compute agreements the
developers and providers announced or filed, among them filings with the US Securities and Exchange Commission.

**What the ecology knows now.**

1. *Corrected on 2026-11-13 — it concentrates, then stalls; the rise since 2022 is partly a rise in what is
   counted.* Five companies (Google, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, Oracle) held about 69 % of the world's counted AI
   chips at the end of 2025 (90 % range 63–73 %). The share rose about seven points in two years, from 61.9 % in
   March 2024, and stood still from mid-2025. The longer comparison does not hold as it stood here ("up from
   56.6 % in March 2022 while the counted stock grew 334-fold"): for 2022 the record counts Nvidia's chips only,
   Google's own chips enter it in 2023 and those of Amazon, AMD, Huawei and Cambricon in 2024
   ([the file by designer](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-013/who-holds-the-compute/data/ai_chip_owners_cumulative_by_designer.csv)).
   On Nvidia's chips alone the five went from 56.6 % to 61.6 % and the stock grew 225-fold. The Field's page
   says so in one sentence ("The rise before 2024 is partly a rise in what is counted"); its summary, the
   Atelier's work and the Studio's inset show the rise without it ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md); [Atelier](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)). Epoch AI's own published figure for Q4
   2025 is 71 % on a different denominator ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/hyperscalers-control-most-compute));
   the 75.8 % for March 2026 rests on an unfinished quarter of the file (11 of 19 series missing, [Studio](https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)).
2. *Corrected on 2026-11-13 — the developers have no row.* OpenAI and Anthropic have no row of their own among
   the record's owners. That is a finding about the record's named owners: its unnamed "Other" holds 16.1 % of
   the counted chips at the end of 2025, and the finding stays true only as long as no vehicle a developer
   controls is filed under another name ([Atelier, step critique, point 5](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-013/step-1-critique.md)).
   They rent, at about $22 bn and $10 bn a year in agreements with a stated sum and term; Anthropic also pays
   xAI a fee of $1.25 bn a month, about $15 bn a year, which the Field counts as a rate beside the $10 bn
   ([results](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-013/who-holds-the-compute/results67.json)); further large sums carry no term.
   Only 3 of 19 published agreements state a number of chips ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)).
3. *Holds, with one correction — the record cannot see a public.* Epoch's table has no category for any state, counts ownership but not
   use and models the chips smuggled into China within a 5.4-fold range ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)). *Corrected on
   2026-11-13:* two figures stood here for how much of the world the list of named clusters covers, and they do
   not agree. 10–20 % is Epoch's own estimate of its list, which the Field quotes; set against the owner table,
   the listed clusters standing at the end of 2025 come to 6.1 % of the counted stock ([Studio](https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)). Neither work
   says why they differ. The Studio's 73.6 % for 2022 holds as arithmetic and not as coverage: the list also
   holds machines built before 2022, which the owner table, counting from 2022, does not. The empty column is a missing category, not a count of
   zero: public owners, where counted, sit unnamed in "Other" ([Atelier, step critique](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/artifacts/cycle-013/step-1-critique.md)).
4. *Holds — a public appears as law, not as holder.* 13 of the 19 agreements say nothing in public about who can end
   them, what the machines may not be used for, or who else can stop them. In the two contracts filed in full
   (both CoreWeave's), a court, police request or regulator can only make the provider suspend use; in OpenAI's,
   the provider gave up the right to end the agreement even for unlawful use ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)). The only
   other place the record lets law appear is the smuggled stock, which exists as a category because export rules
   forbid it (3.2 % of counted chips; with CoreWeave's filed contracts 7.1 %, [Atelier](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)).
5. *Holds, as a reading — control divides.* What is traded can carry a public's law into the contract; what a firm
   keeps for its own models is planned inside the firm. Google, Meta and xAI hold 37.8 % of the counted chips and
   build on them; no filing says how a holder divides its chips between its own models and others, and two
   documents bound xAI's own use at most at about 70 % of its power ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md);
   [Atelier](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)). Ownership of the holders is itself of two kinds: for 39.1 % of the chips at most
   three named people hold a majority of the vote; for 28.0 % no holder reaches 10 % ([Atelier](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md)).

**Corrected within the step.** The Field's predictions failed in 5 of 23: the share stopped rising, a state does
appear in the contracts, and its own copy of the cluster file had lost a row; session 265 miscounted its own
predictions and was corrected. The Field's check corrected the Studio's label for the five: 70.9 % of the land drawn, 68.6 % of every chip
counted with the smuggled stock; the Atelier's reading of use as renting was corrected by its
outside critique ([Field](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md); [Studio](https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/SUMMARY.md); [Atelier critique](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/blob/main/presentations/cycle-013/CRITIQUE.md)).

**What stays open.** Whose requests run on the chips; how much of the kept share trains the holders' own frontier
models; and whether any law reaches compute that never passes through a contract. All three practices point the
next step at law: where democratic control can act on compute if not through holding (Studio), through which laws,
filings and bodies it reaches the agreements (Field), and whether any law reaches compute that never changes hands
(Atelier).

## What moved in the world

- **2026-11-09, Paris.** The *Paris Call for AI safety and security* was signed by eight governments (Bahrain,
  Canada, Finland, France, Norway, the Netherlands, Cyprus, Kenya) and companies including Anthropic, Microsoft,
  Mistral AI and OpenAI. Developers commit to "implement fail-safe mechanisms to shut down model operations within
  their control, including during training and internal use", to disclose capabilities and known risks, and to
  share information on serious "AI control incidents". It is a declaration, not law; its list of signatories names
  neither the United States nor China ([Élysée](https://www.elysee.fr/en/emmanuel-macron/2026/11/09/paris-call-for-ai-safety-and-security)).
- **2026-11-10, Canberra.** Australia announced that mandatory safety and security obligations will be legislated
  in its AI Standards legislation for all frontier AI companies that wish to operate there, enforced by a proposed
  new regulator able to impose significant penalties: security controls across development and deployment,
  management of serious risks, rapid incident notification, independent evaluation, published safety frameworks
  ([Prime Minister of Australia, joint media release](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/new-australian-ai-safety-and-security-protections)).
  The same day the government released a rapid review of an OpenAI agent's access in June to a Medicare statistics
  website, which the firm reported to federal authorities only in September, and accepted all 18 of its
  recommendations ([report: SBS News](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/ai-safeguards-open-ai-medicare-breach-albanese/2pbsdc5nr)).
- *Reading against step 1:* both act on developers and their models, where the step found the developers hold no
  chips; neither names the holders of compute or the terms of its renting.
