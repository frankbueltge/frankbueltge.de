## 1. Living past 1.5°C

**Question.** Now that the world is crossing 1.5°C, what does overshoot commit us to, and can any of it be
brought back?

**At stake.** October 2026 was the warmest October on record, 1.75°C above pre-industrial and the third month
in a row above 1.5°C; the twelve months to it stood at 1.51°C, and Copernicus expects 2026 as a whole to exceed
1.5°C ([Copernicus, October 2026](https://climate.copernicus.eu/surface-air-temperature-october-2026)). The
Global Tipping Points Report 2025 puts the threshold for warm-water coral reefs at a central 1.2°C and the AMOC
at risk of collapse below 2°C ([NOC, 10 October 2025](https://www.noc.ac.uk/news/global-tipping-points-report-2025-reveals-first-earth-system-tipping-point-reached)).

**Who works on it.** The IPCC (seventh cycle), Copernicus and the climate-attribution community, overshoot and
carbon-removal research.

**Why open.** Whether overshoot can be reversed, by how much carbon removal, and which damages are permanent is
contested; the answer differs system by system.

**Step plan.** (1) What has already been committed at today's warming, field by field. (2) What overshoot
reverses and what it does not. (3) Removal at the scale that return would need, against what exists.

- Weight 5 — billions exposed; some losses permanent.
- Openness 4 — the physics of crossing is clear; what returns is not.
- Contribution 4 — "1.5°C" is an average nobody feels; the gap between the number and lived heat is the work.
- Reach 5 — Copernicus, ERA5, EM-DAT, attribution studies: open, monthly, checkable.

## 2. Tipping points

**Question.** How close are the Earth's tipping elements to irreversible change, and how could anyone know in
time?

**At stake.** The Global Tipping Points Report 2025, by more than 160 scientists at 87 institutions, finds the
first Earth-system tipping point reached, with widespread mortality of warm-water coral reefs underway
([NOC](https://www.noc.ac.uk/news/global-tipping-points-report-2025-reveals-first-earth-system-tipping-point-reached)).
From January 2023 to September 2025, bleaching-level heat stress reached about 84.4% of the world's coral reef
area, and the Planetary Health Check 2026 finds seven of nine planetary boundaries transgressed
([PIK, 2026](https://www.pik-potsdam.de/en/news/latest-news/planetary-health-check-2026-finds-mounting-pressures-across-earth2019s-life-support-systems)).
The WEF's survey of over 1,300 experts ranks critical change to Earth systems third among ten-year risks
([WEF, 14 January 2026](https://www.weforum.org/press/2026/01/global-risks-report-2026-geopolitical-and-economic-risks-rise-in-new-age-of-competition/)).

**Who works on it.** The Global Tipping Points consortium (Exeter), PIK, AMOC and ice-sheet modelling,
early-warning-signal research.

**Why open.** Thresholds carry wide ranges, early-warning signals are disputed, and cascades between elements
are barely measured.

**Step plan.** (1) What each element's threshold rests on, and how wide its range is. (2) Which signals would
warn, and which have already been seen. (3) Cascades: what one tipping does to the next.

- Weight 5 — irreversible on human time scales.
- Openness 5 — the central scientific uncertainty of the century.
- Contribution 5 — a threshold nobody sees until afterwards is a problem of perception, and of who is believed.
- Reach 4 — open model and observation data, but some elements rest on few records.

## 3. The sixth extinction

**Question.** Is a mass extinction underway, how would we know, and what would stop it?

**At stake.** The IUCN Red List now holds 175,909 species, of which 49,505 are threatened with extinction
([IUCN, 9 July 2026](https://iucn.org/press-release/202607/desert-frog-deep-sea-molluscs-remarkable-species-risk-iucn-red-list)).
The WEF survey ranks biodiversity loss second among ten-year risks
([WEF](https://www.weforum.org/press/2026/01/global-risks-report-2026-geopolitical-and-economic-risks-rise-in-new-age-of-competition/)).

**Who works on it.** IUCN, IPBES, GBIF and the Kunming-Montreal framework's monitoring.

**Why open.** Most species are unassessed; whether today's rates meet the definition of a mass extinction is
contested.

**Step plan.** (1) What is counted and what is not: assessed species against all species. (2) Rates of loss
against the background. (3) What the 2030 targets would need to change.

- Weight 4 — irreversible, but deaths are not human lives directly.
- Openness 4 — measurement gaps are the core of the dispute.
- Contribution 5 — absence is the hardest thing to show; a species unassessed is a missing record.
- Reach 4 — Red List, GBIF occurrences, Living Planet data.

## 4. Feeding everyone within the boundaries

**Question.** Can the world end hunger without breaking the planetary boundaries that food systems already
press on?

**At stake.** An estimated 645 million people, 7.8% of the world's population, faced hunger in 2025, and around
520 million could still face it in 2030 ([FAO, SOFI 2026, 21 July 2026](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/sofi-2026--hunger-not-inevitable--fao-says-as-global-hunger-report-shows-progress/en)).
Seven of nine planetary boundaries are transgressed ([PIK](https://www.pik-potsdam.de/en/news/latest-news/planetary-health-check-2026-finds-mounting-pressures-across-earth2019s-life-support-systems)).

**Who works on it.** FAO and the SOFI partners, the EAT-Lancet Commission, the Global Network Against Food
Crises.

**Why open.** Whether hunger is a production, a distribution or a political problem is the open question;
the answers imply different futures.

**Step plan.** (1) Where hunger is, and why there. (2) What food systems take from the boundaries. (3) Diets,
waste and distribution: which lever moves both.

- Weight 5 — hundreds of millions of lives.
- Openness 4 — causes and remedies contested.
- Contribution 3 — strong data, but much already visualised; the ecology's cycle 011 was on famine.
- Reach 5 — SOFI, GRFC, FAOSTAT: deep and open.

## 5. The end of antibiotics

**Question.** Will antimicrobial resistance undo modern medicine, and what would prevent it?

**At stake.** The GRAM study counts 1.14 million deaths directly due to bacterial resistance in 2021, forecasts
1.91 million a year by 2050, and more than 39 million in total from 2025 to 2050
([IHME, The Lancet](https://www.healthdata.org/news-events/newsroom/news-releases/lancet-more-39-million-deaths-antibiotic-resistant-infections)).

**Who works on it.** IHME and GRAM, WHO's priority-pathogen list, the antibiotic pipeline.

**Why open.** Forecasts diverge, and whether new drugs, access or stewardship matters most is disputed.

**Step plan.** (1) Where resistance kills, by pathogen and region. (2) The pipeline and why it is thin.
(3) Access against overuse: who lacks antibiotics while others waste them.

- Weight 4 — millions of deaths, slow.
- Openness 3 — trend clear, remedies known in outline.
- Contribution 3 — invisible harm, but mostly a matter for statistics and medicine.
- Reach 4 — GRAM, WHO GLASS; some data closed.

## 6. The return of war between states

**Question.** Is the era in which wars between states were rare ending, and what would mark the turn?

**At stake.** UCDP counted eight interstate conflicts in 2025, double the year before and the highest since its
data begin in 1946; one-sided violence killed around 76,500, the highest since 1994; the war between Russia and
Ukraine caused at least 94,700 deaths ([Uppsala University, 9 June 2026](https://www.uu.se/en/press/press-releases/2026/2026-06-09-ucdp-record-number-of-conflicts-between-states)).
World military spending rose 2.9% in real terms to $2,887 billion in 2025, the eleventh year of growth
([SIPRI, April 2026](https://www.sipri.org/publications/2026/sipri-fact-sheets/trends-world-military-expenditure-2025)).
The WEF survey ranks interstate conflict second among risks for 2026
([WEF](https://www.weforum.org/press/2026/01/global-risks-report-2026-geopolitical-and-economic-risks-rise-in-new-age-of-competition/)).

**Who works on it.** UCDP, ACLED, SIPRI, the long-peace debate in conflict research.

**Why open.** Whether the long peace was real, a statistical artefact, or is now ending is contested.

**Step plan.** (1) Counting war: what UCDP and ACLED see differently. (2) The long-peace argument against the
data. (3) Rearmament and what it has preceded before.

- Weight 5 — deaths at their highest in decades.
- Openness 4 — a live scholarly dispute.
- Contribution 3 — strong data; representation of the dead is the opening.
- Reach 4 — UCDP, ACLED, SIPRI databases.

## 7. Nuclear weapons without arms control

**Question.** With the last treaty limiting the two largest arsenals expired, is the risk of nuclear use
rising again?

**At stake.** SIPRI estimates 12,187 warheads in January 2026, about 9,745 in military stockpiles and 4,012
deployed; China has around 620; New START expired in February 2026 with no successor
([SIPRI, 8 June 2026](https://www.sipri.org/media/press-release/2026/increasing-focus-nuclear-weapons-amid-heightened-escalation-risks-new-sipri-yearbook-out-now)).

**Who works on it.** SIPRI, the Federation of American Scientists, arms-control research.

**Why open.** Risk of use cannot be measured directly; deterrence theory and its critics disagree.

**Step plan.** (1) What is known and estimated about arsenals. (2) Doctrines and escalation paths. (3) What
the end of arms control changes.

- Weight 5 — catastrophic and irreversible.
- Openness 4 — risk is a matter of judgement.
- Contribution 4 — the unthinkable scale is a classic problem of representation.
- Reach 3 — estimates from public sources; few datasets.

## 8. The autocratic majority

**Question.** Why does most of humanity now live under autocracy, and can the turn be reversed?

**At stake.** V-Dem counts 92 autocracies and 87 democracies at the end of 2025; 74% of the world's people,
6 billion, live in autocracies, against 50% in 2005; 44 countries are autocratizing, 18 democratizing
([V-Dem, March 2026](https://www.v-dem.net/documents/75/V-Dem_Institute_Democracy_Report_2026_lowres.pdf)).
Freedom House records a 20th consecutive year of decline: 54 countries down, 35 up
([Freedom House, 19 March 2026](https://freedomhouse.org/article/new-report-global-freedom-declined-20th-consecutive-year-2025)).

**Who works on it.** V-Dem, Freedom House, International IDEA, democracy research.

**Why open.** The causes, and whether expert-coded indices measure the same thing, are disputed.

**Step plan.** (1) How democracy is measured, and who codes it. (2) Paths of autocratization. (3) Where it
reversed, and why.

- Weight 4 — rights of billions, slow.
- Openness 4 — causes contested.
- Contribution 4 — expert coding is itself a representation worth examining.
- Reach 5 — V-Dem data open and deep.

## 9. Shared knowledge when machines answer

**Question.** What happens to a society's shared knowledge when trust in news collapses and machines become
the answer?

**At stake.** Trust in news averaged 37% across 48 markets, the lowest since 2015; 10% of people use AI
chatbots for news, up from 7% ([Reuters Institute, 16 June 2026](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/dnr-executive-summary)).
The WEF survey ranks misinformation and disinformation second on the two-year horizon
([WEF](https://www.weforum.org/press/2026/01/global-risks-report-2026-geopolitical-and-economic-risks-rise-in-new-age-of-competition/)).

**Who works on it.** The Reuters Institute, RSF, misinformation research, AI-and-media studies.

**Why open.** Effects of AI answers on what people know are barely measured.

**Step plan.** (1) Where trust fell and why. (2) What machine answers say about the same matters. (3) What a
public loses when the source disappears.

- Weight 3 — no direct deaths; slow erosion.
- Openness 5 — almost no settled evidence.
- Contribution 5 — the machine's role is the question itself.
- Reach 4 — DNR data, RSF index, own measurements of model answers.

## 10. Who controls advanced AI

**Question.** Can increasingly capable AI be kept under human and democratic control, and by whom?

**At stake.** Epoch AI estimates that five hyperscalers held 75.8% of the world's AI compute at the end of
March 2026, up from 69.7% a year earlier ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/hyperscalers-control-most-compute)).
The International AI Safety Report finds that capabilities have continued to improve and that by 2030 progress
could slow, continue or accelerate dramatically ([industry.gov.au](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/international-ai-safety-report-2026)).
The WEF survey sees adverse outcomes of AI climb from 30th on the two-year horizon to 5th on the ten-year
([WEF](https://www.weforum.org/press/2026/01/global-risks-report-2026-geopolitical-and-economic-risks-rise-in-new-age-of-competition/)).

**Who works on it.** The International AI Safety Report, AI security institutes, Epoch AI, AI-governance
research.

**Why open.** Whether control is a technical, an economic or a political problem is unsettled.

**Step plan.** (1) Where capability and compute sit. (2) What safety evaluation can and cannot show.
(3) Governance: who decides.

- Weight 4 — potentially vast, uncertain.
- Openness 5 — deeply contested.
- Contribution 5 — the ecology is itself run by such machines; it can study control from inside.
- Reach 4 — Epoch data, incident databases, evaluations.

## 11. Wealth that becomes power

**Question.** When a few hold most of the world's wealth, what does that do to who decides?

**At stake.** The top 10% own three-quarters of global wealth and the bottom half 2%; the top 0.001%'s share rose
from almost 4% in 1995 to over 6% ([World Inequality Report 2026](https://wir2026.wid.world/insight/executive-summary/)).

**Who works on it.** World Inequality Lab, tax-justice research, political-economy research.

**Why open.** The link from wealth to political power is argued, rarely measured.

**Step plan.** (1) The distribution and how it is estimated. (2) Channels from wealth to influence. (3) Taxes
and what they would change.

- Weight 3 — indirect harm.
- Openness 4 — the political link contested.
- Contribution 4 — scales beyond grasp.
- Reach 5 — WID data open.

## 12. Poverty under the weight of debt

**Question.** Can extreme poverty end while billions live in states that pay more to creditors than to health
or schools?

**At stake.** About 824 million people lived below $3.00 a day in 2024, and the rate is nowcast at 9.8% for
2026 ([World Bank, 22 September 2026](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/september-2026-global-poverty-update-from-the-world-bank--one-in)).
3.7 billion people live in 51 developing countries that spend more on interest than on health or education,
1.2 billion more than in 2010; the net interest bill reached $995 billion in 2025
([UNCTAD, 6 October 2026](https://unctad.org/publication/world-of-debt)).

**Who works on it.** World Bank, UNCTAD, IMF, debt-justice research.

**Why open.** Restructuring rules and their effect on poverty are disputed.

**Step plan.** (1) Where poverty concentrates. (2) What debt service displaces. (3) What the restructuring
architecture does.

- Weight 4 — lives and life chances of billions.
- Openness 3 — mechanisms known; remedies contested.
- Contribution 3 — strong statistics already exist.
- Reach 5 — PIP, UNCTAD, IMF data.
