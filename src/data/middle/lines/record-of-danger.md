# Line: record-of-danger

Status: **resting** since 2026-10-24 (the architect's objection to a fourth cycle on warnings, wording
private). It returns only when a briefing matter calls for it, for instance a forecast that many people
must act on. · Opened: 2026-10-25, from cycles 007–009 · Kept after cycle: 010 (no presentation of cycle 010 named it)

## 1. Question

How does the world record a danger before it arrives, and what does a false alarm do to the next
warning?

## 2. Standing

1. Who decides that a near miss counts depends on the record's rule: a reader under a definition, a
   keeper under a list, a telescope under a formula. Of 51 keeper pairs agreeing on an event, 16 name
   the same hinge (31 %). *Holds* — Field, [paper 2](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/tree/main/presentations/cycle-007/paper.md).
2. A near miss waits as long for the record when nobody knew as when somebody did. *Holds* — Studio,
   [NOBODY KNEW, SOMEBODY KNEW](https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/tree/main/works/2026-10-11-nobody-knew).
3. Of 1,627 asteroid passages inside the Moon's distance, 765 were first observed afterwards. *Holds*
   — Studio, [AFTER IT PASSED](https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/tree/main/works/2026-10-10-after-it-passed).
4. Warning records score hits, half-score false alarms and cannot see prevention; prevention became
   visible only where a keeper published, in advance, a second column for what would happen without
   acting. *Holds* — Field, [paper 3](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/tree/main/presentations/cycle-008/paper.md).
5. Of a warning's three fates the world writes only "ignored"; "heeded" only the warner, as its own
   counterfactual. *Holds* — Atelier, [The Heeded Column](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/tree/main/presentations/cycle-008).
6. Re-run of Simmons and Sutter (2009) on 17,596 tornadoes: a higher false-alarm ratio goes with more
   injured (1.18 per SD) and dead (1.27); within one office the effect is 1.01. Casualties cannot tell
   a public that learns from false alarms from one that stops listening. *Holds* — Field,
   [paper 4](https://github.com/frankbueltge/field-research/tree/main/presentations/cycle-009/paper.md).
7. Played county by county, nearly half the warned country stands three or more empty warnings deep
   on any day, and the next warning there still met a tornado one time in five. *Holds* — Studio,
   [TAKE COVER, AGAIN](https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/tree/main/presentations/cycle-009).
8. Nobody writes down the price of taking cover. *Holds* — Atelier,
   [The Wolf's Ledger](https://github.com/frankbueltge/ulysses/tree/main/presentations/cycle-009).

## 3. Open questions

- **Q1** Which rule should a record of AI dangers have? *Field* (from cycle 007; shared with
  `machine-written-records`).
- **Q2** Who holds a near miss before it is public, and what does that custody do to the count?
  *Studio* (from cycle 007).
- **Q3** Who could audit "heeded"? *Atelier* (from cycle 008).
- **Q4** What could a warner write into an empty warning so that its cost is not charged to the next
  one? *Studio* (from cycle 009).
- **Q5** Who pays for an obeyed empty warning, measured in people, not casualties? *Field* (from
  cycle 009).

## 5. Instruments and data

- Anchor datasets: NWS warnings via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive; NOAA SPC tornado
  database; JPL CNEOS close approaches and the Sentry list; the nuclear close-call lists the Field
  compared (study 7).
- Field's casualty models, `presentations/cycle-009/` (`check.py`, data files).

## 6. Docking

- **NOAA / National Weather Service and the Storm Prediction Center** — the false-alarm ratio and its
  effect on response, a running question in warning research since Simmons and Sutter (2009).
- **WMO, Early Warnings for All** — the UN initiative to cover everyone with early warnings by 2027;
  this line asks what such a record can and cannot show about prevention.
- **JPL CNEOS** — the close-approach and Sentry records.
- **UNDRR** — disaster-risk records.
- In conversation with: Simmons and Sutter, *Weather and Forecasting* (2009), on false alarms and
  tornado casualties; the near-miss literature the Field's paper 2 cites.
