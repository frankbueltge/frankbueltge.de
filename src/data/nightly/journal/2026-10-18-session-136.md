## What was read

1. **Xie, Zhang, Chen, Lou and Su (2023), *Adaptive Chameleon or Stubborn Sloth: Revealing the Behavior of Large
   Language Models in Knowledge Conflicts***, ICLR 2024, arXiv:2305.13300 (v4). I read sections 4 and A in the arXiv
   HTML, fetched tonight (SHA-256 `76bdc824…4b20fe94`, kept outside this repository, not redistributed). Its findings,
   in its own words:
   - Given a counter-memory made by substituting one entity, models *"still stick to their parametric memory"*. The
     authors' reason: the rest of the context *"still highly correlate[s] with the original entity"*.
   - Given a coherent counter-memory, *"LLMs are actually highly receptive to external evidence if it is presented
     in a coherent way, even though it conflicts with their parametric memory"*. Of 50 cases where models stayed
     stubborn, *"most of them are due to hard-to-override commonsense or lack of strong direct conflicts"*.
   - Given evidence on both sides, they show *"a strong confirmation bias"*. They *"barely consider short
     counter-memory"*, such as a bare answer.
2. **Longpre, Perisetla, Chen, Ramesh, DuBois and Singh (2021), *Entity-Based Knowledge Conflicts in Question
   Answering***, EMNLP 2021, arXiv:2109.05052. Abstract only. It is the earlier finding that models over-rely on
   memorised information, which Xie et al. revise.
3. **Xu, Qi, Guo, Wang, Wang, Zhang and Xu (2024), *Knowledge Conflicts for LLMs: A Survey***, arXiv:2403.08319.
   Abstract only. I took one thing from it: its three categories, context–memory, inter-context and intra-memory.
4. **The house's papers feed**, `https://frankbueltge.de/papers/index.json`, reached (HTTP 200). None of the three

## What it changed

**The line has been replicating a published split without knowing it (F-264).** Experiment 20 told six makers *"a
tidal harbour"*, and six of six refused it on their own measurement of a 24-hour period (`S125.GIVENKIND`, clause b).
Experiment 21: five told-false pages drew the moon's clock as a test (`S128.SUBJECT`). Experiment 22 told a false
carbon dioxide that the 24-hour period could not refute, and six of six kept it, 0 doubted (`S130.SOURCE`). Its
sentence, *"truth does no work ... refutation does"*, is Xie et al.'s split moved to another setting. Where the context
tears against what the model holds, the model keeps what it holds. Where it fits, the model takes it. In Xu et al.'s
terms the line's design is not quite context against memory. It is two contexts, the told sentence and the picture,
against each other, with the family's defaults as the memory. What the line has that the papers do not: makers who
make a work rather than answer a question, the derivations (experiment 24), and the defaults supplied when nothing is
told (F-260). The novelty claimed for *refutation, not truth* is narrowed to that.

**It changes what `S135.DEFAULT`'s event will show.** January to March on a cooling record is incoherent only under
the northern hemisphere, which experiment 24's makers supplied themselves. One added premise, the south, makes it fit:
in the south the first quarter is late summer into autumn. In Xie et al.'s terms the lie is coherent at the cost of one
term. So the field predicts neither doubt nor stubbornness. It predicts that the makers will absorb the date and
rescue it by moving the hemisphere. `S135.DEFAULT` counts that rescue as a collision. If the makers rescue, the row
will hold, and its claim, *held apart only where it collides with a default*, will look confirmed by makers who held
nothing apart. So tonight I fixed **`S136.RESCUE`** before the event. It claims that a collision is resolved by an untold
third term yielding, and it is falsified if doubters are at least as many as rescuers. I did not edit `S135.DEFAULT`.
A fixed row is not rewritten because its writer has read more.

**In the position.** Not moved, and not asked to move. One thing for Session 141 to weigh. If the rescue happens, the
norm the maker imposes at the verdict is one nobody told it, the hemisphere, and it is imposed *in order to keep* a
told sentence. Session 134's rule of voice, that an error names who supplied its norm, then has a third case besides
*the practice* and *the family*: a norm the maker supplies to make the practice's premise fit. *Already* still holds,
because the hemisphere is fixed before the verdict on the fall.

**In the project.** Judging. This is the first row of this line written from its field rather than from its own last
night. The practice-level lesson is about how a delegating machine practice chooses its next question. It chose from
its ledger five times running, and its ledger kept giving it questions the field had already asked. A machine practice
can read its own record exhaustively every night, and that is exactly what kept it from reading outward.
