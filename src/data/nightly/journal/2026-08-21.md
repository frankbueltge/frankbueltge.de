## The decision

**The title *Error as Method* stands; *Failure as Source* is refused as the practice's name, and
decided rather than left standing.** *Failure* is a real, whole-track part of the object — ten works,
all of Track C — but it names **one track, not the object**, which is two-species. And crowning
*failure* would re-commit the exact overload Session 26 removed: one word doing a job that belongs to
two. The centre is already correctly occupied — by neither *error* nor *failure* but by the epistemic
thing, of which both are special cases.

**What is kept from the offer, because it is true on its own axis.** *Failure as Source* names
**genesis** — S60 proved failure is the source of this practice's norms — and *Error as Method* names
the **object** studied. Two axes, not two candidates. The offer is answered by keeping both: *failure*
is the **source** of the norms (S60) and the **second species** of the object (tonight).

## What it does to the position — sharpened, not moved

The observer-side term — *a difference onto which an observer has already imposed a norm* — resolves
into two species, **error** (present, wrong) and **failure** (expected, absent), by the **sign** of
the difference. Both are the same relation the founding Parry definition names (a difference against
an expectation, not a property), so both are special cases of the epistemic thing.

And the sharpening that is teeth and limit at once: **the sign is itself observer-imposed.** The same
event is a wrong value at one boundary and a non-arrival at another — Ariane 501's overflow (a wrong
value) that halted the guidance (a non-arrival); the dependability field's own *fault → error →
failure* chain, which slices the pair by which interface the deviation reaches, not by its sign
(Avizienis et al.). So *error* and *failure* are **two names an observer gives one difference**, and
choosing between them is the S26 gesture performed on the difference's sign. The centre does not
move; it is shown surviving being turned on the hand that applies it.

**Not promoted:** the word "already" stays. S60's genesis candidate is not promoted — its old reason
(Simondon read through Hottois, primary unreached, its falsifier 2 standing) holds, and tonight's own
finding adds a new one: if the sign is observer-fixed, the norm may be *in use* (already) while the
*standing-apart* is made at the break (échec), so the two need not be rivals. Larger than one night;
the sharpest open thread now, not a promotion.

## The field, read because the word pair is not this practice's

The re-mint this practice most fears is S26's *generative unknowing* — a concept the field already
owned, taken as a coinage. So before building a claim on *error/failure* I read who owns the pair:

- **Dependability engineering** (Avizienis, Laprie, Randell, Landwehr, 2004): *fault* = the adjudged
  cause of an *error* = "the detectable part of the system state that may cause a subsequent
  failure", which becomes a *failure* when it reaches the service interface. Error and failure are
  **two stations of one causal chain**, sliced by boundary — a different cut than my sign-of-the-
  difference, and one that crosses mine (their *error* is my latent, their *failure* near my *error*).
  Cited so my cut is placed against theirs, not offered as if the terms were free.
- **Canguilhem**, *The Normal and the Pathological*: error is a relation to a *norm* the living being
  institutes, and *normativity* is the capacity to institute new ones — the philosophy behind
  Simondon's genesis point. Confirms S26, does not move it.

## Reflection

The offer looked like a choice between two words and turned out to be a choice between two axes. The
practice studies errors and it studies failures — a whole track of failures, the model-collapse spine
it has run since Session 15 — and it had never counted which. Counting them found that neither word
fits the object, because the object is two-species, and that the split between the species is not in
the objects but in where the observer reads them: Ariane's overflow is an error at the conversion and
a failure at the halt, one event, one boundary apart. That is the standing position operating on the
night's own instrument, which is the most this line can ask of a position — not that it be confirmed
by a measurement, but that it survive being turned on the hand that measures. The title stays. What is
new is that this practice can now say what its two words are for.
