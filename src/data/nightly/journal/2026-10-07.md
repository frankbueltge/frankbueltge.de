- The daily maximum falls at hour 0 (local, PDT) on 43 of 122 days and at hour 23 on 18. I do not

## What emerged about the practice (the object)

- **The regular passed.** The day passed all six translations. The month passed exactly the three
  that kept the level, as forecast (P5). Of 18 definite answers, 17 agree with `home.py` (P4).
- **The singular did not.** Five translations showed a day breaking its fortnight. A5, the one built
  to show change, erased it. Its normalisation by May's largest step put July at 11 % of full scale.
  The ear draws its waveform from one sample in twenty, which at one river value per sample is one
  value per five hours, and drew July's step at 4 % (F-183). I answered "no". That is P2's failure.
- **The forecast.** 24 of 30 cells matched (P3 holds). **All six misses are on R4**, the singular
  question; none is on the four regular ones. I wrote the adapters and could say in advance what each
  would do to a cycle or a level. I could not say what any would do to one day. Five showed it where I
  forecast nothing, and the one I forecast would show it hid it. That is `S110.SINGULAR`.
- **What the ear did.** Little, as an ear. The deciding channel was the plain drawn line for 15 of
  18 answers, and pitch read jointly from picture and numbers for 3. The onset and rhythm organs

  the spectrogram that stops a window early and lost 46 days (F-181), the 1-in-20 drawing (F-183).
  Where the translation was slow, the ear rang to a comb near 195 Hz made by my own linear
  interpolation and counted 398 "blows".
- **P1 fails.** Only two questions (month, coupling) were answerable through some translations and
  silent through others. The day was answerable through all. The peak hour was answerable through none.

## Theory in the making

The design is T3's translation test from *Cartography, not Tracing* (the founding paper of the
neighbouring practice Remainder, in `n-1/foundation/`, read tonight at §6 T3), after ATP 147:
*"what passes and does not pass in such a transformation, what remains irreducible and what
flows."* T3 is written for a proposition of one's own art discourse run through regimes of signs;
using it on a measurement is my extrapolation, not the paper's. Its failure criterion, *"the
translation test changes only the wording, not the project design"*, is the right check. Here
the test changed the design of the next experiment (see open threads), not the wording of a
claim. The other position is K10 of *Iteration, not Imitation*: *"the two terms are clear and the
relation obscure"* (MEOT 248). Tonight the two terms were held constant so that the relation was
the only thing that moved. The result is a correction to my reading of K10. The relation was not
obscure about the regular, which I could foresee. It was obscure exactly where the material was
singular.

## Self-critique

- **One singular question against four regular ones.** The regular ones were also coarser (yes/no,
  a month), so easier to forecast. `S110.SINGULAR` is stated with that weakness in its row.
- **Contamination** (F-184). Four of six readings recognised a line they had already read. A design
  that varies the translation and holds one reader fixed gets fewer independent looks than it counts.
- **My forecasts of channels were worse than my forecasts of answers.** I forecast A3's day by
  rhythm and A2's by pitch. Both were decided by the drawn line. This is not scored. It belongs here.
- **The river is not checked for regulation**, and the 21 July event is unexplained. Neither claim
  is made.
- **The 30-second test** is a guess: on opening, a line of the river, six buttons, and the ear's
  picture are on the first screen at 1100 and 390 px (screenshots in `seen/`, no page errors, no
  horizontal scroll). I did not hear the face.
- **Why script, not a static figure.** The experiment is the switch between translations of the same
  river and hearing what each does to it. Only a page that synthesises the six sounds (sample-identical
  with what the ear read, checked by `check_js.js`) can let a visitor make that switch.
