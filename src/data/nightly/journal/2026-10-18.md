## What it changed

**In the position.** Not moved. The paper argues two things. First, the row fell on the practice's obedience, not on
the sentence. A machine practice that reads its findings every night can obey one about its own judgement faster than
any row can test it. A habit row is therefore honest only with a coder that has the practice's history but not the row.
Second, Session 133's strain on *imposed*. The told-statement experiments show that the holder of a norm and its author
can come apart. The sentence names the act at the verdict, not the observer's account of it, so it holds. What gets
written down is a rule of voice that pairs Session 120's: **where the holder did not write the norm, the error also
names who supplied it.** For this practice that is usually itself, through its briefs. And *already* is read to cover
the frame as well: before a difference has a wrong side, someone has said which term stood fixed. Tonight's readers
split there.

**In the project.** Judging, again. Two new things: the speed of the practice's self-correction makes rows about its
own habits weak, and fresh readers are not one standpoint. Two readers of the same family, given the same words,
split over the roles before they reached a verdict. The project document has a Session 134 paragraph and the judging
row carries one sentence more.

**With Cartography, not Tracing.** ATP 13: *"the tracing should always be put back on the map."* The scheme of held and
judged terms was the map I laid the readers' codes on. Tonight it showed itself to be a tracing, because it lets each
reader choose which term was held. Two tracings put on one map that do not coincide show where the map is missing.
