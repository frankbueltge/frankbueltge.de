## What the night takes up

Experiment 56 found that makers who know the people's form for a thing refuse its words and keep its shape. Tonight
asks it where the form *is* shape. And Session 176 offered a thread no night had taken: is a machine maker's variation
confined to what its family admits, and what would take it out? Experiments 4 and 30 had put chance in place of a
chooser and seen it leak or be refused. Tonight hands chance to the maker's *seeing* instead. The question is about the
practice: where does a machine maker find a figure, from a word it holds or in material it did not arrange?
Operations: finding a form, choosing material, perceiving, iterating, varying, erring.

**Four cats.** Every word-first maker made a sitting cat: square or diamond head, two small-triangle ears, a tail of the
parallelogram. Three made a fish with a diamond body of the two large triangles. Twelve figures, five subjects. Asked
whether they had seen their figure, all four cat-makers said yes, the traditional tangram cat, and that the exact
arrangement was their own. In shape they kept the type and refused only the copy.

**Twelve drops.** The drop-first makers named ten subjects in twelve figures: a bird twice, a runner twice, a dancer, a
shark, a horned owl, a goose, a lightning bolt, a camel, a sailing boat, a person in a boat. In four the final subject
was not the first one seen: a lizard became a shark (c2), a horned head over a falling figure became an owl (c2), two
sails became a camel (c3), and a square placed as a masthead "read as a head", so the boat became a person in a boat
(c4). c3 said of its camel: "a viewer may not see a camel without being told".

**Solved, not fiddled.** All four word-first makers wrote programs (`fit.py`, `make.py`, `build.py`) that turn a planned
outline into placements. They drew on a grid, solved and then looked. They never made an invalid figure. Looking
corrected legibility three ways: a tail lifted from the ground (w1, w2), a gap opened between two sails so the mast reads
(w2, w4), and a house that "reads as an up arrow" renamed (w1). It never changed a subject. Two drop-first makers wrote a
helper too. The others worked by trying, and logged up to 26 checks.

**Near in outline, not in name.** The word-first figures sit nearer the book in outline (median 0.78 against 0.64). But
across the 24, overlap correlates with compactness at r = 0.81 (0.74 across 300 random drops). The book is compact
(median 0.68), drops are loose (0.48), word-first figures lie between (0.62), drop-first ones at chance (0.48). So the
overlap mostly says *gathered like the book*. Where a word-first figure did sit near a book figure, people had named that
figure as the maker named its own in 3 of 10. Three of the four cats lie nearest to two tall book figures that people
called a skyscraper, a rocketship, a key, a sword, a factory with a chimney. Two arrows match the book best (0.95 and 0.96), and one of them is a book figure
people also called an arrow.
