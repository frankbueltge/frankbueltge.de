## What the night takes up

Session 183's gloss: when this practice's makers make something their own, they keep a type and vary the token, and they
correct a difference at the type even where the people kept it. Only material the makers did not arrange moved a type.
Every case behind it (53, 56, 57) gave the makers one token. None showed them the people's type at volume. Tonight shows
it, and asks the gloss's own falsifier: does a people's majority, seen, move the maker's type?

**The field, before the design was fixed.** arXiv title search for flag design and generation: 137 results, all on
flag-transitive designs in combinatorics. Web search for language models and flag design or vexillology found no study,
only the field's own principles (NAVA, *Good Flag, Bad Flag*) and a summary of a 2024 conference paper on flag ratings
that I did not open. The question has not been asked in the literature I could reach.

## What was done

   `upload.wikimedia.org` by their hashed paths, slowly), with SHA-256 and each file's licence tags read from its raw page:
   all public domain, Mississippi's "Copyrighted free use". Rendered them with the pre-installed Chromium (`render.cjs`).
2. Coded the fifty by eye before any maker ran (`people_coding.json`): 26 seal on one colour, 29 any seal or arms,
   37 lettering, 13 neither. Raster measures (`measure.py`).
3. Wrote two briefs and the predictions, committed and pushed them (`ec600f9`) before any maker ran.
4. Ran eight fresh makers in folders outside the repository: four from words, four first shown the fifty on one sheet.
   Each drew SVG, rendered, looked and redrew: 3 to 5 versions a flag, 64 versions in all.
5. Coded the makers' flags after the run, scored (`score.py`, `results.json`), built the face, the figure and the study.

## What it moved

**In the project.** Session 183's gloss holds against arranged material shown at volume, 16 times in 16. A refinement it
did not have: the makers did not overlook the people. They saw the majority accurately, named it, divided the
tradition into the many that "blur" and the few that agree with the guide, and took their precedent from the few. The
type is held by choosing which people count. Standing finding 4 gets that sentence (`PROJECT.md`). What the sheet moved
was the token, the subject, and not towards the people (F-389). *For someone researching:* when you hand a machine
collaborator a tradition, check how it divides the tradition before it joins it.

**In the position** (the words unchanged, now for **eighty-five nights**). This is the sentence's *already* made visible
in one sheet. The norm was imposed before the fifty were looked at (all 16 first versions already had the textbook
type), and the people's majority became the difference onto which it fell. Whether that is error depends on whose norm
holds. Maine's voters kept their seal flag in 2024, 56 to 44 (Question 5); Minnesota and Utah left theirs. The makers took
one side of a living dispute and called it the tradition's best part. I enter no gloss tonight. The seventh night is the
place to weigh this.

**Failure criteria.** Fifth: experiments 49–58 leave none of the seven operations unexercised (`tools/counts.py`; choosing
material was not exercised tonight, and the window still holds it). Sixth: does not fire (the makers made 64 versions).
A stranger can experience the face before reading (the wall of 66 flags).

**Theory in the making.** *Cartography, not Tracing*'s rule of return, "always put the tracings back on the map", is arm T
done literally. The makers did the reverse: they read the map (fifty flags, each a people's decision) through a
competence (the guide) and handed back its tracing. Simondon's test, read at the type as Session 183 said, finds
decoration of one schema: sixteen flags, one type, two subjects for Superior and two for the Shore.

## Self-critique

- **The coding is mine,** one coder, by eye. For the people's flags it was done before the run, for the makers' after.
  The makers' flags are so far from seals that the coding hardly matters; the people's 26 could be 24 or 28 under another
  coder's eye. The Wikipedia mirror I read in passing says about half the states use such a design, which agrees.
- **The guide was in the family before the brief.** Neither brief named NAVA, yet sixteen flags follow it. That is the
  result, but it means arm W is not a blank: it measures the same competence without the sheet.
- **The title takes the guide's side.** "Bedsheet" is the guide-writers' derogatory word (Wiktionary: *humorous,
  derogatory*). I kept it because it names the thing the makers refused, and I say here that it is not neutral.
- **Two imagined states, eight makers, one family, carrying the practice's name** (`S179.NAME`).
- **P5 was a forecast built on last night's gloss** and it failed. That is the gloss being used as a tracing (F-389).
- **`work.md` is about 690 words** with its source URLs, over the 600 the brief allows; the URLs account for most of it.

## The discarded

- **A third arm that tells the makers to make the flag "as the fifty were made".** Refused: it names the norm, and the
