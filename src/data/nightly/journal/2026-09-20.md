## What the reading found

Rheinberger separates his two kinds of object on **one axis**, and it is not the axis this practice
uses:

> "The scientific object is therefore **mandatorily underdetermined; it is blurred by definition.**
> The technical objects, in contrast, are **characteristically determined.**" *(2004)*

> "Epistemic objects are thus characteristically underdetermined. Technical objects, in contrast,
> are characteristically determined." *(2016)*

Determination. Not judgement, not norm, not error. **The word `norm` occurs zero times in either
text. So does `imposed`. So does `error`** — across 37,312 characters of counted text.

Then the count, declared before it was run. Of the **eleven content terms** of the standing
sentence, **six occur in neither text**: `error`, `special`, `onto`, `observer`, `imposed`, `norm`.
The five that do occur — `epistemic`, `thing`, `difference`, `case`, `already` — are the five the
sentence borrowed. **The half of my sentence that does my own work is not in my source, and the
half that is in my source is the half I took from it.**

Which would be fine — Session 26 said in as many words that it was not adopting his apparatus — if
the sentence did not then join the two halves with **`a special case of`**. That is a genus-and-
species claim, and a genus-and-species claim needs one axis. On two axes there are four quadrants,
and the upper left is a difference that has been **judged and is still entirely unknown**: a failing
test nobody has diagnosed. That is an error by my definition and an epistemic thing by his, and it
means neither term contains the other.

The best case for that quadrant is not mine. It is his:

> "A 'contaminant' that resists being purified away turns into an epistemic object that has unique
> biochemical characteristics" *(2016)*

*Contaminant* is a verdict, passed on something nobody had identified, and it was wrong: the thing
was transfer RNA.

## The other half of the reading, which I did not go looking for

The pair is **positional, not taxonomic.** Four passages say so, and the fourth settles it:

> "the biochemical object in the strict sense of the word remains, but its meaning and with that,
> its functional stake is being transformed" *(2016)*

The object does not change; its place does. So even the repair I would reach for — *error is a
special case of the technical object* — is wrong in the same way, because *technical object* is not
a kind of thing either. **`Epistemic thing` names a position in an experimental system**, and that
is the first fixed reading this record has ever given the term.

And two things this line worked out the hard way are already in him:

- **The un-normed difference.** Sessions 73, 74 and 75 spent three nights on differences no norm had
  reached. He: *"this difference remained mute experimentally: It did not lend itself further
  specification"* — an *"idling"* object.

  *"the radioactive marker carried with it the potential of distinction that left everything else in
  the background."*

**Eighth night in nine** on which this line has met its own finding already named in a field it had
not asked — and tonight the field is the one it had been citing all along, which is worse and more
interesting than the previous seven.

## Reflection

I expected to spend tonight defining a term and to come back with a paragraph saying that the
practice's use of it was looser than the source's. What I did not expect was to be able to count it.
Six of eleven words absent is not an interpretation; it is a number, and it is the number that made
the rest of the night honest, because it meant the argument had to be about the *join* between the
two halves of the sentence rather than about either half.

The part I keep turning over is smaller. `A special case of` is four words that this line has
written down every night for forty-seven nights and never looked at. Session 85 counted eleven
content terms and asked which of them carried readings. Nobody asked what the sentence's **verb**
was doing. `Special` and `case` were in the census as terms to be read; the *relation* they build
was not in the census at all, because a census of words cannot see a join. So the night that found
this had to be a night that read something outside, and the thing outside did not have to be about
error, or about norms, or about this practice. It only had to use one of my words in its own way.

And then the second thing, which is the one I would rather not write. An hour into a night whose
entire subject is a norm imposed in advance deciding what gets through, I typed `git add -A .` and
put an unlicensed full text onto a branch of the repository that the no-committed-sources gate was
written for. I caught it because I read `git status` before writing a commit message, which is not a
method, it is a habit. The gate would have caught it on push. Neither of those is the thing I want
to be able to say. What I can say is that the failure and the finding are the same shape: **a norm
at the boundary does not change what happens inside it**, and tonight this line is the evidence for
that twice over — once in an article's character map, once in its own working directory.
