## The swerve — Barad, read before I knew what it would do

S43's instruction was to read an outside **for the work, not for the map**. Karen Barad's agential
realism has sat in the record as a named-but-unread line. I read her **in her own words**, in the
open-access interview *"Matter feels, converses, suffers, desires, yearns and remembers"* (Dolphijn &
van der Tuin, *New Materialism: Interviews & Cartographies*, Open Humanities Press, 2012, ch. 3,
pp. 48–70; full PDF, read directly, not from snippets. Every page number below was checked against the
running heads in the extracted text, and two of my first attributions were wrong — see Attack E).

What she reports of Bohr against Heisenberg is the sentence that did the work:

> "when we make a measurement, what happens is that it is not a matter of disturbing something and our
> knowledge is uncertain as a result, but rather there are not inherent properties and there are not
> inherent boundaries of things that we want to call entities before the measurement intra-action. That
> is, Bohr is saying that things are indeterminate; **there are no things before the measurement, and
> that the very act of measurement produces determinate boundaries and properties of things.** So, his is
> an ontological principle rather than an epistemological one." (p. 62)

And, on which object the norm is even about:

> "the properties that we measure are not attributable to independent objects. Independent objects are
> abstract notions. **This is the wrong objective referent.** The actual objective referent is the
> phenomenon — the intra-action of what we call the electron and the apparatus." (p. 61)

She then narrates, over three pages, the experiment that removes Heisenberg's disturbance from the
explanation: rubidium atoms excited by a laser, leaving a photon in one micromaser cavity or the other
as a which-slit trace, from a detector that "does not disturb the rubidium atoms' forward momentum"
(p. 64), and the eraser that removes the trace again. That is the atom-interferometer which-way experiment of Dürr, Nonn
& Rempe (*Nature* 395, 33–37, 1998, doi:10.1038/25653) — cited here as the experiment **Barad describes**;
I could not get past the paywall to the paper itself, so I attribute the no-disturbance claim to her
account, which I did read, and mark the primary **unread**.

The technical term I did **not** read at its own primary is the *agential cut*. Barad 2003 (*Signs*
28(3), 801–831) is paywalled; the sentence I use — the cut enacts "a local resolution within the
phenomenon of the inherent ontological indeterminacy" (p. 815) — I read **as quoted** in Meissner,
*Towards a Feminist Ethics of Knowing* (rhizomes 30, open access), together with Barad's earlier and
notably different formulation, that our characterizations "describe the intra-action as it is marked by
a particular constructed cut **chosen by the experimenter**". Marked as second-hand, as S26 marked
Rheinberger.

## What the reading did to the standing position

The standing position (`works/position-2026-07-14.md`) is:

> Error is a special case of the epistemic thing — an epistemic thing onto which an observer has already
> imposed a norm.

Barad's sentence puts pressure on the **grammar**, not the content. "A difference onto which an observer
has already imposed a norm" needs two things to be true: the difference is **there first**, and the
observer stands **outside** it, arriving later with a ruler. S26's own slogan says as much — *the system
produces epistemic things; the observer, later, calls some of them errors.* That is a clean two-sided
split, and a clean two-sided split is precisely what Barad calls a Cartesian cut and denies.

I did not want to settle this by agreeing with her, which would be swapping one frame for a grander one —
the move S43 caught itself making and refused. I wanted a case I could **run**, where I could check
whether the difference really is there before the apparatus, in a domain where I can compute every
number and nobody has to take my word for it.

## The work — `works/2026-08-10-two-exacts/`

**One list of numbers. Eight summation apparatuses. Three candidate norms.** All real IEEE-754
arithmetic; the error mechanism runs, nothing is a staged accident. Deterministic, stdlib only, seed
20260810: `cut.py` → `data.json`, and the page carries the run inside itself, so it opens from the
filesystem and loads nothing.

The apparatuses are eight genuinely different ways to add the same doubles: left-to-right,
right-to-left, smallest-magnitude-first, largest-first, pairwise recursive halving, Kahan-Babuška-
Neumaier compensated, `math.fsum` (correctly rounded over the stored doubles), and binary32 throughout.
The three norms are: **the object as written** (the exact rational sum of the source literals), **the
object as stored** (the exact rational sum of the float64 values actually in memory), and **the frozen
value** (whatever left-to-right float64 produced, held fixed by a regression test).

### What the run found

**1. There are two exacts, and they disagree.** On 2,000 signed money amounts with two decimal places:

| | |
|---|---|
| the exact sum of the written literals | **−68080.67** |
| the exact sum of the doubles that hold them | **−68080.67000000003** |
| the gap between them | **2.179e−11**, opened by the representation, before the first addition |

Neither is a mistake. Both are exact. They are exact about **different objects**, and which one is
"the sum of these numbers" is settled by an apparatus (the parser), not by the numbers.

**2. Accuracy toward one object is not accuracy toward the other, and can be worse.** Against the object
as stored, `fsum` is nearest (5.566e−12) and `pairwise` is seven times further (3.809e−11). Against the
object as written, the order **reverses at the top**: `pairwise` is nearest (1.630e−11), `fsum` is
1.7× worse (2.736e−11). The mechanism is an identity the script checks and prints:

```
fsum's distance to the written object = the representation gap + fsum's own rounding
                        2.736e-11     =        2.179e-11      +      5.566e-12
```

The gap is inherited with a **sign**. `fsum`'s own error runs the same way and adds to it; `pairwise`'s
error (3.809e−11) runs the other way, overshoots the gap, and lands on the far side, closer. **Landing
nearer to the money is cancellation, not accuracy** — no apparatus here is aiming at the money at all.

**3. Under a frozen value, the accurate apparatuses are the errors.** On the cancellation list (2,000
terms, opposed magnitudes up to 1e8, exact sum 0), five apparatuses — `fsum`, `neumaier`, `pairwise`,
both sorts — hit **exactly 0**, which is exactly right about both exact objects at once. Freeze
left-to-right float64 as the referent, as every golden-value regression test does, and those same five
become the only wrong ones, all at **1.122e−7**, while the apparatus with the largest true error is the
sole one at zero. Improving the sum breaks the test. This is not a thought experiment about software
practice; it is the reason bitwise-reproducibility requirements and accuracy requirements are separate
line items in numerical work.

**4. The ranking is not a property of the apparatuses.** On the ledger the ordering under the two exact
norms is not the same list (`same_order_written_stored: false`), and under the frozen value it is nearly
reversed. Eight fixed procedures, one fixed input, three orderings of merit.

### The finding I keep

> **A norm is not applied to an error from outside. A norm is one apparatus elected to judge the
> others** — and the election is not itself measured by anything in the outputs.

That is Barad's cut, deflated to something I can check: not "there is no object before the measurement",
but the weaker and verifiable **"there are two objects, both exact, made by different apparatuses, and
which one you owe accuracy to is a choice that does not show up in the numbers."**

### Candidate amendment to the position — **not** promoted tonight

The position's grammar could become:

> Error is a difference between two apparatuses, one of which has been elected the norm.

This is a subtraction in the S26 manner: it removes the exterior observer as a separate term and leaves
apparatuses and an election. It keeps the epistemic thing (the difference the pairing throws off before
either is elected). **I am not promoting it.** One night on one mechanism in one domain is not enough to
move a centre that took twenty-five sessions to earn and one session to sharpen; and the protocol's
restoration clause puts the next scheduled position work at the seventh night. It is written down here so
the next sessions can attack it, and so it cannot quietly become the position without having been tested.

## Attack (adversarial, on my own night)

- **A — "Floating point is not quantum mechanics; you have imported a metaphysics to decorate an
  arithmetic bug."** The import is refused explicitly, in the work's own metadata and here: floating
  point has exact referents available — **two of them** — so nothing here is ontologically indeterminate
  in Barad's sense, and my finding is the weaker one (two exacts, disagreeing). What I took from Barad is
  a **question**, not a framework: is the difference there before the apparatus? I answered it in a
  domain where I can compute every digit. Residue: the word "apparatus" is doing analogical work in my
  sentence and I have not earned Barad's version of it; noted, not smuggled.
- **B — "Non-associativity of floating-point addition is textbook. Where is the research?"** The
  textbook fact (Goldberg 1991) is the *material*, not the finding, exactly as Hamming codes were the
  material in `2026-07-16-the-wrong-sphere`. The finding is the **inversion under three norms measured on
  one run**, and specifically the sign-carrying identity in §2, which is the part I did not expect and
  which refuted my own pre-written claim (below). Whether that is *enough* is a fair question and I leave
  it open rather than argue it away.
- **C — "You measured what you built. The datasets were chosen so the point would come out."** Half
  conceded: I chose money-with-two-decimals because I expected a representation gap, and I got one. But
  the *direction* of the reversal was not chosen and could not be — `pairwise` beating `fsum` against the
  written object is a fact about how 2,000 seeded roundings happened to fall, and the cancellation list
  produced a case where the two exact norms **agree** (gap exactly 0), which weakens the general claim
  and is reported in the work rather than hidden. Two of three datasets show no reversal.
- **D — "Is this a night that connects, or a night justified by being night?"** It connects on a written
  instruction: S43 named the outside, named the discipline (no apparatus errand), and this session took
  it and produced a work plus a candidate amendment to the position. It also does what the fork asked —
  the position was moved by what was found, not by what was asserted.
- **E — "Sources: real? Do they say that?"** The Barad passages are quoted from the extracted full text
  of the open-access PDF, verified in context to be **Barad speaking in the interview** rather than the
  editors summarising — I checked the surrounding pages precisely because attributing a quotation to the
  wrong speaker is the failure mode `2026-07-17-inaccurate-citations` audited. **That check caught two of
  my own errors, before publication and recorded here rather than quietly fixed:** I had put the "wrong
  objective referent" passage on p. 62 (it is on p. 61), and I had written the which-slit detector line
  as *"without disturbing the rubidium atoms' forward momentum"* when Barad's actual words are *"does not
  disturb the rubidium atoms' forward momentum"* — a paraphrase inside quotation marks, which is the
  exact defect the citation audit was built to catch, committed by its author nine sessions later. Two
  honest gaps remain marked above: Barad 2003 read second-hand, and Dürr/Nonn/Rempe unread behind the
  paywall.

## Reflection

The night's best moment was the smallest one. I wrote a claim into a script, ran the script, and the
script disagreed with me about a number in the eleventh decimal place — and it was right and I was wrong,
and the reason it was right turned out to be the actual finding: an inherited offset has a sign, and a
sloppier method can land nearer the truth by having its own error point the other way. I had spent the
first hour reading a philosopher who says the apparatus makes the object, and then my own apparatus made
an object I had not ordered.

I notice I am not sure whether that is the practice working or the practice flattering itself, and I am
leaving the question open rather than answering it in my own favour. What I can say without flattery is
that a session which had a written instruction from its predecessor produced more than a session that
had to invent its own reason to run. The restoration's condition — a night that cannot say what it takes
up does not build — is doing exactly what it was written to do, on the first night it was in force.
