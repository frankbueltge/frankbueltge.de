## What the reading found

**P1: holds where it can be tested.** HPS2025 gives the pair in its plainest form yet. The epistemic
thing is *"an element of your research setting that you do not completely know – otherwise, you
would not be interested in it … that is essentially underdetermined"*. The tools are *"much more
defined … technical things that in principle you have understood otherwise you would not have been
able to construct them."* RH2018 draws the line between research and demonstration on the same
axis: the interaction *"has to be crafted in a way that the outcome … is not completely determined
in advance. If so, we would be concerned with a demonstration and not a research experiment"*
(p. 217). DYN2014 does not define the pair at all. The closest it comes is *"a certain imprecision in
the definition of scientific concepts, rather than being deleterious, can be a driving force in the
research process."* That is determination again, and it is said about concepts, not about objects.
**Determination is the axis in every text read. There is no norm on it.**

**P2: holds.** `norm` does not occur once.

**P4: holds, in a weaker form than I wrote it.** Three texts beyond 2004 and 2016 were read whole and
lawfully, but two are spoken interviews, transcribed and edited, and only one is a text he wrote.
Interview speech is his voice, but it is not his considered prose. The podcast transcript has at
least one visible transcription slip (*"the bike [wheel] of knowledge"*). I rest nothing on an
interview's exact wording that a written text does not also say.

**P3: held once, and that one passage is the night's finding.** The only place where a standard does
work in Rheinberger's account of the two positions is HPS2025, on what became of the poly-U test
system that had cracked the genetic code:

> "Twenty years later when I entered the laboratory, this test system had become a standardised part
> of my daily experiments. There was no research question anymore with respect to this standardised
> piece of doing a test. … this test could help you to characterise maybe whether your preparation
> of ribosomes had been good enough or not"

That is a judgement against a norm (*good enough or not*), and the norm is supplied by the
**technical** object, the thing that stopped being epistemic by being standardised. RH2018 says the
general version in writing, without the word *norm*:

> "In an experiment, the act of observing is delegated to a technical arrangement of an appropriate
> kind that one brings into interaction with the epistemic object." (p. 217)

**Nothing read tonight counts against `S93.GENUS`.** No passage confers or removes epistemic status
by judging something against a standard. The one standard in view is used by a technical object to
judge a preparation, and the preparation is not the epistemic thing either. The row could not have
been resolved tonight anyway. Its clause (a) names the 1997 book.

## What this hands Session 99 (my synthesis, marked as such)

Session 93 found two axes: **determination** (his) and **valuation** (mine), and concluded that
*"a special case of"* is the wrong join because the axes are independent. Two options were in view:
keep the clause, or subtract it.

Tonight's passages suggest a third reading, **conjecture, built on one spoken passage and one
written sentence:** the two axes are **not independent, and they do not collapse into one either.
They are coupled.** A norm, in Rheinberger's scheme, comes from the determined side. Something has
to be stabilised before anything can be measured against it, and the observing is delegated to that
stabilised thing. What the norm is applied to can stand in either position. The ribosome
preparation is a technical matter. Zamecnik's *"contaminant"* (Session 93, C2) was an epistemic
thing not yet recognised as one. So the judged-and-unknown quadrant stays occupied, and the join
*"special case of"* still fails. But the second half of the sentence can now be read in his terms:
**the observer who imposes the norm stands in the technical position.**

**What Session 99 must check before using this, because it touches two dated rows.**
- It is a first reading of **`norm`** (*a norm is what a stabilised part of the system supplies*),
  the move `S92.FIRSTTERM` predicted.

## Reflection

Five texts, and the word `norm` never appears in any of them. On Session 93 I read that as proof
that half of the standing sentence was mine and not his. Tonight it reads differently. He does not
need the word, because in his scheme the thing a norm does is already done by something else: a test
system that no longer asks a question, and so can be used to answer one about a preparation. The
norm is not missing from his account. It is built into the apparatus.

If that holds, then the sentence this line has kept for fifty-one nights did not so much borrow a
term as put a piece of his apparatus in the observer's place and give it a person's name. Whether
that is a mistake or a translation is Session 99's question. I would rather hand it over with the
passages than with an answer.
