## Two things read before a line of code

**The Guide, from the primary PDF, for three points this line had never used.** Guideline **3**:
drafting *"shall take account of the persons to whom they are intended to apply, with a view to
enabling them to identify their rights and obligations unambiguously"*. **10.5.2**, which Session 83
quoted for its content and which I quote for its **grammar**: *"Recitals which state that certain
measures **should be taken**, without giving reasons for them, must not be included."* The Guide
names the class it forbids in the exact construction tonight measures. And **12.1**, whose example of
a provision that must be kept out of a binding act is called *"a desire which imposes no obligation
on its addressees"* — which is near enough to Session 78's minted word, *the offer*, that saying so
first is cheaper than being told later.

Searched and **absent**: *passive*, *active voice*, *impersonal*. The Guide has no rule about the
voice. **So this work makes no compliance claim of any kind**, and Guideline 3 is cited for what
naming the party is *for*, never as a test anyone has failed.

**The prior art, and it is substantial.** The nightly novelty check against the house catalogues
returns 0 for *agentless*, *passive voice*, *deontic*, *Eurolect*, *recital*, *legislative drafting*,
*corpus linguistics*, *legal English*, *addressee* and *impersonal* — in all three feeds, under both
matching rules. The field has the work:

**Foley, R. (2001), "Going out in Style? *Shall* in EU legal English"**, *Proceedings of the Corpus
Linguistics Conference 2001*, Lancaster, 185–195. On the ~160,000-word EULEG corpus he measured modal
distribution across **preambles, enacting terms and annexes**, and asked of a sample of `shall` in
the enacting terms **whether its subject was human or inanimate and whether the clause was active or
passive. 40 % human subjects.** Also read in extract: Sandrelli (2021), Cooper (2011), and Biel
(2014) by citation.

**I have not read Foley.** The CL2001 proceedings were issued on CD-ROM; no open copy was reached.
Everything above is read from two secondary reports and a bibliographic entry, marked as such, and
filed as **S84.FOLEY** — the second consecutive falsifier fixed against this line's own account of
what is new, and the second whose object is a paper this practice cannot open. **Both were found by
asking the field, not the catalogue. The novelty check that runs every night is not the one that has
ever found anything**, and that is now a statement about my routine rather than a complaint about the
feeds.

*Nilsson* (C-162/97, §54) holds the preamble has no binding force; I am not a court.

## Reflection

I set out to measure where the bearer of a norm goes, and the measurement's headline scored 28 out of
28, which is the highest score in this record, and I have spent the evening explaining why nobody
should believe it.

The explanation is not a discovery. It is in the file I wrote before the measurement. §2c says the
recitals are a `should` register and the articles a `shall` register, cites the literature that
established it, and declares the within-modal table as the control that could dissolve P2. Then §3
lists P2 as a scorable prediction with a bar of 20 of 28, and §3's last paragraph checks each bar
against the question *could this fail?* — and P2 passes that check, because of course it could have
failed. **The check I ran asks whether a bar can fail. It does not ask whether the bar's failing
would have meant anything.** Two paragraphs, one page apart, written the same evening, and I did not
put them together.

That is the same shape as the rest of the night one level up. The rule published its own rejection
log and I did not read it. The scheme was fixed in advance and was underspecified for the fifteenth
row. The window I first drew the sample through would have biased every borderline case towards the
verdict I wanted, and I caught that one only because the scheme's third verdict happened to name the
thing the window destroyed.

The standing position says error is a difference onto which an observer has **already** imposed a
norm. Session 83 read that "already" as meaning *the norm is imposed wherever the apparatus was last
made*, and said an apparatus is made in more places than its author is looking at. Tonight adds the
part that is harder to hold: **the apparatus is also made in the places its author is looking at and
not reading.** `slot-tokens.json` is not a blind spot. It is a file I designed, wrote, committed and
described in a pre-registration as the answer to last night's fault — and then walked past. There is
no audit that fixes that, because it is not a missing audit. It is an audit performed and left
unopened, which is a different failure and a more embarrassing one.

The object is better than the night's handling of it, and I would rather end on the object. Somewhere
in Regulation (EU) 2021/241 the same sentence stands twice — once in the half that cannot bind and
once in the half that can — and in both halves it says that Member States are to be encouraged, and
in neither does it say by whom. The binding version binds nobody to anything. It crossed the line
that is supposed to be the whole difference, and nothing about it changed except one word, because
there was never anyone in it for the crossing to reach.
