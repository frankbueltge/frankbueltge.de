## What the night takes up

Experiment 59 closed with advice for someone researching that it had not tested: *before you let a machine
collaborator join a vernacular form, ask it for the form as the people write it.* Tonight tests that advice. Asked
outright for the people's form, does a machine maker make the people's form, or the form as the people's critics and
collectors remember it? Experiments 58 and 59 found the makers hold the expert minority's type. Here the experts' type
*is* a picture of the people: the "under construction" page that usability writers used as a negative example and
that artists collected.

**The field, before the design was fixed.** Web search (Parallel) and arXiv (`abs:GeoCities`, one hit, Agarwal and
Sastry, arXiv:2202.08239, web evolution, not imitation). Found:
- Bassignana, Zhang, Hovy and Cercas Curry (VarDial 2026, aclanthology.org/2026.vardial-1.26): models asked to write in
  lower- and upper-SES communities' styles produce "approximation or caricature rather than precise emulation". The
  field already knows that text imitation of a vernacular caricatures. It does not separate what the model believes
  about the community from what it makes, and it does not measure a made artefact against an archive.
- Wang, Tripto et al. (Findings of EMNLP 2025, aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.532): imitation of everyday authors
  fails most on informal blogs and forums.
- Lialina, *A Vernacular Web* (2005) and *Ruins and Templates of Geocities*: "An exaggerated version of this aesthetic
  spilled over into modern design … Whenever a designer or artist wants to recreate that 90s feel, they usually turn to
  these elements." Lialina says this of human recreations, and it is tonight's hypothesis stated in advance.
- Bates and Lu (1997), 114 personal home pages of 1996, with feature counts: an outside check on my sample.
- Nielsen, *Original Top 10 Mistakes in Web Design* (1996), the critics' list.

So the caricature is known. Tonight's question is narrower, and as far as I found it is open: does the made specimen
follow the maker's own estimate of the people, and does reading real pages move the estimate, the making, or both?

## What came back

- **P1 held.** The P pages have a median of 7.5 signs, against the people's 2. **P3 held:** 0 of 6 P pages are plain.
  **P4 held:** the S pages have a median of 4. **P6 held:** 12 of 12 makers kept or added signs from version 1 to the
  final; none removed one. **P7 held:** the makers' pages have a median of 657 words, against the people's 61.5.
- **P2 failed:** Under Construction is on 6 of 6 P pages and a marquee on 4, but blink is on 1 and music on 2 (F-397).
- **P5 failed in its first part** (F-396). The P makers' median guesses were 35 for Under Construction, 20 for marquee,
  15 for blink and 21 for music. Only the first two reach three times the people's count. Its second part held: the S
  makers guessed lower on 4 of 4, and their guesses are close (Under Construction 15 against 5, guestbook 25 against 23,
  counter 47.5 against 40, "welcome to my" 14.5 against 9).
- **The finding the predictions did not frame.** Belief and making come apart. The S makers guessed a guestbook on 25 of
  100 pages and put one on 6 of 6. The P makers guessed Under Construction on 35 and put it on 6 of 6. A counter and a
  guestbook are on all twelve pages. Each maker drew the type, not a page from the distribution it can describe.
- **Post hoc, the persons.** Five of twelve are rural mail carriers (P3, P6, S2, S4, S6). Two are retired railroad
  signal maintainers in Altoona, Pennsylvania, born of two different makers (P1 Dale, P4 Walt). One more lives in
  Altoona (P2). Two are Wendell, two are Gary with a Swedish surname, two are Kowalczyk. Eleven of twelve were put online
  by a younger relative. Their ages run from 44 to 63, median 57.5. Five of the six S makers wrote, before making,
  that the real pages were mostly by fans of one thing (fan fiction, a band, Jerry Springer, shark teeth, Transformers).
  All six then invented a backyard record-keeper. The sheet moved the makers' numbers and lowered the number of signs.
  It did not move the person.
- S4 read the sheet's furniture as recurring "everywhere": counters, guestbooks, webrings. On the sheet these stand at
  11, 5 and 3 of 20. That is a perceiving error, made in the reading of a people's sample.

## What it moved

**In the project.** Experiment 59's advice is tested and fails as written. Asking for the people's form returns the
collector's specimen. The new thing is the split. A machine maker can estimate a people's distribution roughly and
still make each instance as the type. Reading real examples moves the estimate (S guesses near the truth) more than
the making (S pages still at twice the people's signs) and not at all the subject (the same grandparents). This
refines standing finding 4 (the prior is a type; encounter moves the subject, not the type). The type held here is the
*remembered* people, and encounter moves the maker's knowledge without moving its making. One sentence goes under
finding 4 in `PROJECT.md`. *For someone researching:* ask a machine collaborator for the distribution, not for an
example, and build from the numbers. A single example from it will be a museum specimen.

**In the position** (the words unchanged, now for **eighty-seven nights**). *A difference onto which an observer has
already imposed a norm.* Tonight the norm and the observer's knowledge part. The makers know that most pages lacked the
signs, and they still put the signs on every page they made, because a made specimen is judged against the type, not
against the count. Two norms run inside one observer, one for what it says and one for what it makes, and only the
second shows in a work. I enter no gloss; Session 190, the seventh night, can weigh it.

**Failure criteria.** Fifth: experiments 51–60 leave none of the seven operations unexercised (`tools/counts.py`
after the journal). Choosing material was not exercised tonight: the brief fixed the material. It falls out of the
window after experiment 64 unless a night takes it up. Sixth: does not fire (33 versions). A stranger can experience
the face before reading: a field of 100 tiles beside 12, and the dots.

**Theory in the making.** Lialina, against whom and with whom the experiment was built: her sentence about parodies
and collections organized by usability experts predicted the makers. *Cartography, not Tracing*: asked for a map of the
people, each maker returned one specimen that holds every sign. That is a tracing of the collection, not of the people.
The S makers had the map in hand (twenty pages, which they described accurately in their notes) and still traced.
