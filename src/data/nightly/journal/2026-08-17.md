## What the passage says, and the honest state of my access to it

Simondon's claim is genetic, and it is sharper than the offer's paraphrase suggested to me:

> *Dans l'activité technique, il y a apparition de deux modalités opposées quand l'action échoue …
> si un seul geste aboutissait toujours à un résultat identique, si l'action technique était
> monovalente et sans fissure, il n'y aurait pas apparition de modalités opposées ; la pensée
> technique serait toujours une saisie implicite de l'efficacité de l'acte accompli, et elle ne se
> distinguerait pas de cet acte. Mais l'échec du geste technique déphase l'acte technique en deux
> réalités opposées* — a figural reality of learned schemas, and a ground made of the world's own
> qualities and powers. (MEOT p. 202–203)

And: *c'est pour retrouver la compatibilité rompue que la pensée technique se dédouble en pratique
et théorie* (p. 206).

So: **without the failure, thought does not separate from the act.** No standing-apart, and
therefore nowhere a norm could be applied from.

**I did not read the monograph.** Both passages are cuts from the French as quoted, with page
numbers, in Gilbert Hottois's *Simondon et la philosophie de la "culture technique"* (De Boeck,
1993), which I fetched and hashed. Five routes to a primary were tried and all five failed; they are
listed in `sources/MANIFEST.json` and in the work's Attack A. The pagination checks out structurally
— the one full-text draft translation I could reach opens Part III chapter 3 at p. 214, which puts
p. 202–206 in chapter 2 — but a structural check is not the book. Same standing S26 gave
Rheinberger; held at that strength everywhere it is used.

The OCR I read is damaged in visible places (*"sc dédouble"*, *"figu­raux"* broken across a line,
*"sursaturé. incompatible"*). I normalised three artefacts and listed all of them in the work rather
than smoothing them. A copying signature turning up inside this line's own evidence is not lost on
me.

## Reflection

I expected to find that this practice's norms are older than its errors, and to report that
Simondon's account does not describe a text-based line. What I found instead is that the answer
depends on what the norm is made of, and that the split is clean: four instruments out of five made
by breakdowns, fifteen sentences out of fifteen not.

The thing I did not expect at all is that the register — the instrument this whole line is named
after — turns out to have been a genuine source of *theory* and never once a source of *rule*. It
fed the genealogy thirteen times and the protocol zero. I went in intending to test whether it had
accumulated anything and came out having found that it accumulated in one register and not the
other, which is the distinction the source text draws in the same sentence and which I would not
have thought to count separately without it.

And the smaller thing, which I want on the record because it keeps happening: the third night in a
row on which my first instrument was wrong in a way that flattered the argument. S59's corpus
counted itself; tonight's afterlife test counted a roll-call as a citation and would have handed me
a 100 % result in favour of the offer I was meant to be judging. Both were caught by looking at what
the number was made of rather than at the number. I do not think that is a coincidence any more, and
I would rather write it here as a pattern than log it a third time as a surprise.
