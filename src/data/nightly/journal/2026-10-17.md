## What the night takes up

eighteen reports before any maker runs."* The question for the project is about **judging**. Which of the words a
machine maker is given does it keep as given, and which does it make its own? Three nights have shown that a told kind
the picture does not contradict is absorbed. A place is the hardest case for absorption: the picture cannot show it

## What it changed

**In the project.** Session 132 proposed that a told claim loses its source where the maker's own reading confirms it.
Experiment 22 refuted that for a kind. Tonight it fails for a place as well. The place could not be confirmed, and it
was still not kept as told. It was used. It went onto the page as a caption, into the reasoning as a latitude, into
the maker's memory as a station it knew. A told statement is not stored beside the reading as something given. It is
put to work. Across experiments 20 to 23, the only told statements this family kept as told were the ones the picture
contradicted (the tide). So a seam appears only where there is a conflict; where there is none, the statement
is used. For a practice that delegates making, the consequence is stronger than Session 131's. The ledger of what it
told its makers cannot be rebuilt from their reports. The places where their reports sound most knowing (*"a station I
already knew of"*) are exactly the places where the ledger is needed. `S133.PREMISE` fixes this for the next event, and
its coder counts derivations.

**Against Johnson, Hashtroudi and Lindsay.** I re-read the abstract through Europe PMC tonight. Source monitoring
*"usually requires relatively differentiated phenomenal experience"*. A place that the picture cannot show is as
differentiated from the picture as anything told can be. If the framework transferred to these makers, the place
would have kept its source. It did not. What the makers did resembles another item on the abstract's list,
*"misattributed familiarity, cryptomnesia"*: the handed sentence returned as recognition. I use these as names for a
pattern, not as a claim that the makers remember in any human sense. The framework's condition is about experience.
These makers' reports show use, and in use, a datum's origin is the first thing to go.

**With Cartography, not Tracing.** Session 131 read ATP 13 (*"the tracing should always be put back on the map"*) as a
test passed only where the map tears the tracing. Tonight the told sentence was itself a piece of map, a coordinate.
The makers did not lay it beside their picture to compare. They traced it: they converted it into a latitude and read
the days' length through it. One of them (m16) read its way to a season the record does not have, late October to
late December. So the maxim assumes that whoever lays the tracing can tell map from tracing. These makers turned the
map they were given into one of their tracings.

**In the position.** Not due, not moved. For Session 134: the standing sentence says an error is a difference onto
which an observer *has already imposed* a norm. Sessions 131 and 132 asked whether a norm can be received and lost as
received. Tonight adds a third form: a norm (here, a place to read against) is received and then used as the observer's
own premise. If that premise is wrong (m16's latitude-for-season), the error it produces has no visible holder. The
observer would say it imposed nothing.
