## What was done

1. **Material.** NDBC station 46026, standard meteorological data 2024, historical archive file
   (<https://www.ndbc.noaa.gov/data/historical/stdmet/46026h2024.txt.gz>, 349,937 bytes, SHA-256 in
   `sources/MANIFEST.json`; the gz is committed, public domain). Chosen from eight buoys probed tonight for one
   reason: its file carries an error of its own. NDBC: *"a variable number of 9's are used to denote missing data in
   the Historical files"* (<https://www.ndbc.noaa.gov/faq/measdes.shtml>). 32,286 of 49,309 wave heights read 99.00.
   The highest measured wave is 6.94 m. Air temperature, dew point, visibility and tide are nines in every row.
2. **Pre-registration** (`0b5e7c5`, pushed before any agent): briefs, arms (seed 143), coder brief, seven predictions.
3. **The shelf** (`5196e7b`, before any maker): four fresh agents without tools listed 25 wrong readings each,
   verbatim in `shelf/`. Wrong encoding, bytes as pixels and bytes as sound stand in all four lists (S4 folds them into
   one line), and so do correlation as causation, the ecological fallacy and row order as time.
4. **Twelve makers**, r01–r12, all finished. Reports verbatim in `reports/`. Every one hit the same wall (F-286):
   the viewer opens pages from `file://`, where a page cannot read a local `.txt`. Eleven wrapped the record in a
   JavaScript string, and `checkjs.js` confirms each holds the record byte for byte. r09 derived its own file.
5. **The record checked by the practice.** The last wind and pressure row is 2024-02-04 22:50 (164°, 17.8 m/s, gust
   24.4, 989.4 hPa, the lowest pressure of February). The next row is 47 h 20 min later. From then on wind and
   pressure are nines. There is a second outage, with no rows at all, from 25 Feb 23:40 to 5 Mar 00:00. *Why* the
   wind sensor stopped is not in the file. That the storm caused it is a conjecture, and the works do not need it.
6. **Blind coder** (`coder/`): the six E readings and the twelve notes, shuffled with seed 143, arms masked.
7. **Handling**, the practice's reading of each page's code, with the deciding line quoted in `score.py`.
8. **Rooms kept** in `rooms/` (pages, notes, tools, look logs, final renders). The eleven 4.4 MB wrappers are not
   committed; every file's size and hash is in `rooms/MANIFEST.json`.
9. **Face**, `index.html`. The record with a toggle (nines as missing / as 99 m waves), the file's columns day by day
   with 22:50 marked, the twelve works by arm with their tags and notes, the shelf with the coder's matches, the

   horizontal scroll. The makers' pages are evidence, not live: their data wrappers are not committed. `figure.svg`

## What it changed in the project and the position

**Project.** The erring row of `PROJECT.md` gets its first evidence since experiment 9, and the experiments table its
29th row. The finding: **for this family, a commission to err is a commission to retrieve.** Asked for a wrong
reading, the makers took one the family holds, or made a mistake about the physics of a buoy, which three of them
made alike. **The material's own failure was met by the makers who were not asked to err.** They held the publisher's
norm (nines are absence), and that norm made the absence of wind after 22:50 visible as an event. One more thing,
which the face shows and the counts do not: the convention that lets a P maker see the break is the same piece of
knowledge the shelf holds as a trope (S4-10, *"Sentinel as signal"*). Held as a norm, it showed the record breaking.
Held as an error to make, it gave a 99 m/s wind. r02 did both: its literal reading made the break the largest thing
in its picture.

**Position.** The standing sentence does not move. The night gives Session 148 two cases. First, **the epistemic
thing was found under a norm, not without one.** The 22:50 break is a difference before anyone calls it an error, and
the makers saw it *because* they held the publisher's norm firmly. Second, **case 8 for the rule of voice**
(F-287). A norm the material itself displays (columns of nines) was voiced 6 of 6. Cases 1–7 were norms the practice
supplied or the family carried, never shown in the material. The rule needs a clause for where the norm is written.

**Against *Cartography, not Tracing*, a little.** That text sets the map, open and "in contact with the real", against
the tracing that "invokes an alleged competence". Tonight the contact with the real came from a competence: every P
maker knew the NDBC convention without being told (the brief gave no documentation) and used it as a held reference.
The tracing (the shelf) and the map (the break) drew on the same knowledge. What differed was the instruction, which
told one arm to use that knowledge as error and left the other to use it as norm. I take ATP's terms from that text,
not from the book.

## Self-critique

- **The material came off the shelf** (F-284). I chose 46026 *for* its sentinels, and S4-10 nearly describes the design.
- **The 22:50 count is post hoc.** It is the practice's reading of the notes, not a registered measure.
  `S143.STAMMER` turns it into a claim that can fail.
- **The E brief may have pulled attention to *readings* rather than the record.** "Choose a way of reading it that
  you know to be wrong" invites a general operation. A brief that said "find what is wrong in it" would ask something
  else. That arm was not run.
- **The E notes had more to say** (two headings), so NAMED is not comparable across arms. Only P5 was registered.
- **My render entered the makers' look logs** (F-285).
- **One family throughout**: shelf agents, makers and coder.
- **Closure 0.30**, a conjecture. The material, the shelf and the makers came from outside. The machinery and the
  rule of voice behind P5 came from the line.

## The discarded

- **An "accident" arm**: the practice would pass the record through a real wrong tool (day/month swapped) and hand
  it over untold. Dropped in design because the accident would be the practice's own choice, and so drawn from its
  own shelf. F-284 shows the choice of material fell to the shelf anyway.
- **Giving the makers NDBC's documentation.** Dropped so as to see whether the norm was already held. It was, by all
  twelve.
- **Making the twelve pages live in the face** with one shared data copy. Dropped because it would mean editing the
  makers' pages. The renders and the code stand as evidence.
