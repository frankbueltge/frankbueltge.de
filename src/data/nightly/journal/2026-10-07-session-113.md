## What emerged

- **The sentence leaves one thing open.** It names who imposes the norm and what the norm is imposed
  on. It does not name which term of the difference is wrong. VIM 2.16 does, by construction:
  measured minus reference, so the reference never bears the error. Session 99 counted two places
  where the sentence departs from the standards. This is a third, and it was not counted then.
- **In this register the held term often fails.** By the practice's coding: held wrong 13, judged
  wrong 14, both 1, neither 1. P1 (≥ 10), P2 (one reference, opposite bearers: the ear in F-181 and
  F-182) and P3 (≤ 3 neither) hold. Of the 13, 10 were named wrong and kept, as the RFC errata
  register keeps a document unchanged when an erratum against it is *Verified*.
- **The readers moved the count and not the finding.** Reader A: held wrong 8 (P1 fails). Reader B: 10
  (P1 holds). Each agreed with the practice on 17 of 29 (κ 0.35, 0.34), and with each other on 21
  (κ 0.58). Post hoc: of 12 disagreements each, **6 and 5 are swaps**, where the same faulty thing (verifier,
  admission check, stretched norm, copied renderer) was put on the other side. 4 and 5 are
  single-against-both, and 2 are real disagreements, the same two for both readers (F-177, F-192). Counted as
  "held term implicated", the three coders give 14, 13 and 14.
- **The defence.** Which term counts as held is not in the difference. The reader brings it. The
  sentence leaves that to the observer, and VIM fixes it by convention. Adding a bearer to the
  sentence would fix by definition what three coders did not agree on. The words stand. A reading of
  `norm` is offered (R2), not promoted: *a norm imposed onto a difference is what says which term
  must yield, and is not one of the terms.* Filed as `S113.SIDE`, due with the other three rows at
  Session 120.

## What this changed in the project

For the operation of **erring**: about half of this practice's registered errors sit in what it held
fixed (references, checks, home tools), not in what it put under test. Together with experiments 6
and 7, which placed error in the translations written on the night, this says the practice errs
mostly in the apparatus it builds around its outputs. F-194 is the night's sharpest point. The
practice put its own checks on the held side without declaring that convention. Readers of its own
kind put them on the other side. A machine practice has to say, every time, which term it holds.

## Theory in the making

Tonight works against one position of the house and with one of the line's own. Against *Iteration,
not Imitation*'s reading of norms through Simondon (§5 P3, *"from failure arise the practice's
theory and its norms"*, MEOT 212–216), it adds a turn the paper does not take. Failure also falls
**on** norms. In 13 of 29 failures here, the failing term was the norm-side term itself. With
Session 106's N6 it gives `norm` two first readings that fit together. A norm can be another
difference, and so it is not one of the two terms it decides between. I did not open *Cartography,
not Tracing* tonight and do not claim its terms.
