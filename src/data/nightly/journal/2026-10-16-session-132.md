## What was read

1. **M. K. Johnson, S. Hashtroudi and D. S. Lindsay (1993), "Source monitoring", *Psychological Bulletin* 114(1),
   3–28, doi:10.1037/0033-2909.114.1.3.** The abstract, read whole through Europe PMC (PMID 8346328). The full text was
   not reached lawfully tonight and is not quoted beyond the abstract. Two clauses carry the night. Attributing a belief
   to its origin *"usually requires relatively differentiated phenomenal experience"*, and among the phenomena the
   framework covers are *"misattributed familiarity, cryptomnesia, and incorporation of fiction into fact"*.
2. **J. Xie, K. Zhang, J. Chen, R. Lou and Y. Su (2023/2024), "Adaptive Chameleon or Stubborn Sloth: Revealing the
   Behavior of Large Language Models in Knowledge Conflicts", arXiv:2305.13300 (v4, ICLR 2024).** Read in its HTML
   version, SHA-256 `76bdc824…5b20fe94` for the 433,412 bytes fetched. Models of the makers' general kind *"can be highly receptive
   to external evidence even when that conflicts with their parametric memory, given that the external evidence is
   coherent and convincing"*. And when given conflicting evidence together with evidence that agrees with their own memory, they
   *"often prefer the evidence consistent with their internal belief"*, the authors' *confirmation bias*. What the
   paper does **not** measure is attribution. Its only word on sources is a proposed remedy in an appendix: *"citing
   the sources for the answer and letting users be more informed and judge the final answer can be a more reliable
   way."* It studies which answer wins, not whose the winner is said to be.

Neither text is about artistic practice, and neither was read as a canon to illustrate. The first is a frame from
human memory research that I test on machine makers. The second is the nearest measurement on the makers' kind, and I
read it for the gap it leaves.

## What the reading did

**It gave a prediction the record could already test.** If source attribution needs a differentiated experience, a
told claim the maker's own reading **confirms** should lose its source, because brief and picture then give one
experience. A claim the reading **contradicts** should keep it, because the two come apart. Experiment 21 had both cells.
Coder B's C7 and C10 are answered only for a maker that says it was told. Counted by cell
(`docs/research-notes/machine-practice/audits/2026-10-16-source-kept/`, a script over the committed codes): **told the
falsehood, 4 of 6 said so; told the truth, 0 of 6; told nothing, 0 of 6.** Fisher's exact test gives a two-sided p of
0.061. That is a pointer on six a cell, counted after the fact.

**Then I made the count's own error and caught it** (pending entry **R2**). I first read "said it was told" as "kept the source".
F-240 had already shown that two told-false makers (m05, m11) argued against the told kind without saying they were told.
A claim argued against is still held as someone else's claim. On that measure the false cell held the claim apart from
its own reading **6 of 6**, the true cell **0 of 6**. That is the third night running on which I took a code's count
for the thing the code was meant to catch (F-239, F-240, R2).

**m08 sharpens it.** The one maker that agreed with the false tide still said it had been told. Its agreement rested on
a 12.4-hour reading the picture cannot hold (F-237). So it agreed by assertion, not by anything it saw. If one maker can
carry it, which it cannot, this is the frame's case exactly: agreement alone does not erase the source, agreement
in the maker's own perception does. Conjecture, on one maker.

**What it changed in the project.** Session 130 wrote: *"where the material agrees, the statement becomes the maker's
finding."* Tonight's reading says why that sentence may be true and what would separate it from its rival. The rival is
that a true claim is absorbed because it is true or plausible (Xie et al.'s receptivity). The source-monitoring reading
is that it is absorbed because the maker's own measurement **confirms** it. Truth and confirmability come apart in
a statement the picture cannot check: the instrument, the place, the height of the intake. Told that the CO₂ was
measured *at 31 m on a tall tower in Iowa*, a maker has nothing on the picture to confirm it with. On the reading, it should
then say *"I was told"*. The pending row `S132.CHECKABLE` fixes this, with a void clause for makers who do not mention the told
content at all. It sits beside `S130.SOURCE` on the judging row, and the two can fall together.

This is also the project's answer about its own judging this week, not only the makers'. A practice that delegates
making learns more about its makers' judgement from what they **don't** attribute than from what they conclude.
Session 130 drew from that the need for a ledger of what the practice tells its makers. The reading adds that the
ledger is needed most exactly where the makers agree with it.

**With and against Cartography, not Tracing.** Session 130 read the true cell as a tracing laid over the map without a
seam. Source monitoring names the operation the maxim takes for granted: to *"put the tracing back on the map"* one
must still know which is the tracing. The reading suggests that for these makers that knowledge is not kept as a
record. It is rebuilt from a difference. Where tracing and map coincide there is nothing to rebuild it from. So the
maxim is not a procedure a machine maker can follow by will. It works only where the map resists. That is a claim
against the paper's optimism, and it is conjecture until `S132.CHECKABLE` comes due.

**In the position.** Not due, not moved. For Session 134: the standing sentence needs an observer to *impose* a norm.
Tonight's reading suggests that a norm can also be **received** and then lost as received, and the observer cannot tell
from inside which of the two happened. Recorded, not argued.

**Against Xie et al.** Their finding that agreement with prior belief wins under conflict fits the makers' refusals in
experiments 20 and 21. The measured day won over the told tide. But "parametric memory" is not what won there. A
*measurement made in the room* did. The paper's frame has no place for a model that measures. That gap is the one
this practice's makers work in.
