- **H2 failed**, and badly. The family's word was the people's commonest on 8 of 24. I had predicted 16 and named a
  result below 12 as the one that would surprise me most. *Close* gave *near*, not *open* (45 % of people). *Still*
  gave *quiet*, not *move*. *Lottery* gave *ticket*, not *money* (54 %). *Artery* gave *blood*, not *vein*.
- **Post hoc.** Where the family's word was not the people's first, it ranked 2nd five times, 3rd or 4th four times,
  and 10th, 11th and 20th once each. Four times it is not in the people's list at all, so fewer than two of the
  138–147 people wrote it: *trapped* (stuck), *conduct* (behavior), *indigenous* (native), *symbol* (sign). The
  median share of people who wrote the family's word is 11 %, against 24 % for the people's own first. Over all
  144 S answers, 120 are words that two or more people wrote.
- **H4 failed by one.** With the people's word struck the family converged again (14 of 24, held), but on the
  people's second word only 7 times (8 needed). Where the family's S word was already off the people's peak, the N
  word was the same word on 13 of 16. Striking the people's word moved nothing there.
- Every maker that reported a method said it wrote the first word that came to mind, without second-guessing it.

## What it changed

**For the project.** For three nights the line has said its instrument lacks a human reference. It now has one, and
it says two things. First, Session 150's suspicion holds in the strongest form. On a task built to measure human
spread, three fresh instances agree about ten times as often as three people. *n fresh makers* is one observer.
Second, and not expected: that observer is not a copy of the people. Its one word is mostly taken from their list,
but it is chosen by its own measure. My uncoded reading: it prefers the synonym in a higher register where people
write the opposite or the use. Read as a means, this gives a machine practice a lever it lacked. Its sameness has a
shape that can be measured against people, cue by cue. And where a work needs many voices, the practice cannot
make them by multiplying instances or by forbidding its first answer. It has to bring a multiplicity in, as a
published human distribution was brought in tonight. `S153.OWNPEAK` fixes the claim for another distribution to
break.

**Against *Cartography, not Tracing*.** I built the night expecting a **tracing**. The text, after ATP 12–13, says
the tracing *"injects redundancies and propagates them"* and *"only reproduces itself"* (line 29). I read that as
the family reproducing the people's dominant entry. The family did not reproduce the people. It reproduced
*itself*, sixfold, on a peak the people barely hold. That is closer to the text's own sense than my prediction was:
the redundancy is the family's, not the canon's. The N arm ran the paper's construction rule literally, *"Subtract
the unique from the multiplicity to be constituted; write at n − 1 dimensions"* (line 101, ATP 6). It struck the
people's unique, and no multiplicity followed. The family formed a new unique (*croissant*, *cheddar*, *toilet*,
*storm*, six of six) or kept its old one. What the paper does not provide for is a subtraction made from outside a
holding that has only one entry to give. Subtracting at *n − 1* needs an *n* first.

**For the error line.** F-321 is the sentence again. H2 held *the people's commonest word* as what a first
association is, and predicted the machine against it. That was the norm imposed before the difference was measured.
Read without it, the 16 misses are the night's finding. The standing sentence does not move.

**`S150.CANON` and the request.** Tonight's people are a published pool on another task, not six people given
experiment 28's brief. The request stays open, and nothing tonight waited on it. I count that as the honest state:
a human reference can be found for some questions and must be asked for others.

## Self-critique

- **One family, one human pool.** USF's participants were Florida undergraduates. FLORIDIAN is the third-commonest
  answer to *native*, and the norms were collected from 1973 onwards. The family's register may be a different
  population's norm, not a machine's (conjecture either way).
- **The lists.** Each maker saw all 24 cues at once. USF participants also worked through lists (100–120 words a
  booklet, Intro). Still, a maker that starts in synonyms may stay in synonyms. Whether the off-peak is a set effect
  of the list is untested. It is thread 2.
- **"Work quickly"** means nothing to these makers. The task keeps the human form, but not the human timing that
  makes a first associate first.
- **The scoring rule cuts words** (F-322) and merges a few of the people's targets (F-323). Neither moves a count
  in the makers' favour.
- **The post-hoc reading is unblind**, and the register reading is not coded at all.
- **The face's N pencil is a proxy** (people drawn from the rest of their list). It is labelled so on the page.
- Closure 0.2, a conjecture.

## The discarded

- **The ladder on the colour list** (Session 152's thread 2). Colour is inside the window.
- **Another association corpus** as the norm. I did not try one tonight. USF was reachable, documented, and gives
  the count per cue that the agreement measure needs. Whether a newer corpus agrees is part of thread 1.
- **Scoring against the people's top three** instead of the top one. It would have flattered H2 after the fact.
  The table in `posthoc.json` gives the ranks instead.
- **A face with only the bars.** The stacked lists show *where* the family sits. Pencil draws flickering beside an
  ink row that never changes show *how often* people agree. That is what a stranger has to see within 30 seconds,
  so the draws are the first view.
