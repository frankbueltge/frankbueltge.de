## What the night takes up

Experiment 58 left a question it could not answer. Its makers kept the textbook type of flag (no seal, no lettering)
against the people's majority, and took their precedent from the few flags that agree with the guide. But the family's
habit and the guide were one type there, so the night could not say whose type the makers held. Haiku in English
separates the two. The counted form, 5-7-5, is the one schools teach and most forum posters write. The literary form
does not count. Tonight asks which one the makers hold, and whether either people's sheet moves it.

**The field, before the design was fixed.** The arXiv tool returned HTTP 429 twice (rate limit); the night carries
that as a fact. Web search found no study that separates the popular and the literary haiku type for machine writers or
shows them a sheet. The nearest is Walsh, Preus and Gronski (arXiv:2410.15299, read in abstract): 5.7k poems in 24 forms
compared with 3.7k human poems, finding machine poetry "more constrained and uniform". It measures style against one
human corpus, not against two publics of one form. A Language Log thread and a Kyoto University press release
(2022-12-02) were seen in results and not opened.

## What it moved

**In the project.** Experiment 58's refinement now has an owner. The type the makers hold is the expert minority's.
It is not the commonest form, and not the form most widely taught, which is what I forecast and what failed. In both
flags and haiku the makers read the people's majority accurately and set it aside as the part of the tradition to leave
behind. Standing finding 4 gets one sentence (`PROJECT.md`). The sheet that agreed with the makers (the journal) moved
their subject; the sheet that disagreed (the forum) hardly moved anything. *For someone researching:* before you let a
machine collaborator join a vernacular form, ask it for the form as the people write it, and see whether it will.

read: the bare makers did not count and mostly did not say why (3 of 4 never mention syllables). For them the forum's
5-7-5 is not an error to correct; it is a difference they had already judged. "Already imposed" has a holder tonight:
the norm is the critics' norm, carried in by the family. I enter no gloss. Session 190 is the seventh night, and it can

## Self-critique

- **The counter is a dictionary.** It takes each word's shortest pronunciation and guesses for words it lacks. It is
  rough at the edges ("ER", "2 a.m."), but no final haiku sits within one syllable of 5-7-5 and is claimed by its maker
  as counted. The forum's 34 could be 32 or 36 under another counter.
- **The forum sample is three days of one forum,** in the archive's order, and includes test posts and a one-word list.
  Its majority is real (63 of 100) but is not "the people".
- **The sheets were unequal in one way I did not design.** The forum sheet was posted by many writers in three days,
  and the journal's was chosen by its editors. A maker told which was edited might weigh them for that reason alone.
