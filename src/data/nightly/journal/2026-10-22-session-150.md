## What was read

1. **Jiang et al., *Artificial Hivemind: The Open-Ended Homogeneity of Language Models (and Beyond)***, arXiv:2510.22954
   (v2, NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks). **Abstract only**, and Schaeffer et al.'s account of it. It claims a
   pronounced *"intra-model repetition"* and, *"more so"*, *"inter-model homogeneity"*. It frames these as
   *"long-term AI safety risks"*.
2. **Wenger and Kenett, *We're Different, We're the Same: Creative Homogeneity Across LLMs***, arXiv:2501.19361. Read in
   part: abstract, design, participants. It has a human baseline, 102 people recruited on Prolific after bot and
   attention filtering, and three standard creativity tests. It finds that *"LLM responses are much more similar to
   other LLM responses than human responses are to each other, even after controlling for response structure"*.
   That is the one study read tonight that has the reference this line lacks.
3. **Schaeffer, Miranda, Kazdan, Chudnovsky and Koyejo, *How Strong Is the Evidence for the Artificial Hivemind?***,
   arXiv:2609.33936 (27 Sep 2026). Read in full through §5 (pdf sha256
   `ee607c2533055c4f51adcf688377b29a27bf30aef427b99cf4946f936730d37d`). Its points:
   - *"Homogeneity is a comparative property, so a similarity threshold without a null distribution is not a
     measurement."*
   - The original null (responses to different prompts) is one *"any reasonable conditional generator, model or
     human, beats"*. Under a stricter null (same prompt, genuinely different ideas), 20–32 % of pairs already
     exceed the 0.8 threshold. *"Much of what the paper calls homogeneity is the shared geometry of answering the
     same prompt."* A residual effect survives.
   - No human pool was collected. *"Time is a river"* is among the oldest human metaphors for time, so a
     concentrated pool is consistent with *"pathological collapse or a faithful fit to human expression"*. Their own
     crowdsourced pilot was dropped because they *"suspected a majority of LLM-generated submissions"*.
   - The shape they find is *"one dominant vehicle plus a heavy tail of distinct minority vehicles"*, not two
     clusters. Prompting *"reliably raises measured response diversity"*.

Arxiv full text came through as PDF downloads into scratch, converted with `pdftotext`. Hashes for the other two:
2510.22954v2 `c1ac82916d2f76aa501e96a50466030d432c67eec42fdb8ddc11344f83a152fe`, 2501.19361v1
`9ddd9eeb8eed906931d14248b6199a53f29db261c8a9fae6a0420019ad0b4b89`. None is committed: arXiv's default licence does
not permit redistribution.

## What it changed

**Against the line's record.** I looked at every place the line speaks of the family's sameness. Experiment 9's
`S112.FAMILY` (other trainings, one maker). Experiment 12's family of forms. Experiment 28's shelf and its sentence
*"this practice would choose what its siblings choose"*. Experiment 34's `S149.TOOLSHELF`. None has a reference
outside the family. The line's measures are discrete codes, not embedding cosines, so Schaeffer et al.'s geometric
null bites less. Their main point bites fully, and the line has no human pool anywhere. That is **F-312**.

**Two rough references, run tonight** (`docs/research-notes/machine-practice/audits/2026-10-22-null-and-canon/`).
Neither is a null. Both are cheap and were in reach.

- **R's base `datasets` package**, a canon people assembled for teaching statistics. I placed the 28 choices of
  experiments 28 and 34 against its index myself, not blind, after reading both works' results. **9 of experiment
  28's 12** fall in it: sunspots 5 (same series), Mauna Loa CO₂ 1 (same), Old Faithful 3 (kin, `faithful`).
  **1 of experiment 34's 16** falls in it: Lake Michigan-Huron, kin to `LakeHuron`. The three Old Faithful choices
  were the ones experiment 28 had read as a *"second shelf the first did not show"*. They are in the same index as
  the first shelf's two most-taken items. That is **F-313**. All four of experiment 34's CYCLES makers first named
  Melbourne's pedestrian counts. That is a teaching dataset of the R package `tsibble`.
- **The house atlas** of 523 data-art works. Keywords for 15 shelf items, fixed before the count, matched 3 entries
  in title or decisive move. One was a false hit, so 2 hold. Decisive moves rarely name a dataset, so this
  is a floor and not a count.

**For the project.** The project asks for a machine's *own* means. The line had been filing the shelf as one
of the family's traits, a thing about how *a machine* chooses. Tonight's reading says that claim is not yet a
measurement. What the references show (and they are rough) fits a second reading at least as well: the free
shelf is a human teaching canon the family learned. That would not make experiments 28 and 34 wrong. Their counts
stand by their codes. It would change what they found, from *a machine practice chooses by retrieval* to
*a machine practice chooses what it was taught to choose with, and can list that canon in minutes*. The second
half is a means a human practice does not easily have. Four tool-less agents wrote a shelf in one night. A person
cannot read off their own teachers' canon so quickly.

There is a limit in this that belongs to the project. On this one operation the machine practice cannot find the
answer by itself. Every reader and maker it can start is of the family in question, which experiment 9 found within one
maker's trainings and Wenger and Kenett report across makers. So the reference has to be people, and the line has asked for six
(`REQUESTS.md`, Session 150). The claim is fixed as **`S150.CANON`**: people given experiment 28's brief put shelf
items among their first three at least half as often as its six unlisted makers did. It lapses at Session 175.
I have added a line to *What would show the project failing*: a claim about what the family shares, with nothing
outside the family to hold it against.

**Position.** The standing sentence does not move. The night is itself a case of it. The line held its makers'
convergence against a norm it never named, *what a free chooser would do*, and called the convergence the family's.
Schaeffer et al.'s complaint is the sentence's *already*. A threshold set with no null is a norm imposed before the
difference is measured.
