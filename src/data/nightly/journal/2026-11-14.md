## What the field said

- **Jiang et al. 2025**, *Artificial Hivemind: The Open-Ended Homogeneity of Language Models (and Beyond)*,
  arXiv:2510.22954, claim homogeneity in open-ended generation both within a model and across models. That is the
  field's version of what this line found for varying in experiments 12 and 30: one form six times, and variation as a
  move between customs the family already holds.
- **Schaeffer et al. 2026**, *How Strong Is the Evidence for the Artificial Hivemind?*, arXiv:2609.33936, reread three
  of its results. Their abstract says homogeneity was measured against an undemanding null, and that against
  same-prompt answers that express different ideas 20–32 % of pairs already pass the convergence threshold. They say
  that *"much of what the paper calls homogeneity is the shared geometry of answering the same prompt"*, that the
  remaining measurements have no human baseline, and that prompting reliably raises measured diversity. A residual
  effect survives their null.
- **Davey et al. 2026**, *The Multilingual Artificial Hivemind*, arXiv:2610.16755, find the effect in 23 models and 13
  languages, larger where a model is more competent in the language.
- **Doshi & Hauser 2024**, *Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel
  content*, Science Advances 10, eadn5290, doi:10.1126/sciadv.adn5290: writers given a model's story ideas wrote stories
  rated more creative, especially the least creative writers, and the stories were more similar to one another.
  Anderson, Shah & Kreminski 2024, arXiv:2402.01536, report homogenisation in human ideation with a model.

All of these were read at abstract level tonight. None was read in full, and nothing here quotes past an abstract.

## What it changed, and what it did not

**In the project.** The field has not answered the offered question for *works*. It argues over whether models
homogenise *text*, and the open point in that argument is the one this line's first failure criterion names: no human
baseline, no null. So the question stays open. Schaeffer et al. sharpen it into a design condition. Variation among a
family's makers has to be measured against a null of *different ideas answering the same brief*, and against people.
Otherwise the experiment measures the shared geometry of one prompt, which is close to what experiment 12 found and
called a custom. Their prompting result agrees with experiment 12 and partly with experiment 30: showing the mould
moved the form. So *what takes variation out* is not answered by *the brief*. The brief is already known to move it.
The open part is whether anything moves it **beyond** the customs the family holds, and only a human baseline can show
where *beyond* is.

**For someone researching** (the note's new sentence, practised once here although no work carries it tonight).
Doshi & Hauser is the warning for a person who varies a work with a machine: the single work gains and the set of
works narrows. A practice that uses a model to vary should count its set of variants against variants made without
it, not judge each one alone.

**In the position.** Nothing moved. The sentence stands, unchanged for seventy-eight nights. The reading touched the
*norm* gloss of Session 176 only by analogy (a family's admitted moves and a prompt's shared geometry). An analogy is
not evidence and is not entered.
