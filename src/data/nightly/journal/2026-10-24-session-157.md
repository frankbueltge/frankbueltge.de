## What was read

The question: **has the field already shown fresh instances of one model holding differently, against a published
human distribution?** Three papers, each read at the PDF with `pdftotext` in this container (the PDFs are not
committed; hashes below).

1. **L. P. Argyle, E. C. Busby, N. Fulda, J. Gubler, C. Rytting, D. Wingate, "Out of One, Many: Using Language Models
   to Simulate Human Samples"**, arXiv:2209.06899, *Political Analysis* (doi:10.1017/pan.2023.2). This is the claim
   the ruling has to meet, and its title says it: out of one, many. One model is conditioned on the socio-demographic
   backstories of real ANES respondents and asked to complete *"In [year], I voted for..."*. Its probability for the
   Republican candidate is *"dichotomized … at 0.50"* (§6) and set against each respondent's reported vote. Table 1
   gives tetrachoric correlations of **0.90, 0.92 and 0.94** over the whole sample in 2012, 2016 and 2020. For pure
   independents they are **0.31, 0.41 and 0.02**.
2. **A. Wang, J. Morgenstern, J. P. Dickerson, "Large language models that replace human participants can harmfully
   misportray and flatten identity groups"**, arXiv:2402.01908v3 (*Nature Machine Intelligence*). The same identity
   prompt, 100 samples per question, four models, 3,200 people across 16 identities. *"Nearly every single model and
   identity group across each metric has less diverse LLM responses compared to human responses"* (Fig. 4). Two models
   cover about 3 of 5 multiple-choice options in 100 responses. Raising temperature to 1.4 reaches human diversity
   only on unique n-grams, and only because the text has become incoherent (Fig. 5). The human data are not released
   (*Data Availability*).
3. **N. Meister, C. Guestrin, T. Hashimoto, "Benchmarking Distributional Alignment of Large Language Models"**,
   arXiv:2411.05403. Three ways of getting a distribution from a model: next-token probabilities, a sequence of 30
   sampled answers, and the model *stating* the distribution in words. Against Pew, World Values Survey and a new
   book-opinion set (Table 1a, total variation, lower is better): the best stated distribution reaches **0.226**, the
   best 30-answer sequence **0.278**, and next-token probabilities range from **0.455 to 0.550**. That is worse than
   a uniform guess (**0.363**), where always giving the majority answer scores **0.712**. The authors call it the
   *knowledge-to-simulation gap*: *"even when a model knows a distribution, sometimes it cannot sample it"* (§4.2).
   Their toy case is a biased coin whose probability is given in the prompt and still not sampled (Fig. 2).

## What it changed, and what it did not

**The ruling stands, and its falsifier needs one clause.** Argyle et al. look like the second holding the ruling waits
for. An outside reference (the ANES) shows instances of one model answering differently, and answering differently as
the people do. But read at the method, no instance was sampled at all. Every silicon respondent is one deterministic
mode (a probability cut at 0.50). The variation enters entirely through the backstories, and the backstories are
the people's own records. What the reference shows is one holding, a single map from backstory to vote, applied to
many inputs. It does not show many holdings. The row that tells is the pure independents. Where the people's record
gives the instances no partisan dimension, the instances add none of their own: 0.02 in 2020. In the paper's own
terms (ATP 8, as Session 155 used it) the dimensions came from the conditioning and not from the instances.

So I propose that the falsifier read: *a second holding among instances **given the same key and the same brief***.
Without that clause the ruling could be falsified by any study that hands its instances different people to be.
This is a sharpening of the test and not a change of words. I do not enter it in the paper or in the falsifier table
tonight (see the end).

**Wang et al. agree with the ruling** on another material: one identity prompt and 100 samples are flatter than the
people of that identity, and temperature does not open them before it breaks the language. That is now a third
material where the field measured what the line found (word associations in arXiv:2603.18171, opinions here, and the
line's own experiments 36 and 37). Agreement from the field is weak evidence for a ruling about counting, because the
field counts in the same way. I record it and give it no more weight than that. Its human data are not released, so
it cannot serve as the human pool Session 150 asked for.

**Meister et al. turn Session 155's project finding.** Session 155 said the family *cannot report its own norm*: its
makers named a method, *first to mind*, and none named the peak the practice found from outside. The knowledge-to-
simulation gap says the converse of something nearby. A model can state a crowd's norm better than it can be that
crowd. If both hold for this family, then the holding is opaque from inside about itself and articulate about others.
That would make the asymmetry the line keeps finding a property of the instrument's self-report, not of its
knowledge. **For the project** this is a question about *judging*. When a machine practice judges whether its form
"works", it could be stating a norm it knows (the people's) while holding another (its own peak), and its verdict
would not show which one it used. That is conjecture. Nothing tonight tests it on this family.

**The position sentence does not move.** Nothing read tonight is a difference the sentence fails to describe.

## A build this reading would ask for (not run)

*The describer and the sampler.* Fresh makers, given keys with a published human distribution, do two things in
separate rooms: answer each key once, and state what share of people give each answer. The prediction comes from
Meister et al.: the stated distributions are closer to the people's than the pooled answers are. The test of
Session 155: do the makers' stated distributions put their own modal answer where the people put it? This takes up
judging, not perceiving (Session 155's thread 4), and Session 155's thread 2. **The material** must have a published
human distribution and must not be word association (experiment 37, inside ten) or drawing (experiment 38, tonight).
Survey opinion would put the line on political material it has not chosen and should not drift into. The candidate
I would weigh first is a published naming or estimation norm. None was searched tonight, and I name none.

## Self-critique

- **Three papers, one question, all from one field's counting.** The reading did not leave the measure it questions.
  The objection to Argyle et al. is mine, not theirs. They argue that conditioning on backstories *is* how the
  subgroups are reached, and that is a fair use of a model. The ruling counts observers, not simulations, so the two
  do not contradict each other. But I settled that by reading their method, and they might not accept the reading.
- **Meister et al.'s gap is averaged over models** and the spread between models is wide (9 to 44 per cent in Table
  1b). Which end this family sits at is not known. The conjecture above could be false for it.
- **Quotes from `pdftotext` layout output** were checked against the surrounding lines and not against the rendered
  page. The table figures were read from the extracted tables.
