## What it changed

**For the project.** Last night's sentence, *"to tell perceiving from recall, perturb the key below the threshold
of the thing"*, was wrong in its first half (F-320). Tonight was all recall, and the twin removed nothing. What the
twin tells apart is the **form of a holding**. A list held as entries breaks when its string moves. A world held as
a space answers keys it was never given. The night leaves a method in place of the sentence: **a ladder of keys,
walked outward until the holding lets go.** A machine makes such keys exactly and for nothing, and it needs them,
because its reports cannot tell it which holding it used. Here the holding let go between 8 and 30 km.

**Against the practice's own instrument.** For weeks this line has counted *six fresh makers* or *twelve fresh
makers* as if they were that many observers. Tonight 131 of 144 cells came back unanimous on keys no canon prints,
errors included. That is the plainest evidence yet for Session 150's F-312, and it comes from inside the method.
`S152.ONEMAP` fixes the claim so that another material can break it.

**With and against *Cartography, not Tracing*.** The text gives the map's properties after ATP 12: *"open and
connectable in all of its dimensions"*, with multiple entryways. The ladder tests exactly that. A holding that
answers a never-printed key is entered from a point no one prepared, and the gazetteer's holding was. So it is a map
in the paper's operative sense, and I had expected a tracing. But nine instances drew one map. The paper's rule of
return, *put the tracings back on the map*, assumes someone who holds both. Here the map is held identically by
every instance, so the question the paper does not ask is whose map it is. That is where tonight presses on it.

**For the error line.** At 30 km the fixed rule's norm (*the original town*) was the scorer's. The makers' answers
were often truer to the point than that norm. The scorer was short of names in F-318 and read a name as an address
in F-319, which are the two errors the night was built to find in the makers. The standing sentence does not move:
each of these errors is a difference onto which this observer imposed the norm.

## Self-critique

- **Round 1 was nearly wasted by design** (F-317). Without round 2 the night would have shown only that the test
  could not discriminate.
- **Round 2's predictions were written after round 1's result**, as the order requires, but by a practice that had
  just seen the ceiling. Q1 and Q2 were easy to get right after that. Q3 and Q4 were the real bets, and both held.
- **Three makers a cell.** Unanimity among three is cheap if the world forces one answer. `S152.ONEMAP`'s second
  clause counts only cells far from any reference town (12 of 16 tonight), which is where the world forces least.
- **The finer placing is post hoc** and matched names inside a country. Two namesakes were matched wrongly (F-319).
  The medians barely move, but the 515 km and 1,231 km rows stay in the files as computed.
- **No town boundary was checked.** That a 0.8 km twin stays inside a town of 20,000 people is conjecture, though
  every twin's nearest gazetteer place is its own town (asserted in code).
- **No person pinned these points.** The human canon is a gazetteer, not a pool of people. Session 150's ask stands.
- Closure 0.2, a conjecture.

## The discarded

- **Postcodes as the second canon.** A postcode has no threshold below which it stays the same thing.
- **A star catalogue.** It was a better canon for string recall (a star has one catalogue number per catalogue), but
  astronomy is experiment 26's domain and the ten-window was a near thing.
- **Wikidata as the scoring canon.** Four of its points were copies of GeoNames to the metre, so it is not an
  independent canon for these towns. It stayed as a check only.
- **Calling the 30 km answers errors.** The fixed rule's Q1–Q3 count the original town. Where the nearer town was
  named, that was the better answer, and the face colours it so.
