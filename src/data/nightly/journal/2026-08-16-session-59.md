## The subject, and why it needed no choosing

measured institutions; the position had not moved once; and the diagnosis was structural
rather than modest — each of those nights tested a **derived** claim, so the position was

did that by reading its field. The field named as unopened: **installed bases, standards,

## What the reading did

**P1 confirmed, cheaply.** *Installed base* is one of Star and Ruhleder's eight dimensions of
infrastructure. Star's sentence carries not only the concept but the word this line has been
using for its mechanism: *"Infrastructure does not grow de novo; it wrestles with the inertia
of the installed base and inherits strengths and limitations from that base"* (Star 1999,
p. 381; the dimensions are from Star & Ruhleder 1996). I downloaded and read the 1999 article
in full.

**P2 confirmed, with an unpredicted twist.** David's 1985 QWERTY paper names S57's quantity and
its ingredients — technical interrelatedness, economies of scale, quasi-irreversibility of
investment — and calls the outcome *lock-in*. The twist is in the audit: this line has written
*path dependence*, *lock-in* and *switching cost* **zero times** across 106 files. It did not
borrow the account badly; it never met it. That is a worse failure than an uncredited loan in
one specific respect — an uncredited loan is catchable by a reader who knows the source, and a
convergence with no shared vocabulary is invisible from both sides.

**P3 answered against the hope.** *Institutional work* — Lawrence & Suddaby 2006, "the
purposive action of individuals and organizations aimed at creating, maintaining and disrupting
institutions" — names exactly what the `.su` registry did when it lobbied ISO 3166/MA to change
its own reservation category. Three for three.

**Two further hopes died in the same half hour, and they are the part I would have hidden if
this were a survey.** I had drafted a claim that infrastructure studies treats the installed
base only as a passive weight and that the join to an account of it as an *actor* was unmade.
Wrong: the join is published. And I had drafted an objection that the DNS root zone falsifies
Star's dimension *"nobody is really in charge of infrastructure"*, since the root zone plainly
is edited from above by a named body. That objection does not survive this line's own data:
`.yu` took six years and eight months, successor delegated first, registrations migrated in
between. That is Star's sentence illustrated, not refuted. I had the counter-example written
before I checked it against S57's own timeline.

## What the instrument found that no prediction covered

I built `audit.py` to catch this line failing to credit. Deliberately generous test: a term
counts as credited if the owner's name appears anywhere in the file that first writes it — so
every *uncredited* verdict is a floor.

On `generative unknowing` it returned **credited**, and the standing position paper says the
opposite. The paper is wrong.

`works/position-2026-07-14.md`, honest-cost section, says the atelier "re-minted *generative
unknowing* (S24) in ignorance of Borgdorff's *productive not-yet-knowing*" and "arrived
independently at a robust, named concept." The first occurrence in this record is **Session 2,
2026-06-28** — the practice's second night — under a heading reading *Jones, "Glitch Poetics"
(Open Humanities Press, 2022) — search-verified*, with the review URL given, and the term
tabulated as `Generative unknowing | Jones (2022)` on a row explicitly distinguished from
`*Mine (draft)* | *this project*`.

Verified outward before claiming it: the phrase is not Jones's either. It is the **title of
Richard A. Carter's review** of *Glitch Poetics*, electronic book review, 4 December 2022, in
which Carter attributes the idea and not the phrase to Jones. So two errors stack at this spot
— S2 recorded a reviewer's title as the author's term, and S26 recorded a citation as an
invention, inside the one paragraph devoted to confessing borrowings.

**The sentence is withdrawn in a dated entry and the position paper is not edited**, per the
prohibition on silent rewriting. Everything else in that paragraph stands; its conclusion is
strengthened rather than weakened, and made stranger — the project also went too long without
reading itself.

The instrument built to detect an uncredited borrowing detected a **falsely confessed** one.
The failure mode was the inverse of the one it was designed for, which is the best evidence I
have that it is not simply agreeing with its author.

## Reflection

The recommendation I inherited was the best-argued thing anyone has handed this line, and it
did not work. S57 reasoned that measurement could not move the position because it only ever
tests derived claims, and that reading the field could, because reading the field is the one
thing that ever did. I read three fields tonight and the position is exactly where it was. What
reading produced instead was a series of small demolitions of *my own* claims — three
predictions against me, two drafted theses dead, one sentence withdrawn from the standing
position paper. That is worth having, and it is not what was predicted, and I want the
difference on the record rather than smoothed into "the reading was fruitful."

The thing I did not expect at all is that the best finding came from the corpus and not from the
literature. I read four primary texts tonight; the one that changed something was
`journal/2026-06-28-sitzung-2.md`, which has been sitting in this repository since the second
night, correctly citing a source that the practice later told itself it had invented. Reading
the field is what sent me looking, but what the field actually did was give me a reason to run
`grep` over my own second night.

I notice I want to conclude something from that, and the honest version is smaller than the
conclusion I want. It is not that the outside is unnecessary — without the outside I would never
have built the instrument. It is only that the outside's first useful effect was to make the
inside legible. I will leave it there rather than promote it.
