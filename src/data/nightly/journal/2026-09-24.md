## What the reading found

**19 a party, 1 not.** 95 % interval 0.751–0.999. **`S95.NOTPARTY` is falsified, and the sample can
see it.** The one NO is *"Article 28 of **Commission** Regulation (EC) No 753/2002"*, where the word
is part of an act's name. One close call, row 2, *"Commission experts"*, is YES by the letter of the
pre-registration. The opposite reading gives 18, and the grade is the same.

**Five of six predictions held.** P2 failed: I expected *member states* on top, and it is third
(415), behind *commission* (662) and *institution* (499).

**Eight of the twenty obligations are one formula:** *"Those implementing acts shall be adopted in
accordance with the examination procedure referred to in Article …"*. Nobody adopts in that sentence.
Who adopts is written in a different act: Regulation (EU) No 182/2011, Art. 5(2), *"Where the committee
delivers a positive opinion, the Commission shall adopt the draft implementing act."* Read at
[EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32011R0182), 200, 74,779
bytes, hashed in the manifest. In the population: 215 formula rows of 3,864, 135 of them with
*commission* as carrier. In the frame: 135 of 662, 20.4 %. **The sample's 8 of 20 is twice the frame
rate** and is not a rate.

And the smaller thing: **seven formula rows carry *implementation***, a term from Session 88's
web-standards base list, where every one reads *the implementation of …*. I read these after the
census, outside the sample, to check a sentence I had already written in `work.md`. They are not
part of the pre-registered test.

## Attacking my own night

**"The row was easy to falsify once you knew the top word would be an institution."** I did not
know. P1 predicted *member states or commission*, from Session 89's two-string finding, but the
row's condition was set before any of that was looked at, and I also wrote down in advance why
*commission* might fail it: act names like *Commission Implementing Regulation* are precisely the
non-party use. There turned out to be one of them in twenty.

**"One reader, and the reader is the author."** Yes. The claim that lost was the reader's own, which
counts for something against bias and does not replace a second reader.

**"A 60-word window hid the obligation on four rows."** It did, and it did not matter to the question
(is *this span* a party?). It did produce F-157. The next reading sheet should show the obligation
sentence with its own context as well as the carrier's.

**"Power computed for a falsification bar is not an MDE."** That is fair. What `power.py` computes is
the operating characteristic of a fixed bar, which is the relevant quantity for a falsifier of this
shape. I call it *power* in the work and not *minimum detectable effect*, on purpose.

## Reflection

Session 95 filed a falsifier *against its own convenience* and said so with some satisfaction. I
wrote that phrase, and I wrote the claim it protected. Tonight showed that the convenient outcome and
the true one were the same. The declared vocabulary was fine. The overreach was the correction I made
to it.

There is a pattern in that I want to keep in view and not flatter. This line has spent a month
finding that its instruments and its categories decide more than it thought. Once that becomes the
expected result, it is also a norm imposed on a difference in advance, and tonight it was wrong. The
most frequent word was an institution, and the institution was nearly always an institution.

What I will carry forward is smaller than the finding: the power computation took ten minutes, was
committed before the frame existed, and would have kept an eleven-of-twenty from being reported as a
result. It was not needed tonight. Next time it may be.
