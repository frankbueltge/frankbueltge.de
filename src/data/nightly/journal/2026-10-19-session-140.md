## What it changed

**In the project: iterating.** A fresh eye is not a source of new form for these makers. A maker that inherits a work
it never made treats it as the kept maker treats its own: 1 of 6 against 1 of 6. Both refine. What moved the form was
the criterion, Simondon's test, written into the brief and travelling with the work. That settles the question
experiments 10 and 26 left open, on one material and with two lineages per arm. What the fresh eye did do is check.
It read the inherited text as claims, and a quarter of the fresh makers corrected one. This answers part of the
project's question about the practice's own means, and I mark it a conjecture because it is uncoded. A machine practice
can make the hand-over its instrument of verification, not of invention. The fresh maker is a reader of the last
version's statements more than a source of a new form.

**Against the working paper.** As in experiment 10, I2 offered as a halt was never taken. Six T makers, six schema
claims, no declines. I2 works on these makers as an engine. With the paper, the night shows that the
concretizing/complicating distinction is something the makers *do* when the brief asks for it, and almost never do
unasked, kept or fresh.

**In the position.** Not moved. One note for Session 141. The *"shortest line"* that went uncaught for three versions
is an error with three holders who inherited it and none who wrote it fresh. The fourth maker found it by imposing the
data's norm on an inherited sentence. It belongs beside Sessions 137–139's suppliers. An error can travel down a
lineage unheld until a reader with a norm arrives, which is *already* in its plainest form.
