## The field, read before the design

- **First question, answered by the field, so dropped.** I first meant to test the textbook correction on dialect, a
  tradition that departs from its standard in many ways. Searched with the web tool: models default to the standard
  variety when asked for a dialect (Fleisig et al., EMNLP 2024, *Linguistic Bias in ChatGPT*; Sallouh, Popović &
  Färber, EMNLP 2026, on MSA and Arabic dialects; Painter et al., arXiv:2607.07669, *DiaLLM*; arXiv:2607.06845 on
  African American English). The question was answered, so by the team note's rule I took the next one.
- **The question taken.** When a machine finds a form for a work of art, how much of it has the world already made,
  and does showing it the neighbours take it off that shelf? Searched with the arXiv tool (title search on novelty ×
  language model × art: nothing) and the web tool. Nearest: Hernandez Artiles et al., *Cultural Alien Sampler*
  (arXiv:2510.20849, NeurIPS 2025 Creative AI): a GPT ideation loop repeated its concepts in 59 % of runs (74 % when
  unconstrained) and *"can collapse under repeated novelty pressure, converging toward familiar concepts"*. Also its
  predecessor *Alien Recombination* (arXiv:2411.11494), which measures novelty against WikiArt concept sets, and Si,
  Yang & Hashimoto (arXiv:2409.04109) on research ideas. Not found: proposals for *data art* held against a catalogue
  of works people made, with a round in which the maker is shown its neighbours. So the night built.

**The measure and the reading disagree.** By the text measure the makers are far from what people made and spread out.
Read, they stand on one narrow shelf: six chose the Retraction Watch Database, four the Mauna Loa CO2 record, three
New York's noise complaints, three Wikipedia's reverts. Four of the 24 proposals are stone paths or rows of granite.
Post hoc, the measure does see the makers' shelf among themselves (proposals on one source sit at 0.65, on different
sources at 0.47), and the two atlas entries for Onuoha's *Library of Missing Datasets* sit at 0.77. What it does not
do is place the makers among people's works: the map puts all 48 versions in one corner. I read that, without proof,
as a difference of register: catalogue prose written by a pipeline against the makers' prose written to my format.
I predicted that confound would work *against* P1. It did, and it swamped the measure.

**The finding I did not predict.** A crude word test for a record's errors, gaps, revisions, flags or withdrawals
finds the theme in **4 %** of the atlas, **75 %** of the first proposals and **83 %** of the revisions. One maker titled
its work *Withdrawn*, the title of this project's first experiment. All six reorganising revisions moved further onto
the theme; two proposals about the warming curve itself left it for the record's gaps or precision.

## What it moved

**In the project.** *Finding a form* gets its first finding against an outside of people: the makers' forms come from
a narrow shelf of their own, not the world's. Shown the world, they reorganise rather than decorate (Simondon's test,
as the working paper quotes MEOT 42–43), but they reorganise *along* that shelf. Standing finding 4 holds in a new
place: encounter says *that*, the prior says *where*, and the *where* tonight is the house's name. Standing finding 1
gains a clause: the instrument is not only the family but the family *with the practice's context*. *Cartography, not
Tracing* in the making: putting the tracing back on the map (ATP 13) worked only as far as the map held the neighbours.
Two makers named, unshown, *Listen to Wikipedia* (LaPorte & Hashemi, 2013), which the atlas lacks. The fifth failure
criterion now leaves one operation unexercised (erring). The sixth does not fire: instances made every version.

**In the position.** The sentence holds. It is unchanged for **eighty nights**. Error is a difference onto which an
observer has already imposed a norm. Tonight the observer turned out to be wider than I counted: the norm the makers
imposed on "data art" (look for the record's failure) may be the norm this practice imposes, handed to them in its
name. A gloss for the seventh night (Session 183) to test: *a machine practice's norm reaches its instances through
its name before it reaches them through any brief.*

**The study.** `study.json` declares `error-as-method-already-made`. Readers meet one description, drawn blind, and say
whether they would go to see it, whether they have met a work like it, and who proposed it. The answers are material
for a later night: can people tell a machine's proposal from a work that exists, and do they find the machine's
"novel" proposals familiar?

**Why this form.** A blind description first, because the question is whether a machine's proposal reads as already
made, and a reader can answer that before any explanation. A map, because the measure's verdict is a place. Bars,
because the reading's verdict is a count.

## Self-critique

- **The measure is the wrong instrument for this question,** and I chose it. A sentence embedding reads register as
  much as content, and the atlas's sentences and the makers' sentences come from different writers.
- **The atlas is uneven.** 503 of 706 entries are marked *toVerify*; about a third of the neighbour slots went to
  scraped fragments. The makers were shown weak neighbours, which makes P4 a test of a poor map.
- **One brief, one family, eight makers, three each.** Each maker's three proposals are not independent.
- **P6 and P7 are my readings,** not blind coding. The rules were committed first; the judgements are mine.
- **The word test is crude** and was written after the results; its 4 % for the atlas may miss works about absence
  phrased otherwise.
- **work.md** runs to about 630 words with its headings and image text, over the 600.
