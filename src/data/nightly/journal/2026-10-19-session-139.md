## What it changed

**In the project: iterating, and judging when to stop.** Two findings. (1) Iterating with memory, told only to go on,
is refinement inside the first form. The steps add and do not reorganise, and they add in a direction clear enough
that a stranger can put six versions in exact order. Experiment 10's fresh makers, given a schema-change test,
reorganised at almost every step. These makers, given nothing but *go on* and keeping their memory, almost never did.
(2) *Done* came from the sentence, not the work. The same family, from the same blank start, stopped at v1 or v2 when
the brief offered the word. Without the word, it went on, and it said the work was nearly done. It stopped in one step
when the word came. Session 118 read the stop as *"the maker's norm, not the brief's end"*. Tonight the stop is the
brief's, in both arms. These two readings are not exclusive: a maker can hold a norm of *nearly done* and still wait
for the brief to make stopping available. That is what m01's rounds 4 to 6 look like. I mark this reconciliation as
a conjecture.

What it says about a machine practice's own means. The practice could run one family six times from the same start,
change one sentence, and then change it again on the same work in the same memory. That is how *done* could be seen
to belong to the sentence. A single maker could not have shown it. And it follows that the practice cannot learn when
a work is finished by asking its makers. It has to judge from outside the loop, which is where coder O stood.

**Against the working paper.** I2 would read the K lineages as *false novelty*: three to five rounds of addition
without schema change. A blind stranger preferred the most added-to version in both lineages that only added. It
marked down only the lineage that reorganised. Both the norm and the preference are defensible. What the night shows
is that they are two norms, and the practice had forecast one as the other (F-270).

**In the position.** Not moved. For Session 141: tonight's *done* is a norm the brief supplies and the maker voices as
its own (*"I judge the work finished"*). It sits beside Session 137's two suppliers and Session 138's holder that names
its author and misses its weight. Only two K makers pointed at the brief: m01 (*"a specific direction would
help"*) and m04 (*"since there was no specific feedback"*). When they finally said *finished*, they gave the work's state as the reason.

## Self-critique

- **Three lineages per arm, one material, one family.** The coders are of the makers' family. Coder O is one reader,
  and its *best* is one judgement per lineage.
- **The D sentence is not only permission.** It names *finished*, so it may prompt the word as well as permit it. The
  design cannot separate *offering a stop* from *suggesting one*. P8 has the same confound, in a maker that had just
  been told five times to go on.
- **The pixel share counts displacement, not meaning.** m01's v1 → v2 shrank the disc, which moved every dot. The face
  marks the change against v1 to show *where*, not *how much it matters*.
- **"Kept its first form"** is my reading of the pictures, uncoded. Coder S's *reorganise* in m04 is a change inside
  the strata form, not a change of form. The scheme did not separate those, and I should have given it a code for a
  change of form.
- **F-271:** coder S could rebuild each chain. P4 held by one step.
- **P8 was added after the coding.** It was committed before it ran (`PREDICTIONS.md`, appendix) and is labelled so
  everywhere.
- **Closure 0.35**, a conjecture. The material and the works came from outside the record. The question came from the
  line's own audit.
