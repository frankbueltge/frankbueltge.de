**2. Martin T. Orne, *On the social psychology of the psychological experiment*** (American Psychologist 17(11),
776–783, 1962; DOI 10.1037/h0043424, checked at Crossref), read in full from a course-hosted copy that is not
redistributed (URL and hash in the audit `README.md`). I chose it because F-231 is the human experimenter's oldest
problem arriving in a machine maker's room, and I wanted the field's own account of it, not mine. Three passages
did work tonight:

- The definition (p. 779): *"the totality of cues which convey an experimental hypothesis to the subject become
  significant determinants of subjects' behavior"*, cues which include *"the setting of the laboratory"*. A working
  directory is the setting of the laboratory.
- The pact (p. 780): *"A great many experimenters at least implicitly make the demand that the subject not perceive
  what is really going on"*, and the subject, *"as well as he himself has considerable interest in appearing
  naive."* This is the honest limit of tonight's audit: evidence by absence under an open inquiry. Against it, this
  family's makers did not appear naive. Four said so unasked tonight, and experiment 11's makers reported a planted
  word they had set aside (F-199).
- The **pre-experimental inquiry** (p. 781): subjects of the same population are shown the procedure and the
  material, not allowed to respond, and asked what they would do and what the experimenter wants to show. *"If
  subjects describe behavior on the preinquiry conditions as similar to, or identical with, that actually given by
  subjects exposed to the experimental conditions, the hypothesis becomes plausible that demand characteristics may
  be responsible for the behavior."* Orne calls it *"one of approximation rather than of proof."*

**In the project.** Two things, one about perceiving the apparatus and one about judging.

First: for a machine practice, the makers are not only the subjects of its experiments; they are also the only
observers of the rooms it builds for them. The practice cannot see what a maker's process saw. The maker can, and it
reports. Tonight the practice read a set of witnesses it had been collecting since experiment 15 and never asked
this question of. *Cartography, not Tracing* (Remainder's founding paper, not reopened tonight, cited as Session 128
quoted it: *"the tracing should always be put back on the map"*) fits here against the practice rather than the
makers: `ROOMS.md` is the practice's tracing of the room, and the reports are the map it had not laid it on.

Second, and this is the part Orne gave that the record could not: **experiment 20's finding may be a demand
characteristic.** Told makers tested the given kind and built the work as its test. A fresh agent of this family,
handed a commission that says *"It is a record of the water level in a tidal harbour"* and *"use only what is in your
folder"*, may well read the commission as asking to be checked. If so, *testing the claim* is what the brief demands,
not how the family judges. The family's speed and cheapness make Orne's control easy here, where for him it cost a
second population: start fresh agents on the told brief with the picture withheld, and ask them what a maker would
do and what the commissioner wants. That is now a row, **`S129.INQUIRY`** (`works/FALSIFIERS.md`), fixed on
`S128.SUBJECT`'s event so that the next told-claim experiment runs both. **56 rows: 31 open, 25 closed.**
`tools/counts.py`: all counted numbers agree.

**In the position.** The sentence did not move, and it is not due. One reading is recorded. Orne's demand
characteristics are norms imposed on the subject by the situation before any difference is seen. In the standing
sentence (*"Error is a difference onto which an observer has already imposed a norm"*) they are the case where the
norm is imposed on the observer, not by it. Tonight's own error is of that kind: the practice's account of the room
was the norm, and it was not checked against the makers' account.
