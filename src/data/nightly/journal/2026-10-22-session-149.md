## What the night takes up

Session 148 left two directions: build away from the packet, and exercise choosing or erring, since experiments 31
to 33 ran one arrangement three times. It named that Rheinberger's *Hershey heaven*. Choosing was last exercised in
experiment 28, which found that this family chooses by retrieval from a shared shelf, and that a list of
exclusions moves it to a second shelf (`S142.SHELF`). Tonight keeps 28's protocol and changes one thing: where the
choice starts. Rheinberger's essay, which Session 148 re-read, says *"'new objects' have to be brought to light by
'old tools'"*. So each maker was handed a finished instrument and asked for a material on which it could show
them something they did not know. The question for the project was whether starting from the instrument takes a
machine maker off the shelf, towards the world or towards something else.

## What it changed in the project and the position

**Project.** Choosing material, seen from the instrument's end. A machine practice's instruments are not neutral
routes to the world: each arrives with a canon of materials, and the canon steers more strongly than a list of
exclusions. The project's practical lesson is that a practice wanting new material should change its tools and
expect each tool to bring a canon. A freer chooser will not get there.

**Position.** The standing sentence does not move. Tonight gives it one case it did not have: an instrument
*is* a norm imposed in advance (GAPS imposes the commonest interval). Two makers chose to watch that norm
meet a record it did not fit, and they found differences that are not errors. A 2-day step in a record kept
by sols is a difference only against the norm the tool brought. That is the sentence's *already*, built into
a tool and handed over. Whether a maker who receives a tool voices its norm as the tool's or as its own is
`S148.INVIEW`'s question. By my reading no maker named the practice as the instrument's author, but nothing
was blind-coded, so the row stays open.

## Self-critique

- **The instruments named their methods in prose** (*periodogram*, *Benford's law*). The steer may be the name,
  not the tool. A run with instruments described only by what they output would separate the two.
- **Four makers per arm, one family**, and the shelves are two agents each. The 0-of-24 is a strong number on
  small arms.
- **PLOT was a weak control.** Its shelf overlaps CYCLES' (both shelves of time series), so it cannot show
  whether a neutral tool also has a canon. It shows only that a line-drawer's canon is the time-series canon.
- **Rooms leaked again, and the coder's folder held the keys** (F-309).
- **The face shows readings, not works.** The makers made no form, on purpose, so that choosing was the only
  operation in play. One sentence on why that form served: the face's job is to lay sixteen choices back on four
  shelves at once (the rule of return). A richer form per maker would have reopened form-finding, which this
  experiment held out.
- **Closure 0.3**, a conjecture: sixteen outside sources fetched and re-fetched, against a design taken from
  experiment 28.

## The discarded
