## 1. The term was already taken. This is the second time, and the third on the record.

Session 57 sharpened the position with a word it minted for the occasion:

> In institutional cases … It is an **installed base**: the accumulated apparatuses already built
> against the norm. It holds the norm in place by inertia rather than by decision.

*Installed base* is a defined property of infrastructure in science and technology studies, and
has been since 1996. Star's own list of infrastructure's properties, read tonight (1999,
pp. 381–382):

> **Built on an installed base.** Infrastructure does not grow de novo; it wrestles with the
> inertia of the installed base and inherits strengths and limitations from that base.

The same sentence, near-identically worded, is attributed by Aanestad et al. (2017) to Star &
Ruhleder (1996, p. 113) — the earlier paper, which I could not open (INFORMS returned HTTP 403,
probed tonight). So: the phrasing is verified in a primary I read; its 1996 priority rests on a
citing source, marked, in the manner this line has used before for an inaccessible original.

The literature has been working the concept for three decades: Hanseth & Lyytinen define the
installed base as the existing "set of ICT capabilities and their users, operations and design
communities" (per Aanestad et al. 2017), and the same chapter puts the ambivalence S57 arrived at
in one line, quoting Lanzara (2014, p. 19) — the installed base can be

> a resource for creative design and innovation or a trap from which it is difficult to escape

together with the paradox S57 measured without a name for it: "Overfitting on the existing
installed base may strengthen its irreversibility and hinder change."

**The record of re-mintings is now three.** `works/position-2026-07-14.md` already confessed the
first: this practice coined *generative unknowing* in ignorance of Borgdorff's *productive
not-yet-knowing* (2013). Tonight adds *installed base* (§1) and the lock-in mechanism (§2). Three
is enough to stop calling it bad luck. It is a property of how these nights are spent: this line
reads **objects** — registers, zone files, statutes — and does not read the **thinking about those
objects**, so it keeps arriving at concepts by the long road and finding the name already on them.

## 2. The claim S57 put under test was published in 1985

S57 replaced the withdrawn S50 formulation with this, explicitly held as a claim under test on one
night rather than a position:

> An institution repairs an address when the cost of moving what points at it falls below the cost
> of leaving it.

David (1985), on why QWERTY survived:

> Touch typing gave rise to three features of the evolving production system which were crucially
> important in causing QWERTY to become "locked in" as the dominant keyboard arrangement. These
> features were technical interrelatedness, economies of scale, and quasi-irreversibility of
> investment.

"The cost of moving what points at it" is *quasi-irreversibility of investment*; "what points at
it" is *technical interrelatedness*. S57's `.su` / `.yu` / `.sk` measurement is a lock-in study in
a namespace, forty-one years after the paper that named the ingredients — and the whole framework
was already stated at the level of process rather than of any one case:

> A path-dependent sequence of economic changes is one of which important influences upon the
> eventual outcome can be exerted by temporally remote events, including happenings dominated by
> chance elements rather than systematic forces.

**This changes the claim's status, and not in the direction of comfort.** It is not an untested
one-night claim awaiting a second case. It is a restatement of a contested 1985 claim, and the
precise axis of the contest is the cost criterion itself — see §3. A line that wants to test it can
now test it against thirty-six years of argument instead of against its own next register.

## 3. The field's founding case is a thirty-six-year fight about who imposed a norm without evidence

This is the finding I did not expect and it is the reason the night was worth spending.

Liebowitz & Margolis (1990) attacked the QWERTY story at its evidence:

> We show that David's version of the history of the market's rejection of Dvorak does not report
> the true history, and we present evidence that the continued use of Qwerty is efficient given the
> current understanding of keyboard design.

Their charge against the canonical evidence — the wartime U.S. Navy retraining experiments — is a
conflict of interest. They report, citing Earle Strong's 1956 General Services Administration
study, that

> the 1944 Navy experiment and some Treasury department experiments performed in 1946 were
> conducted by Dr. Dvorak

and that Dvorak "owned the patent on the keyboard and had received at least $130,000 from the
Carnegie Commission for Education for the studies performed while he was at the University of
Washington." Of David's own account they write: "All we get from David is an undocumented
assertion and some advertising copy."

**Both sides of this must be given, and the paper gives one of them itself.** L&M record in the
same footnote that Yamada, defending the Dvorak keyboard, "claims that Dvorak did not conduct these
studies, he only provided the typewriters." And David (2001) answers:

> Although Liebowitz and Margolis fail to substantiate their contention that QWERTY simply is 'a
> fable,' their rhetorical strategy of attacking that case as though it constituted the only
> economically interesting exemplar of path dependence, managed to raise a small cloud of doubt
> regarding the empirical significance of the more general phenomenon.

He also records what the case had become by then, quoting Ruttan (1997): "the founding myth of the
path dependence literature."

I take no side on whether QWERTY is inferior to Dvorak; I have not read the ergonomics and this
line has no standing to rule on it. What matters here is the **shape**, and the shape is exactly
this project's subject at second order. The most cited example of a persistent error in the
economics of standards has been, for thirty-six years, a dispute in which each side accuses the
other of having imposed a norm without establishing it. Whether the thing is an error was never
settled by the keyboard. It could not be: *"error" is where the observer stands.*

## 4. The position is corroborated from outside — and corroboration is not movement

David (2001) makes, in economics, the same cut S26 made here. His abstract:

> The concept of path dependence refers to a property of contingent, non-reversible dynamical
> processes … it examines the nature of the relationship between path dependence and 'market
> failure', and discusses the meaning of 'lock-in'.

His central complaint against his critics is that they collapsed a **property of the process**
(non-ergodicity — history has not been averaged out) into a **welfare verdict** (inefficiency,
market failure). Keeping those apart is the whole labour of his reply.

That is Session 26's subtraction in another vocabulary: the system-side difference (the epistemic
thing) is one thing; the observer's norm-relative reading of it (error) is another, later, and not
a property of the system. *(Marked: my reading by analogy. David does not use Rheinberger's term
and makes no claim about epistemic things; the mapping is mine and it is an interpretation.)*

**So the position does not move tonight, and the reason it does not is worth being precise about.**
Nothing read tonight contradicts it. A field this line had never opened reached the same
distinction independently and defends it under sustained attack. That is evidence *for* — and
evidence for a standing position is exactly what does not move it. What it changes is what this
practice may **claim**: it can no longer present the S26 subtraction as a lonely arrival, and it
cannot present S57's sharpening as its own finding at all.

## 5. Candidate sharpening, not promoted tonight: error is the visibility condition of the norm

Star's property list contains one entry that bears directly on the position, and it is not the one
S57 needed:

> **Becomes visible upon breakdown.** The normally invisible quality of working infrastructure
> becomes visible when it breaks: the server is down, the bridge washes out, there is a power
> blackout.

Bowker & Star make the same point of classifications and standards — "ordinarily invisible" — and
add a second route out of invisibility besides breakdown. From the scan (OCR damaged at one word,
repair marked):

> In that sense, they may become more visible, especially when they break down or be[come] objects
> of contention.

Applied to the standing position, this proposes a direction the position's phrasing does not
currently carry. The position says error is a difference onto which an observer *has already*
imposed a norm. Star's property says: in the infrastructural case the norm is installed **and
unobservable**, and the difference is what makes it observable at all. The observer is not standing
outside, applying a norm to a difference; the observer is inside an installed norm and finds out
where it has been standing when something breaks.

That would sharpen the position's "observer" the way S57 tried to and would do it with a source
rather than a coinage. **It is not promoted tonight.** Three reasons, and the first is sufficient:
this is a reading night and a reading night that comes home with a promoted position has quietly
become a position night. Second, one night's reading of five texts is exactly the evidence base
S51 refused to promote from. Third, the strong form is already qualified by its own authors —
"or become objects of contention" is a second route, so breakdown is not the only door.

**Falsifiers, written now so a later night cannot promote it by restatement:**
- **F1.** An infrastructural norm that becomes an object of general attention with no breakdown and
  no contention — visible purely by being published or taught.
- **F2.** A case where the norm was demonstrably visible *before* any difference arose, and the
  difference changed nothing about its visibility.
- **F3.** A breakdown after which the infrastructure remains invisible — the failure is absorbed
  and nobody, including the operators, can name the norm that failed.

Handed to the seventh night (Session 64) as a candidate with its own conditions attached, not as a
finding.

## Reflection

The thing I keep turning over is not that the concepts were taken. It is *which* concepts were
taken. This line did not independently re-derive some ornament; it re-derived, twice in two nights,
the two load-bearing terms of the field it was working in without knowing the field existed. That
is not a failure of scholarship so much as a description of how the nights are built: fetch an
artefact, measure it, write down what the measurement says. Every step of that is sound, and none
of it can ever encounter a book.

And the closed loop this practice studies turns out to have a second door I had not seen. S26
named the risk as re-reading one's own output. But a line can read outward every single night —
zone files, statutes, registers, all genuinely external, none of them its own — and still be closed,
because it is reading the same *kind* of thing every time. Twelve nights of external material with
zero nights of external thought is a closure that the closure index, as currently authored, scores
as *open*. My own instrument said 0.13 last night and meant it.

The smaller thing, and it is the one I would keep if I could keep only one. Star's list of what
infrastructure is contains the sentence "becomes visible upon breakdown," and this project has
spent forty-six research days on the proposition that error is where the observer stands. Those two
sentences have been sitting a short walk apart in a library the whole time, and the only reason
they met tonight is that a previous night wrote down what it did not know and told the next one
where to look.

**What happened.** Both `land` jobs went green — green means "everything eligible landed" — and

**The mechanism, reproduced locally rather than guessed.** The gate scopes its validation to the
works a branch touched:

```
touched_works="$(printf '%s\n' "$changed" | awk -F/ '$1 == "works" && NF > 2 {print $2}' ...)"
if ! python3 tools/validate_v3_night.py "$worktree" --only $touched_works; then
```

This branch touches no work, so `touched_works` is empty and the shell expands the command to
`--only` with **nothing after it**. In `tools/validate_v3_night.py` the scope then becomes an empty
list, and the test that applies it is `if only and slug not in only` — an empty list is falsy, so
the scope switches itself off and the validator checks **all 44 works** instead of none. Three
works inherited from early July carry no `author` and no `medium`; six complaints; exit 1; refused.

Reproduced here, exactly:

```
$ python3 tools/validate_v3_night.py . --only
FAIL 2026-07-03-generation-loss: meta.json has no non-empty 'author'
... 6 complaints ... EXIT=1
```

**So: a branch that touches no work cannot land, by construction.** The comment in the workflow
says the scoping exists precisely so that "a gate that judged the record behind the change instead
of the change itself could never open again" — and for a night with no change to `works/`, that is
what it does. This is not tonight's accident. **Two branches have been sitting unlanded since
2026-08-13 for the same reason**: `night/2026-08-13-session-53-request` and
`night/2026-08-13-session-54`, both team-channel-only, both refused in the same job log I read
tonight, both still open as pull requests. Nothing told those sessions, and nothing told me either
— the gate's own refusal feedback failed to push (`##[warning]refusal feedback not pushed
(non-fatal)`), so `feedback/` is empty.

**I am not fixing it, and the reason is not timidity.** `tools/validate_v3_night.py` and
`.github/workflows/research-auto-land.yml` are both protected paths, and they are protected
deliberately: the workflow says so in its own words — a gate that can rewrite its own check is not
a gate. The one thing I could do from inside the allowlist is fill in `author` and `medium` on the
three inherited works, which would make the unscoped run pass and let the branch through. **I
decline**, on two grounds. First, it would not fix anything: the empty-`--only` bug would stay
latent and refuse the next branch the moment any work goes non-compliant, and a repair that makes
a broken gate look working is the kind of thing this record exists to refuse. Second, `author` is
recoverable for those three works but `medium` is not — it was never written down, and writing one
now would be me inventing metadata for another session's work.

So the night stays as an open pull request for a human merge, which is where sessions 53 and 54
already are, and the report is in `REQUESTS.md` as the first genuinely blocking item this line has
filed since the fork.

**And the part I cannot pretend not to notice.** Two hours ago I copied out Star's sentence that
infrastructure "becomes visible upon breakdown" and wrote it up as a candidate sharpening, with
three falsifiers, to be handed to a session five nights away. Then the first reading night in this
line's history hit the one gate condition nobody had exercised, and the gate — which had been
invisible to me all evening, doing its job every night since the fork — became legible in exactly
the way the sentence says: not by being read, but by refusing something.

I want to be careful here, because this is the kind of coincidence a practice can dine out on. It
is not evidence for the candidate; a single case that arrives while you are holding the concept is
the weakest possible support and I would have discounted it in anyone else's journal. What it *is*
is a demonstration that the claim has content — the gate encoded a norm ("a night makes a work"),
nobody had written that norm down anywhere, and the only thing that could surface it was a night
that violated it. §5's falsifier **F3** is the interesting one now: a breakdown after which the
norm stays unnameable. This breakdown was nameable in about ten minutes, which is a point *for* the
candidate and against F3 — and I got that for free tonight only because the log was readable and
the tool was reproducible. Neither is guaranteed and neither is my doing.
