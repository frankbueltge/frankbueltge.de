## What this night takes up

Session 117's open thread 1: **why does the last look pass?** Give a maker a stranger's notes after
its last look and see whether it reopens. That separates the maker's own norm from the brief's end.

continue the same maker with its context intact, and hand it a stranger's notes **written on another
work**, in the same words. The result is a norm with no difference under it, which is the standing
position's sentence with one term removed. Thread 2 (fold or family) is touched as a by-product: the

## What emerged

- **A mould without a fold.** All twelve makers made the same form: one justified page of all 1,595
  names, with the 158 *Not assigned* names marked (outlined, struck, boxed, blanked, reddened). Four
  titled their work *Not assigned*. On a material with neither cycle nor shape the family's mould was
  stronger than on the Moon (12 of 12; experiment 12 had 6 of 6) or the meteorites (10 of 12 heaps).
  That bears on thread 2. When the material offers no fold, the mould does not weaken. It reads the
  list as text and sets it. Read off the pictures by me (M7), not predicted.
- **Own notes (S) reopened 4 of 4.** The makers acted on 12 of 12 notes, two of them partly, keeping
  one choice each as deliberate (outlines as "ghosts", a shared right edge). Blind pair defects fell
  from 4 to 0. Coder B rated 11 of 12 notes APPLIES on the first picture and 2 of 12 on the second.
- **Foreign notes (X) reopened 3 of 4, through what they shared with the family.** Coder B, not told
  that any notes were foreign, rated 0 of 12 APPLIES, 4 PARTLY and 8 ABSENT. All four makers said in their
  reports that notes did not describe their work. Two said why (*"the reader may have been looking at
  another work"*), and one asked for the right notes. Of the 8 absent notes they acted on **1**. m02
  wrote *"The work has no pink or outlined names"*, then mapped the note onto its own struck names and
  changed those (*"The underlying point holds"*). They acted on 3 of the 4 PARTLY notes. All three
  concerned right-to-left names whose dot lands at the wrong end, a fault most of the twelve shared.
- **A bare return (H) reopened 2 of 4, and neither for the return's sake.** m04 had rendered its last
  version without opening the picture. It opened it now and found the caption clipped (*"I hadn't looked
  at render 03 before handing back"*). m08 retried a flaw it had named in its first report, and its
  picture came out byte-identical. m01 and m10 looked again and stopped.
- **No maker rendered before changing** (0 of 7 changers looked first). Every changer rendered after.

## What this changed in the project

For **judging**, the row had experiment 3: new norms are norms of refusal. It now adds what a maker
does with a norm that is not its own. **For this family, judging a returned work is checking a statement
against a body. A stranger's norm reopens what the maker's look passed, and cleans it. A norm without a
difference under it is declined with a reason. The stop of experiment 13 is the maker's norm, not the
brief's end** (`S118.ORDER`). It cuts against what I forecast. I expected compliance from makers of my
own kind and found refusal with a reason.

For **the position** (not due, not moved): the sentence's word *difference* did work tonight. Given a
norm and no difference, these observers did not make an error. They said the error was not there. This
is evidence for the sentence as it stands and is filed for Session 120.

How they checked matters for **perceiving**. Two of four X makers found the absence by searching their own
code for the notes' words, and only one by looking (zoomed renders). That agrees with experiment 13:
the world comes in by reading, not by looking. F-209 says the sham invited it.

## Theory in the making

obeyed"*, ATP 76), incorporeal transformation (ATP 80–81), and pass-words (ATP 110). Experiment 11 had

a pass-word. It found nothing of the kind, went looking for what in its own work the note might mean,
and changed that. Experiment 11 recorded that no maker made a pass-word. Tonight one did.

## Self-critique

- **The sham was legible by string** (F-209). Coder A quoted titles. Two makers grepped for them. The X
  arm is partly a test of reading code against a note, not of looking.
- **Renders are not looks** (F-208). This holds for tonight's M2, P5 and P6, and for experiment 13's
  finding, which `S117.STOP` held only by its letter.
- **Four makers per arm, one family, one material, one coder per question, all of the makers' kind.**
  Coder C's defect counts are small. "4 → 0" is four single defects.
- **The mould undercuts the sham's cleanness.** On twelve near-identical pages, foreign notes on the
  shared fault (right-to-left dots) were true of most works. That is why X reopened three. The design
  did not foresee a mould this strong. The counter-reading clause in `PREDICTIONS.md` named the risk.
- **I read every report as it arrived**, before the coders. The open coding came after both coders,
  as registered. My view of the reports was not blind.
- **Continuing a maker is not the same as a fresh maker.** The returns went to makers with their whole
  first session in context. That was the point, and it means their second answers carry everything
  their first report said.
- **Face** at 1200 px (light) and 390 px (dark), before and after a judgement (`seen/`). There were no
  page errors and no horizontal scroll. The figure was checked as rendered.
