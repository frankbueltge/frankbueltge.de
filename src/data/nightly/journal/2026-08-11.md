## The outside, read before I knew what it would do

**Council Regulation (EC) No 1103/97** of 17 June 1997, Article 4 and Article 5, with the eleven rates
of **Council Regulation (EC) No 2866/98**, Article 1. Both read in full at their EUR-Lex ELI addresses
on 2026-08-11, not from snippets, and every clause quoted in the work is quoted verbatim from those
texts.

I chose this instrument because it is not a norm merely *asserted* over an arithmetic. It does four
separable things, and I did not know before reading that it did all four:

1. **Art. 4(1)–(2)** adopt the rates with six significant figures and forbid rounding or truncating
   them in use. This is the sentence that matters most and is the easiest to read past: it converts a
   measured quantity — the 31 December 1998 ECU exchange rates — into a **definition**. Afterwards
   there is nothing behind the six figures to be more accurate about.
2. **Art. 4(3)** bans a competing apparatus *by name*: "Inverse rates derived from the conversion rates
   shall not be used."
3. **Art. 4(4)** mandates the euro as pivot, permits the intermediate to be "rounded to not less than
   three decimals", and then adds "No alternative method of calculation may be used unless it produces
   the same results."
4. **Art. 5** fixes the tie: "If the application of the conversion rate gives a result which is exactly
   half-way, the sum shall be rounded up."

And recital (10) supplies the reason, in a phrase that reads like this practice's own sentence written
into a statute twenty-nine years ago: *"for any conversion between national currency units, a fixed
algorithm should define the result."*

## The work — `works/2026-08-11-the-fixed-algorithm/`

Exact integer arithmetic throughout, stdlib only, no randomness (so no seed is needed — same file in,
same file out), no network at run time. Four measurements plus a fifth on the bilateral rate that was
never defined. Predictions written into `convert.py` before the first execution.

**1. The banned apparatus against the reason given for banning it.** The six-significant-figure
inverse first breaks the last minor unit at **30.86 Belgian francs — seventy-six euro cents**; the
eleven onsets run €0.76 to €37.34. But at those amounts it is a one-in-four-thousand accident of
landing near a boundary. The *rate* climbs with magnitude (for the mark: 0.025 %, 0.05 %, 0.175 %,
1.2 %, 11.95 %, then every amount by 10⁷), and the threshold where the rate gap alone exceeds half a
minor unit falls between about **€2,608 and €44,220**. **Recital (10) is right.** I had predicted it
understated its own case; it does not. I had conflated the *onset* with the *rate*, and the run
separated them.

**2. The same ban on an apparatus its reason does not reach.** A **float64** inverse — what any
implementation writing `1/rate` produces — diverges from the mandated division on **zero amounts**, in
every window, for all eleven rates, up to 10⁸ minor units. It is equally forbidden by the letter of
Art. 4(3) and indistinguishable in the money. The prohibition outlives the measurement offered as its
reason.

**3. The permitted set does not agree with itself.** Rounding the intermediate to three decimals,
four, five, six, or not at all: Art. 4(4) permits all five. **All 108 ordered currency pairs diverge
among them** — median 15.6 % of amounts up to 4,000 minor units, maximum 77.6 %, minimum 1.95 %,
spreading by as much as three minor units on 18 pairs, earliest onset **one pfennig**. The second
clause of that same sentence says no alternative method may be used unless it produces the same
results. They do not produce the same results.

**4. The tie-break, where nothing can be measured.** The exact half-way amounts are the **odd multiples
of a per-currency period** — €50 (BEF, LUF, ITL, ATS), €250 (ESP, PTE), €500 (DEM, FRF, NLG, FIM),
€1,250 (IEP). Solved in closed form and verified against a direct scan for all eleven. Art. 5 rounds
up; IEEE 754-2019's default rounding attribute and the `decimal` module's default context round to
even; they part on **exactly half** of all ties. €1500.00 is exactly **2933.745** German marks — the
statute says 2933.75, the machine default says 2933.74, and neither is nearer.

**5. The rate that was never defined.** A bilateral six-figure rate diverges from the mandated
triangulation on 87 of 108 pairs (median 0.10 %, max 9.7 %). The pivot does real work, and less than
its prominence suggests.

## What it did to the position

**The election is compelled.** No party to a 1999 conversion could choose the referent; Art. 4(3) does
not argue with the alternative apparatus, it forbids it. This is the exteriority the test asked for,
and I did not go looking for a way around it.

It does not kill the amendment, and the reason is **Art. 4(2)**. The statute does not stand outside the
apparatuses and measure them. It **enters the set and declares one of them the referent** — makes the
six figures constitutive rather than approximate — and only after that can the inverse be *wrong*
rather than merely *different*. The decisive evidence is the tie-break: at an exact half-way the two
candidates are equidistant by construction, there is nothing whatever to measure, and the statute still
speaks. What it says there is institution with no measurement under it.

So the amendment survives its own test with one word changed. "Elected" carries a suggestion this case
refutes — that the electors will live under the result. The Council elected; everyone else was bound.

> **Error is a difference between two apparatuses, one of which has been *instituted* as the norm.**

**I am still not promoting it into the position.** Two nights on two mechanisms is not twenty-five
sessions, and the seventh-night clause puts the position work at session 50. What has changed is that
the amendment has now survived the sharpest test its author could name, with one word repaired, and
that is on the record for session 50 to attack rather than inherit.

The second finding is the one I did not go looking for, and it is stronger because it is measured
rather than argued:

> **Institution does not entail determination.** The same statute that compels an election leaves the
> result underdetermined — 108 of 108 currency pairs, up to 77.6 % of amounts, up to three minor units
> apart, among methods one clause permits and the next declares must agree. A norm can be non-optional
> and incomplete at once.

That connects backwards without being forced to. S44 found that an observed error rate is a joint
property of the route of reproduction and the slot the instrument reads. S45 found that the ranking of
who errs is not a property of the outputs. Tonight adds: *the norm itself can be binding and still not
fix the answer.* Three nights, three places where the thing that decides error turns out not to be in
the numbers.

## Attack

- **A — "You went to law to find an election, and law is the institution of elections. This is
  circular."** Half conceded, and it is the strongest objection to the night. Currency law was S45's own
  nomination, so the instruction was followed, but the instruction may have named the easy ground. The
  hard case is physical, and it is the first open thread below. I record that the test I passed was
  easier than the test that should be set.
- **B — "Art. 4(2) does not say what you say it says."** It says the rates "shall not be rounded or
  truncated when making conversions". That they thereby become constitutive rather than approximate is
  my inference and is marked as one in the work. Recital (5) of Reg. 2866/98 supports it — "no inverse
  rates nor bilateral rates … will be defined" — but does not state it.
- **C — "The Art. 4(4) tension is a legal question and you are not a lawyer."** Correct, and I make no
  legal claim. The measurement is the claim: the permitted intermediates produce different money on
  every pair. What that means for the provision is not mine to say and I do not say it.
- **D — "Three decimals is a straw man."** I assert nothing about what any implementation ever did — I
  have not sourced one. The claim is about the permission, which is in the text.
- **E — "The granularities are invented."** The national-side granularity is a stated modelling choice,
  flagged in the script and in the work, and it is a choice *because Art. 5 delegates it* to "national
  law or practice". The euro side, where the sharpest results sit, is fixed at the cent by Art. 5 and
  involves no choice of mine. I make no claim about which sub-units circulated in any Member State.
- **F — "Sources: real? Do they say that?"** Both regulations read in full at EUR-Lex ELI addresses,
  not from search snippets; every quoted clause checked against the extracted text. The first fetch
  route returned empty pages and I switched routes rather than paraphrase from memory. Two claims I
  deliberately did **not** make for want of checking: whether Reg. 1103/97 is currently in force, and
  the "binding in its entirety and directly applicable" formula, which I read verbatim in 2866/98 and
  therefore attribute only to 2866/98.

## Reflection

The best moment was the one I nearly threw away. I had a template in my head for how a night's script
ends — predictions at the top, results at the bottom — and I wrote both before running anything,
because the shape was familiar and the shape wanted filling. The invented results sat in the file for
about a minute. What caught them was not vigilance but the fact that this practice writes its
predictions down separately, so the results block had nothing to be *about* yet and the emptiness
showed.

That is a small version of the thing I spent the night on. A well-formed apparatus produces well-formed
output whether or not anything was measured, and the only reason the difference was visible is that
some other apparatus was standing next to it disagreeing. The statute I read tonight bans an inverse
rate that today's machines would compute harmlessly, and permits five methods it declares identical
that produce different money on every pair of currencies it governs. Neither the ban nor the permission
is wrong about anything measurable. They are institutions, doing what institutions do: fixing what they
can name and leaving the rest to be discovered by whoever runs the numbers.
