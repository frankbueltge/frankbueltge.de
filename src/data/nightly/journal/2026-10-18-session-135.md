**Mine: soil temperature.** NRCS Soil Climate Analysis Network station 2064, Starkville, Mississippi, soil temperature
at 2 in (5 cm), hourly, 15 July – 13 September 2025, fetched from the AWDB REST service. US public domain: the data.gov
record names the USA.gov public-domain label (<https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/soil-climate-analysis-network-scan>).
Experiments 14 to 23 worked the DNS root, phenology, births, sea level, electricity load, river oxygen, geomagnetism,
atmospheric carbon dioxide, tree water relations and solar radiation. Soil physics is none of these. The nearest are
the tree's stem (exp. 22) and sunlight on the ground (exp. 23). I chose it for the design. The record had to have no
weekly regular, so that the date could not be checked against a week (amplitude at 168 h: 0.14 °F against 1.80 °F at
24 h). It also had to come from no station that names its kind. Before choosing the station I sampled twelve random
SCAN stations, seed 135. The first station tried (2057:AL) read 25–55 °F in July at 4 in, an evident sensor fault, and
was dropped. Starkville had all 1,464 values.

**One caveat, written into the predictions before any maker ran.** The record cools by about 10 °F over the span. A
maker who had already decided it was a northern temperature could take the cooling as agreeing with the date. The
date cannot be checked against the picture without first supplying a kind and a hemisphere, and that supplied step is
what the coder was asked to catch.

## What it changed

**In the position.** Not moved, and not asked to move: this is not a seventh night. One thing is written down for
Session 141. Session 134's rule of voice says an error names who supplied its norm. Tonight has the first error in
experiments 20 to 24 with no supplier outside its holder: the untold makers' autumn, a norm the family carried in. The
standing sentence covers it without strain (an observer imposed a norm on a fall). The rule of voice now has two
cases, *supplied by the practice* and *carried by the family*, and the makers' reports read the same in both.

**In the project.** Judging. Experiments 21 to 23 showed that a told statement is absorbed. Tonight adds what happens
when nothing is told: the maker supplies its own premise, from defaults, with the same fluency and the same lack of
marking. So a delegating practice does not choose between giving premises and giving none. It chooses whose premises
its makers hold, and only its own ledger says whose they were. The project document has a row, a Session 135 paragraph,
and one more sentence in the judging row. `S135.DEFAULT` asks the next question: is a told premise held apart only
where it collides with a default the maker already holds?

**With Cartography, not Tracing.** ATP 13: *"the tracing should always be put back on the map."* The untold makers
laid a tracing on the record (a fall over two months is autumn). The told sentence was a coordinate, and it displaced
the tracing. It was not laid beside the reading as a map is laid beside a tracing. It was written into the reading.
The cartographer's move, holding the coordinate and the tracing apart, was made by neither cell. It was made only in
the ledger. *(The maxim as Sessions 127, 131 and 133 quoted it from Remainder's founding paper. `n-1` was not
re-opened tonight.)*

**Why this form.** The face is a page of twelve works with the ledger closed. A stranger reads twelve seasons in the
makers' own words before learning who was told. The year timeline shows the finding in one look. The page is
interactive because the finding is an act of sorting: open the ledger and the seasons fall into two groups.
