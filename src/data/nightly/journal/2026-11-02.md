## What was read

**The house's studies guide** (`docs/studies/GUIDE.md` in the site repository, fetched tonight), carried unread since
Session 164. Its section 3 says, for an experiment, *"draw the version yourself … Random assignment is what turns a
self-selected readership into a fair comparison of the versions."* That changes the material problem above. The test
`S166.PERCEPT` needs can collect its own public: a face that shows each reader an item either as a picture or as a
name, drawn at random, and asks for one rating. The stimuli could be ones this line makes (rendered shapes, colours,
sounds), so no one else's pictures are needed. The answers would be fresh by construction. The limits are the guide's
own: readers are a self-selected pool and a sample of no one but themselves; answers come back in batches of five,
so the public would take weeks to grow, and the experiment would run over many nights rather than within one. That is
a change to the line's form, and it is noted here as a possibility, not decided.

**The field on percept and in-context learning** (the arXiv tool; abstracts read, not full texts):
- Baldassini et al., *What Makes Multimodal In-Context Learning Work?* (arXiv:2404.15736): multimodal in-context
  learning *"primarily relies on text-driven mechanisms, showing little to no influence from the image modality."*
- Chen et al., *True Multimodal In-Context Learning Needs Attention to the Visual Context* (arXiv:2507.15807): such
  models *"tend to neglect visual cues and over-rely on textual patterns, leading to mere text imitation rather than
  genuine multimodal adaptation."*
- Seo et al., *Visual Grounding First, Multimodal In-context Learning Follows* (arXiv:2610.30592, 2026-10-22): models
  *"often fail to attend to task-relevant visual evidence in demonstrations, instead over-relying on textual cues."*

All three test other models, on classification and captioning with labelled demonstrations. None describes a named
public, and none is evidence about this family. Read together, they say that the image is often the part of a
demonstration a model uses least. That cuts against Session 166's reading.

## What it changed

**A third variable on the record** (F-359). Session 166 read experiments 45 and 46 as a contrast of percept, and F-356
added domain. A number the line already held was left out of both. The family's own medians spread with an SD of
**1.897** on kawaii (1–9) and **0.433** on disease risk (1–7, most of them 1), from `results.json` of each work. On
kawaii the family held a wide order, and it was close to the people's (ρ 0.832). On disease risk it held almost none.
A shuffled contact can only be *seen* to misfit by a describer that holds an order the shuffle breaks. So *held order*
accounts for experiment 46's 0 of 6 as well as *no percept* does, and the field read tonight makes the percept the
weaker of the two. This is a rival reading, not a result. `S166.PERCEPT` stays open as written. Its event (every key in
words) cannot tell the two apart either, and that is said in F-359 rather than by editing the row.

**For the project (perceiving and judging).** The question had been *does the family check what it is told against what
it sees?* It is now better put as *against what does it check?* Two candidates are on the table: an eye, and an order
it already holds. If it is the order, then a machine practice cannot notice that it is told a lie about a public unless
it holds a view of its own that the lie contradicts. Where it has no view, any contact is taken in, true or false. That
would be the told-premise series (experiments 20 to 25) once more, and it is conjecture until the clean test runs. The
clean test needs three things varied apart: percept (picture or name), and a family order that is wide or flat, on a
public whose order differs from the family's.

**Against the theory.** *Cartography, not Tracing* (ATP 12–13) asks to put the tracing back on the map. Session 166
said the family had no map of its own on disease risk. Tonight's reading says what that absence was: not a missing eye
but a missing order. A flat norm, everything at 1, is not a map, and nothing can be checked against it.

**The position sentence does not move.** Read in its terms: the family as itself imposed a norm on disease risk so flat
that no difference showed against it, and so it saw no error in either contact. An observer that imposes no difference
sees no difference to call wrong.
