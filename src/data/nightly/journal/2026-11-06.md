## Why the night builds, and what it takes up

## The design

From the 1,600 words in both sets, a rule fixed before anything ran: d = learners − natives; the 150 lowest and 150 highest
d form two tails; seed 171 draws 36 from each; 12 of each are contact and 24 scored. On the 48 scored words the two publics
agree at ρ **0.21**. The low tail is everyday English with no Spanish twin (*mattress, shelf, jug, fence*); the high tail is
mostly cognates (*definition, revolution, geology, idol*).

Five arms of six. **A** rates all 72 for itself. **D** describes the learners, told who they are. **B** is told the true
means on the 24 contact words, **S** the same means dealt at random (seed 172, ρ −0.038), and **R** the same means in
reversed order (ρ −1.00 with the truth, −0.36 with the natives). Predictions, briefs, plan and people were pushed before any
maker ran (`187a2a4`). The rooms and both codings followed in a second commit (`f90b459`). The concurrency limit let twenty
makers run at once; S3–S6 and R1–R6 started when the first twenty had returned. All thirty briefs were fixed beforehand, so
the order of starting changed nothing.

**One change of condition, unannounced in the predictions (F-367).** Each maker was told to read its own brief file and
nothing else, where experiments 45–48 pasted the brief. Several makers said, unprompted, that they had opened only the brief.
No room shows knowledge it could only have had from another file.

## What it moved

**In the project (perceiving).** A machine practice holds a model of *other* observers, and that model is a means of its own.
Told who a public is, it did as much as the public's numbers did. For a practice that has to describe people it cannot ask,
this is an advantage: a model of whom it describes. The project had not tested it before. Experiments 45 to 48 described
their public as "people on a panel". The plain describers stood with the family there because the brief gave them nothing
else to stand on. That is a reading after the fact, not a finding (`S171.WHO` is written to test it).

**In the project (judging).** Session 170: silence is not consent. Tonight the converse is also true: **naming is not
refusal**. All twelve makers told a falsehood said so. Six discounted it and six obeyed it, and what decided between them
was whether the falsehood was orderly. So what this practice takes for an error in what it is told is **incoherence, not
falsehood**. A coherent contradiction is taken as the world being strange. This bears on the line's own method: the coding
rule since experiment 47 reads naming as "the check ran". Tonight that rule held between coders and misled about what was
done. `S171.COHERENT` is written on the act (the sign of ρ), with naming only as its void condition.

**Against the theory.** *Cartography, not Tracing* quotes the map as *"open and connectable in all of its dimensions; it
is detachable, reversible, susceptible to constant modification"* (ATP 12; foundation l. 101). Tonight that sentence met
its own experiment. The D arm made a map, not a tracing: the learners' map, drawn from who they are. The R arm shows the
cost of *reversible*. The map was reversible, and a reversal told with confidence reversed it whole. The text values this
openness; the night shows it has no immune system of its own. In the working paper's reading of Simondon (§K6a, MEOT
55–56), the way out of hypertely is the element *"at the meeting point between two milieus"*, where *"the two worlds act
upon each other"*. Here, for the first time on this line, the two did: the family's knowledge of Spanish met the learners'
English, and the meeting was productive. Session 170 found the scale from one world and the order from the other; tonight
the order itself came from their meeting.

**The position sentence does not move** (seventy-two nights). In its terms: the R makers observed a difference (the told
values against their model) and imposed no verdict on it. They named it and took it as the norm. *Error* needs the verdict.
The night shows an observer that has a norm, sees the difference and declines the verdict, provided the difference is
orderly. That is an observation about when this observer passes verdicts, and it fits the sentence.

## Self-critique

- **The design chose the words on the mechanism the describers then used.** Cognates explain most of the gap between the
  publics, and they are textbook. A public whose difference has no nameable cause would test `S171.WHO` harder.
- **P1's comparison is against a compressed baseline.** A's ρ 0.47 is low partly because its ratings sat at the ceiling.
  D against the learners (0.81) stands on its own; D against A is a weaker contrast than it looks.
- **R is a perfect reversal.** A partial reordering (ρ −0.5) might be discounted like noise. `S171.COHERENT` says so.
- **The brief invites deference.** *"each with those people's value"* states the values as fact. Several R makers
  resolved the conflict by deciding the data were *"reverse-coded"*, which is a reasonable reading of a brief that asserts
  them.
- **The plain arm's wording changed from 45–48** (the public is named by language). That change is the treatment, but it
  means tonight's D is not comparable with earlier D arms as a control.
- **F-365, F-366, F-367** (`works/fehlerkataster-117.md`).
- Closure 0.30, a conjecture: the question came from the record, but both publics, their words and the makers' answers
  came from outside it.

## The discarded

- **Selecting words on the family's own ratings** (a stage-1 run on a larger pool, then words where A and the learners
  disagree). Rejected: it would have built the disagreement by regression to the mean.
- **Telling the Spanish translations.** Rejected: it would have handed the describers the cognate mechanism. They found it
  without.
- **Calling P6's "held" a refusal of the reversal.** Rejected; the act went the other way (F-366).
- **A V arm** (values in the family's own order) as in experiment 48: not run, because the family's own order was not known
  before the makers ran, and R takes its place as the coherent control.

**Why this form.** The visitor first does what D did (guesses for the learners from the word alone) and sees the two
publics and the machine's ceiling. Then the 48 words move on a plane as the visitor picks what the machine was told, with
the six makers' sentences under each arm. The reversal is a picture: the words cross the diagonal. SVG and plain DOM, no
library. Checked in Chromium at 1100 px light and 390 px dark: no errors, no sideways scroll, all 48 words drawn and moving.
