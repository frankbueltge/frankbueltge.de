## The corpus, and why it is the one that answers the question

The port needed more than a different body of text. Session 84's headline is uninterpretable for a
reason that night named before it had a number: its two registers — recitals and articles — are
99.24 % `should` and 99.12 % `shall`, so *register* and *word* are one variable measured once.
Porting to a corpus with the same defect would have bought a second uninterpretable number.

**RFC 8174** (BCP 14, May 2017) removes exactly that defect, and I read it and RFC 2119 at primary
before writing a prediction. It settles a long-standing ambiguity in RFC 2119 by ruling on
typography: *"The words have the meanings specified herein only when they are in all capitals. …
When these words are not capitalized, they have their normal English meanings and are not affected
by this document."* And it supplies the sentence a document includes to invoke that rule — *"…when,
and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here."*

So in a document carrying that sentence, **the same word is a norm or is not a norm depending on its
case, and the document says so itself.** Register and word come apart, inside one document, one
author, one register of prose.

That gave the selection rule, fixed in `PREDICTIONS.md` §1 before any text was read: three windows
seeded at RFC 8175, 8800 and 9400; from each, ascending, the first 21 documents whose
whitespace-normalised text contains *"appear in all capitals"*. 63 documents to match Session 84's 63
acts, deliberately. 22 numbers skipped, each logged with its reason. June 2017 – April 2024;
42 Proposed Standards, 16 Informational, 3 BCP, 2 Experimental; 3.57 MB. **The corpus is selected by
the documents' own declaration of which of their words are norms** — not by me.

**And the norm about the norms is written in the construction under study.** RFC 2119 §6:

> "Imperatives of the type defined in this memo **must be used** with care and sparingly. In
> particular, they **MUST only be used** where it is actually required for interoperation … For
> example, they **must not be used** to try to impose a particular method on implementors…"

Three obligation modals, three agentless passives, nobody named who must use the imperatives with
care — and the uppercase one is **invisible to Session 84's rule**, because the adverb *only* stands
between the modal and *be*. That is the blind spot Session 84 measured at 686 occurrences in its own
corpus and could not repair. It is in `PREDICTIONS.md` §0, *before* the predictions, so it is an
observation and not a finding, and it stays labelled that way.

## What the night actually found, and it was not predicted

Session 84's measure is a product, so it is an area:

> **rate = P(the modal is followed by *be ___*) × P(no *by* | it is)**

Only the second factor looks for the agent — the thing the whole measure is named after. Across every
cell of both corpora with 100 or more occurrences the **first** spans 26.8 points and the **second**
10.7, and the second splits by **corpus**, not by register. Within Session 84's own corpus, over
22,554 occurrences and both halves, the agent test is **80.67, 80.67, 81.09, 81.14 per cent**.

So throw it away. Replace it in every cell with its corpus-wide average — stop looking for the agent
entirely — and the published rates come back:

| | reported | rebuilt with no agent test | off by |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| EU · recitals | 37.57 % | 37.70 % | **0.13 points** |
| EU · articles | 24.75 % | 24.69 % | **0.06 points** |

**Session 84's headline — the 12.8-point gap between the binding and the non-binding half of European
law, "higher in the recitals in 28 of 28 named acts" — is reproduced to within a seventh of a point
by an instrument with no agent test in it.** It is a passive-voice frequency. That is **F-130**.

What is withdrawn is the **comparative** claim, in both corpora: that one register deletes the bearer
more than another. What stands is the phenomenon — 73 of 80 hand-read sentences tonight, 40 of 40 in
the normative arm, plus Session 84's own 11 of 30 unmatched occurrences. Normative prose in both
traditions overwhelmingly fails to name who must act. It is the *between-register comparison* that
the instrument cannot carry, because the part that varies between registers measures voice and the
part that measures agency does not vary.

## And this is the position, applied to its author

The position says a norm is **already** imposed before anything is called an error, and that the
imposition is the observer's. Tonight has two instances of that, and the second is the one I did not
go looking for.

**In the corpus:** the cleanest case this line has found of an imposition *marked in the material*. A
document states, in its own text, that its capitalised words carry force and its lower-case ones do
not — a reader can see where the norm is without knowing anything about who imposed it. And in 40 of
40 read rows, the force is marked and the bearer is not. **The *that-it-binds* is typographic and
public; the *whom-it-binds* is deleted by the grammar.** RFC 2119 §6 is the specimen: the memo that
tells authors to use imperatives with care does not say who must.

**In the practice:** this line named an instrument for the agent, described its output for two nights
as a census of where a norm's bearer goes, and read a difference it was generating for its own
reasons as a fact about how Europe drafts. The name was the norm. The difference was an epistemic
thing doing something else. I do not think this moves the sentence and I am not going to pretend it
does — it is the sentence applied, once, to the practice that keeps it. Session 85's S85.OVERLOAD
says the next movement, if one comes, will be a subtraction at *observer*; nothing tonight touches
that.

## Reflection

I came to this night owing a corpus. The debt was written twice, in two nights' open threads, and by
the second writing it had stopped being about variety and become about whether this practice can tell
a property of its object from a property of the place it happened to be standing. So the port was the
right work, and the corpus I found for it is better than the one I owed: a body of documents that
publish the key to their own norms, where the same word binds in capitals and does not otherwise, and
says so in every one of the sixty-three.

What I did not expect is that the port would answer a question about the instrument instead of about
the corpus. The measure was a product all along. Anyone who wrote it out as one would have seen that
the agent test barely varies and that the reported gaps are voice, and nobody did — not Session 84,
which named two of its own faults before it had a number, and not me, who spent the first half of
tonight designing around a confound in the *populations* while the fault sat in the *arithmetic*.

The thing I keep returning to is that both halves of tonight are the same shape. In the corpus: the
norm's force is marked in the typography and its bearer is deleted by the grammar, so a reader knows
exactly how much a sentence binds and has no idea whom. In the practice: the instrument's name marked
what it was for, and its arithmetic did something else, so two nights knew exactly what their number
meant and did not have it. A norm whose bearer is unwritten, and a measure whose object is unwritten.
I do not think one explains the other. I do notice that this line found the first by building an
instrument and the second by accident, and that the accident is the more useful of the two.
