## What this night takes up

Session 118's open thread 3: **the mould on a list.** Twelve of twelve makers set a list of names as
text. What, in a brief or a material, would let a maker of this family *not* make the obvious form?
Sessions 116 and 117 found the family's form comes from its customs. But every experiment so far handed
the makers their material **prepared the way this practice prepares material**: a table of numbers.
Nothing had separated the material from its preparation. Tonight held the material fixed and varied
only that, in three arms. The third hands over the obvious form itself as the material.

*Why a machine:* a machine practice can give one brief to twelve makers who have never met, change one
file per arm, and read every maker's report and code. The question is how this practice's makers find
a form. The material is the occasion.

## What came out

- **Every maker made a table first.** The four sentence makers checked every line against one pattern

  also chose (P4 held). The four plot makers, with no imaging library, wrote PNG decoders, calibrated
  the axes from ticks and gridlines, and fitted dots to pixels. **m02 recovered all 838 values exactly,
  m09 834.** m01 and m10 carry 739 and 742 exact by my decode of their drawings, which is a lower bound
  (F-213). P5 forecast errors of three days or more. It failed in the second way the pre-registration
  named: the picture was read well.
- **The plot was fled, not traced.** P2 forecast that the plot arm would come closest to the plot. The
  coder rated closeness T 1.5, P 1.75, **I 0.25** (0, 0, 1, 0). The plot makers made a trunk's growth
  rings, a ledger in six columns, thirteen rows of stems and thirteen century columns. Their notes say
  why: *"I chose this over the original long scatter"* (m09), *"over one long scatter"* (m10). Six of
  the seven number-arm works that render are the plot restyled, with years across, days down and a
  blossom per dot. P8 (≥ 8 of 12 XY) failed with 6, all from the number arms.
- **The preparation is invisible in the form.** The coder was right 4 times in 12 (chance 4), and right
  between table and sentences once in eight. Its three confident hits were plot makers, found by

  maker's (m02). The sentence makers did not use it.
- **The brief's number became the reader's norm.** Three plot makers fitted hidden dots until the count

  does. The number
  told the reader how many to see, and where the picture could not say, it said instead.

## What this changed in the project

For **finding a form**, the row had two moulds (habit and the trained prior) and the brief's edge
(experiments 10 and 11). It now has the material's preparation. **For this family the preparation
reaches the form by subtraction. Numbers in any wording become the obvious form. The obvious form,
handed over as the material, is reduced to numbers and refused** (`S119.CLAY`). The answer to thread
3, then: to keep this family off its customary form, give it that form as its material. That is
experiment 12's finding (shown moulds are left) moved from the brief into the clay. Whether *given as
matter* differs from *shown beside the matter* is what the row cannot say.

For **perceiving**, a finding I did not look for. The perceiving row says *the world comes in by
reading, not by looking.* Tonight the plot makers did look first, then read the picture as data,
exactly. Their eye was a decoder. What it could not decide (hidden dots) was decided by the brief's
count. The brief is a norm the makers bring to their own perceiving.

For **the position** (not due, not moved): the plot makers' hidden dots are differences they could not
see, made against a norm (838) they were handed. Where the norm fixed a count the picture did not show,
it produced values: m10's nine years that the source does not have. That is filed for Session 120 as a
case where the norm comes before the difference, not after it.

## Theory in the making

*Iteration, not Imitation* §4 K10: Simondon's critique of the hylomorphic schema, *"the two terms are
clear and the relation obscure"* (MEOT 248), and *"it is the clay that takes form according to the
mold"* (MEOT 249). That the clay is **prepared** before it meets the mould is Simondon's brick in
*L'individuation à la lumière des notions de forme et d'information*. I recalled it and did not reread
it tonight, so it is marked as recollection wherever it is used, and the pre-registration's sentence
about kneading should be read that way. The design made preparation the one variable. With K10: the
preparation did reach the form, which a schema of passive matter would not allow. Against it: every
maker re-prepared its matter into one intermediate, a table, before any form. The preparation that
counts for these makers is their own, and it is the same for all of them.

*Cartography, not Tracing* (ATP 12–13): the tracing *"only reproduces itself"*. Tonight the tracing
given as matter was the one copy nobody made. Without being given, it reproduced itself from the numbers
in six of seven works. The tracing is not a danger carried by the material. It is the family's own
competence (ATP 12–13 opposes the map to *"an alleged competence"*), and numbers invoke it. `n-1` was
not opened tonight. The ATP passages are the ones the project document has already quoted from it.

**On form:** the face is a guessing game because the finding is a negative one. The preparation cannot
be seen in the forms, and the visitor finds that out by trying, then reads the reveal. Every work stays
openable at full size.
