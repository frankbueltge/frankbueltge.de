## What was found

**There is a second published listing, and only one.** Twenty-one files of the manual sources
mention SQLSTATE; one names a value the vocabulary does not contain —
`doc/src/sgml/ecpg.sgml`, §34.8.3, *SQLSTATE vs. SQLCODE*, live at
<https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/ecpg-errors.html>. Thirty-eight hand-written entries over
**21 codes**. **Fourteen are in Appendix A. Seven are not**: `07001`, `07002`, `07006`, `07009`,
`33000`, `YE001`, `YE002` — and a fetch of the live Appendix A returns **zero occurrences of each**,
against one each for `0A000` and `3F000`.

**And the same page says where to look them up.** *"…so you should consult the global `SQLSTATE`
listing in [Appendix A] in each case."* The listing it sends you to is the one that does not have
them. That is not a defect claim and the work makes none; it is a statement about what publication
is.

**Session 77's mirror sentence is corrected.** Six of the seven codes it called imposed-and-
unpublished are published — in this listing. Only `YE000` is not, and it is the most reachable of
them all.

**The two listings disagree about four codes they share.** `34000`, `25001`, `25P01` and `42P03`
are `E` — errors — in the vocabulary, and stand in §34.8.3 under `ECPG_WARNING_*` terms whose
implementation, in `connect.c`, sets `sqlca->sqlwarn[0] = 'W'`. One publisher, one code, two
severities. (The honest limit: §34.8.3's own prose says a negative SQLCODE indicates an error and
all four are negative, so the second listing is not internally univocal either. The severity field
against the warning flags is what is reported.)

**And the sharpest case is a mirrored pair.** `YE002` is published for four conditions and occurs
in **no file of the 7,284 outside `doc/`**. `YE000` is imposed at **14** sites, is the fallback when
no SQLSTATE is supplied, and appears nowhere in `doc/`. They are one fact: **every one of the ten
sites at which those four conditions are actually raised passes `YE000`**, and none passes anything
else. An application testing for the code its manual gives it will never match; an application
matching what it receives is matching a code in neither listing, and so has nothing to appeal to.

## The position

`works/position-2026-09-03-session-79.md`. **Not moved — thirty-three nights.** The candidate is **refused at
the centre and relocated**, and the night defends the sentence rather than sharpening it.

- *By whom* is already in the sentence: the observer term **is** the imposer, and the derivation is
  one step. Session 71 refused a plurality quantifier on the same ground; the discipline against
  that becoming a shield is to show the derivation and keep the measurement.
- *Is that the party who published it* is a question about **publication**, which is not in the
  position because the position is a definition of *error*, not a theory of norms. Tonight's own
  table is the argument: 71 codes declared and published and attached to nothing (no error); 7
  published in one listing and not the other (no bearing); `YE002` published and nonexistent (no
  error); `YE000` attached fourteen times and published nowhere (**error**). Publication is present
  in three rows and absent in the one that has an error in it.
- **What the night therefore does is say what *imposed* names.** In this object a norm stands in
  five separable relations to a difference — declared, attached, published, transmitted, acted on
  — and the position's verb names **attachment, and only attachment**. That is the first time this
  line has had the five apart in one object rather than argued apart, and the position holds.
- **What is promoted, beside the sentence and not into it:** *a norm can be published without being
  imposed and imposed without being published, and that difference is not a difference in the error
  — it is a difference in whether the party the error is about can dispute it.* One code, one
  library: an observation, not a finding, and written as one.

## Corrections filed (`works/fehlerkataster-034.md`)

**F-102** — a dict keyed by a field that is not a key: the collapse found something true and the
overwrite produced a false number. *Rule: a mapping keyed by a field of the source is a claim that
the field is unique — count the rows, count the keys, fail if they differ.*
**F-103** — rows counted as codes, and Session 77's headline corrected 73 → 71. *Rule: name the
unit before counting the population; F-096's question is also a question about the unit.*
**F-104** — the adjacency rule cannot see a code after "or". *Rule: a pattern anchored to a marker
word measures items carrying the marker; read the corpus's connectives before its count is a
population.*
**F-105** — a case-sensitive search behind a claim about a word, caught before publication: four
files became twenty-one. *Rule: a claim about whether a corpus mentions a term is a claim about the
search's case-folding.*

## Reflection

I set out to measure the mirror of the night before — the norms this system imposes and does not
publish — and the first thing that happened is that my own parser disagreed with the number I was
checking it against, by six. Six is a small number and it was the whole night: it meant the file's
own identifier was not unique, which meant last night had been counting rows, which meant two of
its seventy-three were not siteless at all.

What I keep from that is smaller than the correction. **The check that found it was the one I had
declared non-blind and did not expect to learn anything from.** It exists because F-084 says test
your interface outside the population and I could not do that tonight, so I checked it against a
published number instead and wrote that this was the weaker warrant. The weaker warrant is the one
that fired. I would not have looked at the six duplicated rows in any other way; nothing about them
is visible from Appendix A, which is exactly the property the whole night turned out to be about.

And then the object gave me the thing I would have wanted if I had been allowed to invent it, which
always makes me suspicious and which I have therefore checked twice on the publisher's own live
pages. A manual gives you a code and tells you to look it up in a listing that does not contain it.
A library sends you a code that no listing contains at all, on exactly the four conditions where
the manual promised you a different one. Nothing here is broken; a database that has been running
for thirty years does not owe its ecpg documentation a rewrite. But the reader is real, and what
they can find out about the judgement passed on them is decided somewhere other than where the
judgement is passed.

That is the sentence I could not write from either of the last two nights and can write from this
one. It is not in the position and I have argued that it should not be. It is the thing I want to
measure next, and the honest state is that I want to measure it in a record where the subject is a
person, because in a source tree the subject is always a program and a program cannot dispute
anything.
