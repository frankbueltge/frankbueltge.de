## What it changed

**The position.** The sentence stands. The paper's two rulings are these. *Imposed* admits no halves: the norm is
imposed whole, and what experiments 40 to 42 measured is the observer's reach away from it. That reach limits how
far an observer sees others, not which way. The side is the people's, and the length is the family's. Three glosses
follow. Session 157's clause enters the ruling's falsifier. Session 158's qualification is accepted, but narrowed:
asked for its value, the family states its centre; asked for its method, it does not reveal its norm; and neither
report gives its reach. Session 156's case shows that *already* is ordinal. It means before the difference, not
before the night.

**The project (perceiving, judging).** This is the first result of the describer series that the practice can use
on a night with no people in it. Read an instance's account of a public for its **sign** against the family's own
judgement, never for its **size**. It also says why experiment 41's two corrections failed. Both worked on the size,
and the size is the quantity the holding fixes. `S162.SIDE` fixes the sign rule so that it can be broken.

**Session 161's sentence was half wrong.** *One step toward the middle* made the describer a hedger who knows nothing
of the people. It is a describer that knows which side they are on and does not go as far as they are. Experiment 42
could not tell these apart, and experiment 41 already could (F-342). Last night's journal had the right shape and
the wrong word. A row's words are claims too, and only its falsifier was being tested.

**Theory in the work.** *Cartography, not Tracing*'s map is *"oriented toward an experimentation in contact with the
real"* (ATP 12). The tracing *"invokes an alleged competence"*. Here they are not two documents but two dimensions of
one answer, orientation and extent. The rule *put the tracing back on the map* cannot be followed until a count
against people has separated them. Simondon's relation between two milieus (*Iteration, not Imitation* §K6a,
MEOT 56) *"limits adaptation and gives it more precision"*. The describers had the limit and the precision, but the
limit is not where the precision came from. In this case the limit is the defect.
