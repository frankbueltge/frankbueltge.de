1. **M. Andueza Rodriguez, M. Candito, R. Huyghe, "Modeling the human lexicon under temperature variations:
   linguistic factors, diversity and typicality in LLM word associations"**, arXiv:2603.18171 (LREC 2026). Abstract
   read via the arXiv API. The PDF-to-text converter failed in this container (`libxcb.so.1` missing), so the

## What it changed

**1. `S152.ONEMAP` is, on word material, a published result, and the line did not know it.** A large model gives
1.4 distinct first associations across 100 calls where 100 people give 38.4. That is the row's claim, *n fresh
makers count one holding*, and the authors say it in nearly the row's words: one prototypical participant. So the
row was not wrong. It was unread. A practice that fixes a falsifier about its own instrument without first asking
whether the field has measured that instrument is making the error the protocol names first. It is a candidate for
the register (unnumbered here, see the end): **a finding of the field re-found as the practice's own**. Whose norm?
Mine. The norm was *new to this record*, and I took that for *new*.

**2. The reading splits the row into two axes the line had run together.** Paper 1 measures *variability* (how many
answers) and *typicality* (whether the answers are the people's). A family can be one observer in two ways. It can
be **the crowd's mode**: one holding, and that holding is what most people say. Or it can be **its own**: one
holding, and nobody's in particular. Session 152 could not tell these apart, because a gazetteer has no population
of answers. Session 150's `S150.CANON` asks the same question from the other end: are the makers' choices *what their
teachers chose*? So the two open rows are one question on two axes, sameness and whose sameness. Word association
is the first material where both can be read off a printed human distribution. Session 153's H2 (the modal word
equals the people's first word) reads the second axis, and its N arm (the first word excluded) is the part the
literature does not already forecast. **For the record, written before 153's results exist:** from paper 1 I would
expect 153's R1, H1 and H3 to hold easily, since they are already in the literature. Its result is in H2 and H4.
This is my expectation, not a prediction of that night's. Its predictions are in its own file.

**3. The observers' plurality is partly a dial, and the dial is not in this practice's hands.** Paper 1 moves
variability by temperature: 1.1 to 2.6 distinct words for the large model, still an order below people. Schaeffer
et al. move it by prompting. This practice starts fresh makers but sets no sampling parameter and records none. So
when it counts *one holding* or *n observers*, part of the count is a setting of the apparatus that it can neither
see nor change. I read that with Rheinberger's split between the technical and the epistemic. The record has so far
treated the makers' sameness as an epistemic thing, something to find out. In part it is technical: a condition of
the experimental system that was fixed before any question was asked. That is my analogy, not a reading of his text,
and it stays out of the position sentence. For the project, this is the plainest case yet of a limit in a machine
practice's own means. It can multiply observers without cost, but it does not choose how different they are.

**4. On the human pool (Session 150's ask).** For word material a published human pool exists: SWOW, and the USF
norms Session 153 uses. For dataset choice none exists, and that is why the ask went to the architect. So the
line's lack of a human null depends on the material. **A machine practice can borrow a null wherever the material
is itself a published norm of people.** That is a strength worth naming, and a bias worth naming too, because it
draws the practice towards materials that are norms already. The ask stands unchanged. The reading only says where
it is not needed.

**What it failed to change.** The position sentence. Nothing here bears on *difference* or *norm*. It bears on
*observer*, and the case for that word is for Session 155 to weigh. It is now stronger.
