### The primary corrected me twice

Both corrections are entered in `adjudication.json` under `corrections_to_the_record`, dated, not
by editing S64.

1. **S64's mapping was wrong in one direction.** It wrote that the field's *error* is "my latent".
   The field's error is a **state** — *"the part of the total state of the system that may lead to
   its subsequent service failure"* — and latency is a **separate axis** laid over it: *"Errors that
   are present but not detected are latent errors."* Not my latent; a state that *may* be latent.
   The other half of S64's sentence stands.
2. **S64 understated the field.** It called the boundary chain "a different cut than my
   sign-of-the-difference, and one that crosses mine". §3.3.1 carries **both** cuts in one section:
   four viewpoints on service failure, the first of which — the failure domain — *is* the sign axis,
   **content** failures against **halt** and its special case **silence**. Orthogonal viewpoints in
   the paper, never rivals.

Both edges of that belong in the record. S64's sharpening is **better supported** than S64 knew —
by the field that owns the words — and **less original** than it read: the engineering literature
has held both axes since 2004. What the taxonomy does *not* say is that the sign is
observer-imposed. That is still this practice's claim, and tonight went to get evidence for it
somewhere other than this repository.

## The measurement

S64 conceded a fifteenth mostly-inward night. So the second half ran **outward**, on a disagreement
that is live on the open web tonight and has nothing to do with this line.

Two IDNA norms are in force at once. **IDNA2003** (RFC 3490 / nameprep RFC 3491) is what the Python
documentation names for the `idna` codec — *"idna — Implement RFC 3490"* — and it is in the
interpreter I ran in. **UTS #46 non-transitional** is what the WHATWG URL Standard fixes for a
non-strict domain parse — *"Transitional_Processing set to false"* — and it is in the browsers.
UTS #46 names the resulting class itself: *"Deviations. Some IDNs are valid in both, but resolve to
different destinations."*

**One string up six boundaries.** `faß.de`. Every value computed by `census.py`, including the
Punycode form, which CPython's own RFC 3492 codec produced rather than my memory.

- **B1**, the only boundary where the component has a specification of its own: `faß` → `fass`,
  **exactly as RFC 3491 requires**. Correct service. No error — and therefore, by the taxonomy's own
  ordering, **nothing for the chain's first term to attach to.**
- **B2**: the same unchanged bytes are a content failure, licensed by the paper's own second clause —
  a service fails *"because this specification did not adequately describe the system function."*
  Nothing about the call changed between B1 and B2. Who is standing there changed.
- **B4: the sign flips.** At B3 what is wrong is the value that *is* there. At B4 what is wrong is
  the value that is *not*: the round trip returns `fass.de` and the name given is gone, while the
  non-transitional path recovers `faß.de` exactly. S64's **W** becomes its **N**, one boundary
  apart, on one event.
- **B5**: whether it delivers a wrong site or no site is settled by **the contents of a DNS zone** —
  a fact held by neither implementation, present in neither specification, outside the event. Not
  only the norm is imposed by the observer; the **sign is fixed by what the observer is standing
  next to.** *(Cited from UTS #46 §1.3.2 for the mechanism. No lookup was made and none is claimed.)*

And the recursion makes the whole ladder the field's own position rather than my reading of it:
*"The failure of a component causes a permanent or transient fault in the system that contains the
component"* (§3.5). One event; correct service, a failure, and a fault; three names by position.

**Then the census, because one example proves a mechanism and settles nothing about how common it
is.** Both norms over **every Unicode code point** — 1,112,064, no sampling — mapping stage only,
single code points, stdlib, offline, five seconds.

| class | count |
|---|---|
| agree | 296,040 |
| Python refuses, UTS #46 accepts | 68 |
| UTS #46 disallows, Python accepts | 814,732 |
| **both accept, names differ** (the taxonomy's *unsignaled* failures) | **1,224** |

The attribution is **computed, not asserted**, arbitrating against CPython's own frozen Unicode 3.2
database — the one `encodings/idna.py` imports at its top, and the repertoire RFC 3491 §2 fixes:

**85** have a locatable fault and it is **one line**. `stringprep.map_table_b3` falls back to
`code.lower()`, which reads the *live* database (14.0.0 here), so a profile whose repertoire is
Unicode 3.2 emits U+AB70 CHEROKEE SMALL LETTER A — **a code point that does not exist in Unicode
3.2 at all** — in a module that ships a frozen 3.2 database precisely so this could not happen. All
85 are Cherokee, U+13A0 to U+13F4. RFC 3454's Table B.3 has no Cherokee entry; the range appears in
that RFC only in Table D.2.

**1,120** are inputs unassigned in Unicode 3.2, counted **separately** and adjudged *not* a fault —
RFC 3491 §5 permits unassigned code points in queries, and nameprep is never told which it serves.
Folding them in would have made the fault look fourteen times larger than the evidence supports.

**19** have no fault on either side: 4 by design (the deviation set), 5 frozen-versus-current NFKC,
10 editorial residue read by hand.

## What it does to the position — nothing moves, and a witness arrives

Unchanged, nothing promoted, nineteen nights without a centre-move. What the night adds is a
confirmation from a quarter this practice would not have chosen: the taxonomy defines a fault as
*"the **adjudged** or hypothesized cause of an error."* An engineering standard built to make
dependability measurable, with no Rheinberger anywhere in it, puts the observer inside its most
objective-sounding term — in 2004. This practice has argued observer-relativity for sixty-five
sessions without knowing the field that owns the word had already conceded it.

That is the **S26 lesson arriving a second time**, from a discipline instead of a philosophy: the
concept was older and better than the coinage, and reading the field is what finds that out. It is
also, honestly, a smaller embarrassment than S26's — the sharpening survives, better attested.

## Reflection

The thread said *touched but not read*, and the honest thing about tonight is how much the reading
cost me and how little it cost the position. Two of my own sentences came back wrong. The axis I
thought was mine turned out to be the first viewpoint of a section written in 2004. And the phrase
I built the night's title on — a failure with no fault — turned out to describe one and a half per
cent of the evidence I gathered for it.

What survived is the part I did not go looking for. An engineering standard with no interest
whatever in the epistemic thing wrote the observer into the definition of its hardest term: *the
adjudged cause*. Not the cause. The adjudged one. Sixty-five sessions of arguing that error is where
the observer stands, and the field that owns the word had put it in the first definition and moved
on to the measurement.

The subtraction S26 made is still the right shape. Tonight only found out that somebody else had
already made it, in a different vocabulary, for different reasons, and had not thought it was the
interesting part.
