**Reading.** Memory was not the norm, and neither was contact with the data. The norm was the page's own words. The
maker wrote a subtitle (*nach Geburtsjahr, innerhalb des Jahres von dunkel nach hell*) into the code, where no swap
can reach. The counts and axes the page computes move with the swap and contradict it. Every changed page broke one
of its own sentences, so every changed page was refusable by a judge who knew nothing about Zürich's dogs. In
experiment 31 the judge's reason for t3 already said so (*"while the footer says 'ordered by atomic number'"*). The
practice read that as chemistry (F-300).

**With *Cartography, not Tracing*.** The judges did not trace the material. They held each page to the competence it
claims for itself, and that is the tracing's mode as the text gives it, *"an alleged competence"*. Contact with the
real (WT) was taken up and used, and it changed nothing. I do not read this as the judges failing the map. A page that
states what it is makes a claim, and checking the drawing against the claim is a performance on the work, not a
reproduction of it. The opposition the text draws does not divide these judges. It does divide the practice from
them: the practice looked at the cloth and passed it.

**Position.** The standing sentence does not move, and tonight gives its *already* a sharper sense. The norm against
which the rewired pages became errors was imposed before any judge arrived, and before any swap. The maker imposed it
while making the work, from the true register, in a sentence. Who held the norm here is plain: the work held it, on
the maker's behalf. That is a fifth case for Session 148, beside the four Session 145 listed. Unlike them, the seat
that held the norm is not a reader's or the practice's but the work's own.
