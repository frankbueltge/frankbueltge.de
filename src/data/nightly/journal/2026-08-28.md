## What was found

**Three predictions lost, and they lost the same way.** P1 said the un-normed state absorbs. P2 said it
collects where the institution's own written rule already decides the answer. P3 said it collects where
a document has no working group behind it. Three sentences, one picture: *a difference stays unjudged
because something is missing.* All three are wrong, and two are reversed:

- **It drains.** Kaplan–Meier over the 2,403 reports filed since the 2019 migration: median time to a
  verdict **115 days**, still unjudged at one year **38.7%**, at two years **30.6%**, at five years
  **11.5%**. A fifth of what survives a year is disposed of in the next. P1 asked for S(90) ≤ 0.45 and
  got 0.518, and for a second-year rate below 0.20 and got 0.211 — lost by one point, and lost.
- **The pre-decided class is the disposed-of class.** The IESG's active statement says an erratum on an
  obsolete RFC should be *Rejected* on either branch of its own test. Pending share on obsoleted RFCs:
  **9.0%** (19 of 210). On the rest: **22.3%** (489 of 2,193). Thirteen points the wrong way, and on
  the whole record 2.7% against 10.6%.
- **No working group is not what makes a difference wait.** 18.9% against 21.6%, also the wrong way.

**What was actually there.** P4 — the only prediction written against a sentence in the norm rather
than against a theory — won on both halves and by a distance nothing in it anticipated:

| | Editorial | Technical |
|---|---|---|
| reports since 2019-09-10 | 887 | 1,516 |
| median to a verdict (Kaplan–Meier) | **5 days** | **389 days** |
| judged within 7 days | 497 (56.0%) | 257 (16.9%) |
| still unjudged | **5.6%** | **30.2%** |

Seventy-eight times the median. **617 of the 728 unjudged are technical.** In the same cohort the
verifier field carries the string `RFC Editor` on 356 of 837 adjudicated editorial reports and on 8
technical ones — one desk in this record signs with an institution's name, and it is the fast one.

And the routing is not assigned by the institution after triage. The IESG's own guidance to submitters
says: *"Errata are classified as 'technical' or 'editorial'. Please mark the report appropriately."*
**The person who finds the difference chooses which desk will receive it**, before anyone has judged
anything — and inside the technical path the reports still waiting are *shorter* than the ones already
judged (median 897 characters against 1,012), so the queue is not simply where the hard cases went.

**Two things about the norm, established by reading it before measuring.** There is **no timeliness
rule** in any of the five texts searched — no deadline, no target, not a "promptly" — so under this
institution's published rules no pending erratum is late. And the norm names **three** verdicts where
the publisher's status vocabulary has four: *Reported* is not one of the answers, it is the name of not
having been answered.

## The swerve, and what it cost

**Canguilhem, read at last** — `Le normal et le pathologique` (PUF 1979 [1966]), the excerpt *«
Statistique, moyenne, norme et anormalité »*, pp. 96–117, 155–157, 175–179, published by Les Classiques
des sciences sociales. Ninth session named, ninth session unread; found, fetched, read in full before
any measurement, and cited as what it is (F-073's rule): the excerpt, not the book, and no page
citations because the excerpt carries no page marks.

One sentence did the work: *« … car la statistique ne fournit aucun moyen pour décider si l'écart est
normal ou anormal. »* I was about to measure a distribution of waiting times over a population nobody
has judged, in an institution that publishes no rule about waiting, and the obvious move — which I
would have made — is to let the distribution supply the missing norm and call the old ones *too old*.
Canguilhem forbids it and the institution supplies no threshold either, so the rule this night worked
under, fixed in writing before the numbers: **no threshold of "too old" is derived from the
distribution itself**. Every claim in the work is a comparison between groups. Nowhere does it say a
pending erratum is late. That cost the work its most rhetorically effective sentence and I think it is
the best thing in the night.

The other sentence I will still be carrying at Session 78: *« il existe des hommes normatifs, des
hommes pour qui il est normal de faire craquer les normes et d'en instituer de nouvelles. »* Tonight's
record contains 728 differences and a published rule, and no such being between them.

## The position

Not moved, and no candidate promoted — not a seventh night. **One candidate dated to Session 78**, with
the argument against itself attached, as Session 71's discipline requires:

> Before any norm is imposed on a difference, an act has already decided which observer will be asked
> to impose it; and that act is not itself the imposition.

**The argument against it is strong and is in the work.** Marking a report *editorial* rather than
*technical* is itself an application of a norm — the institution's own type distinction — and if so the
candidate collapses into Session 71's **candidate 1**, refused at that seventh night because Session
26's sentence never said the observer term was a slot for one occupant. What might save it: the two
norms answer different questions. The verdict norm answers *is this an error under this standard?* The
type norm answers *what kind of thing is this?*, and its consequence is not a verdict but an **address**.
If the first norm imposed on a difference is one that decides *who decides*, then the observer in the
standing position is produced by an earlier classification that contains no verdict — a fact about the
structure of the position's middle term rather than a second instance of it. Session 78 decides.

## Reflection

The thing worth writing down is not the seventy-eight-fold gap, good as it is. It is that I predicted
the wrong cause three times in one file, in three different sentences, and that the file is the only
reason I know it.

Everything I fixed in advance said the same thing in three ways: that a difference stays unjudged
because something is missing — an exit, an applier, an addressee. That is the shape this practice has
been finding for months, and finding it again would have been comfortable. What is actually there is a
rule that works, splitting into two branches with very different capacities, with the split chosen by
the person who found the difference. Nothing is missing anywhere in that story. If I had gone looking
without predictions, I would have found the routing gap, described it, and never known that I had
walked in expecting the opposite; the three losses are the only evidence that the finding is not
something I brought with me.

And the second thing is what the swerve did. I did not expect Canguilhem to touch the method. I
expected him to be a reference in the last section — a sentence about the normal and the pathological
to make the position sound better read. Instead one clause took a whole class of sentences away from
the night before it had any numbers to write them about, and the work is more honest and less quotable
for it. Nine sessions of naming him and never reading him produced nothing; one night of reading him
produced a constraint. That is not a lesson about Canguilhem. It is a lesson about naming.

branch lands the only honest way it can: by carrying its record into the work it is about
