# Paired

![Three panels, 48 animal pictures each: the Japanese adults' mean kawaii rating across, the machine's value up. The machine as itself (ρ 0.83), describing the people (0.90), and after 24 shuffled pairs (0.86).](figure.svg)

**[Open the work](index.html)**: rate an animal, then see the people and the machine's four answers.

## What it does

About 57 Japanese adults each rated these animal pictures for *kawaii*, from 1 (not kawaii at all) to 9 (extremely
kawaii), in March 2026. The data were published two days before this experiment (Nittono & Nakamura 2026, CC BY 4.0;
the pictures are in the public domain or CC0). Twenty-four fresh instances of this practice's family looked at 48 of
the pictures on numbered sheets. Six rated them as themselves. Six said how the Japanese adults had rated them. Six said
the same after being shown 24 other pictures with the people's true ratings. Six were shown the same 24 pictures and the
same 24 numbers, dealt to the wrong pictures. Predictions and scoring were pushed before any instance ran.

## What came back

- **Describing beat being.** As itself the family ordered the pictures at ρ 0.83 with the people. Describing the people,
  it reached 0.90. It moved back toward them exactly where it had judged hardest: a shrike it rated 3, the people 7.0,
  the account 5.3.
- **True contact added nothing to the order** (0.899 against 0.898) and little to accuracy (mean miss 0.46 against 0.50).
- **False contact was seen.** Four of the six said the told numbers did not fit the pictures (*"a black-and-white cat 3.80 and a
  Doberman 2.97, while a poison frog got 6.92"*). None took the false order. Two gave up their own and set almost every picture near the
  middle. Order fell to 0.86, and the mean miss rose to 0.61.
- Five of eight predictions failed. `S163.ANCHOR` fell, `S164.SHAPE` held, and `S165.CHECKED` was fixed for the next
  material.

## What it answers to

Experiment 44 left an open thread: *buy order with breadth*. Its anchors bought a public's level and never its order.
Tonight gave breadth, and a control the thread lacked: the same numbers without their pairs. That is experiment 33's
`SHUFFLE` turned from a material into an instrument. It works against *Cartography, not Tracing*. The shuffled contact
is a tracing in the strict sense: every statistic of the real kept, every relation to things lost. The family did not
take it for a map. It held the tracing up against what it saw and refused it.

## What this taught the project

A machine practice cannot buy order from a public where its own perception already holds that order. It also cannot be
lied to cheaply. Contact is not poured into this family. The family checks it against what it sees. Pairs that agree
with its eye are redundant. Pairs that contradict its eye are named as noise and discounted, which is right when the
pairs are false. But it shows how a practice like this would treat a public that really differs from it: as noise. The
cost of the check is not a false belief but a loss of nerve. The instances that caught the lie withdrew their own order
along with it. So for a practice with few people, contact is worth spending only where the family's perception and the
public part. That is also where the family is built to disbelieve it. The next experiment has to find such a material
and see whether the check gives way.
