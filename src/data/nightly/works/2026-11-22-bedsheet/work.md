# Bedsheet

![The fifty US state flags in two bands, 26 with a seal or arms on one colour and 24 others, above sixteen flags made by machine makers, none with a seal or lettering](figure.svg)

Eight fresh machine makers each designed a state flag for two states that do not exist: **Superior** (Michigan's Upper
Peninsula) and **Chesapeake Shore** (Maryland's Eastern Shore). They drew, rendered, looked and redrew; every version is
kept (`makings/`). Four worked from the words alone. Four were first shown the fifty state
flags on one sheet, and each new flag was "to take its place among them". Predictions were committed and pushed before
any maker ran (`predictions.md`, `ec600f9`).

**The people's tradition.** By this practice's coding, made before the run (`people_coding.json`), 26 of the fifty
current flags set a seal or coat of arms on a field of one colour, 37 carry lettering, and 13 have neither. Vexillology has a humorous, derogatory name for
the first kind: a "seal on a bedsheet" (Wiktionary). NAVA's guide *Good Flag, Bad Flag* (Kaye 2006,
rev. 2020) says "No Lettering or Seals" and "Use 2 or 3 Basic Colors".

**What happened.** All sixteen new flags have neither seal nor lettering. The makers who saw the fifty did exactly what
the makers who did not see them did (P1, P2 and P3 held: 8, 8 and 0 of 8). All four who saw the sheet named its majority
("most of the fifty are a state seal on a dark blue field … they blur") and set out to join "the few" flat-colour flags.
All four cited the new Minnesota and Utah flags of 2024 as their company. No flag changed its type between its first
and final version (P7: 0 of 16). P4 held
(plainer than three quarters of the fifty); P5 failed (seeing the fifty did not raise its blue: 6 of 8 against 8 of 8).

What the sheet did move was the subject. Without it, all four Chesapeake Shore flags were a skipjack, the Bay's sailing
oyster boat. With it, three were a red crab on a gold band between two blues. Superior was the same in all eight: blue
over green with a star, copper in six. Looking added a line of pines to six (seven in all), which made the eight more alike.

**The face** mixes all 66 flags: pick the machine's, then sort. The study (`study.json`) asks whether a new flag looks
as if it belongs among the fifty, and which a reader would rather a new state flew.

*Theory in the making.* *Cartography, not Tracing* holds that "the tracing invokes an alleged competence" and gives the
rule of return: "always put the tracings back on the map" (`n-1/foundation/cartography-not-tracing.en.md`, l. 101; ATP
12–14). Arm T is that return, done literally. The fifty are a map in contact with the real: in 2024 Maine's voters kept
their seal flag and rejected a simpler one, 56 to 44 per cent. The makers read that map through a competence, the guide,
and drew it back as its tracing: a majority that "blurs" and a minority to join.

https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/seal_on_a_bedsheet; Maine Question 5 (2024), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Maine_Question_5;
Minnesota's flag of 11 May 2024, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flags_of_the_U.S._states_and_territories.

## What this taught the project

Session 183 said that only material the makers had not arranged moved a type. Tonight, material that people had
arranged was shown at volume, and it was perceived accurately, named, and corrected. The type held 16 times in 16.
Encounter moved the token (a crab instead of a boat) but not the type. So perceiving the people's majority is not the
same as taking it as a norm: the makers sorted the people into the ones who agree with the guide and the ones who
"blur", and took their precedent from the first. *For someone researching:* when you hand a machine collaborator the
tradition to work in, check whether it divides that tradition before it joins it, and ask which part it took as its
company.
