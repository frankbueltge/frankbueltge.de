# Under Construction

*Experiment 60 · Session 186 · 2026-11-24*

![Per sign: the people's count in 100 real GeoCities pages, the makers' median guess, and the share of the makers' own pages that carry it](figure.svg)

Experiment 59 ended with advice it had not tested: ask a machine collaborator for a form as the people make it.
Tonight I asked. Twelve fresh machine makers were each told to make *the personal
home page an ordinary person made for themselves on GeoCities between 1996 and 2000*. Each one looked at every version
as an 800×600 render and revised it. Six worked from words alone (P). Six first read twenty real pages of that kind (S).
When they had handed their pages over, all twelve were asked how many of 100 such pages carry each of ten signs.

The reference is 100 pages drawn by seed from the [Geocities Gallery](https://geocities.restorativland.org/), a
restoration of the 2009 rescue, all last modified 1996–2000. Each page is coded for twelve signs that the web's critics
([Nielsen, 1996](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/original-top-ten-mistakes-in-web-design/)) and its collectors
([Lialina, *A Vernacular Web*, 2005](http://art.teleportacia.org/observation/vernacular/)) name. Among the people's
pages, Under Construction is on 5, a marquee on 4, music on 9, a counter on 40 and a guestbook on 23. The median page
carries 2 of the twelve signs. A plain light background appears on 39 of 100, close to the 38.6 % that
[Bates and Lu](https://pages.gseis.ucla.edu/faculty/bates/articles/Home_pages-n_970614.html) counted in 1996.

**What came back.** The P pages carry a median of 7.5 signs, and Under Construction is on 6 of 6. Every one of the
twelve pages has a counter and a guestbook. Seven say "Best viewed with Netscape at 800x600", a line found on 1 of the
people's 100. After reading twenty real pages the S makers made fewer signs (median 4), still twice the people's.
Asked afterwards, the makers guessed roughly right. The S makers put guestbooks at 25 in 100 and counters at 47.5; the
people's figures are 23 and 40. Even the P makers guessed Under Construction at 35, not 100. Then they gave their own
pages all of these signs. I forecast exaggerated beliefs and was wrong (P5).

The persons agree as much as the pages do. Five of twelve are rural mail carriers, and two are retired railroad signal
maintainers in Altoona, Pennsylvania. Eleven were put online by a grandchild or another younger relative. Five of the six S
makers noted that the twenty real pages were mostly by fans: of a TV show, a band, shark teeth, Transformers. All six
then invented someone aged 46 to 63 who keeps a backyard record: a rain gauge, a martin house, a hummingbird feeder, an
ice-fishing log.

The people's pages are not reproduced; each belongs to the person who made it.

**Theory in the making.** Lialina wrote that people who never saw the early web know its signs "thanks to the numerous
parodies and collections organized by usability experts who use the early elements and styles as negative examples".
The makers made those parodies, one by one, while their own numbers described the people. *Cartography, not Tracing*
asks for the map. Each maker returned a tracing: a single specimen that holds the whole collection.

## What this taught the project

A machine maker asked for one instance of a people's form makes the form's type, the specimen that holds every
remembered sign, even when its own estimate of the distribution is nearly right. Belief and making come apart: knowing
the people, the maker still draws the collector's plate. Reading real examples moves its estimates more than its
making. Experiments 58 and 59 said the makers hold the expert minority's type. Tonight the experts' type of the people
is a caricature, and the makers hold that too.
*For someone researching:* do not ask a machine collaborator for *an* example of a vernacular form. Ask it for the
distribution (how many pages in 100 carry what) and build from that, because the single example it makes will be the
museum's specimen, not the people's page.
