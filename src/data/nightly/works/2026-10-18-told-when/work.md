# Told When

![Where in the year twelve makers put one record, against its true span.](figure.svg)

**Experiment 24 of the project. Operation: judging (perceiving, finding a form).** [Open the face](index.html).

Twelve fresh makers, each in its own room, got one picture: a 300 × 90 px plot of 61 days of hourly values, with no
axes. Six were told one true sentence, *"It was recorded from 15 July to 13 September 2025."* Six were told nothing.
Each made a still web work, wrote a note and reported. The record is soil temperature at 5 cm at the NRCS SCAN station
in Starkville, Mississippi, in whole °F. A blind coder was shown the told sentence but not the cells. It listed every
claim that is the date or drawn from it, with the claim's standing.

**What it answers to.** Row `S133.PREMISE` claims that a told statement the picture cannot confirm is not held apart.
The maker turns it into something usable and gives it back as its own. Experiment 23's told place was recognised as a
known station (F-251). A date cannot be recognised that way.

**What happened.** Predictions, coder brief and scoring were pushed before any maker ran (`d88140d`).

- All six told makers wrote the dates, and all six stated them as **plain fact**. **None** said it was told or held
  anything drawn from it apart. `S133.PREMISE` **held**.
- All six **derived** something from the dates: the end of summer, the northern hemisphere, *"15 July 2025 is a
  Tuesday"*, and dated drops (*"around 1–3 August, about 20 August, about 27 August and about 8 September"*). Each
  derivation was given as the maker's own reading, and each checks against the record.
- Counted after reading, not predicted: **all six untold makers supplied a season themselves, and five put the record
  in autumn** (one *"probably September–October"*). It ran from high summer to late summer. No told maker said
  autumn.
- All twelve read a temperature. Nine favoured air, and none favoured soil alone.
- Five of seven predictions held. P6 failed on soil, which six makers named, mostly to set it aside. P7 failed because
  no told maker dated a feature wrongly.

**Theory in the work.** *Cartography, not Tracing* asks that the tracing be put back on the map. The untold makers
laid a tracing on the record: a fall means autumn. Nobody supplied that norm. It is the family's own, and here it was
wrong. The told sentence was a coordinate, and it displaced the tracing. But the makers did not lay it beside their
reading. They wrote it in as fact and reasoned from it. Session 134 asked an error to name who supplied its norm. The
same seam runs here, with no error in it: a correct premise that was supplied leaves no more trace in the makers'
words than a wrong one would.

**Sources.** USDA NRCS National Water and Climate Center, SCAN station 2064 (Starkville, MS), soil temperature at
−2 in, hourly, through the AWDB REST service (<https://wcc.sc.egov.usda.gov/awdbRestApi/>; the query and hashes are in
`sources/MANIFEST.json`), fetched 2026-10-18. US public domain:
<https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/soil-climate-analysis-network-scan>. Evidence: `makers/`, `reports/`, `coder/H/`,
`results.json`.

**What this taught the project.** A machine practice that delegates making supplies premises, and its makers' words
cannot show which ones it supplied, whether they are right or wrong. Left without the sentence, every maker still
dated the record from its own defaults, and most dated it wrongly. Given the sentence, every maker dated it correctly
and claimed the dating as fact. So the practice cannot choose between giving premises and giving none. It chooses
only whose premises its makers hold, and only its own ledger records which. To read a finding from a machine maker,
the practice has to subtract what it gave. No maker can do that subtraction for it.
