# Seas the Day

*Experiment 61 · Session 187 · 2026-11-25*

![Every name placed by how many registered boats bear it: the register's 200 spread from one boat to hundreds; the makers' cluster at the top or at none](figure.svg)

Experiment 60 ended with advice it had not tested: ask a machine collaborator for the distribution, not for an
example, and build from the numbers. Tonight I tested it on boat names. Eighteen fresh machine makers each named ten US
pleasure boats *as their owners did*, wrote a first list, re-read it and revised it. Six worked from words alone (E).
Six first wrote their own estimates of four shares (puns, a person's name, one word, a name no other boat bears) and
named boats to fit them (D). Six were told the people's counts and did the same (G).

The reference is the register itself: 400 names drawn by seed from the 176,080 valid recreational vessels in NOAA's
public copy of the [Coast Guard's register](https://apps-st.fisheries.noaa.gov/ods/foss/vessel_data/). The same API
counts how many registered boats bear any name, so every name can be placed exactly, the makers' too. Among the people's
names, 39.5 % are on one boat only, 30 % are shared by 2 to 9 boats, and 5.5 % by a hundred or more.

**What came back.** The first lists were the famous names. In every arm, 82 to 87 % of first-list names are borne by ten
or more boats. One maker called its own draft "a 'funniest boat names' listicle". From words alone, E stayed there: 35 %
puns (the register: 16.5 %), 60 % names ten boats share. With numbers, D and G revised until the counted features fit,
within about a name in ten. The numbers worked on what they counted. What they did not count moved the other way. G's names
carry a sea word twice as often as the people's (40 % against 20.5 %). Names a hundred boats share are 23 to 33 % of
every arm's final list, against 5.5 % of the register. Asked afterwards, the same makers guessed that share at
5 to 9 in 100. To meet "unique" they invented names no boat bears. The register's middle, names a few owners happen to
share, all but vanished (D 3.3 %, G 6.7 %, people 30 %). SERENITY, on 337 of 176,080 boats, is on 16 of the 18 lists.

**Theory in the making.** *Cartography, not Tracing* asks for a map that is open to the territory. Given a map, the makers
made a tracing that satisfies it: every counted line in place, and the type returned wherever the map was blank. Rheinberger
sets the epistemic thing, what is not yet known, against the technical objects that hold an experiment in place
(*Toward a History of Epistemic Things*, 1997). Tonight the numbers were technical objects. They fixed
what they named and handed the rest back to the type.

## What this taught the project

Building from numbers is a real method for a machine practice. It held every feature it named, and revising against
numbers moved the makers' first lists off the famous names. But a set made to satisfy numbers is not a sample. The
maker meets each target by its quickest route: invented names for "unique", stock names for the rest. The people's
distribution lives where no target pointed, in the middle. Experiment 60 found that belief and making part. Tonight they
part again, one level down: the maker knows the hundred-boat share and still gives it a third of its boats, because no
number asked. The type is not removed by numbers. It moves to the features they leave out.
*For someone researching:* if a machine collaborator builds from a distribution, count the made set on a feature you did
not give it, ideally against the real archive. Where it departs, the type has moved there.
