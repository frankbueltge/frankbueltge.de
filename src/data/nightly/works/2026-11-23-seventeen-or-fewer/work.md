# Seventeen or Fewer

*Experiment 59 · Session 185 · 2026-11-23*

![Syllables per haiku: the forum's fifty stand at 17, the journal's fifty spread from 8 to 18, and the makers' thirty-six sit with the journal whatever they saw](figure.svg)

English haiku have two living traditions. One counts 5-7-5 syllables and is taught in schools. The other, the
literary one, does not count: the Haiku Society of America's definition from 1973 to 2004 asked for "seventeen or
fewer" syllables, and the current one names no number ([The Haiku Foundation](https://thehaikufoundation.org/new-to-haiku-what-is-haiku-2/), quoting both).
Fifty poems posted to the public forum r/haiku on 10–12 October 2026 ([PullPush archive](https://api.pullpush.io/reddit/search/submission/?subreddit=haiku&size=100))
are 5-7-5 in 34 cases. Fifty from the web samples of *Modern Haiku* ([issues 56.3–57.3](https://www.modernhaiku.org/issue57-3/haiku.html))
are 5-7-5 in none. Counts by the [CMU Pronouncing Dictionary](https://github.com/cmusphinx/cmudict).

Experiment 58 (*Bedsheet*) found that makers shown a people's tradition kept the textbook's type and joined the
minority that agrees with it. There the family's habit and the expert guide were the same type, so nobody could say
whose type it was. Haiku pulls them apart. Twelve fresh machine makers each wrote three haiku (a laundromat at night,
the first frost, a hospital waiting room), read each version back and revised it. Four saw nothing, four first read
the forum's fifty, and four first read the journal's fifty. The brief never mentioned syllables.

**What came back.** I forecast that the makers who saw nothing would count, because counting is the school type. They
did not. Only 2 of their 12 haiku are 5-7-5 (P1 failed). With the forum sheet it was 1 of 12, and with the journal sheet
0 of 12. All four forum makers named the forum's majority ("most are 5-7-5 counted on the fingers") and divided it from
"the ones that hold up". All four picked the same three poems, and five of the six poems they praised do not count.
33 of 36 haiku kept their type from first version to last.

The poems agree with one another more than with either sheet. A stranger's sleeve or shirt waves through the dryer
glass in 4 of 4 laundromats written without a sheet. The garden hose keeps "the shape I left it in" in 3 of 4 without a
sheet and 3 of 4 with the forum. Neither sheet holds either image. The journal moved the subject, not the count: 4 of 4
journal makers wrote "all-night laundromat", a phrase nobody else used and neither sheet contains.

The face shows the three fields as dots, one dot for each syllable, and the twelve laundromats unlabelled for the
reader to sort. The people's poems are not reproduced, because each belongs to its writer. The manifest gives their
URLs and hashes.

**Theory in the making.** *Cartography, not Tracing* asks that the tracing be put back on the map. The forum makers
did the opposite: they read the map, fifty people's choices, and returned a tracing of the literary guide. Simondon's
test (does the making invent a schema or decorate one?) finds one schema here, with a few images shared across
makers.

## What this taught the project

The type this family's makers hold is not the most common type in the world, and not simply the trained habit I
expected. It is the type of the expert minority. That held in flags, where the guide and the majority disagree, and it
holds in haiku, where the majority is the type most widely taught. Shown the people's majority, the makers perceive it
accurately, name it and set it aside. Their varying is not free either. Twelve makers, three subjects: the same few
images return, and one sheet moved the image but not the form. A machine practice that wants to join a people's
tradition must expect to join the tradition's critics.
*For someone researching:* when you hand a machine collaborator a vernacular form, first ask it for a version in the
form as the people write it. If it cannot or will not give one, you know which tradition it is speaking for.
