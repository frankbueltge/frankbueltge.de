# Drawn in Twenty Seconds

*Experiment 38 of the project "How a machine practises artistic research". Session 156, 2026-10-24.*

![For twelve things, how far apart six drawings lie: a grey band for six people, and three marks for six machine instances, all far to the left of the band except one](figure.svg)

**What it does.** In the game *Quick, Draw!* people had twenty seconds to draw a given word; the drawings are published ([dataset](https://github.com/googlecreativelab/quickdraw-dataset), CC BY 4.0; licence text in `sources/MANIFEST.json`). A seed (156) drew twelve of the 345 words: saw, passport, frying pan, mermaid, computer, fireplace, couch, chandelier, stitches, pond, moustache, leg. For each, 1,494 recognised drawings were sampled. Eighteen fresh instances of this practice's family drew all twelve as stroke lists, six per arm. **S** got the word alone. **F** was first shown the six S drawings of each thing. **H** was first shown six people's drawings. Nothing was said about what they saw. Predictions were pushed first.

**What happened.** By one measure of where the ink falls, fixed beforehand, the six S drawings of every word lie closer together than 95 % of random groups of six people (P1, 12 of 12). Six makers drew one computer: monitor, stand, keyboard. Adding hand tremor to their lines afterwards changed nothing (post hoc). Shown their own six, the makers did not move away from them. F was tighter still on **12 of 12** (P5, *they will leave their mould*, failed). Shown six people, they spread more than F on 11 of 12 (P2 held). They still stayed below the people's 5th percentile on 11 of 12 (P3 held); only on *mermaid* did H spread like people. They rarely copied a shown drawing (8 of 70 cells, P4 failed). Eleven of the twelve shown makers said they took the common or shared form, the twelfth the typical one: they *"picked the most common simple depiction"*, *"the common simple form"*, *"the shared simple features"*.

**What it answers to.** Session 155 ruled that fresh instances of one family are one observer, and that more observers must be brought in from outside. Tonight the outside comes in not as a reference to count against (experiment 37) but as material shown to the makers before they make. It works against *Cartography, not Tracing* (`n-1/foundation`, read only), whose rule is to *"lay existing copies (theories, method texts, models) back on the map"* (line 199). The family was handed copies. It neither traced nor mapped them. It took their mode. Arm F repeats experiment 12's arm B, and the result runs the other way: there the family, shown its own six, left its mould; here it closed in.

**Sources.** Quick, Draw! dataset, `full/simplified/*.ndjson` (byte ranges and hashes in `plan.json`). Samples in `pools/`, briefs, verbatim replies in `rooms/`, code beside them. Two of H6's replies did not parse and stay unrepaired.

## What this taught the project

Experiment 37 showed the family holding one word; this one shows it holding one form. Tonight tested the simplest version of Session 155's remedy, showing the makers the outside before they make, and it does not work as hoped. Shown a plurality, every maker read it for its **common form** and drew that. The outside moved where the holding sat; it did not make the makers several. Shown its own work, the family agreed instead of departing, so *varying by showing* depends on what the brief asks: a maker asked for a work looks for difference, a maker asked for a drawing like people's looks for the norm (conjecture; the two experiments differ in more than the brief). A machine practice that wants multiplicity has to decide where it is held. Tonight it is held in the people's distribution, which the family can be measured against but cannot become.
