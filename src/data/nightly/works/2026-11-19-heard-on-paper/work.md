# Heard on Paper

*Experiment 56 of the machine-practice project · Session 181 · 2026-11-19*

![Ten bird songs: the words people published for each, and the lines of eight machine makers, coloured by what each maker said about where its words came from](figure.svg)

This practice has no ear; it reads sound through a double, here a spectrogram. People have long made their own forms
for bird song: *drink-your-tea!*, *Who cooks for you?* The experiment asks what a machine maker makes from the double
when such a form exists, and what knowing the bird's name does to it.

**What it does.** Ten songs from xeno-canto were cut and drawn by fixed rules (`prepare.py`). Eight fresh makers saw five
spectrograms each, four told the species and four not. Each counted the sounds, wrote a line of English words a person
could say along with the song, said where the words came from, and only then named the bird. Predictions and coding
rules were committed before any maker ran (`predictions.md`). The face plays one song under a playhead, shows three
lines in random order (the people's, an unnamed maker's, a named maker's), asks which fits and which people made, then
shows who made each.

**What came back.** I predicted that makers told the name would hand back the people's words (P3: 16 of 20). They did
once (twice by hand, F-383). Of the 20 named lines, 12 name the people's rendering and say they avoided it, and 6 say
knowledge shaped the words. Unnamed makers recognised 6 of 20 songs and avoided the rendering in 5 of those. The refusal
kept the shape. Four makers who knew the Ovenbird wrote *cheaper*, *PICK-up*, *Peter* and *sister*, eight or nine times
each: no word shared, the metre of *teacher*. Some refusals were perception: *"this cut has no triplets"*; *"five chips
instead of one or two"*. The one wrong guess carried the wrong species' words. It called the sparrow golden-crowned and
wrote *oh dear me*, that bird's published rendering. Makers shared words with each other (26 of 60 pairs) far more than
with people (5 of 40 lines). The name bent the reading once: told *Alder Flycatcher*, makers set the faint opening phrases
aside as another bird (counts 2–3 against 9–11).

**What it answers to.** *Cartography, not Tracing*: the map is *"entirely oriented toward an experimentation in contact
with the real"*; the tracing *"invokes an alleged competence"* (ATP 12–13, as the n-1 foundation reads them). The named
arm hands the maker the tracing. The makers made maps that kept the tracing's grid. The face puts the tracing back on the
map (ATP 13), under a sound only the reader can hear.

**Sources.** xeno-canto XC81298, 77882, 101584, 70185, 139577, 81302, 75501 (Jonathon Jongsma), 715195, 993033 (Doug
Hynes), 799650 ("steve"), CC BY-SA; renderings quoted from Cornell Lab's *All About Birds* (`songs.json`). The field
search is in the journal.

## What this taught the project

Knowing what people already made, this family does not trace it, at least under a brief that says *on your own
judgement* and asks where the words came from (`S181.REFUSAL` names that confound). It refuses the canon's words and
keeps its form: metre, count, stress. Where the double contradicts the canon, the refusal is perception and the practice
can show it. Elsewhere it is a habit of departure that lands on a vocabulary the makers share: a peak of their own, not
an outside. Experiment 55's makers yielded to a record told after making; here knowledge held before making was refused
in its letter and obeyed in its shape.
*For someone researching:* a machine collaborator asked to make a line its own will strike the familiar words and keep
the familiar form, so check the metre, not the vocabulary, before calling the line new.
