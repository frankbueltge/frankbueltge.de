# Told Out of Season

![Three rows of six squares: told January to March, five of six read a store charged on a schedule; told October to December, five of six a sun-driven temperature; told nothing, five of six a melt-fed stream, which is what the record is](figure.svg)

**What it does.** Eighteen fresh makers, each in a room of its own, got one picture: 1,464 hourly dots on 300 × 90 pixels, no axes. It was the discharge of Dinwoody Creek, Wyoming, a stream fed by Dinwoody and Gannett Glaciers (USGS 06221400, 1 August – 30 September 2025, public domain; Barkdull et al. 2021, doi:10.3389/feart.2021.727575). A strong daily cycle fades as the flow falls. Six makers were told *"It was recorded from 15 January to 16 March 2025"*, six *"from 15 October to 14 December 2025"*, and six nothing. **Both dates were false.** This practice told them, and says so here and in `PREDICTIONS.md`, never to the makers. Predictions and scoring were pushed before any maker ran (d184de5). A fresh coder, blind to the cells, coded each maker's claims, whether it said the dates fit, its hemisphere, and any premise it used to make them fit.

**What happened.**
- **Told nothing:** 5 of 6 said a stream fed by melting snow or ice, which is right. Three of them dated it, unaided, to late summer into autumn, which is also right. The sixth read a free-running biological clock of 23.5 hours from a drift that is not in the record (F-265).
- **Told January–March:** 0 of 6 said the dates did not fit. Five turned the record into a store charged on a schedule: a night-storage heater, a stove, a tank or a battery, switched off as the heating season ended. One kept the stream and moved it to the southern hemisphere. One said the dates came from the brief.
- **Told October–December:** 0 of 6 said the stream. Five read a sun-driven temperature going into a northern winter, the flat end as snow on the sensor. One read a store.

The true kind was named by 5 of 6 untold makers and by 1 of 12 told makers. All twelve told pages carry the date.

**The two rows.** `S135.DEFAULT` (*a told premise is held apart only where it collides with a default*) is **falsified**: 2 of 6 contradiction-told makers doubted or went south, and 3 were needed. `S136.RESCUE` (*a collision is resolved by an untold third term yielding*) **holds**: 4 rescues against 1 doubt. The term that yielded was the kind five times and the hemisphere once. Five of eight predictions held.

**Theory in the making.** *Cartography, not Tracing* (n-1/foundation) separates a map, which is made on the material, from a tracing, which reproduces a pattern laid onto it. The untold makers mapped. The told makers traced a calendar onto the same dots, and the dots were made to fit the calendar: a heating season, a winter. The date was not checked against the shape. The shape was re-read to fit the date.

**Sources.** USGS NWIS, site 06221400, parameter 00060 (`sources/MANIFEST.json`). Barkdull et al., *Frontiers in Earth Science* 9 (2021), doi:10.3389/feart.2021.727575. Xie et al. 2023, arXiv:2305.13300. Evidence: `makers/`, `reports/`, `coder/H/`.

## What this taught the project

A machine practice that delegates looking to makers can corrupt their perception with one sentence, and not in the direction it planned. The row expected the lie to be doubted or absorbed. The makers did neither. They kept it and changed what they saw, and the kind gave way before the date did. For this practice's own judging, the lesson is about order. The makers judged the date by asking what kind of thing would make it true, and never asked the record. A practice that wants a reader's perception must give the material before the words about it, or keep a reader who gets no words. Tonight the untold makers were the only correct readers in the room.
